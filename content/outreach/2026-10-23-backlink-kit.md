## Draft replies

### 1. Seasoned Advice: dime test with a glass bowl

With a glass bowl, KitchenAid says to skip the dime. Its support pages say the dime test is meant for stainless steel bowls only. Glass bowls are made differently, so the clearance comes out slightly different, and KitchenAid says to adjust without the dime until you're satisfied with how it mixes. It also notes yeast doughs can take a bit longer to come together in glass.

On the coin question, the dime is only a rough gauge. A US dime is 1.35 mm (0.053 in) thick, a little under the 1/16 in clearance America's Test Kitchen gives. The test watches whether the coin gets nudged slowly on each pass, not whether it fits through. If you do adjust, KitchenAid says the screw turns no more than 1/4 turn either way, so make small turns.

Disclosure: it's my site. I put KitchenAid's pages and the places where sources disagree on screw direction in one place here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/kitchenaid-dime-test/

### 2. Seasoned Advice: air fryer after 3 years in storage

You shouldn't need to open it up. The Ninja AF101, Cosori Pro LE and Instant Vortex Plus manuals all say never to immerse the main unit. They say to wipe the inside with a slightly damp, non-abrasive cloth instead.

For the element, Ninja's guide says to brush food residue off it with a cleaning brush. Keep water away from it. America's Test Kitchen says not to get the element very wet, and Cosori's manual says the coil must be completely dry before you switch it on. Don't use steel wool. Cosori warns that fragments of a metal pad can break off and touch electrical parts.

The basket depends on the model. Ninja and Cosori list it as dishwasher safe, but Instant's Vortex Plus manual says damp cloth only.

Disclosure, it's my site: I compared the three manuals side by side here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer/

### 3. Seasoned Advice: do you need an oven if you have an air fryer?

For heating, an air fryer is a small convection oven. Nebraska Extension describes it as an element above the food plus a fan that moves hot air quickly. Consumer Reports calls it the same technology as convection. The difference is size. That puts the heat close to a small basket, which is why it crisps small batches fast. It's also why it can't replace an oven for everything.

Capacity is the limit for baking. Prudent Reviews puts typical baskets at 2 to 6 quarts, and Consumer Reports says a range holds more than twice as much food as a standard air fryer. Frigidaire positions oven convection for baking and roasting on several racks. Sheet-pan dinners, multi-rack baking and a whole bird still need the oven. I also couldn't find any published test that cooked the same food in a basket fryer and a full-size convection oven, so the evidence here is thin.

My site, for disclosure: the side-by-side is here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/air-fryer-vs-convection-oven/

### 4. Seasoned Advice: generic citric-acid descaler

Follow the instructions on the product you actually bought. The coffee maker manuals I've read that name a commercial descaler tell you to use it according to that descaler's own directions. Moccamaster's US manual says to use Urnex or Durgol "per its directions," and OXO's 8-Cup manual says to follow the solution's label. Brand descalers aren't all plain citric acid, either: Urnex's Dezcal ingredient sheet lists citric and sulfamic acid.

One caution worth knowing. Moccamaster Europe warns against citric-acid descalers on its brewers because of their copper heating element, while Moccamaster USA names Dezcal. Other brands aren't covered. I found no independent test of vinegar against citric acid or commercial descaler. Rinse advice also varies, from one to three full reservoirs of water depending on the brand.

Disclosure: my site. Manual-by-manual comparison: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/

### 5. Coffee SE: slow Mr. Coffee

Seven minutes isn't automatically a fault. The SCA home-brewer benchmark, as CNET and Moccamaster report it, is 4 to 8 minutes for a full pot. Ninja calls 12 to 15 minutes normal for its CE251. What matters is whether yours used to be faster.

If it has slowed down, mineral scale is the usual cause. America's Test Kitchen says scale gradually narrows the tubes, so descale it first. If it's still slow after descaling, the top iFixit answers for slow Mr. Coffee brewers point to the one-way valve being clogged with grounds or residue. One owner found paper towel lodged in the intake hose.

As for brewing short and topping up with hot water, I haven't seen anyone measure that, so I can't tell you whether it fixes the bitterness.

It's my site, for disclosure: the brew-time table and fixes are here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/why-is-my-coffee-maker-slow/

### 6. Coffee SE: drip ratio calculations

Your math is fine. The surprise is the "cup." Coffee maker cups aren't 8 oz. OXO's manual defines a cup as 5 fl oz, Ninja's CE251 marks 12 cups at 60 fl oz (5 oz each), and Moccamaster uses a 4-oz European cup. So your ~60 oz reading is normal.

For dose, the SCA Golden Cup figure, as America's Test Kitchen reports it, is 55 g of coffee per liter of water. ATK calls that 1:18. For about 1.77 L that's roughly 97 g, which is close to your "mild" number, so that is the standard strength, not a mild one. If it tastes too strong, the National Coffee Association's range is 1 to 2 tablespoons per 6 oz, and its low end is about half the SCA dose. ATK also recommends weighing over scooping, which you're already doing.

Disclosure, my site: the cup-size and ratio table is here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/coffee-to-water-ratio-drip/

### 7. Coffee SE: drip maker without plastic

The honest answer is that I couldn't find one documented. None of the sources I went through identify an auto-drip machine with a fully plastic-free water path. iFixit's DCC-3200 page shows how many parts touch the water: the reservoir, the hose under the base, the filter basket and the carafe lid.

The closest thing to a material claim is Moccamaster's. Its EU blog (Nov 2025) says the plastics in its machines are free of BPA, BPF and phthalates. That covers the brand as a whole, and it names no testing body. Polypropylene, a common appliance plastic, is cleared for food contact in the US under 21 CFR 177.1520.

If no plastic is the hard requirement, a manual brewer with a glass, ceramic or steel path is the practical route. One study still found BPA and BPF in French press coffee, which suggests some comes from the coffee itself.

My site, for disclosure: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/are-plastic-coffee-makers-safe/

### 8. iFixit: Ninja Professional 1100 W flashing red

On Ninja's corded blenders, a flashing light usually means the safety interlock is keeping the motor off. Check these in order:

1. **Lid and pitcher seating.** The top answer in an older iFixit thread on the 1100 W model says to line up the arrow on the lid with the arrow on the handle. Ninja's BL610 guide says the same.
2. **Overload or overheat.** Unplug it and wait. Ninja's BN700 guide gives about 15 minutes for an overload reset and about 30 for a thermal reset, so wait the full 30.
3. **Worn interlock parts.** Owners report that the plastic piece on the handle can slip out of place, or that the lid tab that presses the switch breaks off. Ninja says not to bypass the interlock. Replace the lid or pitcher, or call Ninja at 1-877-646-5288.

Also check the model number. The 1100 W BL663CO is one of 12 models in CPSC recall 16-034 (Nov 2015) for a loose stacked blade assembly.

Disclosure: it's my site. Light patterns by model: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/ninja-blender-blinking-lights/

### 9. iFixit: Tefal Actifry not working and smoking

First, unplug it and keep it closed until the smoke stops. The air fryer manuals I've read (Cosori, Instant and Ninja, not Tefal) all say that. If there are flames, NFPA's advice is to get out, close the door and call 911.

Then look at the color. White or grey smoke is usually fat or steam hitting hot metal, and Cosori's manual calls it normal. Dark or black smoke means food is burning or there's a circuit problem, according to Cosori. Instant lists food residue on the heating coil and low-smoke-point oil as other black-smoke causes.

Since yours has also stopped working, treat it as a fault, not a cleaning problem. All three manuals say to stop using a unit that malfunctions and contact the maker, so I'd contact Tefal support before switching it on again.

My site, for disclosure: smoke colors and causes: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/why-is-my-air-fryer-smoking/

### 10. The Fresh Loaf: C-hook vs spiral

From what I've found, the shape alone doesn't seem to decide it. TechGearLab kneaded the same whole-wheat sandwich dough in several mixers. The KitchenAid Artisan's C-hook and the Aucma's spiral both finished in 7 minutes. The Cuisinart SM-50's C-hook took 13, and the KitchenAid 7-qt bowl-lift's spiral took 10. The mixer matters as much as the hook. I couldn't find anyone who compared both hook shapes on the same machine, so it's still an open question.

On dough riding up the hook, KitchenAid's support page says climbing usually means kneading is done. Egg doughs and rye climb more easily. KitchenAid suggests adding flour a tablespoon at a time without overdoing it. ATK credits the Ooni's breaker bar with stopping the climb, which fits what you read about spirals pushing dough down.

Disclosure: my site. I wrote up hook vs paddle vs whisk here: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/dough-hook-vs-paddle/

### 11. The Fresh Loaf: first stand mixer for ~300 g batches

At 300 g of flour, I'd worry more about the mixer being too big than too small. Two points from published tests:

- **Usable space is smaller than the label.** ATK measured every bowl it tested and found none used its full volume. Its rule of thumb is to subtract about 2 quarts from the stated capacity.
- **Big bowls can struggle with small amounts.** TechGearLab found some 6-qt mixers couldn't whip a single egg white or half a cup of cream because the whisk didn't reach. That matters for panettone, with its egg-rich stages.

On Ankarsrum vs KitchenAid, the labs don't agree. ATK's overall winner is the Ankarsrum Assistent Original, while CNET's top pick is the KitchenAid Artisan Plus. Look at the minimum batch, not just the maximum, in whichever manual you're considering.

Disclosure, it's my site: labeled vs measured capacity table: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/what-size-stand-mixer-do-i-need/

### 12. Seasoned Advice: how do you put out a grease fire?

Adding the sourced steps, since several answers here disagree:

1. Turn off the burner, but only if it's safe to reach.
2. Slide a metal lid or baking sheet over the pan. NFPA, Montgomery County Fire and Rescue and a Lincoln County, NC fire handout all say to slide it. Lincoln County says to use metal because a glass lid can shatter.
3. Don't move the pan. Burning oil splashes.
4. Leave it covered until it is completely cool. The lid works by cutting off oxygen, and lifting it early lets air back in.
5. Never use water. Lincoln County says water or milk makes burning grease explode into a fireball, and Essex Fire and Rescue says even a single drop can cause a flash fire.

Baking soda works only on a small fire, and Lincoln County says it takes a lot. No source gives an amount. If the fire spreads beyond the pan, get out, close the door and call 911.

Disclosure: my site, with each source linked: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/how-to-put-out-a-grease-fire/

### 13. Seasoned Advice (protected): blending hot liquids

It depends on the blender, and the manuals disagree:

- **Vitamix 5200:** allowed with caution. Don't fill to max, lock the lid with the lid plug in, start on variable speed 1 and work up slowly.
- **nutribullet Combo pitcher:** allowed with the vented lid cap on, below the MAX line, on LOW.
- **nutribullet sealed cups:** never. The guide says heated ingredients can pressurize a sealed container and "violently expel hot contents."
- **Ninja Professional (BL660 guide):** not intended for hot blending. Cool soups to room temperature first.

The lid blows off because hot liquid expands and builds steam pressure under a sealed lid. So a vented lid, a part-filled jar and a slow start matter most, and only on a blender whose manual allows hot liquids. If yours doesn't, let the liquid cool first.

Disclosure: my site. Manual-by-manual table: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/

### 14. Seasoned Advice (protected): is a coffee maker "cup" 6 oz?

No, and in current manuals it's usually smaller than 6 oz. OXO's 8-Cup manual defines a cup as 5 fl oz. Ninja's CE251 marks its 12-cup line at 60 fl oz, which is also 5 oz per cup. Moccamaster uses a 4-oz "European cup," so its 10-cup brewer holds 40 oz. Cuisinart's Canadian DCC-3200C booklet doesn't state its cup size at all. There's no single standard across brands.

The 6 oz on your coffee bag probably comes from brewing guidance rather than machines. The National Coffee Association's ratio is 1 to 2 tablespoons of ground coffee per 6 oz of water. So measure the water itself, or weigh it, rather than counting cups.

Disclosure, my site: cup sizes by brand: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/coffee-to-water-ratio-drip/

### 15. Seasoned Advice: 325 W vs 575 W stand mixer

Wattage is a poor guide. The number is power drawn from the outlet, not power delivered to the dough. Hamilton Beach's manual defines its rating as Underwriters Laboratories' maximum stabilized power consumption at high speed under load. Some listings print a peak start-up figure instead, which can't be compared with a rated one.

Independent timings don't follow the watts. In TechGearLab's tests, the 325 W KitchenAid Artisan whipped a cup of cream in 1.83 minutes and kneaded whole-wheat dough in 7. The 660 W Aucma took 3.50 and 7. The 500 W Cuisinart SM-50 took 2.67 and 13.

So choose on bowl size, build and how it handles the dough you'll actually make, not on watts.

My site, for disclosure: wattage vs measured results: https://kitchenwatcher.com/guides/stand-mixer-wattage-explained/
