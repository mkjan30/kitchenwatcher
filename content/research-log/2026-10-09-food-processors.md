## Recalls
- Cuisinart/Conair 17-050 (2016-12-13): about 8 million riveted blades in Cuisinart processors with model numbers beginning CFP-9, CFP-11, DFP-7, DFP-11, DFP-14, DLC-5, DLC-7, DLC-8, DLC-10, DLC-XP, DLC-2007/2009/2011/2014, DLC-3011/3014, EV-7/10/11/14, KFP-7, MP-14. Only four-rivet blades. Sold July 1996–Dec 2015, $100–$350. 69 reports of blade pieces in food, 30 mouth or tooth injuries. Remedy: stop using the blade, free replacement (877-339-2534). Covers the DFP-14 prefix of our kept model: older or used units need the rivet check.
- Cuisinart 14-029 (2013-11-21): about 25,000 MFP-107 7-cup processors; the slicing/shredding disc can loosen and crack the cover. Free replacement lid and disc.
- KitchenAid 01-004 (2000-10-05): about 220,000 5- and 7-cup processors (KFP300, KFP350, KFP450 families; serials WJG–WJK33); blade cap can dislodge into food, 2 choking reports. Free replacement blade unit.
- Hamilton Beach 14-193 (2014-05-28): about 790 (US) bébé baby food makers; lid pin can loosen into food. Not a kept model.
- Others in the API: Walmart/GE 14-cup 169203 (11-227, 2011; interlock failure, 21 fingertip injuries, 3 fires; refund), Mainstays mini chopper MS14100094536S1 (24-178, 2024; blade can run during assembly; refund), Ultimate Chopper (05-258, 2005; interlock; 1.5 million), Mueller onion chopper (manual, 21-017), Moulinex bowl covers (1986, 1989).
- No food processor recall for Ninja/SharkNinja, Breville, Magimix, Hamilton Beach full-size or KitchenAid since 2000 in the API. SharkNinja's 25-247 (Foodi pressure cookers) and 16-034 (BL660 blenders) are not food processors.

## Notes for drafting
- Wattage is a marketing number here: the Cuisinart's 720 W made nut butter in 19 min, the 450 W Hamilton Beach in 10 min (TechGearLab). Label every wattage as a spec.
- ATK measured blade-to-bowl gaps: about 2.9 mm side / 2.2 mm bottom on the best models vs 6.1 / 5.4 mm on the worst. Good table material.
- Thermal reset times differ by brand (manufacturer): Hamilton Beach 15 min, Ninja about 15 min, KitchenAid 5–15 min, Breville 30 min.
- CNET's food processor guide is dated April 2025 (older than its "2026" title).
