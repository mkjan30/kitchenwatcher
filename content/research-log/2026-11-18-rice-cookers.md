# Research log — rice cookers (2026-11-18)

Scope: countertop electric rice cookers (conventional one-switch, micom/fuzzy logic, induction heating, pressure rice cookers) plus grain cookers that are sold and tested as rice cookers (KitchenAid Grain and Rice Cooker). Multicookers and pressure cookers are out of scope (see /best/pressure-cookers/); the Instant Pot rice page is /guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/. The 2026-11-13 pressure-cooker log listed starting candidates (Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy, Micom, Induction; Tiger Micom; Aroma 8-cup; Instant Zest; Hamilton Beach Digital); all were checked below.

Method: research.md scoring (sources 7 each, cap 35 / Amazon reviews log scale, 10k = 25 / price fit 20: $60–$250 full, under $60 = 15, $250–$400 linear to 0 / released in the last 24 months 10 / complaint volume 10). ## Primary sources (readable)
- Zojirushi rice-cooker FAQ, https://www.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers: parts and accessories "not dishwasher safe" (Q5); parboiled/converted rice turns mushy (Q6); starch film prevented by rinsing until clear (Q7); keep warm: "for most rice cookers, 12 hours is the maximum", check your manual (Q8, Q20); soak about 30 min, steam about 15 min, which is why micom cookers are slow (Q9); no beans (Q11); conventional vs micom vs IH vs pressure IH (Q16); ratios for micom models: long-grain white 1 : 1¼ on Mixed, wild rice 1 : 1½ on Brown, quinoa 1 : 1¼ on Quick, not valid for conventional models (Q19); the cup is about 6 oz, about ¾ US cup, and capacity is uncooked short-grain rice (Q22); a basic cooker shuts off when the pot passes 212 °F (Q23); quick cycles cut up to 20 min (Q25); tested to 5,000 ft altitude (Q31).
- Zojirushi NS-ZCC10/18 manual, https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf: H01/H02 = lid or center sensor too hot, wait for it to cool; H04 = inner pan not in place (beeps 4 times, shows 5 s); E01/E02 = breakdown, contact service; clock blinking 7:00 = lithium battery weak (4–5 year life); steam leaking between lid and body = deformed inner lid or cut gasket. Troubleshooting matrix maps hard / half-cooked / too soft / scorched / boiling over / odor during keep warm to causes (wrong water level, objects on heating plate or sensor, deformed pan, unwashed rice, lid not closed, inner lid not attached, keep warm over 12 h or small amount kept warm, cold rice reheated, spatula left in pan, wrong menu). Keep warm: don't exceed 12 hours, don't add cold rice, don't keep anything but white rice warm, don't leave a spatula in; Extended Keep Warm at about 140 °F. Cleaning: don't soak the pan long (top can rust); wet #320 sandpaper only on the heating plate and center sensor; no vinegar in the pan, no brushes or abrasive cleansers; coating may discolor with use without affecting nonstick or sanitary properties; contact service if pan or inner lid is deformed or corroded. Regular white 48–55 min (1.0 L).
- Zojirushi NL-BAC05/10 manual, https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf: nonstick coating "can peel off if damaged" (no whisks, metal strainers, metal ladles, vinegar, dishwasher, nylon or melamine sponges); peeling "will not affect the cooking / Keep Warm performances or its sanitary properties, and is harmless to your health"; replace the pan if concerned. QUINOA and STEEL CUT OATMEAL water levels must only be used with those menus or it may boil over. Keep warm up to 12 hours (none for quinoa/oatmeal); E01/E02 referenced in troubleshooting.
- Zojirushi NHS-06/10/18 manual, https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nhs.pdf: NHS-06 has no keep warm; NHS-10/18 keep warm no more than 5 hours or rice may become hard and dry; steam 15 min with lid closed; brown rice capacity and water chart (NHS-06: 1–2 cups, 0.2 L per cup); rinse for fluffy, don't rinse for stickier.
- Zojirushi NW-QAC10/18 manual, https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nw-qac_en.pdf (downloaded and extracted; only used to confirm the model exists; cut model).
- Hamilton Beach 37548 use and care, https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840264200.pdf: 6-oz (177 ml) cup, about ¾ US cup; settings White, Quick (skips the soak), Whole Grain, Hot Cereals, Steam Cook, Heat/Simmer; WARM up to 12 hours then the unit turns off; rest 10–15 min on WARM for softer rice; rice chart at max (7 cups to the 7 line): long-grain white 50–52 min, brown 58–60, basmati 48–50, jasmine 46–48, sushi 42–44; grain chart: quinoa 7 cups to 7 cups water, Whole Grain, 58–60 min (rinse first), barley, farro, millet etc.; teff and amaranth not recommended; Hot Cereals: old-fashioned oats 3 : 9 cups, 28–30 min, steel-cut 3 : 9 about 1 hour; troubleshooting: rice not cooked = not enough water, pot not seated or bent/warped, let rest 10–15 min; mushy = too much liquid; pot, basket and lid liner dishwasher-safe, no SANI cycle.
- Cuckoo CR-0655F manual, https://cuckooamerica.com/cdn/shop/files/CR-0655F_User_Manual.pdf, and https://cuckooamerica.com/pages/error-codes: codes cover temperature sensor fault, inner pot not detected, preset over 13 hours, micom memory fault, center sensor/heater causing abnormally long cook. The code glyphs are images on both (not extractable as text); a human must read the code labels before any code-to-meaning table is published. Manual: coating may peel if the pot is misused or heated on a stove; wears with long use; no metal scrubbers; preset limited to 13 hours.
- FDA Bad Bug Book, B. cereus chapter, https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download: the vomiting-type illness "most often affects rice and other starchy foods", onset 0.5–6 h; growth from 4 °C to 48 °C; the emetic toxin (cereulide) is heat-stable (survives 121 °C for 30 min), so "Cooking may kill the bacteria, but it might not disable the toxin"; keep food refrigerated at 40 °F or lower.
- FoodSafety.gov, https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety: keep cooked food at 140 °F or above if not served right away; danger zone 40–140 °F; refrigerate perishables within 2 hours. https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures: reheat leftovers to 165 °F.
- ATK "How to Clean Your Rice Cooker", https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7279-how-to-clean-rice-cooker: wash pan after every use, don't soak long (rust), no abrasive sponge side or vinegar on nonstick; wipe the heating plate immediately; wet #320 sandpaper on metal heating plates (skip on ceramic plates); wash removable inner lid and steam vent.

## Notes for drafting
- Every recommender agrees most rice cookers make acceptable white rice; differences are brown rice, short-grain texture, cleanup and speed. CR: all six it tested made Very Good or Excellent white rice, "a lot more variation" with brown. ATK: slower cycles with a low-temperature start made better rice; fastest large long-grain batches took about 30 min, slowest about 65 min.
- Speed vs quality conflict: ATK prefers slow cycles; Serious Eats says "the best rice cookers were fast" and its winner is the fastest it tested; CNET's top pick (a Tiger 10-cup) won partly on speed (white in 21 min). Say so on the hub.
- Induction: ATK and CNN found induction didn't consistently improve rice; Reviewed (NP-HCC10XH) and CR 2022 (NP-GBC05 "most foolproof") rated induction models best. Sources disagree.
- Fuzzy logic: ATK says not every fuzzy-logic cooker made excellent rice, but every one that did had fuzzy logic. CNN's over/under-water test on the NS-ZCC10 supports that.
- Pressure cookers vs rice cookers: CNN cooked rice in three pressure cookers (Cuckoo and Instant Pot Duo Plus) and every batch was "extremely gummy and wet"; CR 2022: even its lowest-rated rice cookers made better rice than a multi-cooker. The existing /guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/ uses ATK, TechGearLab and CR; the new vs guide should link it, not repeat it.
- Water ratios disagree by brand and test: ATK found medium-grain brown rice too wet at the brands' ratios and used the long-grain white line; resting helps: Engadget found half an hour on warm improved its Hamilton Beach's sticky white rice, CNET let every cooker steam 15 more minutes because almost every manual said to, and Hamilton Beach's manual says rest 10–15 min. Always say which cup (6 oz / about ¾ US cup).
- Keep-warm durations (manufacturer): Zojirushi micom 12 h, NHS-10/18 5 h, NHS-06 none, Hamilton Beach 12 h then off, KitchenAid 6 h max (Serious Eats). Measured: Serious Eats logged the Aroma ARC-914SBD at 200 °F at done, 170 °F at 30 min, 154 °F at 60 min; ATK says the NS-ZCC10 and Toshiba TRCS01 kept rice food-safe for 12 h; CNN cites about 140 °F; CNN saw the Cuckoo CR-0675F brown the bottom after 3 h on warm. Do not state a safe-hold time we can't source: FoodSafety.gov gives 140 °F, not a duration; FSIS's rice page was unreadable.
- Measuring cup: Zojirushi and Hamilton Beach both say about ¾ US cup (6 oz / 177–180 ml); ATK says every scoop it tested held about ¾ cup. "8-cup" or "14-cup" names often mean cooked rice (CR on the Instant Zest; Engadget on Hamilton Beach). Good for the size and cup-size guides.
- Dishwasher conflict: Zojirushi FAQ says parts are not dishwasher-safe; Hamilton Beach says its pot, basket and lid liner are; CNET's FAQ says pots "can almost always be" run through the dishwasher. Say it depends on the manual.
- Engadget's tester saw the Cuckoo lids "spring open"; only Cuckoo did that in its tests. Attribute it.
- Recipes remain paused. Water-ratio, brown rice and quinoa pages are how-tos with manufacturer charts, not Recipe cards.
