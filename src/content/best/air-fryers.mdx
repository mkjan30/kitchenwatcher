The best-supported air fryer for most kitchens is the Instant Vortex Plus 6-quart. TechGearLab measured it closest to its set temperature of any model here (401.5°F at 400°F), and Food Network named it best overall. If you cook for one or two, the smaller Ninja AF101 has the strongest lab record. The ranking below follows the evidence, not commissions.

## How these six air fryers are ranked

Each model is ranked on, in order: independent lab measurements, how consistent owner reports are, known failures and recalls, then price.

The full method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/). The main lab source is [TechGearLab's air fryer roundup](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer), updated April 17, 2026, which publishes temperature accuracy and exterior temperature for all of these except the AF141. [Food Network's roundup](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-air-fryer) covers three of them. RTINGS and Consumer Reports keep their data behind JavaScript or a paywall, so nothing from them is used here.

## 1. Instant Vortex Plus 6-quart: our pick

TechGearLab measured an average of 401.5°F at a 400°F setting, the closest to target in its test, and a 146°F hottest exterior point ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)). Food Network named it best overall and recorded 66 decibels of fan noise ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-air-fryer)). Per Instant's product page it's 1700 W, 95 to 400°F, with a top-rack dishwasher-safe basket.

One caveat: TechGearLab lists its basket coating as containing PTFE. Food Network listed it at $90 (regular $150) when I checked on Sep 27, 2026. Read the [Instant Vortex Plus 6-quart review](/reviews/instant-vortex-plus-6qt/).

## 2. Ninja AF101: best for one or two people

TechGearLab gave it an Editors' Choice and the highest overall score of these six (80), measured 396.8°F at a 400°F setting and a 160°F exterior, and noted it needed only a 3-minute preheat ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)). Food Network named it best mid-size. Owner feedback is consistent: it averaged 4.56 stars across 1,314 ratings on Target's listing on Sep 27, 2026, where it was $119.99.

Complaints there include fan noise and one owner's fan slowing after about 50 uses. It ranks second only because the Vortex Plus measured closer to target and holds more food.

The AF101 may be on its way out: SharkNinja's store sells its replacement basket and crisper plate, but a search for AF101 there on Sep 27, 2026 returned only parts, not the fryer itself. See the [Ninja AF101 review](/reviews/ninja-af101/), [Ninja AF101 vs AF141](/vs/ninja-af101-vs-ninja-af141/) and [how to clean the Ninja AF101](/guides/how-to-clean-ninja-af101/).

## 3. COSORI TurboBlaze: best under $100

TechGearLab made it a Top Pick for families and measured the coolest exterior of the group, 130°F. It ran a little low, at 391.6°F on a 400°F setting ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)). Per COSORI's product page it's 6 quarts, 1725 W, 90 to 450°F, with five fan speeds and a 2-year warranty, and it was $89.99 (list $119.99) there on Sep 27, 2026.

The sources disagree on the coating: COSORI says PFAS-free ceramic, TechGearLab lists PTFE. It is not on the February 2023 CPSC recall of about 2 million older COSORI air fryers, sold June 2018 to December 2022 after 205 reports of fires and overheating. That recall lists 21 model numbers, and none is the TurboBlaze ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Two-Million-COSORI-Air-Fryers-Recalled-by-Atekcity-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards)). See the [COSORI TurboBlaze review](/reviews/cosori-turboblaze/) and [COSORI TurboBlaze vs Ninja AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/).

## 4. Ninja DZ201 DualZone: best for two foods at once

Two independent reviewers back it for dual-basket cooking: TechGearLab's Top Pick for meal prep and Food Network's best dual-basket. It ranks fourth because TechGearLab measured it furthest below target of the four with lab data, 386.4°F at a 400°F setting, with a 135°F exterior ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)). Food Network listed it at $160 (regular $200) on Sep 27, 2026.

Ninja's owner's guide lists 1690 W, and Target's listing gives a 105 to 450°F range across its cooking functions. See the [Ninja DZ201 review](/reviews/ninja-dz201/).

## 5. Ninja AF141: promising, but no independent data

Per SharkNinja's product page it's 5 quarts, 1750 W, up to 400°F, with a 1-year warranty, listed at $129.99 on Sep 27, 2026. TechGearLab mentions it only as the newer version of the AF101 it tested, and I found no lab that has published measurements for it. Target's listing had no customer ratings yet.

It's here because it's the obvious step up from the AF101, but without evidence it can't rank higher. See the [Ninja AF141 review](/reviews/ninja-af141/), [AF101 vs AF141](/vs/ninja-af101-vs-ninja-af141/) and [TurboBlaze vs AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/).

## 6. Ninja Crispi FN101: proceed with caution

The Crispi is a glass-container air fryer with a detachable heating pod that TechGearLab would not recommend. Per SharkNinja it's 1500 W, up to 450°F, and was $179.99 and out of stock there on Sep 27, 2026. TechGearLab measured the glass at 280°F and an average of 383.1°F at a 400°F setting. It said it would not recommend it to a friend because of the hot exterior ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)).

Separately, one owner's Crispi container shattered on its second use in July 2025, per [The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/ninja-crispi-explodes/); that is a single report, not a pattern, and there is no recall. See the [Ninja Crispi review](/reviews/ninja-crispi-fn101/).

## Are any of these air fryers recalled?

Neither CPSC recall I found covers any of the Ninja or COSORI models on this list. Beyond the 2023 COSORI recall above, the only recent SharkNinja recall I found is the May 2025 recall of about 1.85 million Foodi OP300-series pressure cookers, which have an air-fry lid. It does not include any of the Ninja air fryers on this list ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/SharkNinja-Recalls-1-8-Million-Foodi-Multi-Function-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard-Serious-Burn-Injuries-Reported)).

## What size air fryer do you need?

A 4-quart basket (AF101, Crispi) suits one or two people. The 5- and 6-quart models (AF141, TurboBlaze, Vortex Plus) suit two to four. The DZ201 is for cooking a main and a side at once. No source I could read has published how much food each one fits in a single layer, so go by quart size and the maker's serving guidance.
