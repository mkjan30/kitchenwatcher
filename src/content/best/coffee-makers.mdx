The best drip coffee maker for most kitchens is the OXO Brew 8-Cup. It is on the Specialty Coffee Association's certified list, CNET named it best overall in its Oct 3, 2026 update, and America's Test Kitchen (ATK) called it "Also Great." If you want a thermal carafe and can spend more, ATK's winner, the Moccamaster KBT, kept its brewing water in ATK's target range for 91% of the cycle. Under $100, CNET's pick is the Ninja CE251.

Nothing here was tested hands-on. Every number below is a manufacturer spec or a third-party measurement credited to whoever took it, and the method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Quick picks: the 6 best drip coffee makers

1. **OXO Brew 8-Cup, best drip coffee maker overall:** thermal carafe. The only pick that is on the SCA list and also a top pick at both CNET and ATK. [OXO Brew 8-Cup review](/reviews/oxo-brew-8-cup/).
2. **Ninja CE251, best under $100:** CNET's best coffee maker under $100, with a 24-hour delay brew per Ninja's owner's guide. [Ninja CE251 review](/reviews/ninja-ce251/).
3. **Cuisinart PerfecTemp DCC-3200, biggest pot:** 14 cups and a limited three-year warranty per Cuisinart's booklet, but CNET measured it as the slowest here. [Ninja CE251 vs Cuisinart DCC-3200](/vs/ninja-ce251-vs-cuisinart-dcc-3200/).
4. **Moccamaster KBT, best thermal carafe coffee maker:** ATK's overall winner; ATK logged its brewing water in range for 91% of the cycle. [Moccamaster KBT vs KBGV Select](/vs/moccamaster-kbt-vs-moccamaster-kbgv-select/).
5. **Moccamaster KBGV Select, best glass-carafe premium pick:** Tom's Guide's best overall after a retest in early 2026. [Moccamaster KBGV Select review](/reviews/moccamaster-kbgv-select/).
6. **Fellow Aiden, best programmable:** SCA certified in 2024 and ATK's best programmable pick. [What SCA certified means](/guides/what-is-sca-certified-coffee-maker/).

## How we ranked the best drip coffee makers

This best drip coffee maker ranking follows weight of evidence, in this order: independent measurements (ATK, CNET), how consistent owner reports are, documented failures, warranty and price. It is never by commission. See [how we research](/how-we-research/) for the full method.

The SCA brewing window, as CNET states it, is 4 to 8 minutes of water contact at 197 to 205°F. ATK also cites the SCA but scores against 194 to 205°F, and Moccamaster and Tom's Guide state 196 to 205°F, so "in range" means slightly different things at each source. The [SCA certified explainer](/guides/what-is-sca-certified-coffee-maker/) covers what certification tests.

Owner-report evidence is thin this round. Reddit was not reachable from our research setup and Amazon's review summaries were not opened, so this page cites no owner complaints yet. Documented failure modes come from manuals and iFixit.

Price weighs heavily. The KBT has the strongest single lab result here, but ATK's page showed it at $339 at Amazon on Oct 3, 2026, and CNET's page showed the Ninja at $90 at Amazon that day. That gap is why the KBT sits fourth rather than first.

## 1. OXO Brew 8-Cup: best drip coffee maker for most people

The OXO is the only brewer here that two labs both put near the top and that also appears on the SCA's own list (with the variant caveat below). CNET named it best overall and measured 4 minutes 35 seconds for 8 cups, 177°F off the brew and 169°F after an hour in its thermal carafe. ATK called it "Also Great" and measured 6 minutes 26 seconds and 183°F, with the brewing water in its target range for 54% of the cycle. Those two brew times don't match, and neither lab explains the gap, so both are shown.

The SCA list includes the OXO Brew 8-Cup, certified in 2025, and ATK, CNET and Tom's Guide all call it SCA certified or approved. But the SCA entry describes a glass carafe, while the model those three rate has a thermal carafe (stainless, per CNET).

Tom's Guide made it its best budget drip pick and lists two weaknesses: the water tank isn't removable, and it isn't programmable. CNET's spec table also says it isn't programmable and gives it a 2-year warranty.

Who it's for: anyone who brews up to 8 cups, wants a thermal carafe and doesn't need a timer. Read the [OXO Brew 8-Cup review](/reviews/oxo-brew-8-cup/).

## 2. Ninja CE251: best cheap coffee maker with programming

The CE251 is CNET's best coffee maker under $100, and it pairs a delay timer with independent lab numbers. Per the [Ninja CE251 owner's guide](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/da/da60176b-126f-490a-b1a4-f1bd1654c887.pdf), it is 1100 W with a 60 fl oz reservoir. It has Classic and Rich brews, a Small Batch setting for 4 cups or fewer and a 24-hour delay brew. Its warming plate can run up to 4 hours and shuts off after 2 hours by default.

CNET measured 10 minutes 30 seconds in its 8-cup test, 178°F off the brew and 173°F after 60 minutes on the plate. CNET says it kept temperature stable for more than two hours. It also lists flimsier plastic and thinner glass than pricier picks among its cons, and a 1-year limited warranty.

The weaknesses are speed and the glass carafe on a hot plate. Ninja's own guide suggests running a clean cycle when a full carafe takes longer than 12 to 15 minutes to brew.

Who it's for: someone replacing a dead budget machine who wants coffee ready on a timer. See the [Ninja CE251 review](/reviews/ninja-ce251/) and [Ninja CE251 vs Cuisinart DCC-3200](/vs/ninja-ce251-vs-cuisinart-dcc-3200/).

## 3. Cuisinart DCC-3200: the 14-cup pick, with caveats

The PerfecTemp brews 14 cups, the most here, but no lab we could read recommends it. CNET measured it at 12 minutes in its 8-cup test, tied for the slowest it timed, with 176°F off the brew and 172°F after 60 minutes. CNET also noted the top of the machine got very hot.

Per [Mashed](https://www.mashed.com/2256142/consumer-reports-best-drip-coffee-maker-2026-bunn-hb-heat-n-brew/), Consumer Reports ranked the Cuisinart PerfecTemp 14-cup third of more than 160 coffee makers. We could not read Consumer Reports directly, and Mashed gives no model number.

Per the Canadian edition of the [DCC-3200 instruction booklet](https://www.cuisinart.ca/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-master-ca/en_CA/v1775207086817//Instruction%20Booklet/DCC-3200C.pdf), the warming plate has Low, Medium and High settings, and the booklet recommends decalcifying whenever the CLEAN light comes on. It carries a limited three-year warranty, longer than the OXO's and Ninja's.

[iFixit's troubleshooting page](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DDC-3200_Troubleshooting) lists leaks and says that in many cases the bottom water hose is damaged or worn out "after a year or two" from overheating. The DCC-3200 is not on the SCA list.

It ranks third because the measurements and warranty are documented, but its brew is slower than the Ninja's and nothing independent backs it as a pick.

Who it's for: households that need a full 14-cup pot. See [what the Cuisinart CLEAN light means](/guides/cuisinart-coffee-maker-clean-light/).

## 4. Moccamaster KBT: best thermal carafe coffee maker

On lab numbers, the KBT is the strongest brewer here. ATK named it its overall winner: brewing water in its target range for 91% of the cycle, 6 minutes 36 seconds and 186°F freshly brewed. That is the highest in-range share and the hottest coffee of any model ATK measured on this list.

CNET says its stainless thermal carafe keeps coffee hot for a full 6 hours, but CNET's temperature table doesn't include it. ATK, which checked carafes up to 2 hours, says it kept coffee piping hot for 2 hours.

Per Moccamaster's KBT product page, it is 1400 W and 40 oz (10 cups) with a 5-year warranty. Moccamaster says it brews a full carafe in 4 to 6 minutes at 196 to 205°F. CNET lists it as not programmable, pricier than other options and taking up a lot of space.

The SCA status is disputed. Moccamaster's page says the KBT is "SCA approved" and ATK's spec list says "SCA Certified: Yes," but the SCA's certified list as fetched on Oct 3, 2026 shows no Technivorm or Moccamaster entry.

Who it's for: someone who drinks a pot over a morning and wants the best measured brew, with no timer. See [Moccamaster KBT vs KBGV Select](/vs/moccamaster-kbt-vs-moccamaster-kbgv-select/) and [thermal vs glass carafe](/guides/thermal-vs-glass-carafe/).

## 5. Moccamaster KBGV Select: best glass-carafe premium pick

Tom's Guide has kept the KBGV Select in its guide for five years and named it best overall again after re-reviewing it in early 2026. Tom's Guide says it reaches 196 to 205°F in under a minute and stays there through the brew. Neither ATK nor CNET published numbers for this exact model, so it has less independent data than the KBT.

Per Moccamaster's KBGV product page, it is 1475 W and 40 oz (10 cups) with a 5-year warranty. It has a full or half carafe selector, an automatic drip-stop, a separately heated hot plate and auto-off at 100 minutes.

Moccamaster claims it is the only brewer certified by the SCA and the ECBC for both a full and a half carafe. Tom's Guide lists "Specialty Coffee Association-approved" among its reasons to buy. The SCA list as fetched does not show it, so treat that as a manufacturer claim.

Tom's Guide's one listed con is no delayed brew. Who it's for: people who brew half pots often and prefer glass. Read the [Moccamaster KBGV Select review](/reviews/moccamaster-kbgv-select/).

## 6. Fellow Aiden: best programmable drip coffee maker

The Aiden is on the SCA list, certified in 2024 as the Fellow Aiden Precision, a 10-cup brewer with a removable tank and app control. ATK made it its best programmable pick. It measured the brewing water in range for 57% of the cycle, 6 minutes 30 seconds in regular mode (10 minutes guided) and 182°F. ATK's complaints: a carafe lid that stuck tight and a narrow tank opening on the left side.

[Coffee Chronicler](https://coffeechronicler.com/fellow-aiden-review/) measured 20 to 21% extraction with a refractometer. It took carafe readings at 50 and 120 minutes and reports both the Aiden and a Moccamaster on its hot plate ended around 70°C (158°F). The same review criticizes the app and workflow, and says the machine won't brew without its thermal carafe in place.

Reliability is the open question: we have not yet confirmed any owner-report evidence for it, which is why it ranks last. Who it's for: people who want app control and a removable tank, and will pay the most for it.

## Thermal vs glass carafe: which keeps coffee hotter?

Carafe type is the first choice to make when picking the best drip coffee maker for you. On CNET's numbers, glass on a hot plate held slightly more heat over an hour. The glass-carafe Ninja CE251 was at 173°F after 60 minutes on its warming plate, while the thermal OXO was at 169°F.

A thermal carafe keeps working after the brewer shuts off, though. CNET says the KBT's holds heat for 6 hours; ATK, which checked only to 2 hours, found it piping hot at that point. The Ninja's plate turns off after 2 hours by default. The [thermal vs glass carafe guide](/guides/thermal-vs-glass-carafe/) goes further.

## What we left out of this drip coffee maker ranking, and why

- **Bunn HB Heat N Brew:** Consumer Reports' top-rated drip maker per Mashed, but we could read no independent measurement of it.
- **Breville Luxe Brewer:** on the SCA list (2025), and CNET measured it at 4 minutes 41 seconds and 183°F. Only one roundup we read recommends it; it's the next candidate.
- **Zojirushi ZUTTO:** ATK's Best Buy (60% in range, 7 minutes 30 seconds, 179°F), but no second source.
- **Bonavita 8-Cup and 5-Cup:** SCA certified in 2026, with no independent review we could open.
- **Ratio Four, Aarke, Caraway and pod machines:** one recommender each among the roundups we read (Tom's Guide for the Ratio, CNET for the Aarke and Caraway), or not drip brewers.

If you want espresso rather than drip, see the [best espresso machines](/best/espresso-machines/).

## Descaling intervals from the manuals

Moccamaster's KBT page says scale builds up in the boiling element and can slow, then stop, the water flow. It says to descale every 100 cycles or at least every three months.

Ninja's guide says to run a clean cycle once a full carafe takes longer than 12 to 15 minutes. Cuisinart's booklet recommends cleaning whenever the CLEAN light comes on. Step-by-step instructions are in [how to descale a coffee maker](/guides/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/) and [vinegar vs descaler](/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/).
