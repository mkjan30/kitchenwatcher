The best espresso machine for most people who want a grinder built in is the Breville Barista Express: TechGearLab and CNET both name it best overall. The best espresso machine for beginners is the Breville Bambino Plus. The best budget espresso machine is the CASABREWS CM5418 at about $133 (Amazon, Oct 3, 2026), with the De'Longhi Stilosa as the cheapest option at about $100.

The order below is by weight of evidence: independent measurements and recommendations, how consistent reviewer complaints are, failures and recalls, then price. Nobody at Kitchen Watcher used these machines; every number is credited to whoever measured it ([how we research](/how-we-research/)).

## The six picks at a glance

1. **CASABREWS CM5418**: best budget espresso machine, about $133, with the highest extraction yield TechGearLab measured among these six. [Review](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/).
2. **Breville Bambino Plus**: best for beginners, with automatic milk steaming. [Review](/reviews/breville-bambino-plus/).
3. **De'Longhi Stilosa**: cheapest, about $100, but the weakest lab result. [Review](/reviews/delonghi-stilosa/).
4. **Breville Bambino**: best value under $250 if you steam milk by hand. [Bambino vs Bambino Plus](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/).
5. **Breville Barista Express**: best espresso machine under $500, and the only one here with a built-in grinder. [Review](/reviews/breville-barista-express/).
6. **Gaggia Classic E24**: for learners who want a 58mm semi-pro machine to grow into. Details below.

A note on the order: no independent source calls the CASABREWS best overall. It leads here because it pairs strong lab numbers with a low price and a large volume of Amazon reviews. If budget isn't the constraint, the Barista Express has the stronger expert backing, as explained under how these six were ranked.

## 1. CASABREWS CM5418: best budget espresso machine

The CM5418 is the best budget espresso machine for a beginner who wants milk drinks in a small kitchen. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-espresso-machine/) names it best budget espresso machine and lists it as a compact 20-bar machine (manufacturer spec) with a 34-ounce tank, 5.47 x 12.28 x 11.97 inches. CNET's own spec boxes give two weights for it, 2.1 and 8 lbs, so neither is used here. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-espresso-machine) gave it Best for Tight Budgets and measured a 20.7% extraction yield, the highest of the six here.

[TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/coffee-machines/casabrews-cm5418-review) says its thermoblock is ready in about 40 seconds and calls it beginner friendly, partly because it ships with double-walled (pressurized) baskets that forgive an uneven grind. The trade-off is that it doesn't include non-pressurized baskets, and TechRadar says brew temperature can't be reprogrammed.

The complaints line up across reviewers. TechGearLab reports strong vibration during extraction and a cool-down needed between drinks. CNET flags the plastic scoop and tamp and awkward toggling between brew and steam, and TechRadar calls the basket loud and sometimes unstable in the portafilter.

See the [CASABREWS CM5418 review](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/), [CASABREWS vs De'Longhi Stilosa](/vs/casabrews-cm5418-vs-delonghi-stilosa/) and, if water stops flowing, [CASABREWS CM5418 not pumping water](/guides/casabrews-cm5418-not-pumping-water/).

## 2. Breville Bambino Plus: best espresso machine for beginners

The Bambino Plus is the best espresso machine for beginners who want café-style milk without learning to steam by hand. TechGearLab named it Best for Novices and measured an 18.4% extraction yield. CNET credits its ThermoJet heater with PID temperature control and a roughly three-second heat-up, and Coffee Chronicler notes it brews at 9 bars and has pre-infusion.

[Tom's Guide's review](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/breville-sage-bambino-plus) praises automatic milk frothing, customizable pre-infusion and the included tamper, trimming tool and milk pitcher. It also lists four baskets, which Coffee Chronicler contradicts (note 2 above), so check the box contents before you buy.

The consistent complaint is the light body. Tom's Guide says it needs steadying when you lock in the portafilter, and CNET calls it a little tippy. [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/coffee-machines/best-espresso-machine) adds that it has no auto switch-off and says it isn't a machine for complete beginners, since you still choose baskets, tamp and trim. CNET and TechRadar also disagree on its weight by more than 7 lbs (note 1).

Read the [Breville Bambino Plus review](/reviews/breville-bambino-plus/), compare it with the cheaper model in [Bambino vs Bambino Plus](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/), or decode its warning lights in [Bambino Plus flashing lights](/guides/breville-bambino-plus-flashing-lights/).

## 3. De'Longhi Stilosa: the cheapest machine, with mixed evidence

The Stilosa is for buyers who need the lowest price, about $100, and are willing to upgrade the accessories. [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/best-picks/best-espresso-machines) makes it its budget pick and lists a 15-bar pump (manufacturer spec), 8.1 x 13.5 x 11.2 inches and a stainless steel boiler in a plastic body. It also calls the tamper awful and notes there's no milk jug.

The evidence is mixed. TechGearLab measured a 12% extraction yield, the lowest of the six and well below its 18 to 22% target, scored it 44 out of 100 and gave it no award. Tom's Guide, by contrast, says it pulls great espresso for the price.

The basket question matters here. Tom's Guide lists single-walled portafilters as a pro, while Coffee Chronicler says only EU and Asian units get a non-pressurized basket as standard, and US units get a panarello wand (note 3). Coffee Chronicler also warns it has no solenoid valve, so it holds pressure after brewing and you should wait before removing the portafilter.

See the [De'Longhi Stilosa review](/reviews/delonghi-stilosa/) and [CASABREWS CM5418 vs De'Longhi Stilosa](/vs/casabrews-cm5418-vs-delonghi-stilosa/).

## 4. Breville Bambino: best value under $250

The Bambino is the Bambino Plus without automatic milk steaming, for about $175 less at the Oct 3, 2026 Amazon prices above. TechGearLab named it Best Espresso Machine on a Budget, measured an 18% extraction yield and scored it 71, one point behind the Plus. Tom's Guide makes it its best compact pick and lists a 9-bar pump, a 64 fl oz tank, 13.7 x 6.3 x 12 inches, and single- and double-walled baskets in the box.

The weaknesses are what's missing. TechGearLab lists no built-in grinder and a dinky tamp as cons, and Tom's Guide notes you steam milk by hand, where the Plus does it automatically. TechGearLab also measured its tank at 47 oz, well short of the 64 fl oz Tom's Guide lists (note 1).

There's no single review of the Bambino yet, so the deep comparison is [Breville Bambino vs Bambino Plus](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/).

## 5. Breville Barista Express: best espresso machine under $500 with a grinder

The Barista Express is the best espresso machine under $500 for anyone who doesn't own a burr grinder. CNET calls it best overall because it "ticks the most boxes", and TechGearLab named it Best Overall with the top score of 77 and a 19.4% extraction yield. CNET reports shots up to 12.4% TDS, against 1.3 to 1.4% for excellent drip coffee.

Per the [BES870 instruction manual](https://www.breville.com/content/dam/breville/us/assets/miscellaneous/instruction-manual/espresso/BES870-instruction-manual.pdf), it has a 15-bar Italian pump, Thermocoil heating with electronic PID and water temperature adjustable ±4°F in 2°F steps. It also has a 67 fl oz (2 L) tank, a half-pound bean hopper and an integrated conical burr grinder. It ships with single-wall baskets for fresh beans and dual-wall baskets for pre-ground coffee, in 1-cup and 2-cup sizes, and uses low-pressure pre-infusion. The 1-cup preset pours about 1 oz (30 ml), and the manual's milk target is 140 to 149°F.

The "under $500" label depends on a sale: the $468.77 was seen at Amazon on Oct 3, 2026, while TechGearLab lists it at $700 and CNET's price box showed $700 at Amazon the same day.

The trade-offs are size and skill. CNET lists it at 24.03 lbs and 13 x 15 x 16 inches and calls it a bit bulky, while TechGearLab reports a slow grinder and a learning curve. Per the manual, the CLEAN/DESCALE light covers both a back-flushing cleaning cycle and descaling, which are separate jobs.

Read the [Breville Barista Express review](/reviews/breville-barista-express/) and the [Barista Express descaling guide](/guides/breville-barista-express-descaling/).

## 6. Gaggia Classic E24: for learners who want a 58mm semi-pro machine

The E24 suits buyers who want a traditional single-boiler machine with a 58mm portafilter they can modify over time. In its [E24 review](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/coffee-makers/gaggia-classic-e24-evo-pro-review) (27 Aug 2025), Tom's Guide credits the brass boiler and group, a boiler 25% larger than previous models, quick heat-up and a powerful steam wand, and says it pulls at 9 bar. The review gives a $499 Amazon price; its price box showed $450.83 at Amazon on Oct 3, 2026.

The main weakness is temperature. Tom's Guide says that without PID, temperatures still aren't properly stable, and the box holds little beyond a plastic tamp, two single-wall baskets, a pressurized basket and a scoop. Its roundup lists an 80 oz tank and a 20-bar rating, but read that 20 bar as a pump rating: the review says the machine pulls at 9 bar. Coffee Chronicler says many Gaggia Classic Pro owners fit a new valve spring to reach 9 bar (note 4).

Watch the version, because the model names overlap. CoffeeBlog's boilergate post (updated 28 Nov 2024) says flaking boiler coating affected only the 2023 Evo, not the 2019 Pro, and that Gaggia blamed how the coating was applied on affected units ([CoffeeBlog](https://coffeeblog.co.uk/boilergate-gaggia-classic-pro-evo-boiler-problems/)). Coffee Chronicler instead says to avoid "the new 2024 update, the so-called EVO Pro", and Tom's Guide calls its reviewed unit the E24 Evo Pro.

CoffeeBlog adds that only the version listed as E24 has the new brass boiler, so confirm the listing says brass boiler before buying.

TechGearLab measured the older Gaggia Classic Evo Pro at 17% extraction and scored it 53; those numbers are for a different model, not the E24. For upkeep on a boiler machine, see [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## How these six were ranked

Each model was scored in our research log on independent sources (35%), Amazon review volume (25%), price fit (20%), recency (10%) and reviewer complaints (10%). Scores: CASABREWS 73, Bambino Plus 66, Stilosa 65, Bambino 60, Barista Express 54, Gaggia E24 49. Amazon review counts ran from about 2,400 to 27,900 when recorded on Oct 3, 2026.

That weighting rewards cheap, widely bought machines. It's why the CASABREWS leads and the Stilosa ranks third even though TechGearLab scored the Barista Express 77 and the Stilosa 44. By TechGearLab's scores alone, the Barista Express (77) comes first, then the Bambino Plus (72), Bambino (71) and CASABREWS (69).

A search of the CPSC's saferproducts.gov recall database for products named "espresso" on Oct 3, 2026 returned 16 recalls, none for these six brands' machines; recalls filed under other product names would not show up in that search. Wirecutter, Serious Eats and Reddit could not be read, so no owner reports from them are used. Method details are on [how we research](/how-we-research/) and [about Kitchen Watcher](/about/).

## How to choose an espresso machine

### Ignore the 15 bar and 20 bar on the box

Tom's Guide quotes Sage's Luke Lane: "You want to hit 9 bars for optimal extraction." Lane told Tom's Guide that machines use over-pressure valves to hold extraction near 9 bars whatever their maximum, so the box number is a pump rating, not brewing pressure. More detail is in [espresso machine bar pressure explained](/guides/espresso-machine-bar-pressure-explained/).

### Pressurized vs non-pressurized baskets

TechRadar explains that the extra wall in a pressurized basket does the work of regulating extraction pressure, so less precision is needed and pre-ground coffee works, at a cost in peak flavor. Per the sources above, the CASABREWS ships pressurized only, the Barista Express, Bambino and Gaggia ship both, and the Bambino Plus and Stilosa are disputed. See [pressurized vs non-pressurized baskets](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/).

### The best espresso machine for beginners heats fast and holds temperature

The Breville machines use fast heaters with PID: ThermoJet on the Bambino Plus, per CNET, and Thermocoil on the Barista Express, per its manual. TechRadar says the CASABREWS uses a thermoblock and TechGearLab lists PID for it and the Bambino. The Gaggia uses a brass boiler with no PID, which Tom's Guide says still isn't fully stable. Read [single vs double boiler espresso machines](/guides/single-vs-double-boiler-espresso-machine/) for the trade-offs.

### Built-in grinder or separate

TechGearLab notes that buying a separate grinder means spending more and using more counter space, yet it still recommends the Bambino for buyers who prefer to buy a grinder separately. Tom's Guide calls the grinder the single most important part of brewing espresso, and says some enthusiasts prefer separate machines so one breakdown doesn't take out the whole setup.

### Upkeep

Plan on descaling. The Barista Express manual says replacing its water filter every two months reduces how often it's needed, and Tom's Guide says the Gaggia has no water filter, so descale regularly depending on your water hardness. Steps are in [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## Machines left out and why

The De'Longhi Dedica Duo was cut: Tom's Guide says it's expensive for what it is next to the cheaper Stilosa. The Rancilio Silvia ($995 list per TechGearLab) and Breville Barista Pro (over $800 in our research log) are over budget, and the La Specialista Touch had one source and a likely price over $700. The Gaggia Classic Evo Pro (2023) was replaced by the E24 and carried the boilergate coating issue. Pod, lever and superautomatic machines are a different category and aren't covered here.
