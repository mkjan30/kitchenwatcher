## How we ranked these food processors, and where the labs disagree

The order follows weight of evidence, never commission. That means how many independent outlets recommend each model, what they measured, how consistent the complaints are, any recalls, and then price. The method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

The labs disagree in four places:

- **Best overall.** CNET and Reviewed pick the KitchenAid KFP1318. ATK picks the Cuisinart and gives the KitchenAid reservations. Its light base jumped around while kneading, the pulse ran on for more than three seconds, and dough glued the multi-part blade in place. TechGearLab's top two are the Breville BFP800 (83) and the Magimix 4200XL (82), neither of which is on this list.
- **Watts.** Wattage didn't track speed in TechGearLab's nut-butter test: it timed nut butter at 19 minutes on the 720 W Cuisinart and 10 minutes on the 450 W Hamilton Beach.
- **Dates.** CNET's page was updated April 30, 2025 and TechGearLab's June 11, 2025, so both are older than ATK's August 4, 2026 review. Reviewed was updated October 30, 2025 and Food Network February 5, 2026.
- **Models.** Outlets sometimes test siblings: TechGearLab's Breville is the BFP800, and ATK tested a different Ninja and Hamilton Beach than the two here.

The Cuisinart ranks first because four outlets recommend it, ATK's review is the newest, and none of them gives it reservations. The KitchenAid ranks third, not first, because ATK's specific failures go against its two best-overall awards.

The strongest model left out is the Magimix 4200XL. TechGearLab scored it 82 with 62.5 dB and a 3-minute nut butter, and ATK recommends it too. But it lists at $449 on TechGearLab and was $400 at Williams-Sonoma on Oct 9, 2026, per CNET. It's missing because it costs nearly as much as the Breville, which has the newer ATK verdict.

## 1. Cuisinart Custom 14 DFP-14BCNY: best food processor overall

The Cuisinart is the best food processor for anyone who wants one big, simple machine. In [ATK's August 2026 review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) it chopped mirepoix uniformly and was one of a few models to turn its yogurt dye test solid green. ATK lists a 14-cup bowl, a 3-cup liquid max and a 12.45 lb base, and notes that all three blades store inside the bowl, so there's no accessory box.

[Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-food-processors) found it kneaded pizza dough into a smooth, elastic ball without stalling or moving, and called it quiet. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor) scored it 66/100 and measured 61.5 dB at 3 feet, the quietest of the three models here that it measured.

The weaknesses are specific. ATK says the redesigned chopping blade leaves slightly bigger gaps, so it couldn't make a single batch of mayonnaise, and there is no mini bowl. TechGearLab says you can't adjust slicing or shredding without buying more discs, and nut butter took 19 minutes. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-food-processor/) notes it doesn't come with a dough hook and says its weight may keep it in the cabinet. Reviewed found the chute lock slightly inconvenient and its mozzarella shreds uneven.

**Recall check for used units.** [CPSC recall 17-050](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) covers Cuisinart processors with model numbers starting DFP-14 that have four-rivet blades, sold July 1996 through December 2015. The DFP-14BCNY shares that prefix, so the sale date and the blade decide it. If you buy used, check the blade for four rivets. If it has them, stop using it and call Cuisinart at 877-339-2534 for a free replacement. See the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## 3. KitchenAid 13-Cup KFP1318: best for storage and smooth hummus, with reservations

The KitchenAid suits cooks who want everything stored in the bowl and make a lot of dips. ## 4. Hamilton Beach 70730: best budget food processor

The Hamilton Beach 70730 is the best budget food processor here for chopping and purées, if you can live with the noise. CNET named it best budget, shows it at $50 at Amazon on Oct 9, 2026, and its April 2025 page reported 4.6 out of 5 from more than 35,000 Amazon ratings. CNET says it diced pico de gallo in about seven pulses and likes its built-in bowl scraper.

TechGearLab named it Best on a Tight Budget at 64/100, with a 9/10 for puréeing. It also measured 96 dB at 3 feet, the loudest of the three models here that it measured. In TechGearLab's tests it leaked with water and mixed heavy dough poorly, and its base weighs 3.1 lb.

The [use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) is blunt: "do not use your food processor for the following: grinding grain, coffee beans, or spices; slicing frozen meats; kneading dough; or crushing ice." If the motor stops from overheating, unplug it and let it cool for 15 minutes. After extended continuous use, rest it about 4 minutes. Parts are top-rack only, and the guide says not to use the SANI setting.

That contradicts CNET's spec block, which lists 12 cups and a dough hook (CNET's own comparison table says 10 cups and no dough hook). We follow the manual. The Breville is for people who cook in large batches and have room for it. TechGearLab's 83/100 was for the older BFP800, so no lab score from it applies to this model yet.

## How to choose a food processor: size, liquid max, blades and noise

### Bowl size vs liquid max

The number on the box isn't how much liquid it holds. ATK lists a 3-cup liquid max for the 14-cup Cuisinart and 9 cups for the 13-cup KitchenAid. CNET says 8 to 12 cups serves most people most of the time. See [what size food processor do I need](/guides/what-size-food-processor-do-i-need/) and [food processor leaking](/guides/food-processor-leaking/).

### Blade gap and pulse

ATK found the best machines had gaps as small as 2.9 mm between blade tips and bowl sides, and 2.2 mm to the bottom. The worst had up to 6.1 mm and 5.4 mm. It also marked down a pulse that runs on, as on the KFP1318. See [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/), [how to shred cheese](/guides/how-to-shred-cheese-in-a-food-processor/) and [how to slice vegetables](/guides/how-to-slice-vegetables-in-a-food-processor/).

### Dough

Check the manual before buying for bread. Breville recommends no more than 2.0 lb of flour; Hamilton Beach says not to knead dough in the 70730. See [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/), or the [best stand mixers](/best/stand-mixers/) for regular bread baking.

### Noise

TechGearLab measured 61.5 dB on the Cuisinart, 80 dB on the Ninja and 96 dB on the Hamilton Beach, all at 3 feet. CNET found the KitchenAid KFP1318 quieter than the others in its hummus run.

### Food processor, blender or chopper

A food processor slices, shreds and chops; Hamilton Beach's guide rules out crushing ice in the 70730. See [food processor vs blender](/guides/food-processor-vs-blender/), [mini chopper vs food processor](/guides/food-chopper-vs-food-processor/) and the [best blenders](/best/blenders/).

## Food processor safety and recalls: what the CPSC record shows

- **CPSC 17-050 (December 13, 2016).** About 8 million Cuisinart processors in the US with riveted blades that can crack and shed small metal pieces into food. Only processors with four rivets in the blade (silver stainless steel with a beige hub) are included. Conair received 69 reports of blade pieces in food, including 30 mouth lacerations or tooth injuries. Stop using the blade and get a free replacement from Cuisinart at 877-339-2534; the model number is on the bottom ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair)).
- **Other recalls.** A search of CPSC's recall database for "food processor" on Oct 9, 2026 returned eight recalls. The other ones from these brands cover models not on this list: Cuisinart's MFP-107 7-cup models (14-029, 2013), KitchenAid's 5- and 7-cup processors (01-004, 2000) and Hamilton Beach's bébé baby food makers (14-193, 2014). Details are in the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).
- **Interlocks.** The Hamilton Beach guide says to be certain the lid is securely locked before operating and never to defeat the lid interlock. Breville says the processor will only operate when the bowl, lid and large pusher are correctly assembled. See [food processor safety interlock](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
- **Hot liquids.** Breville says not to process hot or boiling liquids. Hamilton Beach warns hot liquid "can be ejected out of the appliance due to a sudden steaming." See [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/) for the blender side.
