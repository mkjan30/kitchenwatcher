The best kitchen safety setup covers three different jobs: an extinguisher by the exit for a small fire you're there to fight, an automatic suppressor over the stove, and a shut-off that turns the stove off when nobody's in the kitchen. On the evidence, the Kidde FA110G extinguisher comes first, the StoveTop FireStop Rangehood second, and the iGuard stove shut-off third.

This is a research page: the order comes from published tests, certifications, owner reports and CPSC recalls, not from hands-on use. [How we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method, and ranking is never by commission.

## Why the stove is where to start

Cooking caused an average of 158,400 reported U.S. home fires a year in 2017 to 2021, 44 percent of all home fires, and unattended cooking was the leading factor. Ranges or cooktops were involved in 53 percent of those fires and 88 percent of the deaths, and more than half of non-fatal injuries happened when people tried to control the fire themselves ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)).

None of these devices replaces the basics. The U.S. Fire Administration says to stand by your pan and turn the burner off if you leave the kitchen ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA's [cooking safety tip sheet](https://sanfordfl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/CookingSafety.pdf) says to smother a small pan fire with a lid and turn off the burner, and if you have any doubt, get out, close the door and call 9-1-1 from outside. No device on this page is guaranteed to stop a fire.

## Why this order

Our ranking weighs independent measured performance first, then how consistent owner reports are, then failures and recalls, then price. None of these three has a published lab measurement, so the order rests on certification, independent recommendation, track record and recalls.

**1. Kidde FA110G: our pick.** It carries a UL 1-A:10-B:C listing, and Consumer Reports picked it as the extinguisher to keep in a kitchen, [per The Kitchn](https://shopping.yahoo.com/home-garden/kitchen/articles/only-fire-extinguisher-kitchen-according-164500715.html). It has a long sales history, and at about $20 it's the cheapest of the three. The recall is real but specific: the CPSC lists FA110G among plastic-handle models with date codes from January 2, 2012 through August 15, 2017 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/Kidde-Recalls-Fire-Extinguishers-with-Plastic-Handles-Due-to-Failure-to-Discharge-and-Nozzle-Detachment-One-Death-Reported)), and Kidde's [recall ID guide](https://www.kidde.com/product-safety-notices-fire-extinguishers-recall-id-guide) says metal-handle, metal-pin units are not affected. Kidde rates it for 8 to 12 seconds of discharge, so it's for a small fire with a clear exit. Read the [Kidde FA110G review](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/) for how to check a date code.

**2. StoveTop FireStop Rangehood.** It's designed to act with nobody in the room, and fire departments have demonstrated it: [CCX Media reported](https://ccxmedia.org/cities/maple-grove-fire-department-demonstrates-stovetop-firestops-that-could-save-lives-and-property-damage/) a 2024 Maple Grove Fire Department demo in which a hood-mounted canister released its powder over a burning pot of oil and the fire went out. But the maker's FAQ says it isn't UL-certified, its own manual says it may not suppress all stovetop fires and isn't recommended for gas stoves, and a November 2016 batch was [recalled by the CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires) for failing to activate. [Reviewed.com](https://www.reviewed.com/home-outdoors/best-right-now/the-best-fire-extinguishers-for-homeowners) handled one without setting a fire and noted the loud pop and the amount of powder. Read the [StoveTop FireStop review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/), and the [installation guide](/guides/stovetop-firestop-installation/) for mounting height and the stainless-hood adhesive kit.

**3. iGuard for Electric Stoves.** It's the only one aimed at preventing a fire rather than fighting one, and [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/gear/home-of-the-future-awards-2026/) named it the best stove safety device of 2026. But the evidence is thin: no published measurements of its shut-off delay, no owner reviews of this model yet, and sources disagree on price and timing. [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/seven-fascinating-inventions-unveiled-at-the-2026-consumer-electronics-show-180987980/) reported $399 and a five-minute shut-off at CES; iGuard now lists $549 and gives no shut-off time. It's also the most expensive, and electric only. Read the [iGuard review](/reviews/iguard-electric-stove/).

## Which one do you need?

They do different jobs, so the ranking isn't a replacement order.

- **Everyone:** a working extinguisher by the kitchen exit, checked against the recall if it's older. That's the Kidde.
- **Electric stove under a range hood, worried about a pan fire when you step away:** the StoveTop FireStop is an extra layer, not a certified one.
- **Someone at home who forgets the stove is on:** a shut-off like iGuard addresses the leading factor NFPA identifies, once it has a track record you're comfortable with.
