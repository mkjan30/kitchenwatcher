The best kitchen safety setup covers three jobs: an extinguisher by the exit for a small fire you're there to fight, an automatic suppressor over the stove, and a shut-off that turns the stove off when nobody's in the kitchen. I'm testing one of each: the Kidde FA110G, the StoveTop FireStop Rangehood and iGuard for Electric Stoves. {/* MEASURE: top pick and one-line reason, from final scores */}

All three are still in testing. I rank by score after testing, never by commission; [how we test](/how-we-research/) explains the process.

## Why the stove is where to start

Cooking caused an average of 158,400 reported U.S. home fires a year in 2017 to 2021, 44 percent of all home fires, and unattended cooking was the leading factor, according to NFPA. Ranges or cooktops were involved in 53 percent of those fires and 88 percent of the deaths ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). None of these devices replaces the U.S. Fire Administration's basics: stay with your pan, and if it catches fire, cover it with a lid and turn off the burner ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)).

## Kidde FA110G: extinguisher by the exit

A 2.5 lb disposable dry-chemical extinguisher. Kidde's data sheet gives an 8 to 12 second discharge and a 6 to 8 foot range; I'm measuring both on two units. Older plastic-handle FA110G units made from 2012 to August 2017 are on a [CPSC recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/Kidde-Recalls-Fire-Extinguishers-with-Plastic-Handles-Due-to-Failure-to-Discharge-and-Nozzle-Detachment-One-Death-Reported); the review shows how to read the date code. NFPA found more than half of non-fatal cooking-fire injuries happened when people tried to fight the fire themselves, so an extinguisher is for a small fire with a clear exit behind you.

Read the [Kidde FA110G review](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/). {/* MEASURE: Kidde FA110G discharge time and range, one line */}

## StoveTop FireStop Rangehood: automatic, over the burners

Two powder canisters that hang by magnet under a range hood and are designed to open when flames touch a fuse. The maker's FAQ says it is not UL-certified, and a November 2016 batch was [recalled by the CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires) for failing to release powder. Stainless hoods may need the maker's adhesive kit.

Read the [StoveTop FireStop review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/), and the [installation guide](/guides/stovetop-firestop-installation/) for mounting height, placement and the stainless-hood kit. {/* MEASURE: StoveTop FireStop activation time, one line */}

## iGuard for Electric Stoves: prevention

A box between an electric range and its outlet that uses radar to notice an empty kitchen, warns, then cuts power to the burners. Popular Science named it the best stove safety device in its [2026 Home of the Future Awards](https://www.popsci.com/gear/home-of-the-future-awards-2026/). At $549 it costs far more than the other two, and it's electric only.

Read the [iGuard review](/reviews/iguard-electric-stove/). {/* MEASURE: iGuard shut-off delay and false shut-offs, one line */}
