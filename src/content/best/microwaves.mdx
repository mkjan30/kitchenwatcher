## How these six microwaves are ranked

The order is editorial, by weight of evidence, never by commission. Independent lab picks count most: [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537-the-best-microwaves), [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/countertop) and [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/small-appliances/best-right-now/best-countertop-microwaves) all describe their own tests. Editor picks that aren't based on a test count less: [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/best-microwaves,review-6316.html) says it "prioritized trusted brands with years of user testimonial", and only its GÅTEBO entry describes the author using the oven at home. After that come readable manuals, recalls and safety alerts, then price.

Two disclosures. Two of the six are Toshiba-brand, and Consumer Reports says "Toshiba-brand microwaves are manufactured by Midea." And the outlets barely agree: ATK's two Highly Recommended models are both Breville, RTINGS's top countertop pick is the Breville Smooth Wave, [Food Network's](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-microwaves) best overall is a Panasonic family-size model, and none of the six appears on more than two of these lists.

## 1. Toshiba EM131A5C-BS: best microwave for most people

This is the default with the broadest support for a mid-size kitchen. Toshiba's manual lists 1,100 W output, 1.2 cu ft, a 12.4-in turntable, a sensor menu (Sensor Reheat, Frozen Pizza, Frozen Entrée, Potato, Rice, Veggie) and a one-year warranty. RTINGS says it cooks food very fast and is very quiet, with a dedicated mute button, a steam sensor and a programmable Favorite button. Its main drawback, per RTINGS, is the membrane panel: fine-tuning times and power levels takes more steps.

Tom's Guide names it best overall and says it heats evenly and quickly, but its write-up contradicts itself. It says the oven has "no sensors" and later praises its humidity sensor, and it says 1,000 W where the manual says 1,100 W output.

On safety, [Consumer Reports' June 25, 2026 alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) counts one incident citing model EM131A5C (no suffix given) among 28 SaferProducts.gov reports of Toshiba-brand microwaves starting or adding time on their own, most of them on the ML2-EC10SA combo. CR says it "has not experienced a similar problem with the Toshiba microwaves we have tested." This is a safety alert, not a recall. Read the [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS review](/reviews/toshiba-em131a5c-bs/) and [why a Toshiba microwave turns on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/).

## 2. Breville Compact Wave Soft Close BMO650: ATK's winner, easiest to use

ATK (Lisa McManus and Valerie Sizhe Li, October 2, 2026) rates it Winner and Highly Recommended. Its tests covered melting butter and chocolate, toasting coconut, defrosting ground beef, reheating mac and cheese and cooking bacon. ATK praises the intuitive controls, the soft-close door and a brushed finish that hid fingerprints, and notes there is no separate timer.

Breville's instruction book lists 1,350 W input, 900 W output, 0.9 cu ft (25 L), a 10.6-in (270 mm) turntable, a mute button and a net weight of about 27.5 lb. It lists no sensor cooking.

The evidence is thin in one way: like the Sharp and the ML-EM45P(BS), it has a single recommender. It is also near the top of the price range here: ATK's page listed it at $250 at Amazon on Nov 27, 2026. ATK and RTINGS both rank the bigger Breville Smooth Wave BMO850, a 1.2 cu ft inverter model per ATK, highly; it was left off this list on price. ## 4. Sharp SMC2266KS: best large, family-size microwave

RTINGS makes it Best Upper Mid-Range Countertop (list updated November 4, 2026). It calls the Sharp powerful and quick to cook, with capacity that rivals many over-the-range models. RTINGS also lists it as exceptionally quiet, with a cooking sensor and a +30 s button. It has no dedicated mute button, per RTINGS; you hold "8" for three seconds.

Sharp's spec sheet lists 2.2 cu ft, 1,200 W output, inverter technology, a 16.5-in glass turntable, 11 power levels, 24.4 × 19.6 × 13.6 in and 33.7 lb. The warranty is one year parts and labor plus four more years on the magnetron, part only. Sharp's own footnote says "Actual capacity for holding food is less." Sharp's store listed it at $239.99 on Nov 27, 2026, a Black Friday sale price against a $269.99 MSRP.

It is the only one of the six listed with an inverter, and that feature is contested. ## Which microwave size and wattage do you need?

ATK found about 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft "perfectly adequate" unless you routinely microwave or defrost very large amounts, and at about 900 to 1,000 W you can use full power for the suggested time. Food Network says 0.8 to 1.2 cu ft is sufficient for most people and recommends at least 800 to 1,200 W if you plan to do more than reheat. Tom's Guide says to measure the turntable, not trust the cubic feet.

Rated capacity overstates usable space: Sharp and IKEA both footnote that "actual capacity for holding food is less." If you want the best small microwave, the 0.9 cu ft Breville BMO650 and BLACK+DECKER EM925AB9 are the compact picks here. ## Are any of these microwaves recalled?

As of November 27, 2026, none of the six appears in the CPSC recall database, searched through its recall API by product name and brand. Most microwave-oven recalls in that database are over-the-range or built-in units.

The Toshiba self-starting reports are a Consumer Reports safety alert, not a recall, and CR is calling on the CPSC to investigate. CR also asks affected owners to report it at SaferProducts.gov.

Midea's statement concerns "a limited number of units of Toshiba Microwave Air Fryer Oven ML2-EC10SA in which the power button can stick in a way that causes unintended activation of the 30-second add-time function." Midea says it has "found no evidence that this issue poses a safety risk", and says owners of that model who have the problem can call 866-646-4332 for evaluation and free service. See the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/) and [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/).

## Microwave safety basics from the manuals and FDA

- **Leakage limit.** Per the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens), 21 CFR 1030.10 caps leakage at 5 mW/cm² at about 2 inches from the oven surface over its lifetime, and every oven needs two independent interlock systems. See [is microwave radiation safe](/guides/is-microwave-radiation-safe/).
- **Damaged door.** The FDA says not to use an oven whose door doesn't close firmly or is bent, warped or otherwise damaged, to stop using one that keeps running with the door open, and to contact the manufacturer about damaged hinges, latches or seals.
- **Fire inside.** Toshiba's manual says that if materials inside ignite, keep the oven door closed, turn the oven off and unplug it, disconnect the power cord and shut off power at the fuse or circuit breaker panel. See [microwave fire: what to do](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/).
- **Foil rules differ.** Toshiba's manual allows small, smooth pieces for shielding only, at least 1 inch from the oven walls; Breville's says food-grade foil should not be used. Follow your own manual. See [can you put aluminum foil in the microwave](/guides/can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-the-microwave/) and [why is my microwave sparking](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/).
- **Superheated water.** The FDA says water heated past boiling can erupt when disturbed, and adding instant coffee or sugar before heating greatly reduces the risk.
