The best electric pressure cooker for most people is the Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-quart: TechGearLab named it Best Overall with 86/100, and Reviewed named it its pick for most people. The upgrade is the Instant Pot Pro 8-quart, America's Test Kitchen's winner. The best pressure cooker under $100 is the COSORI 6.0-Quart, ATK's Best Buy, listed on ATK's page at $90 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026. The labs agree that most electric cookers pressure-cook well; they split on usability and slow cooking.

This page compiles published lab results, Instant's FAQ and the CPSC recall record. I did not use any of these cookers myself; the method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Our electric pressure cooker picks at a glance

Each pick has its own numbered section below. Three of the labels carry a warning, because the labs disagree or only one lab covered the model.

1. **Best for most people, and the best Instant Pot overall:** Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-Quart.
2. **Best upgrade for big batches (8 qt):** Instant Pot Pro 8-Quart.
3. **Best smart cooker:** CHEF iQ Smart Cooker 6-Quart, though ATK has reservations.
4. **Simplest Instant Pot:** Instant Pot Rio 6-Quart, though ATK has reservations.
5. **Best pressure cooker under $100, and strong at slow cooking:** COSORI 6.0-Quart, on one lab's evidence.
6. **Best splurge:** Breville the Fast Slow Pro, with the labs split.

## How these six are ranked

The order is editorial and follows the weight of evidence, not commission. First come independent lab awards: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) (May 14, 2025), [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker) (Nov 24, 2024), [Reviewed](https://reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-electric-multi-cookers-and-instant-pots) (Nov 12, 2025) and [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/multi-cookers/best-multi-cookers-of-the-year-a7136971834) (Jan 1, 2026, public part). Then where those labs disagree, then the CPSC recall record, then price.

Four of the six are Instant Pots because that is what the labs pick. No Ninja is ranked: the two Ninja models TechGearLab scored list at $250 and $350, and SharkNinja recalled about 1.85 million Foodi OP300-series pressure cookers in 2025 (CPSC 25-247, OP300-series models only). For the full comparison, see [Instant Pot vs Ninja Foodi](/guides/instant-pot-vs-ninja-foodi/).

## 1. Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-Quart: best electric pressure cooker for most people

This is the pick for a first multicooker. TechGearLab made the Duo Plus V4 its Editors' Choice Best Overall at 86/100 (list $150). It cited excellent overall cooking performance, a quieter and safer steam valve, a versatile 9-in-1 design and an easy-to-use control panel ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker)). Its cons: the pot can be hard to clean, the cooker is too large for some shelves, and sauté can be finicky.

Reviewed separately named the Duo Plus its Best Pressure Cooker for Most People (updated Nov 12, 2025). It praised the quiet steam release, nine cooking functions, 25 recipe presets and dishwasher-safe pot and lid ([Reviewed](https://reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-electric-multi-cookers-and-instant-pots)). Reviewed says it tested a previous version of the model. One error to watch: Reviewed's spec box lists 8 quarts while its text describes a 6-quart.

America's Test Kitchen did not test the Duo Plus. Its list covers the plain Duo 6 and Duo 8, so ATK's Duo verdicts don't apply here. That leaves two labs behind this model and no lab against it that I could read.

For depth, read the [Instant Pot Duo Plus review](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/). If you're choosing between this and the cheaper Rio, see [Instant Pot Rio vs Duo Plus](/vs/instant-pot-rio-6qt-vs-instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/).

## 2. Instant Pot Pro 8-Quart: best upgrade for big batches

The Pro 8 suits people who cook for a crowd or batch-cook and want better browning. It is America's Test Kitchen's Winner, Highly Recommended ([ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers)). ATK's page listed it at $150 at Amazon (regular $200) on Nov 13, 2026, and gives $149.99 as the price at time of testing. ATK lists an 8.25-inch cooking surface, the widest of the models on this list that ATK lists, and says the flat-bottomed pot allows for even searing.

Its weakness, per ATK, is slow cooking: it couldn't slow-cook large cuts of meat well. If you mainly want a slow cooker, look at #5 or #6 instead.

For a smaller kitchen, ATK also rates the Pro 6-quart Highly Recommended, with a 7.75-inch cooking surface, listed at $170 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026. ATK says it didn't perform well as a slow cooker either.

The [Instant Pot Pro 8-quart review](/reviews/instant-pot-pro-8qt/) goes deeper, and [Instant Pot Pro vs Breville Fast Slow Pro](/vs/instant-pot-pro-8qt-vs-breville-bpr700/) compares it with the premium pick.

## 3. CHEF iQ Smart Cooker 6-Quart: best smart multicooker, with reservations

This suits a cook who wants guidance on screen. Reviewed made it its Best Value pick. It cited a built-in tare function for weighing ingredients into the pot, a display of how much pressure is building, and a quick release that beeps before it starts; the app is optional ([Reviewed](https://reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-electric-multi-cookers-and-instant-pots)). Consumer Reports calls it "highly rated," though its free article shows no scores.

Popular Science named it best overall, but that page dates from February 2023 ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/gear/best-pressure-cookers/)). America's Test Kitchen disagrees with the others: it rated the CHEF iQ Recommended with Reservations and said its dark pot ran hotter than expected. ATK's page listed it at $150 at Amazon (regular $200) on Nov 13, 2026.

ATK also says setup took almost 30 minutes (app download, account, Wi-Fi and an update), and slow cooking was mixed: beans turned tender, but stew vegetables stayed underdone. It lists an 8-inch cooking surface.

So two current outlets and one dated one back it, and ATK has doubts. That puts it third, behind the two models that no lab I could read rated with reservations.

## 4. Instant Pot Rio 6-Quart: the simplest Instant Pot

The Rio is for buyers who want a basic Instant Pot. The evidence I could read is thin and negative. America's Test Kitchen rated it Recommended with Reservations ([ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers)). It found the sauté function underpowered and hard to brown with, said the Rio didn't slow-cook well, and noted the pot lacks handles. ATK's page listed it at $156 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026.

Reviewed covers only the 4-quart Rio Mini, Consumer Reports' free article doesn't rank the Rio, and TechGearLab didn't test it.

That is why it sits fourth. Compare it directly in [Instant Pot Rio vs Duo Plus](/vs/instant-pot-rio-6qt-vs-instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/) and [COSORI vs Instant Pot Rio](/vs/cosori-cmc-co601-vs-instant-pot-rio-6qt/).

## 5. COSORI 6.0-Quart: best pressure cooker under $100

The best pressure cooker under $100 on lab evidence is the COSORI 6.0-Quart (CMC-CO601). America's Test Kitchen made it its Best Buy, Highly Recommended, and said it excelled in both pressure and slow cooking tests ([ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers)). ATK's page listed it at $90 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026. Of ATK's three Highly Recommended models, it is the only one ATK rated top marks for slow cooking.

The weaknesses, per ATK: its 7-inch cooking surface is relatively narrow, and the dark nonstick pot made browning hard to monitor. The bigger caveat is thin evidence, since ATK is the only lab I found that covered it.

COSORI's 2023 recall (CPSC 23-130) was of air fryers, a different product; the [best air fryers](/best/air-fryers/) page covers it. To weigh it against the cheapest Instant Pot, see [COSORI vs Instant Pot Rio](/vs/cosori-cmc-co601-vs-instant-pot-rio-6qt/).

## 6. Breville Fast Slow Pro: best splurge, but the labs split

The Breville suits someone who wants strong slow cooking and steaming and will pay a premium; ATK's page listed it at $330 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026. TechGearLab made it Editors' Choice Runner Up at 84/100 (list $330) for its automatic steam release, user-friendly controls and great results with meat; its con is that it's very expensive ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker)).

Consumer Reports' Jan 1, 2026 update covers the 6-quart, 1,100-watt BPR700BSS. CR said it aces its pressure-cooking test and is masterful at slow cooking: beef stew tender in 5 hours, chili in 7 ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/multi-cookers/best-multi-cookers-of-the-year-a7136971834)). CR called its steaming top-notch because it doesn't use pressure for that mode, and the lid doesn't lock during steaming. CR also found the ceramic nonstick unscratched after testing.

America's Test Kitchen disagrees on usability: it rated it Recommended with Reservations because the attached lid stood vertically when open and got in the way while sautéing. ATK still said it seared beef well and made good pressure- and slow-cooked beef stew and beans.

Its predecessor, the BPR600XL, was recalled in December 2015 because its sealing gasket could be inserted upside down and release pressure unexpectedly (CPSC 16-066). The BPR700 ranked here is not in the CPSC record. See [Instant Pot Pro vs Breville Fast Slow Pro](/vs/instant-pot-pro-8qt-vs-breville-bpr700/).

## Which size electric pressure cooker do you need?

Instant's FAQ says the quart size is the complete volume of the inner pot, and the volume when cooking is lower (Instant Pot FAQ). You fill only to the PC MAX line (2/3), or to the 1/2 line for foods that expand, such as rice.

ATK's winner is the 8-quart Pro, while the other five picks here are 6 quarts. The [what size Instant Pot](/guides/what-size-instant-pot/) guide works through it, and [how much water in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/) covers the fill lines.

## Which electric pressure cookers can also slow-cook well?

Slow cooking is the recurring weak spot in America's Test Kitchen's multicooker tests. ATK found the Instant Pot Pro 8 and Pro 6 weak at it. Its Duo 6 left the potatoes and carrots raw in slow-cooked stew, and its Duo 8 left stew underdone after 9 hours.

The exceptions here are the COSORI (ATK) and the Breville (ATK and CR). If slow cooking matters as much as pressure cooking, those are the two to shortlist.

"Fast" needs a caveat too. Instant's FAQ says pressurizing "can take anywhere from 10 to 40 minutes or longer" before the countdown starts. Release time adds to that; see [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

## Are any of these pressure cookers recalled?

None of the six is in the CPSC recall record, checked through the saferproducts.gov API on Nov 13, 2026. The largest recent electric pressure cooker recall is SharkNinja's CPSC 25-247, announced May 1, 2025 ([CPSC record](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=25247)). It covers about 1,846,400 Ninja Foodi OP300-series multi-function pressure cookers in the US, whose pressure-cooking lid can be opened during use. SharkNinja had received 106 reports of burn injuries. The CPSC says to stop using the pressure function and get a free replacement lid.

Other pressure cooker recalls in the record:

- Ambiano electric pressure cookers sold at ALDI, about 46,660 units (26-007, Oct 9, 2025).
- Insignia pressure cookers, about 930,000 units (24-013, Oct 26, 2023).
- Sensio's Bella, Bella Pro Series, Cooks and Crux electric and stovetop cookers, about 860,000 units (23-256, Aug 10, 2023).
- Crock-Pot 6-Quart Express Crock multi-cookers, about 914,430 units (21-035, Nov 24, 2020).
- Breville's BPR600XL, about 35,600 units (16-066, Dec 22, 2015).
- Instant Pot "Smart" and "Smart-60" Bluetooth models, about 1,000 units, for a shock hazard (15-741, Jul 15, 2015). No current Instant Pot on this page is included.

The [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/) lists every one with model numbers, and [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/) explains the hazard pattern.

## How to use an Instant Pot safely, per Instant's FAQ

These rules are from the Instant Pot FAQ and apply to Instant Pots; for the CHEF iQ, COSORI and Breville, follow that model's own manual:

- Never force the lid open. Set the valve to venting and open it only after the float valve has dropped.
- Don't fill past the PC MAX line (2/3), or past the 1/2 line for foods that expand, such as rice.
- Use at least this much thin liquid: 1 cup in a 3-quart, 1½ cups in a 6-quart, 2 cups in an 8-quart, 2½ cups in a 10-quart.
- Replace the sealing ring every 12 to 18 months, or sooner.

For canning, the National Center for Home Food Preservation says "we do not recommend our canning processes for use in electric multi-cookers at this time," including models sold with canning buttons ([NCHFP](https://nchfp.uga.edu/blog/can-i-can-in-a-multi-cooker)). More in [can you can in an Instant Pot](/guides/can-you-can-in-an-instant-pot/).

Before first use, run the [Instant Pot water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/). General kitchen safety is in [how to prevent kitchen fires](/guides/how-to-prevent-kitchen-fires/).
