For most kitchens, the best rice cooker is the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 Neuro Fuzzy: it is America's Test Kitchen's Winner, Reviewed's Best Overall and CNN Underscored's top pick. For far less, Serious Eats' best rice cooker is the Hamilton Beach 37548, $67 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026. For one or two people, the 3-cup Zojirushi NHS-06 ($60 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026, per Serious Eats) is the cheapest of these six.

Nobody at Kitchen Watcher cooked in any of these. This ranking pools six published test roundups, the manufacturers' manuals and the CPSC recall database, and every number below names who measured it. The full method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## The six best rice cookers at a glance

1. **Zojirushi NS-ZCC10: best rice cooker overall and best Zojirushi rice cooker.** ATK Winner, Reviewed Best Overall, CNN Underscored's top pick.
2. **Cuckoo CR-0655F: most customizable, for less.** Engadget's best for most people, CNN Underscored's budget pick and Serious Eats' best Cuckoo.
3. **KitchenAid KGC3155BM: best upgrade, with a scale that measures the water.** ATK Highly Recommended, Reviewed Best Upgrade.
4. **Hamilton Beach 37548: best rice cooker under $100 and best for big batches.** Serious Eats' best rice cooker since 2019.
5. **Zojirushi NL-BAC05: best small rice cooker with fuzzy logic.** ATK Recommended; made as little as 1 cup of cooked rice in ATK's tests.
6. **Zojirushi NHS-06: best small, simple rice cooker under $100.** Serious Eats' best small pick.

## How these six rice cookers are ranked

The order is editorial, by weight of evidence, never by commission. It rests first on the published results from [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) (Oct 23, 2025, 50 batches), [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-rice-cookers) (updated Oct 14, 2026, 26 cookers), CNN Underscored (Jan 8, 2026), [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-rice-cookers) (Oct 28, 2025), Engadget (Jan 2, 2026) and [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-rice-cooker/) (Sep 30, 2026). After those results come how consistent the complaints are, the CPSC recall record and price. No review counts or star ratings were used.

Three of the six are Zojirushi because that is where the recommenders' picks fall. No induction model made the cut: the outlets split on whether induction helps, which the [induction vs micom guide](/guides/induction-vs-micom-rice-cooker/) covers.

## 1. Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 Neuro Fuzzy: best rice cooker overall

This is the safest buy if you cook rice often and can wait for it. ATK made it its Winner because it "consistently produced excellent rice in both small and large batches," and praised its clear white water lines and stay-cool pan handles. ATK also notes long-grain white goes on the Mixed setting, which it found only in the booklet. Reviewed named it Best Overall for consistently good brown, short-grain and jasmine rice, and Serious Eats calls it its test-kitchen rice cooker.

CNN Underscored added a cup too much or too little water and found the rice "still edible and actually not that bad." Its Quick Cook cut sushi rice from 42 to 31 minutes. It is also the best Zojirushi rice cooker on this evidence, with more independent awards than any other model here.

The main weakness is speed. Zojirushi's manual lists regular white at 48 to 55 minutes and brown at 84 to 107 minutes for the 1.0 L size. Engadget measured 48 minutes for white and just under 2 hours for brown. Reviewed had to press the control panel firmly.

Keep warm is where sources conflict. The manual says not to keep rice warm for more than 12 hours. Per the manual, Extended Keep Warm holds about 140°F, switches back to regular Keep Warm after 8 hours, and can't be started once regular Keep Warm has run 12 hours. CNN says Extended Keep Warm runs 8 hours past the 12-hour period. Go by the manual's 12-hour limit.

See the [Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 review](/reviews/zojirushi-ns-zcc10/), [Zojirushi vs Cuckoo](/vs/zojirushi-ns-zcc10-vs-cuckoo-cr-0655f/) and [Zojirushi error codes](/guides/zojirushi-rice-cooker-error-codes/).

## 2. Cuckoo CR-0655F 6-cup Micom: most customizable for less

It suits cooks who want to tweak soak time and heat and would rather pay about $140 than about $240 (ATK's Amazon listings showed $140 and $239 on Nov 18, 2026). Engadget named it best for most people for its preset results and its custom My Mode. CNN Underscored made it a budget pick, timing a batch at 34 minutes (Quick Cook 32) and noting an Auto Clean steam cycle. CNN found its rice stickier than the Zojirushi's, and the unit has no handle.

Serious Eats calls it its best Cuckoo for its adjustable soak time, heat and cycle length, but says beginners may find the number of presets and options overwhelming.

The main dissent comes from ATK, which rated it Recommended with Reservations. Long-grain white was great, but short-grain and brown rice came out a little wet at Cuckoo's ratios, and the water lines imprinted into the metal were hard to read. Engadget says the lid springs open hard enough to rock the unit.

CNN calls it a pick for "less than $100" while listing it at $140, so we don't use that label. Cuckoo's manual gives a capacity of 2 to 6 cups and recommends keeping rice warm for less than 12 hours. See the [Cuckoo CR-0655F review](/reviews/cuckoo-cr-0655f/) and [Hamilton Beach vs Cuckoo](/vs/hamilton-beach-37548-vs-cuckoo-cr-0655f/).

## 3. KitchenAid KGC3155BM: best upgrade, with a scale that measures the water

You add the grain; a built-in scale and reservoir dispense the water. ATK rated it Highly Recommended but called it "quite expensive," and notes it makes at most 8 cups of cooked rice against 11 for the Zojirushi. Reviewed named it Best Upgrade for its 21 presets, but it weighs 10.5 lb and takes up a decent amount of counter space.

Serious Eats calls it the most foolproof, with delay start up to 24 hours. Its Keep Warm maxes out at 6 hours, though, and it has fewer rice-specific features than the Zojirushi or Cuckoo. CNN Underscored says the countdown only shows remaining time in the last 10 minutes.

Prices vary widely: on Nov 18, 2026, Reviewed's listings showed $299.99 at Walmart and Target, and Serious Eats' showed $260 on sale at Amazon against a $400 list. See [KitchenAid vs Zojirushi](/vs/kitchenaid-kgc3155bm-vs-zojirushi-ns-zcc10/) and [cooking quinoa in a rice cooker](/guides/can-you-cook-quinoa-in-a-rice-cooker/).

## 4. Hamilton Beach 37548: best rice cooker under $100 and for big batches

It suits families who want a lot of rice fast for $67 (Amazon, Nov 18, 2026, per Serious Eats). Serious Eats has named it its best rice cooker since 2019: the fastest of 26 not counting quick modes, a large batch in about 30 minutes, and forgiving of small measuring errors. Serious Eats also says large batches can dry out or brown, and an editor notes the hinged lid can fall shut.

CNN Underscored says its Hamilton Beach Digital Programmable Food Steamer & Rice Cooker "almost made our winners' circle" before the Cuckoo edged it out. CNN's retailer links point to two different models (one of them the 37530), so it's unclear CNN tested the 37548.

The dissent: Reviewed found it overcooked the bottom of a 1-cup batch, and rice stuck to the pot.

Per Hamilton Beach's use and care guide, its cup is 6 oz (177 ml), about 3/4 US cup, and you shouldn't exceed 7 cups uncooked. Delay start runs up to 15 hours, WARM up to 12 hours before it turns off, and long-grain white at the 7-cup maximum takes 50 to 52 minutes. Engadget's budget Hamilton Beach is a different model, so its results aren't counted. See [Hamilton Beach vs Cuckoo](/vs/hamilton-beach-37548-vs-cuckoo-cr-0655f/) and [why rice burns on the bottom](/guides/rice-cooker-burning-rice-bottom/).

## 5. Zojirushi NL-BAC05 3-cup Micom: best small rice cooker with fuzzy logic

This is for one or two people who still want fuzzy logic and extra menus. ATK rated it Recommended: it made as little as 1 cup of cooked rice, the smallest amount of any model ATK tested, and brown rice came out better with less water than directed. Reviewed ranks it fifth on its list, without an award, noting its quinoa, steel-cut oatmeal and cake presets but that its 3-cup capacity won't feed a crowd.

Per the manual, its cup is about 180 ml (6.1 oz), it cooks 0.5 to 3 cups of white rice, and keep warm runs up to 12 hours. The manual also says the nonstick coating "can peel off if damaged" and that peeling is "harmless to your health"; replace the pan if you're concerned. See [peeling inner pot coating](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/) and [what size rice cooker you need](/guides/what-size-rice-cooker-do-i-need/).

## 6. Zojirushi NHS-06 3-cup: best small, simple rice cooker under $100

It suits small households who want one switch for $60 (Amazon, Nov 18, 2026, per Serious Eats; $70 list). Serious Eats named it best small: the fastest 3-cup cooker it tested, a full 3-cup jasmine batch in 26 minutes, no hot spots, and a glass lid. [Consumer Reports](https://news.yahoo.com/best-rice-cookers-consumer-reports-135809338.html) named it its best classic rice cooker in 2022, scoring it Excellent and fastest in its test, but that test is now dated.

The dissent: CNET's Zojirushi 3-cup "disappointed" with rice that was "too wet and slightly broken down." CNET doesn't print a model number, but its link for that cooker goes to the NHS-06 listing.

Per Zojirushi's manual, capacity is 180 to 600 cc (3 cups). The NHS-06 is the non-keep-warm type and shuts off when done; the NHS-10 and NHS-18 shouldn't stay on keep warm more than 5 hours. See [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Where the rice cooker reviewers disagree

- **Speed vs quality.** ATK found slower cycles made better rice, with large long-grain batches taking about 30 to 65 minutes. Serious Eats disagrees: "the best rice cookers were fast." CNET's top pick, a Tiger, won partly on 21-minute white rice.
- **The Hamilton Beach.** Serious Eats' best rice cooker scorched a 1-cup batch at Reviewed.
- **The Cuckoo.** Engadget's and CNN's pick is only Recommended with Reservations at ATK.
- **Induction.** ATK says induction models "didn't consistently perform better or faster," and CNN says quality didn't improve much. Reviewed still has a Best Induction pick. Serious Eats' best Zojirushi is the induction NW-QAC10.
- **The Toshiba TRCS01.** It is ATK's Budget Winner, but Serious Eats says it "made wet rice in almost every test," which is why it isn't ranked here.

## How to choose a rice cooker for your kitchen

### Rice cooker cups vs US cups: picking a size

Rice cooker cups are smaller than US cups: about 3/4 US cup per Hamilton Beach's guide, about 180 ml per Zojirushi's. CNET says 5 to 6 cups is plenty for two or three people. See [what size rice cooker you need](/guides/what-size-rice-cooker-do-i-need/) and [rice cooker cup size](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/).

### Is fuzzy logic or induction worth paying for?

Per ATK, every cooker that made excellent rice had fuzzy logic, but not every fuzzy-logic model did. Induction is the most disputed upgrade. See [induction vs micom](/guides/induction-vs-micom-rice-cooker/).

### Keep warm limits and cooked-rice food safety

FDA's [Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download) says the vomiting-type Bacillus cereus illness most often affects rice and other starchy foods, and that cooking may not disable its toxin. [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) says keep cooked food at 140°F or above and [reheat leftovers to 165°F](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

Manual limits differ: 12 hours on the Zojirushi micom models and Hamilton Beach, 5 hours on the NHS-10 and NHS-18, none on the NHS-06. FoodSafety.gov gives no keep-warm duration, so never go past your manual's limit. See [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

### Which inner pots can go in the dishwasher

Dishwasher safety depends on the manual. Zojirushi's NL-BAC manual says not to use a dishwasher; Hamilton Beach's guide marks parts dishwasher-safe (no SANI cycle) and says the lid liner can be removed to wash by hand. See [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).

### Should you use an Instant Pot instead?

CNN Underscored found rice from three pressure cookers "extremely gummy and wet." See [rice cooker vs Instant Pot](/guides/rice-cooker-vs-instant-pot/), the [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) and [how to cook rice in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/).

## Recall record for these six rice cookers (CPSC)

The [CPSC recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=rice), queried Nov 18, 2026, shows no recall for any of the six models. The only rice-cooker recall it returns is 17-017 (Oct 18, 2016): about 175,000 3 Squares rice and slow cookers from Sherwood Marketing, recalled for fire and electric shock hazards.

Searches of the same database by recall title the same day returned nothing for Zojirushi or Cuckoo. The Hamilton Beach and KitchenAid results were other product types, such as toasters, slow cookers and kettles. See the [rice cooker recall check](/guides/rice-cooker-recall-check/).

## Common rice cooker problems and the guides that fix them

- **Mushy or hard rice:** Zojirushi's NS-ZCC troubleshooting chart lists causes to check, including rice and water not measured correctly, rice not washed well and the wrong menu. See [rice cooker rice mushy](/guides/rice-cooker-rice-mushy/).
- **Burnt bottom:** Serious Eats saw large Hamilton Beach batches brown, and Reviewed saw a 1-cup batch overcook. See [rice burning on the bottom](/guides/rice-cooker-burning-rice-bottom/).
- **Peeling coating:** the NL-BAC manual says peeling is harmless and the pan can be replaced. See [inner pot coating peeling](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/).
- **Error codes:** the NS-ZCC manual explains H01, H02, H04, E01 and E02. See [Zojirushi error codes](/guides/zojirushi-rice-cooker-error-codes/).
