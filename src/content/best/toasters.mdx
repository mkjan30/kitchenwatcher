The best toaster for most people is the Breville Bit More BTA720XL. TechGearLab named it the Best 2-Slice Toaster for Most People, and America's Test Kitchen lists it as recommended. The best budget toaster is the BLACK+DECKER T2569B, and the best 4 slice toaster here is the Cuisinart CPT-180, with caveats. No single model wins everywhere: the published lab results conflict on at least three of these six.

This page compiles published lab results, owner manuals and the CPSC recall record. I didn't put any of these toasters through a test myself; the method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Our toaster picks at a glance

Each pick has its own numbered section below. The labels follow the weight of evidence, and two of them come with a warning about conflicting lab results.

1. **Best 2 slice toaster overall:** Breville Bit More BTA720XL.
2. **Best if you want features and build:** Breville Die-Cast BTA820XL.
3. **Best 4 slice toaster:** Cuisinart Metal Classic CPT-180, on thin and conflicting evidence.
4. **Best budget toaster:** BLACK+DECKER T2569B.
5. **Best value for plain bread:** Cuisinart CPT-122.
6. **ATK's Best Conventional Toaster:** GE G9TMA2SSPSS.

## How these six toasters are ranked

I weigh the evidence in a fixed order: independent measurements from [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) (published April 15, 2026), [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) (updated August 14, 2025) and [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) (updated October 22, 2025), then how consistent owner reports are, then recalls, then price. Commission plays no part. I couldn't verify owner reviews on Amazon for this page, so none are cited.

RTINGS, Consumer Reports, Good Housekeeping and Serious Eats were paywalled or unreadable when I checked, so nothing from them is used here.

## 1. Breville Bit More BTA720XL: best toaster for most people

This is the pick for anyone who wants one 2-slice toaster with the fewest red flags. TechGearLab named it the Best 2-Slice Toaster for Most People with 69/100, scoring bread 6.0, bagels 7.0 and frozen waffles 8.0. America's Test Kitchen recommends it but timed it at 2 min 32 s to medium, the slowest of its recommended toasters, and said it "hardly toasted on its lowest setting".

The weakness is evenness at the edges: TechGearLab noted that "one corner of each slice remained a bit lighter than the rest." ATK lists A Bit More, Bagel, Frozen and Cancel buttons on the BTA720XL; Breville's [BTA720/BTA730 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf), the Australian edition, says pressing A Bit More automatically increases the toasting time.

Read the full [Breville BTA720XL review](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) or the [Bit More vs Die-Cast comparison](/vs/breville-bta720xl-vs-breville-bta820xl/).

## 2. Breville Die-Cast BTA820XL: the best-built, but the evidence splits

This suits buyers who want electronic controls and a heavier build and will pay for them. Reviewed named it Best Overall, praising its construction and electronic controls, and lists it at 11 x 6.75 x 7.75 in and 5.7 lb. Its main con, per Reviewed, is that it's expensive for a pop-up toaster: Reviewed's page showed $186.67 at Amazon on Nov 6, 2026.

America's Test Kitchen disagrees on results. It found the BTA820XL "consistently made toast with pale spots" while averaging 2 min 11 s to medium. TechGearLab scored it 66/100 with no award, below the cheaper Bit More.

So the three labs give three different answers. It ranks second for its build and Reviewed's Best Overall award, not for its toasting results. The [BTA720XL vs BTA820XL page](/vs/breville-bta720xl-vs-breville-bta820xl/) settles the pair.

## 3. Cuisinart Metal Classic CPT-180: best 4 slice toaster, with caveats

This is the only 4-slice toaster in the ranking, for households that toast for several people at once. [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/reviews/best-toaster/) (updated March 29, 2024) named it best 4-slice and found four slices and split English muffins toasted well edge to edge. It has independent left and right controls plus Bagel, Defrost and Reheat settings, and Popular Science lists it at 10.6 x 11.15 x 7.5 in and 6.06 lb.

The rest of the evidence is less kind. TechGearLab scored it 58/100, the lowest of these six, citing a large footprint and uneven toasting, though its 3-year warranty ties the CPT-122 for the longest TechGearLab lists here. Reviewed's 2023 roundup, which tested the CPT-180P1 version, found one or two slots often much darker and levers that often needed several pushes, though it was the fastest of that group.

The 4-slice evidence is dated and conflicting, and I'd rather say so than dress it up. See the [Cuisinart CPT-180 review](/reviews/cuisinart-cpt-180/) and [2-slice vs 4-slice toaster guide](/guides/2-slice-vs-4-slice-toaster/).

## 4. BLACK+DECKER T2569B: best budget toaster, if you trust two of three labs

This is for buyers who want the cheapest toaster with a lab award. TechGearLab named it Best Toaster on a Budget at 68/100, with a bread score of 8.0, though it says the shade dial runs "hotter than labeled." Reviewed found it "really shines at toasting evenly", with minimal variation over five batches in a row and the coolest exterior of the models it tested. Reviewed's page showed it at $29.99 at Amazon on Nov 6, 2026.

America's Test Kitchen saw the opposite. It rated the T2569B Recommended with Reservations because "it just didn't toast evenly, no matter what settings we tried", and its bagels came out with untoasted sections. TechGearLab lists a 2-year warranty.

Two labs for, one firmly against: that split is why it sits fourth. The [GE vs BLACK+DECKER comparison](/vs/ge-g9tma2sspss-vs-black-decker-t2569b/) goes through it side by side.

## 5. Cuisinart CPT-122: best value for plain bread

This suits someone who mostly toasts sliced bread and wants consistency. Reviewed named it Best Value: five 2-slice batches in a row on medium gave 10 identical slices in about six minutes, and dark toast took under 3 minutes. TechGearLab agrees on bread, scoring it 8.0.

Bagels are the weak spot. TechGearLab scored its bagels 4.5, the lowest of the six, and Reviewed lists it as not compatible with thick bagels and muffins. TechGearLab lists a 3-year warranty, and Reviewed's page showed it at $55.95 at Best Buy on Nov 6, 2026.

The [Cuisinart CPT-122 vs GE comparison](/vs/cuisinart-cpt-122-vs-ge-g9tma2sspss/) covers the closest alternative.

## 6. GE G9TMA2SSPSS: ATK's Best Conventional Toaster

This suits people who want speed. America's Test Kitchen named it Best Conventional Toaster: it was the fastest of the four models here that ATK timed, at 1 min 39 s to medium and "produced relatively evenly toasted slices of white bread and bagels in under 2 minutes", though ATK notes the sides and top get hot. Reviewed found it even and consistent with three distinct shades, but English muffins need the lift lever to come up high enough to grab.

TechGearLab is the outlier: 62/100, uneven toasting coverage, and a frozen-waffle score of 4.5. Per [GE's owner's manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf), it has Bagel, Frozen and Cancel buttons, and the lever "won't lock down unless" it's plugged in. Reviewed's page showed it at $59.00 at Amazon, Walmart, Home Depot and Best Buy on Nov 6, 2026.

Compare it with the [BLACK+DECKER T2569B](/vs/ge-g9tma2sspss-vs-black-decker-t2569b/) or the [Cuisinart CPT-122](/vs/cuisinart-cpt-122-vs-ge-g9tma2sspss/).

## Which toaster size do you need?

Most of the lab-backed picks here are 2-slice, which is why the best 2 slice toaster is easier to name than the best 4-slice. ATK measured the 2-slice slots at 5.5 x 6.25 in (T2569B), 5.63 x 5.88 in (GE), 5.68 x 6.5 in (BTA820XL) and 5.75 x 6.25 in (BTA720XL). If you need four slots, GE's manual notes the 4-slice sibling, G9TMA4SSPSS, has a crumb tray on each side. The [2-slice vs 4-slice toaster guide](/guides/2-slice-vs-4-slice-toaster/) covers the decision.

## Toaster settings and features that matter

Per GE's manual, Bagel mode needs the cut side facing the inside of the toaster, Frozen is the button to press for frozen bread, and Cancel ejects whatever is in the slots. Breville's Australian BTA720/BTA730 booklet describes 12 browning settings from 1 to 5, a Frozen button and a Lift & Look lever that lets you check progress without stopping the cycle.

Don't trust the dial numbers blindly. TechGearLab found the T2569B's dial runs hotter than labeled, and ATK found the BTA720XL barely toasted on its lowest setting. More in [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/) and [the toaster bagel setting](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Are any of these toasters recalled?

No. The [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=toaster), re-checked November 6, 2026, lists no toaster recall for Breville or Cuisinart and none covering any of the six models here. The older recalls under these brands cover different models: GE toasters sold by Wal-Mart (models 169115 and 169116, 2008), and Black & Decker's Infrawave ST2000 (2007), VersaToast (2002) and Spacemaker Optima T1000 (1998 remedy notice). The [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/) lists the full history.

## Toaster safety basics from the manuals

These come straight from GE's owner's manual for the G9TMA2SSPSS and G9TMA4SSPSS and Breville's Australian instruction book for the BTA720/BTA730. Where they disagree, follow the manual for your own model.

- **Unplug when not in use.** GE says "Unplug from outlet when not in use and before cleaning." Breville says to always remove the plug when the toaster isn't in use. See [is it safe to leave a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/).
- **Keep the crumb tray in and empty it.** GE says to operate with the crumb tray in place. Breville warns that failing to clean it regularly "may result in a fire hazard". See [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/).
- **Never use metal to free toast.** GE says "Never use a metal object" for stuck bread, and Breville says a knife can damage the elements and lead to electrocution. See [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/) and [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).
- **Give it room.** GE recommends 4 inches of clearance on all sides and warns of fire if the toaster is covered or touching curtains or walls.
- **The manuals conflict on shaking.** Breville says to turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake crumbs free. GE says "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it". Follow your own model's manual.

If something goes wrong, read [why is my toaster smoking](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-smoking/), [toaster fire: what to do](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/) and [how to prevent kitchen fires](/guides/how-to-prevent-kitchen-fires/).

## Common toaster problems and quick fixes

- **Uneven toast:** America's Test Kitchen counted wires and found the best toaster of one style it tested had 15 on each side of its slots, while lower-rated models had as few as six or eight. See [why is my toaster toasting unevenly](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-toasting-unevenly/).
- **Lever won't stay down:** GE's manual says the lever won't lock unless the toaster is plugged in, and bread that pops straight up means the lever wasn't pushed all the way. See [toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).
- **Smoke:** GE's manual says to press Cancel and empty the crumb tray.
