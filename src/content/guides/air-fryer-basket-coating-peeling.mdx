If your air fryer basket is peeling, replace the basket. No authority we read documents poisoning from coating flakes, but the safety advice is clear. [Poison Control](https://www.poison.org/articles/teflon-flu) says scratched or chipped nonstick coating can release more fumes when heated and that damaged cookware should be replaced. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/process-contaminants-food/authorized-uses-pfas-food-contact-applications) says only negligible PFAS migrate to food from intact nonstick coatings, and Philips calls peeling in its air fryers not harmful.

For the wider question of whether air fryers and PFAS are safe at all, see [are air fryers safe](/guides/are-air-fryers-safe/). This page is only about a coating that is already damaged.

## Is peeling air fryer coating safe?

There are two separate questions here: swallowing a flake, and breathing fumes from an overheated or damaged coating. The sources treat them very differently.

One caveat first. The FDA, Poison Control and StatPearls material below is about PTFE and other PFAS nonstick coatings. Some baskets use a different coating: Ninja's parts store describes the AF101 basket as "ceramic-coated, nonstick" and doesn't say what the coating is made of. Check your manual for your basket's material.

### What if you swallowed a flake?

The FDA says PFAS used in nonstick pots and pans are polymerized, meaning joined into large molecules, then applied to the cookware at very high temperatures. It says that process vaporizes off virtually all the smaller molecules that could migrate, and that studies show negligible amounts reach food. A footnote on the same page says polymerized, large-molecule PFAS are not absorbed by the human body when ingested.

That is the closest any authority we opened comes to the flake question. The FDA page is about cookware in general and doesn't mention flakes or air fryers, and Poison Control and StatPearls don't address swallowing coating at all. Philips' support page says the spots in its air fryers are "not harmful in any way" because all the materials are food-safe. That is a manufacturer claim about its own models.

### The bigger risk: fumes from overheated or damaged coating

Poison Control says Teflon coating on cookware starts to break down above about 500°F, and that scratches in the surface can also lead to fumes being released. The symptoms, often called Teflon flu, are chills, fever, fatigue, headache, body aches and sometimes chest tightness and airway irritation. They usually appear within a few hours and resolve within 1 to 2 days.

[StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK594276/) gives more detail: PTFE releases fumes from as low as 500°F (260°C), human symptoms generally don't begin until about 662°F (350°C), and pyrolysis begins at 752°F (400°C). It says the risk is higher when the cookware is heated dry. StatPearls marks this chapter as archived and possibly out of date.

If you notice fumes, Poison Control says to turn off the heat, open windows, run the exhaust fan and get everyone, including pets, out of the area. If symptoms develop, contact Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) or a doctor.

### Are fumes dangerous to pet birds?

Poison Control says birds are extremely sensitive to polymer fumes, and the first sign can be sudden death. It advises keeping birds out of the kitchen and keeping the home well ventilated.

## Why does air fryer nonstick coating chip and peel?

The causes the manufacturers name are all mechanical:

- **Harsh cleaning tools.** Philips' support page blames "incidental scratching or touching of the coating," including cleaning with metal scrubbers and hard brushes.
- **Inserting the basket.** Philips also names inserting the basket as a cause and says to do it gently.
- **Steel wool and sharp tools.** Cosori's care blog says to avoid steel wool, harsh brushes and sharp tools because they can scratch the coating. It adds that damaged coating may make food stick and cleaning harder.
- **Metal utensils.** Cosori recommends wooden or silicone utensils instead.
- **The dishwasher, possibly.** Cosori says hand washing is "typically recommended to preserve the non-stick coating," and to check the manual for which parts are dishwasher-safe. It gives no numbers on how much faster dishwashers wear coatings.

No source we opened supports other causes often repeated online, such as lecithin in cooking spray.

## How to replace an air fryer basket

1. **Find the model number.** Replacement parts are listed by model, so check the unit or your owner's manual for it first.
2. **Buy the OEM part from the maker.** For example, Ninja's own parts store lists part 102KY100, a "4-qt. ceramic-coated, nonstick air fryer basket," for the AF100, AF100C, AF101, AF101BRN, AF101C, AF101NP, AF101QAQ, AF101QBK, AF101QCN and AF101QW. It was $33.99 as of Sep 27, 2026. Shipping is free on orders of $99 or more, and smaller orders may carry a $10 shipping fee.
3. **Check warranty and registration first.** Philips' support site advertises a two-year warranty in its banner, but the peeling page doesn't say whether coating damage is covered. Ninja's basket listing only mentions a 60- or 90-day money-back guarantee on select products. None of the pages we read states warranty coverage for coating wear, so contact the manufacturer before paying.

Third-party "compatible" baskets exist, but none of our sources verifies their fit or what their coatings are made of, so the OEM part is the safer choice.

## How to keep a new basket from peeling

- Use a soft sponge and silicone or wooden tools, and skip steel wool and metal scrubbers (Cosori, Philips, Poison Control).
- Insert the basket gently (Philips).
- Hand-wash when you can (Cosori).
- Stay within the unit's rated range. Per our reviews citing manufacturer specs, the [Ninja AF101](/reviews/ninja-af101/), [Ninja AF141](/reviews/ninja-af141/) and [Instant Vortex Plus 6 qt](/reviews/instant-vortex-plus-6qt/) top out at 400°F and the [Cosori TurboBlaze](/reviews/cosori-turboblaze/) at 450°F. Those are air settings; no maker we read publishes basket surface temperatures.
- Poison Control's general nonstick advice is low to medium heat, no preheating of an empty pan and good ventilation, and StatPearls says dry heating raises the fume risk. Neither addresses air fryers specifically, so follow your manual on preheating; see [do you need to preheat an air fryer](/guides/do-you-need-to-preheat-an-air-fryer/).

If you want a liner to protect the basket, check [aluminum foil in an air fryer](/guides/aluminum-foil-in-air-fryer/) first. ## What are the alternatives to a nonstick basket?

- **Glass container.** The [Ninja Crispi](/reviews/ninja-crispi-fn101/) cooks in a glass container; see [how to clean the Ninja Crispi](/guides/how-to-clean-ninja-crispi/).
- **Ceramic-coated baskets.** Ninja's parts store labels the AF101 basket "ceramic-coated, nonstick." That is still a coating, and Ninja's page doesn't say what it is made of.

Poison Control lists stoneware, anodized aluminum, cast iron, stainless steel and ceramic cookware as alternatives for avoiding Teflon flu, though it is talking about pots and pans, not air fryer baskets.
