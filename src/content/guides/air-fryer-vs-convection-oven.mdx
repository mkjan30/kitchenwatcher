An air fryer is a countertop convection oven. Both use a heating element and a fan to move hot air, so the air fryer vs convection oven difference is size and intensity, not technology. The air fryer puts its element and fan right above a small basket, which is why it crisps small batches faster, per Nebraska Extension and Prudent Reviews. A convection oven holds far more food and bakes on several racks.

*Kitchen Watcher compiles manufacturer documentation and independent test results; we did not cook with these appliances. See [how we research](/how-we-research/).*

## Is an air fryer a convection oven?

Yes, in how it heats food. [Nebraska Extension](https://food.unl.edu/sites/unl.edu.ianr.extension.food/files/articles/documents/food-safety-air-fryers-fffyc.pdf) describes an air fryer as cooking by moving very hot air around food, which is convection cooking. Its heating element sits above the food and a fan moves the air quickly.

[Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/cooking-baking/how-to-air-fry-without-an-air-fryer-a3761040766/) agrees: convection, hot air moved by one or more fans, is the same technology behind countertop air fryers. CR adds that a little oil on the food also transfers heat by direct contact. [Prudent Reviews](https://prudentreviews.com/air-fryer-vs-convection-toaster-oven/) says air fryers cook faster than convection toaster ovens because the chamber is smaller and sealed, the element is closer to the food and the fan blows faster.

So an air fryer is not a separate heating method, and it does not fry in a vat of oil. Nebraska Extension notes it uses little or no oil.

## Is an air fryer faster than a convection oven?

Usually, by about 5 to 10 minutes in the few published side-by-side tests, but the evidence is thin. Three third-party sources report cook times, and none used the same method, so read them as rough signals.

| Food | Air fryer | Comparison appliance | Tester and method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken wings | 25 min | 35 min (convection toaster oven) | Prudent Reviews, both in air-fry mode at 390°F |
| Frozen waffle fries | 15 min | 20 min (convection toaster oven) | Prudent Reviews, both in air-fry mode at 350°F |
| Brussels sprouts | 13 min | About 20 min (convection toaster oven) | Prudent Reviews, both in air-fry mode at 390°F |
| Frozen fries | Countertop fryer's fries looked better | Range and wall-oven air-fry modes cut 20 to 30 min vs regular bake | Consumer Reports, 2 ranges and 1 wall oven |
| Fries, salmon, bacon | About 5 min faster | Convection oven, about 5 min slower | [The Spiffy Cookie](https://www.thespiffycookie.com/2021/12/12/air-fryer-vs-convection-oven/), informal blog test |

Each test has limits. Prudent Reviews used a toaster oven, and Consumer Reports compared air-fry mode with regular bake rather than with convection, crediting most of the gain to skipping the preheat. The blog test gave no wattage or model details.

Prudent Reviews also lists package directions, such as Ore-Ida fries at 400°F for 12 minutes in an air fryer vs 425°F for 25 to 29 minutes in a convection oven, but those are label instructions, not tests. No public test we found cooks the same food in a basket air fryer and a full-size convection oven head to head.

## Convection vs the air fry setting on an oven: what's the difference?

Per Frigidaire, Air Fry circulates hotter air sooner on one rack, while Convection heats more gradually for baking on several racks. Brands describe the two modes differently, so treat these as manufacturer statements.

Frigidaire says both modes use all the elements, burners and the convection fan, but the convection element in its Air Fry ranges is more than twice as powerful as a standard convection element. Air Fry heats up quickly and circulates hotter air sooner. Frigidaire says Air Fry is meant for one rack, while Convection is for baking and roasting on several racks.

GE Appliances describes its No Preheat Air Fry as fast-moving hot air sent from above to surround the food, with the fan cycling on and off. GE's air-fry tips say to:

- Put food into a cold oven, with no preheat.
- Use dark baking or sheet pans; parchment is fine.
- Spread food in a single layer without crowding.
- Use the center rack for most foods and a lower rack for thicker ones.
- Lightly spray with a high-heat oil such as avocado oil; if food browns too fast, move it lower and/or lower the temperature.
- For countertop air fryer recipes, expect to cook a little longer or slightly hotter.

[Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/cooking-baking/how-to-air-fry-without-an-air-fryer-a3761040766/) also says dark bakeware absorbs more heat than shiny pans and helps food brown, and that a convection oven without an air-fry setting can get similar results on convection roast. Behavior differs by brand, so your owner's manual is the final word on rack position and bakeware.

## Air fryer or convection oven: which should you use?

Match the appliance to what and how much you cook:

- **One or two servings of crispy food:** a countertop air fryer. The Spiffy Cookie notes that beyond one or two servings you end up cooking in batches.
- **Sheet-pan dinners, multi-rack baking or a whole bird:** the oven on convection. Frigidaire positions convection for several racks, and Consumer Reports says a range holds more than twice as much food as a standard air fryer.
- **Feeding a family fries or wings:** the oven or its air-fry mode. Consumer Reports says the larger capacity and shorter cook time help families. Frigidaire notes its Air Fry mode is still meant for one rack.
- **You already own an air-fry range:** try it before buying a countertop unit. Consumer Reports found all three ovens it tested made crispy fries, though not quite as good-looking as a countertop fryer's.
- **Short on counter space:** by the listed dimensions Prudent Reviews compiled, four basket air fryers need 103 to 172 sq in, less than the 196 to 376 sq in of three convection toaster ovens.

## Energy use: why the 20% figure doesn't apply at home

You may see a "20% more efficient" figure attached to convection ovens. It comes from the [ENERGY STAR commercial ovens factsheet](https://www.energystar.gov/products/tools_resources/commercial-ovens-factsheet), which says ENERGY STAR certified commercial convection ovens are about 20% more efficient than standard commercial models. It does not compare convection with regular baking or with an air fryer, and it does not cover home ranges.

None of our sources measured home energy use for either appliance. For per-use cost, see [how much electricity an air fryer uses](/guides/air-fryer-electricity-cost/).

## Food safety: the same temperatures either way

[Nebraska Extension](https://food.unl.edu/sites/unl.edu.ianr.extension.food/files/articles/documents/food-safety-air-fryers-fffyc.pdf) warns not to overfill an air fryer: food won't cook evenly or reach the proper temperature, and it browns poorly. It says a food thermometer, placed in the thickest part away from bone, fat or gristle, is the only way to confirm meat, poultry and egg products are safe. It lists these USDA minimum internal temperatures, which apply whatever you cook in:

- All poultry, including ground poultry: 165°F
- Ground beef, pork, veal and lamb: 160°F
- Steaks, chops and roasts of beef, pork, veal and lamb: 145°F, then rest at least 3 minutes
- Eggs: 160°F
- Fish and shellfish: 145°F
