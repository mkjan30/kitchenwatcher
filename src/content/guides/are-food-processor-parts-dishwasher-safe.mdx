Are food processor parts dishwasher safe? Usually yes. For all six models whose manuals or support pages I read, the bowl, lid and blades can go in the dishwasher (plus the pusher and discs on models that have them), and the motor base never can.

The exceptions are about how. Breville says its bowl and lid should go in only occasionally. Hamilton Beach says never to use the SANI setting. KitchenAid says to use a gentle cycle and avoid high-temperature ones on its 13-cup processor.

Everything below is quoted or paraphrased from each maker's own instructions, not from hands-on use; see [how we research](/how-we-research/). For hand-washing steps, see [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

On paper, every model above lets the blades go in. What differs from maker to maker is the rack, the cycle and how often, not the part.

## Cuisinart: the booklet actually recommends the dishwasher

Cuisinart is the only maker here that pushes the dishwasher rather than just allowing it, and the Canadian booklet's answer is an unqualified yes. Its Classic Series DFP-14NC booklet says all parts except the motor base are dishwasher safe, "and we recommend washing them in the dishwasher." It says to insert the work bowl upside down, remember where you put the sharp blade and discs, and unload carefully.

That booklet is the Canadian version. The US DFP-14BCNY booklet wouldn't open for me, so I can't confirm its wording matches. {/* VERIFY: US DFP-14BCNY booklet dishwasher wording matches the Canadian DFP-14NC booklet */}

## Ninja: top rack only, blade out of the bowl

Ninja's BN600C owner's guide says all attachments are BPA free and dishwasher safe. It recommends that the bowl, lid, disc and blade assemblies be "placed on the top rack of the dishwasher ONLY." Take the blade assembly out of the bowl before it goes in, and handle it with care. For hand washing, Ninja says to use a dishwashing utensil with a handle so your fingers stay off the blades.

The BN600C is the Canadian sibling of the BN601, and Ninja's US support site blocked my reader. {/* VERIFY: BN601 US owner's guide dishwasher section matches the BN600C guide */} [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor), whose testers washed each bowl, blade and lid by hand and in the dishwasher, gave the Ninja Professional (it groups the BN600 and BN601) a 5 of 10 for cleaning. It says the dual-blade system "can be more difficult to clean by hand."

## Hamilton Beach: top rack, and no SANI or sanitize cycle

If your dishwasher has a food processor sanitize cycle option, Hamilton Beach says to skip it. Its use and care guide for the 70730 and 70731 marks the parts "DISHWASHER-SAFE (TOP RACK ONLY)" and says: "DO NOT use the 'SANI' setting when washing in the dishwasher." The reason it gives is that SANI cycle temperatures could damage the product.

The guide gives no temperature number, so I can't tell you how hot is too hot. It also says to use caution washing the chopping/mixing blade and the slicing/shredding disc, and never to immerse the cord, plug or base in any liquid.

## KitchenAid: top rack, gentle cycle, no high heat

KitchenAid's 13-cup cleaning page says "All parts are top-rack dishwasher safe." It adds three conditions: don't lay the bowl on its side, "use gentle dishwashing cycles such as normal," and "avoid high-temperature cycles." Dry all parts thoroughly afterwards. The page opens by saying the BPA-free bowl "requires special handling" and gives these as the guidelines to follow if you use a dishwasher.

For its corded food chopper, KitchenAid says the work bowls, lids, whisking accessory and blade can go in the dishwasher, with the top rack recommended.

One KitchenAid-specific problem: water or food can get into a small drainage hole at the bottom of the bowl handle. KitchenAid's [handle page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Product_Info/Food_Processor_Tips_and_Tricks/Water_or_Food_Stuck_Inside_Handle_of_Work_Bowl) says it happens most when the bowl soaks in a sink, and also with unrinsed dishes in the dishwasher. KitchenAid's fix: hold the bowl by the handle while washing without submerging it, wash and dry it right away, and don't put it in the dishwasher with unrinsed dishes.

## Breville: the bowl and lid only occasionally

Breville is the outlier, and the one model here where the answer is a partial no. The BFP810 book says the bowl and lid "may occasionally be washed in the dishwasher (top shelf only)," but not regularly. Its reason: "prolonged exposure to harsh detergents, hot water and pressure will damage and shorten the life of the plastic and the interlock components."

Because of the interlock parts, it also advises against immersing the bowl for long periods. The pusher stack can go on the top rack.

On the spindle, the book contradicts itself. The first-use section says all attachments "except the geared spindle" may go in the dishwasher, while the care section says "Spindle, blades and disks can be washed in the dishwasher." Until Breville clarifies, hand-washing the spindle is the safe reading. {/* VERIFY: whether the BFP810 geared spindle is dishwasher safe; book says both */}

Breville also warns that citrus-rind oils can damage the bowl, so clean it immediately after processing rind.

## The part no manual lets in the dishwasher: the motor base

All six sources keep the motor base out. Hamilton Beach's warning is the most explicit: "Do not immerse cord, plug, or base in any liquid," under an electrical shock heading. KitchenAid says "Do not immerse the base in water," and Ninja, Breville and Cuisinart say to wipe it. For how to clean the base and the cord, see [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

## Can food processor blades go in the dishwasher, and do they dull?

This question has the clearest manufacturer answer: every manual and support page I read says the blades can go in, though Breville's book contradicts itself on the spindle the blades sit on. The cautions are about handling, not damage. Cuisinart says to remember where the blade and discs are and unload carefully, Ninja says to exercise care handling blade assemblies, and Hamilton Beach says to use caution washing the blade and disc. Ninja also says to store the blade assemblies inside or attached to the bowl with the lid locked.

The claim that the dishwasher dulls blades comes from blogs, not manufacturers. [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1815325/how-to-clean-food-processor/) says blades "can get dull from the etching caused by harsh dishwasher cleaning detergents," and [homegeargeek](https://homegeargeek.com/can-i-put-my-food-processor-in-the-dishwasher/) says dishwasher detergents "can dull the blades over time." Neither cites a source or a measurement, and homegeargeek calls blades "generally dishwasher safe" on the same page.

No manufacturer I read says the dishwasher dulls blades. On [Hungry Onion](https://www.hungryonion.org/t/are-the-plastic-bowl-and-blade-of-a-food-processor-dishwasher-safe/1610), one Cuisinart owner who switched to putting the blade in the dishwasher said in 2015 that they hadn't noticed dulling yet. That is one report, so the evidence either way is thin. For which blade does what, see [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## Why a food processor bowl goes cloudy in the dishwasher

Cloudiness has more than one possible cause. Each maker names a different one, and one blog adds a fourth:

- **Food.** Cuisinart says chopping certain foods "may scratch or cloud the work bowl," including ice, whole spices and oils like wintergreen. It suggests a second bowl if you grind your own spice blends.
- **Abrasives.** KitchenAid says that when hand washing, abrasive cleansers or scouring pads "may scratch or cloud the Work Bowl and Cover."
- **The dishwasher itself.** Breville says harsh detergent, hot water and pressure damage and shorten the life of the plastic, though it doesn't use the word cloudy.
- **Detergent etching.** Chowhound says the bowl "can become cloudy" over time in the dishwasher, without a source.

Staining is a separate issue. Breville says garlic, fish and carrots can stain or leave an odor on the bowl or lid. None of these sources says cloudiness can be reversed, so I won't promise a fix.

## Does a cloudy or stained bowl need replacing?

No source I read says cloudiness makes a bowl unsafe or that it needs replacing, so I can't tell you it does. Breville's stated worry is different: repeated dishwasher runs damaging the plastic and the interlock components. If your bowl or lid no longer locks, see [the safety interlock guide](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/); if it leaks, see [food processor leaking](/guides/food-processor-leaking/).
