Yes, electric pressure cookers are safe when the lid locks properly, the steam vents are clear and the pot is filled below its max line. The serious injuries in CPSC records came from defective lids that could open while the cooker was still under pressure. None of those records covers current Instant Pot Duo, Rio or Pro models, per the CPSC recall database checked on 2026-11-13.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) calls electric multicookers "exceptionally safe" compared with old stovetop cookers. For your own cooker, the answer mostly depends on two things: whether the model is in the table below, and whether you follow its manual.

## When a pressure cooker lid opened under pressure: the CPSC record

Every serious failure in the record since 2020 comes back to the lid or the fill markings. I pulled each recall from CPSC's [recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=pressure%20cooker) by its record number and read the two newer warnings on cpsc.gov. Together they cover about 5 million US units.

| Brand / model (CPSC no.) | Action and date | Units (US) | What failed (CPSC wording) | Injuries reported | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ninja Foodi OP300 series (25-247) | Recall, 2025-05-01 | About 1,846,400 | "The pressure-cooking lid can be opened during use" | 106 burn reports, more than 50 second- or third-degree; 26 lawsuits | Free replacement lid; air fry and other functions still usable |
| Ambiano, sold at ALDI (26-007) | Recall, 2025-10-09 | About 46,660 | Lid "can be opened before sufficient steam pressure has been released" | 11 incidents, 8 severe burns | Refund |
| Sensio Bella, Bella Pro, Crux, Cooks (23-256) | Recall, 2023-08-10 | About 860,000 | Lid "can unlock and be removed during use" | 63 incidents, 61 burns | Refund |
| Crock-Pot Express 6-qt SCCPPC600-V1 (21-035) | Recall, 2020-11-24 | About 914,430 | "Can pressurize when the lid is not fully locked" | 119 lid detachments, 99 burns | Free replacement lid |
| Insignia, Best Buy (24-013) | Recall, 2023-10-26 | About 930,000 | "Incorrect volume markings on the inner pot" lead to overfilling | 31 incidents, 17 burns | Free replacement inner pot and floating locking valve |
| Gourmia GPC625 (26-280) | CPSC warning, 2026-02-24 | About 43,500 | Lid "can open while it is still pressurized"; float valve hidden in the handle; wrong volume markings | 5 incidents, 4 severe burns | None: firm refused an acceptable recall; stop using and dispose |
| Tristar Power Quick Pot 6-qt Y6D-36 (27-030) | CPSC warning, 2026-10-15 | About 393,000 | Lid "can open while pressurized" | 24 reports of hot contents expelled, with serious burns | None: Tristar refused to recall; stop using and dispose |

Six of the seven actions involve a lid that opens or detaches while the pot is pressurized. Two involve wrong fill markings, and Gourmia has both problems.

A CPSC warning is not a recall. In both warnings above, the company would not agree to a recall, so no refund or repair is offered and CPSC's advice is to stop using the cooker and dispose of it. ## Can an Instant Pot explode?

Not in the sense most people picture. No source I read documents the body of a modern electric pressure cooker rupturing. The injuries CPSC records describe are hot contents expelled when a lid opened or came off under pressure, or ejected on quick release when the pot was overfilled (Insignia, 24-013).

Instant Pot has two recalls in the CPSC database, and neither involves the lid. Recall 18-110 (2018) covered Gem 65 multicookers that could "overheat and melt on the underside", a fire hazard with no injuries reported; CPSC's description of the Gem 65 lists no pressure-cooking function. Recall 15-741 (2015) covered about 1,000 Smart Bluetooth cookers whose thermal probe could conduct electricity, with three shocks reported.

In the records I read, no electric pressure cooker burst. The real risk is a lid that opens too early, or steam and food you release yourself.

## The safety systems, and what each one actually stops

What people call a pressure cooker explosion is, in these records, a lid opening too early. Most of the protection against that sits in the lid:

- **Lid lock and float valve.** The [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) says the cooker shows Turn to Lock Lid and will not start pressurizing if the lid is not locked, and to open the lid only after the float valve has dropped. Instant's FAQ says a raised float valve or a lid that is hard to open means the cooker is still pressurized. Gourmia's warning shows what goes wrong without a visible valve: CPSC says its float valve sits inside the handle and is "difficult to see".
- **Anti-block shield.** It keeps food particles out of the steam release pipe. The Rio Plus Wide manual calls it "an integral part of product safety" and says to install it before use and clean it often.
- **A release switch away from the vent.** ATK says the Instant Pot Pro's pressure-release switch keeps your hand away from the steam and its vent diffuser disperses steam more gently. It also credits a Cuisinart model's button for venting "without getting our hands near the valve".
- **Low-liquid and not-sealed warnings.** ATK says most electric models flash warnings when liquid is low or the lid isn't sealed properly.
- **Leaky-lid detection, overheat protection and fuses.** Instant Brands South Africa's blog lists "at least 10" safety mechanisms, including these. That is a manufacturer claim, not an independent finding.

None of these helps if the lock itself is defective, which is what the Ninja, Sensio, Ambiano and Crock-Pot recalls describe.

## What the Instant Pot manual says to do, and not do

These are the safety instructions in Instant's Rio Plus Wide manual, with two additions from Instant's FAQ. Other brands' manuals differ, so follow your own; I only read Instant's.

1. Don't fill the inner pot above the PC MAX 2/3 line. The manual warns overfilling can clog the steam release pipe and build excess pressure.
2. Don't fill above the 1/2 line for foods that expand, such as rice, dried beans or dried vegetables.
3. Keep foaming foods at or below the 1/2 line: applesauce, cranberries, pearl barley, oatmeal or other cereals, split peas, noodles, macaroni, rhubarb and spaghetti. The manual says they can clog the steam vent.
4. Before each use, check the steam release valve, steam release pipe, anti-block shield and float valve for clogging.
5. Turn it off if steam streams from the steam release valve or float valve for longer than 3 minutes, or escapes from the sides of the lid. Check the sealing ring once it has cooled.
6. Never force the lid off, and don't open it until all pressure is released.
7. Never cover the steam release valve or float valve with a cloth or anything else.
8. Keep your face and hands off the valves, and don't lean over the cooker when removing the lid.
9. Don't move it while it is under pressure.
10. No deep frying or pressure frying with oil.
11. Don't leave it unattended while in use, and don't use an extension cord.
12. Use only accessories, inner pots and sealing rings authorized by Instant Brands.
13. Replace the sealing ring every 12 to 18 months (the manual), "or sooner" (Instant's FAQ).

The ring interval is where Instant's sources disagree. Instant Brands South Africa's blog says every two years. The US FAQ and the Rio Plus Wide manual both say 12 to 18 months and are written for US models, so follow those. How much liquid to add has its own guide: [how much water goes in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/).

## Quick release and burns: the most common everyday risk

The everyday burn risk is steam and splatter during release. Instant's FAQ says not to quick release fatty, oily, thick or high-starch foods, such as stews, chili, pasta and congee, or foods that expand, such as beans and grains, because they can splatter. The Rio Plus Wide manual suggests quick release for foods that overcook, like vegetables and seafood.

Keep your face and hands away from the valve while it vents. ATK rewards models whose switches or valves keep hands "out of harm's way". For which method suits which food, see [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

## Warning signs: stop using it if...

- The lid closes or turns without locking. That is the Crock-Pot 21-035 pattern.
- The lid opens while the float valve is still up.
- Steam escapes from the sides of the lid (Rio Plus Wide manual).
- Steam streams from the valve for more than 3 minutes (Rio Plus Wide manual and Instant's FAQ).
- The sealing ring is damaged or no longer fits (Instant's FAQ).
- Oil or fat spilled on the heating element. Instant's FAQ says not to use the appliance and to dispose of it.
- Your model appears in a CPSC recall or warning.

## Electric vs stovetop: is electric safer?

ATK calls electric multicookers "exceptionally safe" and contrasts them with stories of old stovetop cookers that "have exploded". That is ATK's framing. I didn't read stovetop manuals or find data comparing the two, so the evidence here is thin. Stovetop models aren't automatically exempt from lid defects either: Sensio's 23-256 recall covered Bella stovetop cookers as well as electric ones.

## Two things a pressure cooker is not safe for

**Canning.** The [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/blog/can-i-can-in-a-multi-cooker) says it does not recommend its canning processes in electric multi-cookers, even ones with a canning button. It says temperature, not pressure, is what matters, that come-up and cool-down time count toward the process, and that canners should hold four or more quart jars. The risk is botulism. Its one exception is the Ball Automatic Home Canner, for acid foods only. More in [can you can in an Instant Pot](/guides/can-you-can-in-an-instant-pot/).

**Deep frying.** The Rio Plus Wide manual says not to deep fry or pressure fry with oil.

## So, are electric pressure cookers safe to use?

Electric pressure cookers are safe when the model isn't on CPSC's list and you follow its manual. None of the six picks on our [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) page has a CPSC recall in the database. If your cooker is older or off-brand, check it first.
