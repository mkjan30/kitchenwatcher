If your blender smells like burning, the manufacturer sources I read describe two causes. On a new machine, Vitamix's support FAQ says heavy use "will cause further curing of the motor windings," a smell it calls "typical for wound motors." Mid-blend, the same FAQ says an overheated motor "may emit a burning smell." The Vitamix, Ninja and nutribullet manuals all say to stop, unplug and let it cool. All three also say not to operate a blender after it malfunctions.

## Quick check: what a burning smell from your blender is telling you

| Thick or low-liquid load, then the motor stops | Overload cutout (thermal switch or breaker) | Switch off, unplug, empty the jar, clear any jam | Vitamix 5200: up to 45 min (manual). Ninja BN400, BL621: about 15 min (manual). nutribullet NBF50500: at least 15 min (manual) | Pause until it resets |

## Is a new blender burning smell normal?

Vitamix is the only maker we found that explains a new-motor smell. Its support FAQ says "heavy use will cause further curing of the motor windings in new machines," and that this curing "gives off a burning smell and is typical for wound motors." It adds that the smell "will quickly dissipate after a few heavy uses."

No manual or FAQ we read gives a number of uses before the smell fades. [World of Blenders](https://worldofblenders.com/vitamix-smells-like-its-burning/) says to use a Vitamix "2-3x," and [Rise and Blend](https://riseandblend.com/blender-is-smoking/) blames oils and lubricants left over from manufacturing. Neither cites a source, so treat both as unconfirmed.

Ninja's [BN400](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/70/70cc8fb2-37d6-472d-aa56-6c5535a40f14.pdf) and [BL621](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/69/69ceb435-842c-47ff-9622-0a061a909e0d.pdf) owner's guides and nutribullet's [NBF50500 user guide](https://nbmedia.imgix.net/NBF50500_BlenderCombo_UserGuide_English_LoRes_PROOF_F20221010.pdf?dl=) don't mention a new-motor smell, so the evidence is one manufacturer FAQ.

## Blender overheating smell mid-blend: the motor protecting itself

All three brands build in a cutout that shuts the motor off when it overheats. What differs is how long each manual says to wait.

### Vitamix: Automatic Overload Protection

The [Vitamix 5200 manual](https://www.vitamix.com/media/catalog/product/file/Vitamix-5200-Owners-Manual.pdf) says that when Automatic Overload Protection engages, it "will cause the motor to shut off and possibly emit a light odor." On 120-volt machines, turn off the power switch "for up to 45 minutes to reset," longer in a hot room. To shorten it, unplug, remove the container and blow air into the center of the base's underside "with a hair dryer on the cool setting."

Vitamix's own FAQ page isn't consistent: one answer repeats the 45 minutes, another says "the majority of shut-downs will take up to 30 minutes." The manual's 45 minutes is the longer of the two.

### Ninja: thermal switch

Ninja's BN400 guide says that if the appliance overheats, "a thermal switch will activate and temporarily disable the motor." To reset, unplug it and let it cool for about 15 minutes, then remove the blade assembly, empty the container and clear any jam. The BL621 guide gives the same 15 minutes. I did not read the guides for other Ninja models, such as the BN701 or BL610.

### nutribullet: internal thermal breaker

nutribullet's NBF50500 guide says the blender has "an internal thermal breaker that shuts off the motor when it overheats." Unplug it and let it cool for at least 15 minutes; the breaker resets once cool. The guide also limits blending to 1-minute intervals, with at least a 1-minute rest between them and no more than three in a row.

## What makes a blender overheat, according to the manuals?

- **Too slow for too long (Vitamix).** "Blending too long at too low a speed setting will overheat the machine," per the 5200 manual. High speed "maximizes the motor's cooling fan." Don't run recipes slower, faster or longer than recommended.
- **Tamper overuse (Vitamix).** No more than 30 consecutive seconds.
- **Nut butters (Vitamix).** Stop within one minute after the mix starts to circulate, or risk "dangerous overheating."
- **Air pockets (Vitamix).** A trapped air bubble stops circulation; release it with the tamper through the lid plug.
- **Overfilling and jams (Ninja).** Exceeding maximum capacities "is the most typical cause of appliance overload," per the BN400 guide.
- **Long runs and blocked vents (nutribullet).** The NBF50500 guide sets 1-minute intervals "to prevent the contents from overheating," and says the openings under the motor base should be free of dust and lint and never covered by items like dishtowels, to prevent a fire hazard.

Independent context: [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/blender/vitamix-5200) reports the 5200's motor "became exceptionally warm" making nut butter, and the mix stayed thick and grainy. For power and wattage questions, see [blender wattage explained](/guides/blender-wattage-explained/).

## Vitamix motor smell: normal or a problem?

Vitamix documents two cases: winding curing on a new machine (FAQ) and the light odor of an overload cutout (5200 manual). Anything else goes to Vitamix Customer Service; the 5200 carries a 7-year full machine warranty.

Separately, if the retainer nut under the container loosens and no longer holds the blade assembly, the manual says "DO NOT CONTINUE TO USE THE CONTAINER" and to call Customer Service.

A friction-heated soup run is hot by design: TechGearLab reports the 5200 heated soup to "just over 153°F," though the spec table on the same page lists 152.4°F. For hot-liquid safety, see [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/).

## When a burning smell means stop using the blender

All three manuals say not to operate the appliance after it malfunctions. nutribullet's says to "immediately discontinue use and contact customer service," and Ninja's BN400 guide says it "has no user-serviceable parts." Unplug before inspecting, as Ninja's troubleshooting guide says.

The manuals don't list which smells count as a malfunction. The other sources I read point to:

- A smell that won't go away: Rise and Blend says to contact the manufacturer, and Chefman's culinary director, quoted by The Takeout, says a smell that persists after adding enough liquid means it's time to look at new blenders.
- Smoke from the motor base, or liquid that has seeped past a worn gasket into the base (Rise and Blend).
- Grinding, or blades that don't spin properly (Food Republic and Rise and Blend, both pointing to the blade assembly or drive coupling).
- Running it empty, which Ninja's BN400 and BL621 guides say not to do.

On causes, [Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/2218349/why-blender-smells-like-burning/) lists a broken drive coupling, bearing seal or blade assembly, and Rise and Blend says a burning rubber smell may come from a stripped drive coupler. Neither cites a source. [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com/2219216/blender-burning-smells-you-need-to-know/) quotes Chefman's culinary director, Megan Scrivner, naming worn motor brushes. Treat these as general mechanics, not diagnoses.

## Portable blenders and the BlendJet 2 fire recall

CPSC recall 24069 (December 28, 2023) covers about 4.8 million BlendJet 2 portable blenders, plus about 117,000 sold in Canada. Per the [CPSC recall record](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=24069), they can overheat or catch fire, and the blades can break off.

BlendJet received 17 reports of overheating or fires with about $150,000 in property damage claims, and 49 reports of minor burns. The original remedy was a free replacement base, but [CPSC's recall notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/BlendJet-Recalls-4-8-Million-BlendJet-2-Portable-Blenders-Due-to-Fire-and-Laceration-Hazards) was updated on May 13, 2025: BlendJet Inc. has gone out of business and the remedy is no longer available. CPSC now says to discard the blender and not to donate or resell it. Older news stories that describe the replacement process are out of date; see our [blender recall check](/guides/blender-recall-check/).

In our October 4, 2026 search of CPSC's recall database, none of the six blenders we track had a fire or overheating recall. See the [blender recall check](/guides/blender-recall-check/).

## What to do right now if your blender smells like burning

1. Switch it off and unplug it.
2. Remove the jar, then empty it or loosen the load and clear any jam.
3. Let it cool for the manual's time: Ninja about 15 minutes, nutribullet at least 15 minutes, Vitamix up to 45 minutes.
4. Restart with a smaller load, within the max fill line, on the speed the manual recommends.
5. If it smells again on a normal load, or you see smoke, stop and contact the manufacturer.

If it won't restart after cooling, see [blender won't turn on](/guides/blender-wont-turn-on/).
