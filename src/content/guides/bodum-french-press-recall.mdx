Your Bodum French press is recalled if it is an 8-cup or 12-cup Bistro, Brazil, Caffettiera, Chambord, Crema, Java, Jesper or Kenya with a glass carafe. The 3-cup and 4-cup sizes are not. You keep the press. Stop stirring with metal, check the glass for cracks, and register with Bodum for a free plastic spoon and warning label.

The Bodum French press recall is CPSC recall 27-055, announced October 29, 2026. It covers about 5.9 million presses sold from 1974 through October 2026 for about $15 to $65. CPSC says the carafe "can unexpectedly break during use if the glass carafe is damaged or cracked, including by use of a metal spoon to stir the coffee." Bodum USA has 70 reports of carafes breaking during use, including 48 reports of burns.

I read the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Bodum-USA-Recalls-5-9-Million-French-Press-Coffee-Makers-with-Replaceable-Glass-Carafes-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Burn-Injury) and Bodum's [recall site](https://www.bodumfrenchpressrecall.expertinquiry.com/) on November 13, 2026. Nothing below comes from handling a press; every fact is from those pages.

## Which sizes are recalled: 8-cup and 12-cup only

Size is the first check. Bodum's recall FAQ says "units with 3-cup and 4-cup replaceable glass carafes are not recalled." The recall site's size guide gives these figures, with the 8-cup and 12-cup marked as the subject presses:

| Cup size | Capacity (Bodum size guide) | Height (Bodum size guide) | Recalled? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-cup | 0.35 l, 11.8 fl oz | 18.9 cm (7.44 in) | No |
| 4-cup | 0.5 l, 17 fl oz | 19 cm (7.48 in) | No |
| 8-cup | 1.0 l, 34 fl oz | 24.5 cm (9.65 in) | Yes |
| 12-cup | 1.5 l, 51 fl oz | 25.3 cm (9.96 in) | Yes |

The 3-cup and 4-cup are less than 1 cm apart in height, and the 8-cup and 12-cup are less than 1 cm apart too. The guide pictures a Chambord, so for other models treat these heights as a rough check: about 19 cm suggests a small, unrecalled size, about 25 cm a recalled one.

If your box or receipt is long gone, the name on the frame and the cup size are enough: the registration form opens by asking only whether Bodum is imprinted on the side and which cup size you have.

## What to do now if your Bodum is recalled

1. **Stop stirring with metal.** CPSC's remedy starts with this: stop using "metal spoons or utensils to stir coffee in the glass carafe immediately."
2. **Look at the carafe.** Bodum's FAQ: "If your carafe is cracked or damaged, please immediately stop using the French press." Neither CPSC nor Bodum describes how to inspect the glass or what counts as damage, so if you're unsure, treat it as damaged and ask Bodum.
3. **Keep using an undamaged one, with care.** Bodum's FAQ says that if you see no cracks or damage, "you can continue to use the French press with any plastic or wooden spoon until the recall remedy arrives."
4. **Register every unit.** The FAQ says each recalled press is registered on its own, and you get a spoon and label for each.
5. **Don't take it back to the store.** Bodum's FAQ says the remedy "will not be provided by retailers and online sellers."

## The remedy: a spoon and a label, not a refund

CPSC lists the remedy type as "Repair." In practice that is a free Bodum plastic stirring spoon plus a warning label for the carafe. The label reminds you not to use a cracked or damaged carafe and not to stir with metal.

There is no refund or replacement carafe in either the CPSC notice or Bodum's FAQ. Asked whether someone who no longer has the press but kept the receipt can get a refund, the FAQ answers "No." It also says you don't need a receipt if you still have the press. Bodum says the spoon and label ship within about 4 to 6 weeks once registration is processed.

Bodum's FAQ points out that the carafes already carry printed warnings against metal spoons and damaged glass. It says the glass is borosilicate from a German supplier, and that Bodum won't change the carafe. New production will ship with the plastic spoon and the new sticker. Bodum calls the incident rate "extremely low" for the number sold; CPSC's figure is 70 breakage reports against 5.9 million units.

## Are plastic, steel or smaller Bodum presses affected?

The notice covers French presses "with replaceable glass carafes" in the eight named models, 8-cup and 12-cup only. A press without a glass carafe isn't in it. Bodum's FAQ adds that a metal spoon can damage "any glass carafe," including other brands' presses, which this recall doesn't cover. For the materials question in drip machines, see [are plastic coffee makers safe](/guides/are-plastic-coffee-makers-safe/).

Outside the US, a search of Canada's recalls site for "bodum" on November 13, 2026 returned only three notices from 2019, none for this hazard. Other countries I didn't check.

## Earlier Bodum French press recalls

Two older recalls are easy to confuse with this one; both offered refunds (store credit, in the Starbucks case):

- **CPSC 19-114 (May 2019):** about 230,000 Bodum + Starbucks recycled-material 8-cup presses, SKU 011063549, sold at Starbucks from November 2016 to January 2019. The plunger knob could break.
- **CPSC 14-092 (January 2014):** about 28,000 rose gold Chambord 8-cup presses, SKU 11029732, sold at Starbucks in late 2013. The carafe could fall out of the frame.

Bodum's Bistro toaster recall from 2019 is on our [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).
