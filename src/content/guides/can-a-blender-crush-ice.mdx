Yes, many full-size blenders can crush ice. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-blender/) put 4 cups of ice in the Ninja Professional BL610 and got crushed ice with no chunks in 7 seconds, with no water added. Some manuals forbid it, though: the [nutribullet Blender Combo user guide](https://nbmedia.imgix.net/NBF50500_BlenderCombo_UserGuide_English_LoRes_PROOF_F20221010.pdf) says it is not intended as an ice crusher. Check your manual first.

So can a blender crush ice? It depends on the model. Below is what Ninja, nutribullet, Vitamix and KitchenAid instructions say, what CNET, TechGearLab and Tom's Guide measured, and where they disagree.

## Do blender manuals allow crushing ice?

Some do and some don't, so your manual matters more than your wattage. KitchenAid's UK FAQ on crushing ice says "no liquid needs to be added for processing ice" in its blenders. The nutribullet Combo guide says the opposite for its machine: never use it without liquid, as that may damage the blades, and "ALWAYS ADD LIQUID."

Vitamix sits in between. The [Vitamix 5200 owner's manual](https://www.vitamix.com/media/catalog/product/file/Vitamix-5200-Owners-Manual.pdf) says to put liquids and soft foods in first and ice last, and that High speed breaks down ice to a smooth consistency. It doesn't say whether ice alone is fine.

Ninja expects ice: the [Ninja BL610 owner's guide](https://www.teklib.com/library/ninja-professional-blender-1000-bl610-owners-guide/) has a troubleshooting entry for when you cannot get snow from solid ice.

You'll often read that you need 1,000 W or more. TechGearLab's buying advice says so, but no measurement on the pages I read backs it up, and CNET's 1,440 W Vitamix VX1 left large cubes. Wattage gets its own page: [blender wattage explained](/guides/blender-wattage-explained/).

### Where sources disagree

The nutribullet Combo looks good or bad depending on the test. TechGearLab gave it 8.0/10 for frozen drinks and called the margarita smooth, yet the same review found it left near-whole cubes when crushing ice alone. TechGearLab attributes the difference to the liquid: a bit of limeade and a splash of tequila.

Vitamix is the other conflict. CNET's Vitamix VX1 left several large cubes after 26 seconds, while Vitamix's 5200 manual says High speed breaks down ice. Those are different models, so neither settles the other. CNET's own numbers for the VX1 also differ: its results table says 26 seconds, its write-up says the ice compacted against the blade after 50 seconds.

CNET's NutriBullet Power Core XL is a third: its write-up says it "effectively crushed ice," but its results table lists 30 seconds with several large cubes left over. CNET's test is ice alone and TechGearLab's is a drink with liquid, so compare results only within one test.

## Does a nutribullet crush ice?

Not as plain crushed ice, per nutribullet itself. The Blender Combo user guide says the Combo "is not intended to be used without liquids, or as an ice crusher," and tells you to always add liquid. TechGearLab's plain-ice result matched the warning, with chunks ranging from small bits to nearly full, unbroken cubes, as if the blades couldn't grab them.

Other nutribullets are mixed. In CNET's results table the Power Core XL left several large cubes after 30 seconds, though the write-up calls it effective. CNET also says the Nutribullet Ultra personal blender struggled with ice but did well once liquid was added.

Ice in a smoothie is a different job: the same guide says the Extract program breaks down fruits, vegetables and ice into smoothies. So for the Combo, ice in a drink with liquid is what the guide supports; dry crushed ice is not.

## Can a blender crush ice without water?

Yes, for some models. CNET says the BL610 blended ice with no water added and the blade never got stuck. KitchenAid's UK FAQ says no liquid needs to be added for processing ice in its blenders.

No, for the nutribullet Combo: its guide says running it without liquid may damage the blades. Extractor-style designs aren't all the same, so read your own guide.

Vitamix's 5200 manual says nothing either way about ice with no liquid; it describes the wet blade container as designed for liquids and frozen mixtures. Until Vitamix answers, follow the manual's layering order and include some liquid.

## How to crush ice in a blender, step by step

1. **Check your manual first.** If it says the blender isn't an ice crusher, as the nutribullet Combo guide does, add liquid or don't do it.
2. **Load liquids first and ice last.** Vitamix's 5200 manual and KitchenAid's FAQ both put liquids at the bottom and ice cubes on top.
3. **Use the Ice Crush preset if you have one.** TechGearLab got powder from the BN701 by pulsing on Crush Ice; CNET and Tom's Guide used the DB351's Ice Crush setting.
4. **Pulse first, then blend continuously.** That is Ninja's fix in the BL610 owner's guide when ice won't turn to snow.
5. **Use ice straight from the freezer.** The same Ninja guide says not to use ice that has been sitting out or has started to melt.
6. **Match speed to the amount.** KitchenAid says to use a high speed for large amounts of ice and a medium setting or Pulse for smaller quantities.
7. **On a Vitamix, start on Variable 1, then go to High.** The 5200 manual says to always start on variable speed 1, rotate slowly to 10, then switch to High, and not to begin on Variable 10 or High. If you use the tamper, keep the container no more than two-thirds full and don't use it for more than 30 seconds at a time.

For safety, Vitamix's manual says not to reach into the container while the machine is running, and to always use the complete 2-part lid. When you're done, see [how to clean a blender](/guides/how-to-clean-a-blender/).

## Signs your blender is struggling with ice

Ingredients that stop moving over the blades are the first sign. Vitamix's 5200 manual says the tamper keeps things circulating by preventing air pockets, and that if food stops circulating the machine may have trapped an air bubble. It also says a lower speed may let food circulate when a higher one doesn't; once it moves, go back to High.

The second sign is ice packing against the blade. That's what CNET saw with the Vitamix VX1, which left large cubes compacted against the blade after 50 seconds.

If the motor cuts out or you smell something hot, stop and read [blender smells like burning](/guides/blender-smells-like-burning/) or [blender won't turn on](/guides/blender-wont-turn-on/). Vitamix's manual says blending too long at too low a speed overheats the machine and trips the overload protection.
