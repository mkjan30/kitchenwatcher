Yes, you can cook quinoa in a rice cooker: rinse it first, then use your cooker's own ratio. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual uses equal parts quinoa and water on WHOLE GRAIN (58 to 60 minutes at the maximum load). Zojirushi's FAQ gives 1 cup quinoa to 1 1/4 cups water on Quick for its cookers, excluding its conventional models. The Zojirushi NL-BAC05 has its own QUINOA menu, about 38 to 44 minutes.

The same cookers handle oatmeal, with limits. Below is each manufacturer's instruction side by side, where they disagree, and the boil-over and keep-warm warnings that the recipe blogs skip.

## Why the quinoa ratios disagree: 1 : 1 vs 1 : 1 1/4 vs 1 : 2

The sources don't agree, and none of them explains why. Hamilton Beach uses 1 : 1 in its rice cup on WHOLE GRAIN. Zojirushi's FAQ says 1 : 1 1/4 on Quick, and the NL-BAC05 uses its own QUINOA water line, telling you to add less water for firmer quinoa and more for softer.

Outside the manuals, the numbers go up. Utah State University Extension's [quinoa handout](https://extension.usu.edu/fscreate/files/handout_quinoa.pdf), a stovetop method, uses 1 cup quinoa to 2 cups water. Two recipe blogs use 1 : 2 in US cups: [Pinch and Swirl](https://pinchandswirl.com/how-to-cook-quinoa-in-a-rice-cooker/) on the white rice setting, and [Hungry Huy](https://www.hungryhuy.com/quinoa-in-a-rice-cooker/) on a "quick" setting, or the normal rice setting if there is no quick one.

My advice: start with your manual's number. Zojirushi's 1 : 1 1/4 is written for its own cookers with a Quick setting, and the FAQ says its ratios won't work on its conventional models; no other manufacturer I read publishes it for their cookers.

## Which rice cooker setting should you use for quinoa?

### Cookers with a QUINOA menu

The Zojirushi NL-BAC05 takes 0.5 to 1.5 cups of quinoa, filled to the QUINOA water line. Its manual warns that filling to the QUINOA or STEEL CUT OATMEAL line and then cooking on any other menu may boil over. The manual also says the QUINOA and STEEL CUT OATMEAL selection isn't saved, so select it each time.

### Cookers with a whole-grain setting

Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual puts quinoa on WHOLE GRAIN with the 1 : 1 ratio above. KitchenAid's KGC3155BM has a quinoa preset, per KitchenAid's launch release and Reviewed, and its built-in scale senses the grain and dispenses the water, per KitchenAid. Reviewed reports its "quinoa was perfectly cooked" in its testing.

### Cookers without a grain menu

The Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 has no quinoa menu, and its manual says of the PORRIDGE menu that rice other than white rice can't be cooked properly. Zojirushi's FAQ ratio (1 : 1 1/4 on Quick) is general advice for its non-conventional cookers; the FAQ points to a model-specific Grains Page that now returns a 404, so I found no NS-ZCC-specific quinoa instruction. More on the cooker in our [Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 review](/reviews/zojirushi-ns-zcc10/).

The Cuckoo CR-0655F has no quinoa menu either. Its manual describes Multi Cook as "To cook porridge, soup, tea, etc." and says not to use the product for anything other than cooking, warming and its designated menus, so there's no Cuckoo quinoa ratio to cite.

### One-switch cookers

Zojirushi's FAQ says conventional cookers are designed for short- and medium-grain white rice and its ratios won't work on them. Pinch and Swirl uses the white rice setting, but no manufacturer chart I opened covers quinoa on a one-switch cooker.

## How to cook quinoa in a rice cooker, step by step

These generic steps come from the manuals and the Extension handout above. They aren't a tested recipe.

1. Rinse the quinoa. Hamilton Beach's grain chart says to rinse it before cooking, and Utah State Extension says to rub it between your palms in water to remove the bitter saponin coating, then drain.
2. Measure grain and water with the same cup, ideally the one that came with the cooker.
3. Choose the setting for your cooker from the section above, and stay within its capacity.
4. Serve with a nonmetal utensil. Zojirushi's NL-BAC manual says not to use a metal ladle when serving quinoa or steel-cut oatmeal.
5. Refrigerate leftovers rather than holding them on keep warm (see below).

## Can you make oatmeal in a rice cooker? Steel-cut and rolled oats

### Steel-cut oats in a rice cooker

Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual uses 3 rice cups of steel-cut oats to 9 of water on HOT CEREALS, about 1 hour. The NL-BAC05's STEEL CUT OATMEAL menu takes 0.5 to 1.5 cups, about 53 to 59 minutes; use less water for firmer oats and more for a moister porridge. Per Reviewed, the KitchenAid's oats preset covers rolled and steel-cut.

### Rolled (old-fashioned) oats

Hamilton Beach's chart uses the same 3 : 9 ratio for old-fashioned oatmeal on HOT CEREALS, 28 to 30 minutes. None of the Zojirushi or Cuckoo manuals I read gives a rolled-oats instruction.

### Boil-over is the main failure

Zojirushi's NL-BAC manual says quinoa and steel-cut oatmeal are particularly likely to boil over if you use the wrong amount of water. The NS-ZCC manual says to select PORRIDGE for rice porridge or the contents may boil over. Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual says not to make porridge over the designated amount.

## Can quinoa or oats go on the delay timer or keep warm?

Zojirushi's NL-BAC manual says to set the timer for less than 8 hours for quinoa and steel-cut oatmeal (13 hours for rice), especially in summer. It also says not to use Keep Warm for quinoa or steel-cut oatmeal, and Extended Keep Warm isn't available on its BROWN, QUINOA and STEEL CUT OATMEAL menus.

Hamilton Beach's Overnight Oatmeal recipe in the 37548 manual (2 US cups old-fashioned oats to 4 US cups water) uses Delay Start of at least 6 hours, then HOT CEREALS for 5 minutes; its tip for steel-cut oats is 15 minutes on HOT CEREALS. Each manual is written for its own cooker; on the NL-BAC05, the limit is under 8 hours.

FDA's [Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download) says the vomiting-type B. cereus illness most often affects rice and other starchy foods, that cooking may not disable its toxin, and that food should be refrigerated at 40 °F or lower. It doesn't name quinoa or oats specifically. For rice, see [how long rice can stay on keep warm](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## What other grains can a rice cooker cook?

Hamilton Beach's 37548 grain chart (manufacturer spec, rice cup, WHOLE GRAIN, maximum loads):

| Grain | Grain : water | Approx. time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barley | 2 : 6 | 90–92 min | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |
| Buckwheat | 3 1/2 : 10 1/2 | 55–57 min | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |
| Farro | 7 : 9 | 68–70 min | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |
| Millet | 4 1/2 : 9 1/2 | 58–60 min | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |
| Sorghum | 3 1/2 : 10 1/2 | About 2 hours | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |
| Wheatberries | 7 : 8 | About 1 3/4 hours | 37548 manual (manufacturer spec) |

Hamilton Beach says teff, amaranth and other very small grains aren't recommended. Zojirushi's FAQ says its rice cookers are designed for rice, not beans, while KitchenAid's release lists beans, couscous, barley and farro among its presets.
