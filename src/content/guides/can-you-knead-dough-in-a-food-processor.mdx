Can you knead dough in a food processor? Yes, on most full-size models, and fast: [Cook's Illustrated](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/how_tos/5798-how-to-knead-bread-in-a-food-processor) gives about 45 to 90 seconds with the metal S-blade, against 8 to 10 minutes in a stand mixer. But some manuals forbid it, such as the Hamilton Beach 70730's, and each model has a flour ceiling. Breville's is 2.0 lb (900 g) for the BFP810 in the US and Canada.

So the real answer depends on your machine. Below are the dough rules from five manufacturers' manuals, next to what America's Test Kitchen, Reviewed and TechGearLab reported when they made dough in some of the same machines. For which processor to buy, see the [best food processors](/best/food-processors/).

## How much flour can a food processor handle?

Follow your own manual; the published numbers disagree. Breville gives 2.0 lb (900 g) for the BFP810 in the US and Canada. Cuisinart's DLC-2011N booklet allows 5 cups of all-purpose flour for bread dough, 4.5 cups for pizza dough and 2.75 cups for whole grain only, and says sweet dough over 3.5 cups goes in batches.

Cook's Illustrated is far more conservative: a 10 to 12 cup processor handles about 1.5 lb of dough, roughly 2 to 3 cups of flour, and larger recipes should be split and combined by hand. For an 11-cup machine, Cuisinart's 5 cups is roughly double that. Neither source explains the gap.

The field results sit between them. [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) reports the Cuisinart Pro Custom 11 and Custom 14 both easily processed a double batch of pizza dough with 4 cups of flour, while the KitchenAid 9 Cup Food Processor Plus (KFP0919BM) maxed out at 3 cups and its motor shut down temporarily just as each batch was done. When your manual is silent, Cook's Illustrated's 2 to 3 cups is the more conservative starting point. Bowl size in general is covered in [what size food processor do I need](/guides/what-size-food-processor-do-i-need/).

## Dough blade vs S-blade: which should you use?

The testers and the manufacturers disagree. Cook's Illustrated and [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/how-to-knead-dough-with-your-food-processor) say use the metal blade, because plastic dough blades drag the dough and leave it stuck to the sides. ATK's buying guide FAQ says not to bother with the dough blade at all.

The manufacturers point the other way. KitchenAid's 13-cup chart lists Knead, Low, Dough Blade; Breville's manual calls its dough blade a "soft material blade for kneading dough"; Ninja's BN600C guide lists a Dough Blade Assembly and a DOUGH program. TechGearLab's results don't settle it: the Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY, with no dough blade, scored 8 out of 10 on pizza dough, the same as the Breville BFP800, which ships with one. TechGearLab doesn't say which blade it used in the Breville.

If your manual names the dough blade for kneading, follow the manual. The full rundown of what each blade and disc does is in [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## How to knead dough in a food processor, step by step

This is technique only, not a recipe. Each step is credited.

1. **Check your manual.** Confirm it allows kneading (the Hamilton Beach 70730's does not) and find the flour maximum.
2. **Pulse the dry ingredients** about 5 seconds, per Cook's Illustrated.
3. **Add cool liquid with the motor running** (ATK), through the feed tube (Cook's Illustrated) or Breville's small feed chute. Cook's Illustrated's 130 rule: 130 minus the flour temperature gives the water temperature, so 70°F flour takes 60°F water, for a final dough of about 75°F.
4. **Process until a ball clears the sides of the bowl**, about 45 to 90 seconds (Cook's Illustrated).
5. **Adjust after 45 seconds.** Add 1 to 2 tablespoons of flour if sticky or 1 to 2 tablespoons of cold water if dry (Cook's Illustrated). Breville says 1 tablespoon at a time through the small chute.
6. **Use the right setting.** Ninja's guide says do not run on HIGH with the dough blade and offers a DOUGH program; its LOW and HIGH each run continuously for 60 seconds. KitchenAid's 13-cup chart says Low. Cuisinart's DLC-2011N has a DOUGH button that works together with ON or PULSE.
7. **Check doneness.** ATK says kneaded dough is smooth, almost shiny, very stretchy and springs back. Underkneaded dough breaks; overkneaded dough turns tough and dense.

## Food processor pizza dough: what's different

Pizza dough is a good fit, per [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/how-to-knead-dough-with-your-food-processor), which notes that building enough gluten in a wet mixture like pizza dough can take up to 20 minutes by hand. The exception, per Cook's Illustrated, is extremely wet dough over 75 percent hydration, such as its Pizza Bianca dough. For pizza, Cuisinart's DLC-2011N booklet lowers its flour limit from 5 cups to 4.5.

In [Reviewed's](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-food-processors) pizza dough test, the Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY kneaded a smooth, elastic ball without manual adjustments, stalling or moving on the counter. The KitchenAid KFP1318 needed some manual adjustments in the bowl and may move around. More on both in the [Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY review](/reviews/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/) and [Cuisinart vs KitchenAid](/vs/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny-vs-kitchenaid-kfp1318/).

## Signs your food processor is struggling with dough, and what to do

Breville's BFP810 manual gives the most complete fixes:

- **Blade slows on stiff dough:** don't process for longer than 1 minute.
- **Motor slows while kneading:** the dough may exceed the recommended quantity, so remove half and do two batches; or the dough may be too dry, so add water 1 tablespoon at a time until the motor speeds up.
- **A dough nub forms on the blade:** divide the dough into 3 pieces and redistribute them.
- **Dough feels tough:** divide it into 2 or 3 pieces and process 10 seconds; resting relaxes the gluten.
- **Unit vibrates:** turn it off and redistribute the dough.
- **Overload Protection icon flashes:** press POWER, unplug and let it cool 30 minutes before resuming.

Ninja's BN600C guide says a thermal switch temporarily disables the motor if it overheats; unplug it and let it cool about 15 minutes. Testers saw similar strain: ATK reports the KFP1318's lightweight base jumped around the counter and dough glued its multi-part blade in place, and one lower-ranked model, the lowest-wattage in ATK's lineup, stalled due to overheating. TechGearLab said the Hamilton Beach 70730 shook somewhat violently. Other shutdown causes are in [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).

## Doughs to skip in a food processor

- Very wet dough above 75 percent hydration (Cook's Illustrated).
- Dough too stiff to knead comfortably by hand; Cuisinart's DLC-2011N booklet says this in capitals.
- Over-kneaded enriched dough, because heat softens the butter (ATK).
- Long runs on sweet dough: Cuisinart's DLC-2011N booklet says 30 seconds is usually enough, 60 if the machine doesn't slow down.
- Any dough in the Hamilton Beach 70730 (its manual) or in small choppers. Reviewed says the KitchenAid 5-Cup Cordless Food Chopper can't knead dough, and the KitchenAid 7-Cup (KFP0718) stalled kneading yeast dough and couldn't finish.

## Food processor vs stand mixer for dough

A food processor is faster: 45 to 90 seconds against 8 to 10 minutes in a stand mixer, per Cook's Illustrated. The trade-off is batch size: Cook's Illustrated puts a 10 to 12 cup processor at 2 to 3 cups of flour, though ATK's 11- and 14-cup Cuisinarts handled 4. For big or very wet batches, a stand mixer fits better; see the [best stand mixers](/best/stand-mixers/).
