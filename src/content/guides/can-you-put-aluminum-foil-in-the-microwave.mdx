Can you put aluminum foil in the microwave? Sometimes, and only if your microwave's manual allows it. Toshiba's EM131A5C-BS manual allows small, smooth pieces of foil to shield thin parts of meat, kept at least 1 inch from the walls. Breville's BMO650 manual says food-grade foil should not be used at all, and FDA says metal pans and foil "generally" should not be used. Foil trays: transfer the food, and if your manual says nothing, skip the foil.

## Foil in the microwave: what each source says

I read three microwave manuals, the FDA's microwave page, America's Test Kitchen and the foil maker Reynolds. They don't agree on sheets of foil, but every one of them rules out foil trays.

## What the Toshiba, IKEA and Breville manuals say about foil

Toshiba's EM131A5C-BS booklet lists aluminum foil under "Materials you can use," limited to shielding. It says small smooth pieces "can be used to cover thin parts of meat or poultry to prevent overcooking," that arcing can occur if foil is too close to the walls, and that foil "should be at least 1 inch (2.5cm) away from oven walls." Its "Materials to be avoided" table lists aluminum trays and food cartons with metal handles ("May cause arcing. Transfer food into microwave-safe dish.") and metal twist ties, which it says "could cause a fire in the oven."

IKEA's GÅTEBO manual doesn't list foil in its "Materials you can use" table, but its safety section says to keep aluminum foil used for shielding at least 1 inch away from the walls, ceiling and door. It adds: "Do not cover racks or any other part of the oven with metal foil," because that causes the oven to overheat.

Breville's BMO650 instruction book goes the other way. It says "Food grade foil should not be used in the microwave oven as this could cause sparking and/or fire," and says not to use metal containers or utensils either.

FDA points the same way: "The instructions that come with each microwave oven indicate the kinds of containers to use." Your own manual wins. If it allows shielding, follow its limits. If it says nothing about foil, treat that as a no, which is where FDA ("generally" not) and Reynolds ("not microwave-safe") land.

## Why foil sparks in a microwave

FDA explains that microwaves are reflected by metal. With foil or a metal pan, that reflection makes food cook unevenly and may damage the oven.

The sparks themselves are called arcing. IKEA's GÅTEBO manual names what causes it:

- Metal or foil touching the side of the oven.
- Foil not molded to the food. The manual says upturned edges "act like antennas."
- Metal such as twist-ties, poultry pins or gold-rimmed dishes.
- Recycled paper towels containing small metal pieces.

Reynolds gives a similar list from the foil side: sparking is more likely when the foil is crumpled, has sharp edges or touches the sides of the microwave. None of the six sources I read gives a safe amount, size or time, so I haven't added one.

If your microwave sparks with no metal inside, that's a different problem with different causes. See [why is my microwave sparking](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/).

## How to use foil if your manual allows it

These steps come only from the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS and IKEA GÅTEBO manuals, the two I read that give rules for foil shielding. Don't apply them to a microwave whose manual bans foil, such as the Breville BMO650.

1. Check your manual first. If foil isn't listed as allowed, don't use it.
2. Use small, smooth pieces only (Toshiba). Smooth means no crumples; Reynolds says crumpled foil and sharp edges raise the sparking risk.
3. Cover only thin parts of meat or poultry that would otherwise overcook (Toshiba).
4. Mold the foil flat against the food, with no upturned edges (GÅTEBO).
5. Keep it at least 1 inch from the walls (Toshiba), and from the ceiling and door too (GÅTEBO).
6. Never cover the racks or any other part of the oven with foil; GÅTEBO says this causes overheating.
7. Watch it. If you see arcing, stop the oven at once. GÅTEBO's manual says to press "Cancel" and correct the problem; Breville's says STOP/CLEAR. Use your oven's equivalent button.

## Can you microwave foil containers and takeout trays?

No. This is the one point every source agrees on. Toshiba's and IKEA's manuals both say an aluminum tray "may cause arcing" and tell you to transfer the food into a microwave-safe dish. They say the same about food cartons with metal handles.

America's Test Kitchen (Miye Bromberg, March 14, 2024) rates foil wrappers and bowls a "No": while some metals can be used in a microwave, it says, they generally aren't the kind used for takeout, and foil containers can cause arcing. It adds that the metal fasteners on Chinese takeout boxes can create sparks that damage the microwave. Its advice is to take food out of its packaging and heat it on a microwave-safe glass or ceramic plate, bowl or cup. Reynolds also says it doesn't recommend any metal or foil products in the microwave, including disposable baking and grill pans and pasta and casserole pans.

ATK's "No" covers foil wrappers too, so a foil-wrapped sandwich, burrito or leftover goes the same way: unwrap it and put it on a plate. If you want to cover it, see [is it safe to microwave plastic](/guides/is-it-safe-to-microwave-plastic/) before reaching for plastic wrap.

## Other metal: twist ties, handles, gold rims and utensils

The manuals list these metal items as off-limits:

- Metal twist ties: may cause arcing and a fire (Toshiba, GÅTEBO).
- Food cartons with metal handles: transfer the food (Toshiba, GÅTEBO).
- Metal or metal-trimmed utensils: GÅTEBO says metal shields food from microwave energy and metal trim may cause arcing. Breville says no metal containers or utensils.
- Gold-rimmed dishes and glassware with metallic trim (GÅTEBO; Toshiba says to make sure glassware has no metallic trim).
- Poultry pins (GÅTEBO).
- Recycled paper towels containing small metal pieces (GÅTEBO). Breville goes further: no recycled paper or paper towels when cooking, because recycled paper may contain small metal or other impurities.

Not sure whether a dish is safe? Toshiba's manual gives a check: fill a microwave-safe container with 1 cup (250 ml) of cold water, put it in with the dish in question, run it at maximum power for 1 minute, and don't use the dish if it comes out warm. The manual says not to exceed 1 minute. For everything else that shouldn't go in, see [what not to put in a microwave](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-microwave/).

## Foil and metal racks in convection and air-fry combo microwaves

IKEA's GÅTEBO manual says not to put its air fry basket or grill tray in the oven in microwave-only mode. They're allowed in grill only, convection only and the two combination modes (microwave plus convection, microwave plus grill), always placed on the glass tray. The manual gives no separate foil rules for those modes; its foil rules (1 inch from walls, ceiling and door; never cover racks) are the only ones it states.

Evidence here is thin: GÅTEBO is the only combo manual I read, and other combo models may set different rules. Check yours. For whether a combo is worth buying, see [is a microwave air fryer combo worth it](/guides/microwave-air-fryer-combo-worth-it/). For foil in a standalone air fryer, see [can you put aluminum foil in an air fryer](/guides/aluminum-foil-in-air-fryer/).

## Foil sparked in the microwave: what to do now

Stop the oven straight away. Breville's manual says to press STOP/CLEAR if you see sparks, arcing or flashing, then correct the problem or contact Breville Consumer Support. Reynolds' advice is the same first step (stop the cycle or unplug the microwave), then remove the foil only once it's cool to the touch with no sign of sparking or flames.

If materials inside ignite, Toshiba's manual says to keep the oven door closed, turn the oven off and unplug it, and shut off power at the fuse or circuit breaker panel. More on that in [microwave fire: what to do](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/).

Reynolds says to check the inside for damage before using the oven again and never to use a damaged microwave. If the cavity looks scorched, or the oven keeps sparking with no metal inside, stop using it and call service. Don't open the cabinet yourself.

## Where the foil sources disagree

Toshiba allows small foil shields with a 1-inch gap, and IKEA gives rules for foil used for shielding with the same gap. Breville says no foil. FDA hedges with "generally" not. Reynolds says its own Reynolds Wrap is not microwave-safe, while acknowledging that some microwave makers give limited instructions for small amounts of foil, and tells you to follow your microwave's instructions.

Evidence on how much foil is safe is thin: none of these six sources gives an amount, and I haven't relied on any study I didn't open. The plain conclusion: follow your own microwave's manual, and if it's silent, don't use foil.
