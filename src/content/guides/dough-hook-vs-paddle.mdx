The dough hook vs paddle answer: use the paddle (flat beater) for batters, cookie dough, frosting and creaming butter, and the dough hook for kneading bread, pizza and pasta dough. Use the whisk to beat air into thin mixtures like cream and egg whites. Cuisinart's SM-50 and Hamilton Beach's 63391/63392 manuals split the jobs this way, and KitchenAid's support pages knead with the hook on speed 2.

## When to use the paddle (flat beater)

The flat beater is the default tool for anything without yeast. Cuisinart's SM-50 manual says its paddle is best for cookies, cakes, batters and frostings, plus pie crusts, biscuits, shortcakes, meatloaf and meatballs. Hamilton Beach calls its flat beater the tool "for most of your mixing needs".

Cookie dough is where the paddle works hardest. Hamilton Beach calls it one of the thickest doughs and says to use room-temperature butter and add flour one cup at a time. Cuisinart says to scrape the bowl and paddle often: "The more you scrape the bowl, the better."

### Flex-edge (scraper) beaters

A flex-edge beater has a rubber or silicone edge that wipes the bowl as it turns. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) says the one on the KitchenAid 7 Quart Bowl Lift "greatly decreased the need to scrape down the bowl" when creaming butter and sugar. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) says finned scraper beaters made mixing faster and easier, but found the scraping paddle on the Breville BEM825 trapped food on its silicone fins. It's a convenience, not a requirement.

## When to use the dough hook

The dough hook kneads, and kneading is the job the paddle doesn't do in either manual. Cuisinart's manual lists breads, rolls, pizza and focaccia, pasta dough and yeast-raised coffee cakes. Hamilton Beach says the hook is for recipes that require kneading.

KitchenAid's UK FAQ says "Speed 2 is the only speed that should be used for kneading," and that 2 minutes of machine kneading equals 10 to 12 minutes by hand. Its US bread page describes properly kneaded dough as smooth, slightly tacky but not sticky, and elastic, springing back when pressed. It warns that over-kneading makes dough tough and tight, and that time varies with flour, hydration and enrichment. TechGearLab judged its kneading results with the windowpane test, stretching dough thin enough to see light through.

### C-hook vs spiral hook

In TechGearLab's whole-wheat sandwich-bread test, the KitchenAid Artisan's C-hook and the Aucma's spiral hook both kneaded in 7 minutes, while the Cuisinart SM-50's C-hook took 13. TechGearLab says the Artisan's dough came together in a ball in under a minute with no scraping. Its KitchenAid 7 Quart Bowl Lift, which has a spiral hook, took 10 minutes, so hook shape alone didn't decide the times.

On [The Fresh Loaf](https://www.thefreshloaf.com/node/35914/dough-hook-v-standard-paddle), one baker says a spiral hook circulates dough evenly, while a C or J hook may need stopping to reposition the dough. Another says his hook engaged only part of the dough, and a KitchenAid owner says a spiral hook worked better than his J hook. No source we read compared hook shapes on the same mixer, so the evidence is thin: don't choose a mixer for its hook shape alone.

## Dough climbing the hook: what it means

Dough climbing the hook usually means kneading is done, according to KitchenAid's [dough-climbing support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Mixer_Performance/Dough_Climbing_Up_the_Dough_Hook_-_Stand_Mixer). Egg doughs and most specialty grains, especially rye, are stickier and climb more easily.

KitchenAid's US page recommends speed 2 for kneading and suggests adding flour 1 tablespoon at a time, without overcompensating, since too much flour makes the bread too dry. Some mixers handle it in the design: ATK says the Ooni Halo Core's breaker bar keeps dough from climbing.

## Flat beater vs dough hook for bread: can you use the paddle?

The manuals say no, while some owners disagree. Cuisinart's and Hamilton Beach's manuals give kneading to the hook and never suggest the paddle for yeast dough. The only KitchenAid page we opened that names a tool for kneading discusses the dough hook.

On The Fresh Loaf, one baker says the paddle slams dough against the bowl harder than the hook, giving it more energy. He adds that dough climbs the paddle even more, so it needs turning more often. An earlier reply says the paddle mixes and the hook kneads, and another baker used only the hook on a Bosch Universal Plus for a roughly 73% hydration dough by adding the dry mix very slowly.

The approach both manuals support: combine with the paddle if you like, then knead with the hook.

## When to use the whisk vs the paddle

The whisk adds air. Cuisinart lists eggs, egg whites, heavy cream, meringue, marshmallows, and angel food and chiffon cakes, and says the bowl and whisk must be spotlessly clean for whites, since any fat stops them whipping.

The [Hamilton Beach manual](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840285300.pdf) says not to use the whisk for thick mixtures such as dough, fudge or mashed potatoes. It needs at least 2 large egg whites or 1 cup (237 ml) of cream; for less, Hamilton Beach recommends a hand mixer. Cuisinart differs on potatoes, saying its whisk "quickly whips potatoes."

Creaming is where Hamilton Beach's manual contradicts itself. Its mix chart lists "Creaming / Whisk / Frosting" at 5 to 7, yet its own cake and cookie recipes cream butter and sugar with the flat beater. Cuisinart's speed guide lists creaming butter and sugar at speeds 2 to 5 without naming a tool. Follow the manual for the mixer you own.

For a sense of speed, TechGearLab timed 1 cup of cream to stiff peaks at 1 minute 50 seconds on the KitchenAid Artisan and 2 minutes 10 seconds on a Hamilton Beach 7-Speed. TechGearLab prints neither model number; its 5-quart, 325 W Artisan matches the model in our [KitchenAid KSM150PS review](/reviews/kitchenaid-ksm150ps/).

## Stand mixer attachments explained: coated, burnished or stainless

Each tool comes in different materials, and the material decides how you wash it. KitchenAid's [Beater Care page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Cleaning_and_Maintenance/Beater_Care) says burnished tools are polished aluminum, aren't dishwasher safe, and can oxidize, which KitchenAid says is harmless and removable. Coated and 100% stainless beaters and hooks are dishwasher safe; most wire whips have a burnished hub and aren't.

ATK reports KitchenAid's white-coated aluminum tools chip and recommends stainless. Cuisinart's SM-50 paddle and hook are top-rack dishwasher safe, but its whisk is hand-wash only. KitchenAid says its stainless 3-pack fits all tilt-heads except the Artisan Mini and KN15E1X; more on fit in [KitchenAid attachments compatibility](/guides/kitchenaid-attachments-compatibility/), and on washing in [are stand mixer parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-stand-mixer-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## Common dough hook and paddle mistakes

- Whisk on thick mixtures: Hamilton Beach warns against dough, fudge and mashed potatoes.
- Kneading above your manual's speed: KitchenAid's UK FAQ allows speed 2 only.
- Overloading: Cuisinart lists a maximum of 6 cups white flour for yeast dough. If its mixer shuts off, Cuisinart says to turn it off, unplug it, remove some ingredients and let it stand a few minutes; see [stand mixer overheating](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/).
- Too small a batch for the whisk: Hamilton Beach's minimum is 2 egg whites or 1 cup of cream.
- A beater that hits the bowl or misses the bottom: ATK found that where tools sat far from the bowl, it got unmixed pockets of ingredients. Check yours with the [KitchenAid dime test](/guides/kitchenaid-dime-test/).

If you knead bread often, note that TechGearLab's kneading times ranged from 7 minutes (KitchenAid Artisan and Aucma) to 13 (Cuisinart SM-50).
