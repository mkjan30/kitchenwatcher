Espresso machine bar pressure explained in one line: the 15 or 20 bar printed on the box is the most the pump can produce, not the pressure your coffee brews at. Reference espresso brews near 9 bar at the puck. The Italian Espresso National Institute's spec, as reproduced in [Wikipedia's Espresso article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espresso), sets it at 9 ± 1 bar. A 20-bar pump doesn't make better espresso on its own.

What decides the pressure the coffee actually sees is the puck, the basket and, on some machines, a valve that bleeds off the excess. Everything below comes from manufacturer manuals and independent reviewers; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains why we don't test hands-on.

## What one bar of pressure means, and how espresso settled on 9

One bar is 100 kilopascals; Wikipedia gives the 9 bars of a classic pump machine as 900 kPa. That is roughly nine times the air pressure at sea level, or about 130 psi.

Wikipedia's history of espresso shows how the number settled. Early steam machines from Bezzera and Pavoni made about 1.5 bar, which the article calls too little for modern espresso. Gaggia's 1947 lever machine pushed water at up to 12 atmospheres, and the 1961 Faema E61 used an electric pump that pressurized water to 9 bar, the same figure the Italian standard below uses.

## What bar pressure is right for espresso?

The Italian standard is a definition of what counts as espresso, not a measurement of any machine. Here are the parameters as reproduced on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espresso):

| Parameter (INEI standard) | Value |
|---|---|
| Water pressure entering the coffee | 9 ± 1 bar |
| Water temperature leaving the machine | 88 ± 2 °C |
| Percolation time | 25 ± 5 s |
| Temperature in the cup | 67 ± 3 °C |

Not every source treats 9 bar as the only right answer. [Coffee Chronicler](https://coffeechronicler.com/gear/espresso-machines/) says espresso should be brewed at around 6 to 9 bar and notes a movement away from high pressure, with around 6 to 7 bar now the preferred setting. Nine bar is the reference, and lower pressure is an accepted alternative, not an error.

## Pump rating vs brew pressure: 9 bar vs 15 bar espresso machines

The box figure is a pump rating. CASABREWS lists a 20-bar Italian pump for the CM5418 (manufacturer spec). De'Longhi's Stilosa EC260BK page lists "Pump Pressure (bar): 15" (manufacturer spec). Breville's [BES870 Barista Express manual](https://www.breville.com/content/dam/breville/us/assets/miscellaneous/instruction-manual/espresso/BES870-instruction-manual.pdf) calls its pump a "15 bar Italian pump" (manufacturer spec).

Even GEVI, a brand that sells 20-bar machines, says on its blog that the rating is the pump's maximum pressure capacity, not the brewing pressure. [Homegrounds](https://www.homegrounds.co/how-many-bars-of-pressure-for-espresso/) makes the same point: what matters is the pressure at the group, where the water hits the coffee. With a regular basket, that depends on how much the coffee puck resists the water, as [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/coffee-machines/casabrews-cm5418-review) puts it. On some machines an over-pressure valve, or OPV, also bleeds off pressure above its setting. So a 15-bar machine and a 9-bar machine can brew at similar pressure, or at very different pressure, depending on those parts.

Several ranking pages go further. GEVI says machines with 15- or 20-bar pumps deliver 9 bar during extraction, and [Espresso Academy](https://espressoacademy.it/en/guide-en/what-is-the-best-pressure-for-an-espresso-machine/) says an internal pressure regulator ensures only 9 bar reach the puck. Neither names the budget machines this applies to, and our sources don't show it for them. Neither CASABREWS nor De'Longhi mentions an OPV or its setting on the pages we read. Tom's Guide's spec table for the [Gaggia Classic E24](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/coffee-makers/gaggia-classic-e24-evo-pro-review) lists "OPV control: No", meaning there's no user adjustment.

## Is a 20 bar espresso machine better?

No. A 20-bar rating says nothing reliable about the shot you'll get, for three reasons:

- **Brew pressure is set at the puck and the valve.** The pump's ceiling only matters if nothing else limits it.
- **Independent results don't follow the rating.** [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-espresso-machine) measured extraction yield with a refractometer and calls 18 to 22% ideal. The CASABREWS CM5418 (20-bar spec) reached 21% and the Breville Barista Express (15-bar spec) 19.4%. The Bambino Plus reached 18.4%, the Bambino 18%, and the De'Longhi Stilosa (15-bar spec) 12%.
- **The machines described as brewing at 9 bar are not the budget ones.** See the next section.

One caveat: TechGearLab didn't measure brew pressure. Its numbers show the bar rating doesn't predict results; they don't show pressure causing them. GEVI argues the extra power of a 20-bar machine may compensate for inconsistent grind size or tamping, but offers no measurements.

## Which espresso machines brew at 9 bar?

Coffee Chronicler says the Breville Bambino Plus "brews at the correct 9 bars, instead of 15". Breville's [BES500 manual](https://assets.breville.com/BES500/BES500_KOR-EN_IB_C22_LR.pdf) gives no bar figure at all, so that claim rests on one reviewer. Our [Breville Bambino Plus review](/reviews/breville-bambino-plus/) and the [Bambino vs Bambino Plus comparison](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/) cover the machine itself.

The Gaggia Classic is where sources conflict. Tom's Guide says the Classic E24 Evo Pro "pulls at 9-bar". Coffee Chronicler says many owners of the earlier Gaggia Classic Pro open the machine and install a special OPV spring to reach 9 bar. Both may be right for different model years, but no source we read confirms that.

## Pressure gauges: what the needle tells you, and what it doesn't

Breville's BES870 manual (pp.16-17) describes four zones on the Barista Express gauge. The needle sits in a pre-infusion zone at the start; the solid grey Ideal Espresso Zone means ideal pressure; the lower zone means insufficient pressure and an under-extracted shot; the upper zone means too much pressure and an over-extracted shot. The manual gives no bar values for any zone. Our [Breville Barista Express review](/reviews/breville-barista-express/) has the rest of that machine's record.

CASABREWS markets the CM5418's gauge as a way to monitor each shot and fine-tune the brew (manufacturer claim). But [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/coffee-machines/casabrews-cm5418-review) reports that both included baskets are pressurized, and that in a pressurized basket the extra wall, not the puck's resistance, "does the heavy lifting" in regulating extraction pressure. CASABREWS' own spec lists two dual-wall filters. So on that basket, a needle in the ideal range says more about the basket than about your grind. Our [CASABREWS CM5418 review](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/) covers the machine in detail.

Grind isn't the only cause of weak flow: Breville's BES870 manual says hard-water mineral build-up reduces brewing flow, brewing temperature and the machine's power. See [CASABREWS CM5418 not pumping water](/guides/casabrews-cm5418-not-pumping-water/) and [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## How do pressurized baskets change the pressure?

Breville's BES870 and BES500 manuals say dual-wall baskets "regulate the pressure" and help optimize extraction regardless of grind, dose, tamp pressure or freshness. Both manuals point them at pre-ground coffee; the BES500 manual adds beans past 30 days from roast. Single-wall baskets, per the BES870 manual, let you experiment with grind, dose and tamp. Coffee Chronicler says pressurized portafilters "should be avoided" if you're serious about learning the barista craft. Our guide to [pressurized vs non-pressurized baskets](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/) explains how each type works.

## Do pre-infusion and pressure profiling change brew pressure?

Both Breville manuals describe low-pressure pre-infusion that applies low water pressure at the start of extraction to gently expand the grinds. The BES870 manual adds that it "gradually increases water pressure" before full pressure. CASABREWS also lists low-pressure pre-infusion for the CM5418 (manufacturer claim). On the Bambino Plus, holding the 1 or 2 CUP button gives manual pre-infusion, per the BES500 manual.

Pressure profiling goes further: it changes pressure through the shot. None of our sources lists it for the machines in the table below. Coffee Chronicler notes the MiiCoffee Apex V2 has an externally adjustable OPV, so you can dial in 9 bar or go lower around 6 to 7. That is the kind of feature to look for if you want control over pressure.

## What to check instead of the bar number

- **An OPV, and its setting.** Look for one set to, or adjustable to, about 9 bar. Few budget makers publish this.
- **A non-pressurized basket in the box.** Without one, grind and tamp matter less and the gauge tells you less.
- **A pressure gauge,** keeping the basket caveat above in mind.
- **Pre-infusion,** which both Breville manuals and CASABREWS describe.
- **Your grind and tamp.** Breville's manuals say to tamp with about 15 to 20 kg of pressure, and that consistent pressure every time matters more than the amount.
