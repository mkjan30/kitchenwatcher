In the mini chopper vs food processor choice, a 3- to 4-cup chopper covers small-batch mincing, pesto, hummus, sauces and mayo. A full-size food processor (11 to 16 cups, per America's Test Kitchen) is what you need for slicing, shredding, dough and big batches. If you rarely do those four jobs, a chopper is enough.

[America's Test Kitchen's small food processor review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1643-small-food-processors) (published June 3, 2026) says small models lack slicing and grating blades, hold about 2 cups of vegetables and handle dough poorly. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-food-processor/) found its 3.5-cup pick still made a full pint of hummus, matching the larger machines. The reference chopper here is the KitchenAid KFC3516, the small pick of both. Nobody at Kitchen Watcher used these machines; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What can a mini food processor do, and what can't it?

ATK ran the KFC3516 through these tests and says most ingredients turned out uniform, with two exceptions noted below (almonds and mayo):

- minced garlic and parsley
- chopped mirepoix (onion, celery, carrot) and almonds
- Parmesan pulsed into coarse crumbs
- basil pesto, mayonnaise and hummus

ATK also says small processors suit dressings, dips and marinades.

**What it can't do:**

- Slice or shred. ATK says small processors have no grating or slicing blades, and CNET lists the KFC3516 with no slice or shred discs.
- Knead dough. ATK says the bowls are too small and the motors too weak; CNET lists no dough hook.
- Big batches. ATK found the bowls maxed out at about 2 cups of vegetables.

## 3.5 cup chopper uses that reviewers back up

### Garlic, herbs and onions

ATK minced whole garlic cloves and parsley in every model and says the KFC3516 did a stellar job of mincing. ATK lists its blade height as 2 mm; the review says models with blades sitting close to the bowl floor, at 3 to 4 mm of clearance, processed food more evenly.

### Nuts and Parmesan

ATK's tests included pulsing Parmesan cubes until coarsely grated and chopping almonds. ATK says the KFC3516 grated well but doesn't chop hard almonds quite as evenly as some models.

### Pesto, hummus and dips

CNET says the KFC3516 made "a full pint of perfectly smooth hummus" and performed the chopping and pureeing functions "equal to just about all" the larger models. ATK made basil pesto and hummus in its test as well.

### Mayo and dressings

ATK calls a drizzle basin, the reservoir in the lid with a small hole, essential for emulsions, because it feeds oil in slowly. On the KFC3516 it found oil sometimes got trapped in the basin, which made the mayo slightly runnier and the lid harder to clean.

## Where a mini chopper falls short

Bowl size is the first limit. ATK says the small bowls it tested topped out at about 2 cups of vegetables. Models under 3.5 cups were too small to make full recipe yields of sauces or to chop nuts evenly. It calls those "not worth buying."

With no discs, there is no way to slice or shred in volume. On dough, ATK's FAQ simply answers "No."

The labels overlap: CNET says the KFC3516 is too modest by not calling itself a food processor. Check the box for discs and a dough blade rather than trusting the name.

## When a full-size food processor is worth it

A full-size machine pays off when you slice, shred or make dough regularly, or cook for a crowd. ATK's full-size review says the Cuisinart Pro Custom 11, like its 14-cup sibling, easily processed a double batch of pizza dough with 4 cups of flour. The discontinued KitchenAid 9-cup it tested maxed out at 3 cups of flour, so it couldn't take a double batch. Its motor also stalled, shutting down temporarily, as each batch finished. Dough detail is on [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/), and disc types on [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

Bigger isn't automatically better: CNET says "8-12 cups will serve most people most of the time," and that unless you cook for large gatherings, less is probably more.

You can also get both in one machine. ATK says the Breville Sous Chef 16 (BFP810) comes with a 2.5-cup mini bowl, and CNET says the Magimix 4200XL includes a separate mini bowl. The Cuisinart Custom 14's listed accessories are an S-blade, slicing and shredding disks and a spatula, with no mini bowl. Our ranked picks are on [best food processors](/best/food-processors/).

## Do I need a food processor or a chopper (or both)?

- **Chopper:** small kitchen, cooking for one or two, mostly sauces, dips and aromatics. CNET says the KFC3516's cord winds into its base and the machine is barely the size of a French press.
- **Full-size processor:** you slice or shred vegetables, grate cheese in volume, make bread or pizza dough, or batch-cook.
- **Both, or a full-size with a mini bowl:** you want small emulsions like mayo and also volume work.

## Cleaning, storage and safety for choppers

KitchenAid's [corded food chopper cleaning page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Chopper/Corded/Cleaning_and_Maintenance/How_to_Clean_Corded_Food_Chopper) says:

1. Be sure the chopper is unplugged before taking parts off or putting them on, and before cleaning.
2. Wash the work bowls, lids, whisking accessory and blade in the dishwasher (top rack recommended) or by hand in hot, soapy water.
3. Wipe the base with a damp cloth, with no abrasive cleaners. Do not immerse the base in water.
4. Wrap the cord counterclockwise around the base for storage.

KitchenAid's [13-cup processor cleaning page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Cleaning_and_Care/How_to_Clean_the_Base_and_Attachments_-_Food_Processor) says all parts are top-rack dishwasher safe, to use gentle cycles and avoid high-temperature ones. More on [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

Electric mini choppers have been recalled before. On April 4, 2024, the [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Walmart-Recalls-Mainstays-Electric-Mini-Choppers-Due-to-Laceration-Hazard-Sold-Exclusively-at-Walmart) announced recall 24-178 of about 51,750 Mainstays Electric Mini Choppers (model MS14100094536S1), sold only at Walmart. The reason: the blade can operate unexpectedly during assembly or when not enclosed in the container. Walmart received five laceration reports, two needing medical attention including stitches, during assembly or cleaning. The CPSC tells owners to stop using it and return it to Walmart for a full refund.

The CPSC recall records checked on October 9, 2026 show no recall of the KitchenAid KFC3516. Check your own model on our [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Where the sources disagree or are thin

ATK says it liked 3 to 4 mm of blade clearance and that blades set higher than 4 mm underprocessed food. Yet it still recommends the Hamilton Beach 4-cup (70510) with a 5 mm blade height.

On cleaning, KitchenAid's chopper page says the blade can go in the dishwasher (top rack recommended), while ATK's small-processor FAQ says to hand-wash food processor blades because the dishwasher can dull them. CNET's guide carries a current-year title but says it was updated on April 30, 2025. The KFC3516 use and care PDFs we found are scanned images we could not read, so this page gives no list of foods KitchenAid says not to process and no max-fill line.
