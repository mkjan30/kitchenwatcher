Which food processor blade to use depends on the job. The S-blade (chopping/mixing blade) chops, minces, purées and mixes. The slicing disc slices fruit, vegetables and firm chilled cheese, and the shredding disc shreds cheese, carrots and cabbage. Use a dough blade only if your manual includes one, because some say not to knead at all. On a reversible disc, the word stamped on the side facing up is the function you get, per Hamilton Beach's manual.

Everything below comes from four manufacturer documents, Breville's blade explainer, two independent reviewers and the CPSC recall record. We read the manuals; we did not test the machines (see [how we research](/how-we-research/)). Where the brands disagree, the table shows each one rather than averaging them.

## Food processor attachments explained: which blade for which job

The table below matches each blade and disc to the jobs its manufacturer lists, the speed it names and what its manual rules out.

The "right" speed changes by brand. Ninja keeps its disc on DISC or LOW, while KitchenAid puts hard produce and cheese on High. Hamilton Beach's 70730 has 1, 2 and Pulse and says pulsing gives the best chopping results. Breville's BFP810 has no speed settings at all, only start/pause and PULSE, so its guidance is about which chute you use and how hard you press.

We used four documents. They are the [Hamilton Beach 70730 use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) (HB), the [Ninja BN600C Series owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf), the Canadian sibling of the BN601 (Ninja), the [Breville BFP810 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf) (Breville) and [KitchenAid's 13-cup Accessories Selection Guide](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Disc_Selection/Accessories_Selection_Guide_-_Food_Processor) (KitchenAid). We couldn't open a US BN601 manual (Ninja's US support PDFs blocked us), so the Ninja rows come from the BN600C guide.

## The S-blade (chopping/mixing blade): what it does and doesn't do

Ninja's guide calls it the Chopping Blade Assembly and Breville's the Micro-serrated S-Blade. Hamilton Beach's chart lists nuts, meat, garlic, onions, herbs, bread for crumbs and crackers, plus purées, dressings, soups and sauces. Breville adds ground meat (freeze proteins for 30 minutes first), purées of cooked food once it has cooled, and batters mixed with PULSE.

Both HB and Breville say to cut food into 1-inch cubes first. HB's maximum loads for its S-blade are 2 to 3 cups of fruit and vegetables, 0.7 lb (350 g) of meat, 5 oz of Parmesan or Romano and 8 oz of cheddar, Jack or mozzarella. HB also says not to purée or mash potatoes, and Ninja says not to process dry ingredients or grind with its chopping blade.

Where the blade sits in the bowl matters too. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) measured gaps as small as 2.9 mm to the bowl side and 2.2 mm to the bottom on the best models. The lowest performers had as much as 6.1 mm and 5.4 mm. ATK preferred blades that sit low and close to the walls.

## Food processor S-blade vs dough blade: the sources disagree

[Cook's Illustrated](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/how_tos/9577-dont-bother-with-a-dough-blade) found that dough blades have short, blunt arms that struggle to pick up small amounts of flour. On pizza dough with 4 1/2 cups of flour, both blades kneaded comparably, but the metal blade was 15% faster. ATK now uses the metal blade for all doughs, and its food processor guide sums up the dough blade as "don't bother".

The manufacturers mostly disagree. Breville includes a "soft material blade for kneading dough" with a 2.0 lb (900 g) flour maximum in the US and Canada. Ninja has a DOUGH program and warns, "DO NOT run the unit on HIGH if using the dough blade." KitchenAid's 13-cup guide pairs kneading with the dough blade on Low.

Hamilton Beach goes the other way: the 70730 manual lists kneading dough among the jobs not to use the processor for. The Cuisinart Custom 14 ships without a dough blade, per ATK's parts list and [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor). ATK's undated dough-blade article says the Custom 14 advises using its dough blade only for recipes with at least 3 1/2 cups of flour, which doesn't square with ATK's own current accessory list. That advice may refer to an earlier version or an optional blade; we couldn't open Cuisinart's booklet to check.

Whichever blade you use, Breville says that if stiff dough slows the blade, don't process for longer than 1 minute. Flour limits per model and technique are in [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/).

## Slicing vs shredding disc: which side up, and fixed vs adjustable

On a reversible disc, orientation is the whole answer. Hamilton Beach stamps "SLICE" and "SHRED" on the two sides: the word facing up is the function you get, and the manual says to store the disc slicing side down. Ninja labels its sides "Slicer" and "Shredder" and allows only the DISC program or LOW with it. HB also says not to process past the MAX LEVEL with a disc, and its bowl scraper can't be used with the disc.

Fixed discs give one thickness. TechGearLab lists a fixed 4 mm slicing disc and a medium shredder for the Cuisinart Custom 14, and a single slice thickness on the HB 70730 and Ninja. KitchenAid's 13-cup has an externally adjustable slicing disc and a reversible fine/medium shredder. Breville's manual says its adjustable slicer cuts "1/8in – 5/16in (0.3mm – 8.0mm)", which doesn't add up, since 1/8 in is about 3.2 mm. The manual doesn't say which figure is right.

TechGearLab's slice and shred scores, each weighted 15% of its overall score:

- Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY: slicing 7, shredding 6
- Ninja BN600/BN601: slicing 7, shredding 7
- Hamilton Beach 70730: slicing 5, shredding 6
- Breville Sous Chef 16 Pro (BFP800, the BFP810's predecessor): slicing 7, shredding 8

For cheese, chill it first: HB says mozzarella goes in the freezer for 30 minutes, and Breville says to freeze soft cheese for 45 minutes and doesn't recommend fresh mozzarella. Technique is covered in [how to shred cheese in a food processor](/guides/how-to-shred-cheese-in-a-food-processor/) and [how to slice vegetables in a food processor](/guides/how-to-slice-vegetables-in-a-food-processor/).

## Specialty discs: French fry, dicing kit, crinkle cut, mini blade

Breville's BFP810 ships with a French fry disc for potatoes, sweet potato and carrots laid horizontally in the large chute. Its mini blade works only in the mini bowl, and the S-blade can't go in the mini bowl. The Paradice dicing kit is sold separately, and Breville says BFP005 and BFP006 accessories aren't compatible.

Hamilton Beach sells an optional crinkle cut/fine shred disc for the 70730, and KitchenAid's 13-cup guide runs its dicing kit on Low. ATK is lukewarm on dicers. It calls the Breville's "overkill" for a single onion, and says the dicing attachment on the Hamilton Beach Professional 14-Cup Dicing Food Processor (70825, not the 70730) left a good deal of waste and seven parts to clean. ATK's view is that three blades (chop, slice, shred) cover most tasks.

## What the manuals say never to use a food processor blade for

- **Hamilton Beach 70730:** no grinding grain, coffee beans or spices; no slicing frozen meats; no kneading dough; no crushing ice.
- **Ninja BN600C:** no ice cubes for crushing, no hot liquids, no dry ingredients or grinding with the Chopping Blade Assembly. Don't fill past the 9-cup line, never feed food by hand, and don't attempt to sharpen blades.
- **Breville BFP810:** no hot or boiling liquids, which should cool to room temperature first. Hard produce such as pumpkin and sweet potato goes through the small or medium chute, because the large chute could damage the disks. Never force food down the chute.

For ice and hot soup, see [food processor vs blender](/guides/food-processor-vs-blender/) and [can a blender crush ice](/guides/can-a-blender-crush-ice/).

## Handling blades safely and checking a Cuisinart blade for the recall

HB says to insert and remove the blade and disc by their hubs, and its manual says to always unplug the unit before removing the lid. Ninja says to lift the chopping blade out by the top of its shaft before emptying the bowl, since it isn't locked in place. The Hamilton Beach, Ninja and Breville manuals all warn that blades are sharp and need care when washing.

The [CPSC 17-050 recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) of 13 December 2016 covers about 8 million Cuisinart processors in the US, with model numbers including the DFP-14 prefix, sold July 1996 to December 2015. Only the riveted blades with four rivets are included. Conair received 69 reports of blade pieces in food, including 30 mouth lacerations or tooth injuries. The remedy is to stop using the riveted blade and get a free replacement from Cuisinart. An older or used Custom 14 needs the rivet check in our [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

Lid and pusher locks are covered in [food processor safety interlock](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/), and washing blades in [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/) and [are food processor parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).
