Food processor leaking from the bottom or lid? The first cause KitchenAid's support article names is too much liquid: anything above the bowl's maximum liquid level flows under the blade and out onto the base. That limit can sit well below the bowl's cup rating: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) lists a 3-cup liquid maximum for the 14-cup Cuisinart Custom 14.

This page is built from manuals, manufacturer support articles and one third-party leak test ([how we research](/how-we-research/)). Nobody here ran these machines.

## Why is my food processor leaking? 5 causes

The sources we read name five causes of a food processor leaking from the bottom or around the lid:

1. **Liquid above the max line.** Per KitchenAid's [Liquid Leaking During Use](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor_Tips_and_Tricks/Liquid_Leaking_During_Use) article, liquid above the maximum level flows under the blade and onto the base. ATK adds that centrifugal force makes the liquid level climb once the machine is on, which is why it wants the max fill line clearly marked on the bowl.
2. **The wrong blade.** KitchenAid says to process liquids only with the Multipurpose Blade, because the Egg Whip creates a higher chance of leakage.
3. **Bowl not fully locked.** iFixit's [Hamilton Beach 70730 wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Hamilton_Beach_70730_Troubleshooting), written by a university student team, says to make sure the bowl is twisted all the way to the right, into its locked position. If the lid won't lock at all, see [lid won't lock](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
4. **A cracked bowl.** The same wiki says to check the base and sides of the bowl and replace it if it is cracked.
5. **A missing or misseated seal.** KitchenAid says some models have a red seal on the work bowl that "eliminates leaking". Breville's [BFP810 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf) lists a silicone seal on the processing lid and on the mini bowl, and ATK mentions a leak-preventing gasket on the Cuisinart SFP-13 and a leakproof gasket on the Breville Sous Chef 12 Plus.

## How much liquid can a food processor hold?

The cup size on the box is not the liquid limit. The food processor max liquid line is a separate limit, either marked on the bowl or given in the owner's manual, per KitchenAid. To find how much liquid your food processor takes, check the bowl first, then the manual. For four of the five models below, the published liquid figure is lower than the cup rating.

Here is what the sources say for the five models in our [best food processors](/best/food-processors/) research. The figures disagree in places, and the table shows that rather than picking one.

| Model | Bowl size (manufacturer spec) | Liquid limit and its source | Third-party leak or fill-line measurement | Seal or gasket noted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cuisinart Custom 14 DFP-14BCNY | 14 cups | ATK lists 3 cups; iFixit's DFP-14BCN wiki: 3 cups thin, 6 cups thick | TechGearLab: about 3¼ cups of water to the max fill line, no leak | Not found in sources read |
| Ninja Professional Plus BN601 | 9 cups (2.1 L) | No separate liquid figure; "do not fill container past the 9-Cup line" (Canadian BN600C owner's guide, manufacturer spec) | Not found | Not found in sources read |
| KitchenAid 13-Cup KFP1318 | 13 cups | ATK lists 9 cups | Not found | KitchenAid: some models have a red bowl seal (models not named) |
| Hamilton Beach 70730 | 10 cups | Do not fill above the marked maximum fill line (use and care guide, manufacturer spec; no cup figure) | TechGearLab: about 2½ cups reached the max fill line, then it leaked when the motor switched on | Not found in sources read |
| Breville Sous Chef 16 BFP810 | 16 cups | ATK lists 5 cups; the BFP810 book names a MAX LIQUID mark with no number in its text | Not found (TechGearLab tested the older BFP800) | Silicone seal on lid and mini bowl (BFP810 book) |

Two conflicts are worth knowing. For the Cuisinart, ATK's 3 cups and TechGearLab's roughly 3¼ cups to the line are close, but iFixit adds a 6-cup figure for thick liquids. We couldn't read the Cuisinart booklet itself, so go by the line on your bowl.

For Breville, the sibling [BFP820 manual](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/USCM/BFP820_USCM_IB_2020.pdf) gives two figures: its safeguards say not to fill above the 12-cup (2.8 L) liquid level, and its parts list says 10 cups "for diced and liquid (thick, wet ingredients e.g. soups)". Neither matches ATK's 5 cups for the BFP810. The BFP820 is a different model, so go by the MAX LIQUID mark on your bowl.

ATK doesn't say whether its liquid figures are manufacturer specs or its own measurements, so they are credited here as "ATK lists".

## Food processor leaking from the bottom: what to do

These steps come from the manufacturers' manuals, plus one from iFixit, credited:

1. **Turn it off and unplug it.** Hamilton Beach's [use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) says to turn the appliance off and unplug it before taking off parts, and never to immerse the cord, plug or base in any liquid.
2. **Remove the lid before the bowl.** Hamilton Beach says this avoids possible damage to the appliance.
3. **Lift the bowl and blade off together.** Per iFixit's student-written [Cuisinart DFP-14BCN wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DFP-14BCN_Troubleshooting), don't pull the metal blade first: when bowl and blade come off together, the blade drops and forms a liquid-tight seal against the bowl. Once the bowl is off the base, Ninja's guide says to remove the blade assembly, grasping the top of the shaft, before emptying the bowl.
4. **Wipe and dry the base.** Breville says to wipe the motor base with a soft, damp cloth and then dry it thoroughly. Ninja's [BN600C owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf) says not to submerge the motor base or control panel in water or other liquids, or spray them with any liquid.
5. **Watch for odor or smoke.** If a strong odor or any visible smoke appears, Hamilton Beach says to unplug the unit immediately and call its customer service number.

None of the manuals says how long to wait before running a base that got wet. If you're unsure, ask the manufacturer's service line. For washing the bowl and lid afterward, see [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

## How to process liquids without a leak

- **Dry or thick ingredients first, liquid after.** Breville's BFP810 book says to always add drier or thicker ingredients before fluids.
- **Start the motor, then add liquid slowly.** iFixit's Cuisinart wiki gives this tip so the other ingredients absorb the liquid.
- **Stay under your bowl's line.** Use the mark on the bowl, or the figure in your manual if the bowl isn't marked (KitchenAid).
- **Use the right blade.** KitchenAid says the Multipurpose Blade, not the Egg Whip.
- **Consider a blender for big batches of soup.** ATK prefers a blender or immersion blender for puréeing soup, saying they blend liquids smoother than food processors.

Hot soup is a separate question. Ninja's guide says not to blend hot liquids, Breville says not to process hot or boiling liquids and to cool them to room temperature first, and Hamilton Beach warns that hot liquid poured into the bowl can be ejected by sudden steaming. The details are in [food processor vs blender](/guides/food-processor-vs-blender/) and [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/).

## Which models leaked in TechGearLab's water test

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor) filled each bowl with water to the max fill line and ran it on full blast (page updated June 11, 2025). The Hamilton Beach 10-Cup (70730) reached its max fill line with about 2½ cups of water and, in its words, "promptly leaked water everywhere after the motor switched on". It reports zero leaks from the Breville, the Magimix 4200XL, the Cuisinart Custom 14 and the Hamilton Beach Professional 14-Cup. That Breville is the Sous Chef 16 Pro (BFP800), the predecessor of the BFP810, not the current model.

ATK calls a sealed workbowl "nice but not an essential feature". The evidence is thin: TechGearLab's is the only published water leak test we found, and no source tracks seal wear over time. For each model's full record, see the [Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY review](/reviews/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/) and the [Ninja BN601 review](/reviews/ninja-bn601/).

## When to replace a bowl or seal, and when to call the maker

A cracked bowl means a replacement bowl, per iFixit's Hamilton Beach wiki, and Hamilton Beach's guide says not to use the food processor if any part is broken. If a seal is missing or torn, get the replacement part from the manufacturer. Ninja's Canadian BN600C guide lists customer service at 1-877-646-5288. Hamilton Beach's troubleshooting table says not to attempt to repair the unit and to call its customer service number for the nearest authorized service center. If you want to rule out a safety notice on your model, see the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).
