The Cuisinart food processor recall is [CPSC recall 17-050](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) (December 13, 2016). Conair recalled the riveted chopping blades, not whole machines, in about 8 million Cuisinart processors sold July 1996 to December 2015. Covered model numbers start with CFP-9, CFP-11, DFP-7, DFP-11, DFP-14, KFP-7, MP-14 or one of the listed DLC and EV numbers, and only four-rivet blades count. Stop using that blade; Cuisinart replaces it free (877-339-2534). As of October 9, 2026, the CPSC database shows no newer Cuisinart food processor recall.

Is there a food processor recall in 2026? Not in the CPSC data. A [ProductName search for "food processor"](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=food%20processor) returns nothing after 17-050, and the newest entry in the [chopper search](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=chopper) is the April 2024 Mainstays recall.

The CPSC 17-050 page now shows a note reading "Consumer Contact Information updated. 8/11/2026." That is a contact update, not a new recall. Cuisinart's 2026 entries in the [CPSC data](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Cuisinart) are grill brushes and a gas grill, and its most recent kitchen-appliance recall before those (23-056, December 2022) was the CBJ-450 compact blender and juice extractor, not a food processor.

## How to check if your Cuisinart is part of the riveted blade recall, step by step

This check uses only what CPSC's 17-050 notice says.

1. **Unplug the processor and turn the base over.** Per CPSC, the model number is on the bottom of the food processor.
2. **Match the start of the model number to the 17-050 list.** If it doesn't start with one of these, the riveted blade recall doesn't cover it:

   | Prefix family | Model numbers beginning with |
   |---|---|
   | CFP | CFP-9, CFP-11 |
   | DFP | DFP-7, DFP-11, DFP-14 |
   | DLC | DLC-5, DLC-7, DLC-8, DLC-10, DLC-XP, DLC-2007, DLC-2009, DLC-2011, DLC-2014, DLC-3011, DLC-3014 |
   | EV | EV-7, EV-10, EV-11, EV-14 |
   | Other | KFP-7, MP-14 |

3. **Look at the S-shaped chopping blade, holding it by its plastic hub.** CPSC says recalled blades have four rivets, are silver-colored stainless steel and have a beige plastic center hub.
4. **Count the rivets.** A listed prefix plus a four-rivet blade means it's covered. CPSC: "Only food processors with four rivets in the blades are included in this recall." A blade with fewer or no rivets isn't part of the Cuisinart riveted blade recall.
5. **If you can't tell, call Cuisinart** at 877-339-2534 before using the blade again.

Consumer Reports' [2016 recall story](https://www.consumerreports.org/food-processors/cuisinart-food-processor-recall) counts 22 Cuisinart models in the recall and gives the same two-part check: model number, then four stainless steel rivets on the blade.

### Is the current Cuisinart Custom 14 (DFP-14BCNY) affected?

The prefix alone doesn't clear it, because DFP-14 is on the 17-050 list. What limits the recall is the sale window, which CPSC gives as July 1996 through December 2015. Consumer Reports wrote in 2016 that Cuisinart told it the Cuisinart models then in CR's ratings were newer and didn't contain the problem blade.

So a DFP-14BCNY bought new after 2015 falls outside the sale window. A used, inherited or long-stored unit still needs the rivet check in step 4. We haven't found a manual or photo source that confirms the rivet count on today's blade. Whether the Custom 14 is worth buying is covered in our [Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY review](/reviews/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/).

## What to do if your Cuisinart blade is recalled

CPSC's remedy for 17-050: "Consumers should immediately stop using the food processor's riveted blade and contact Cuisinart for a free replacement blade." The phone line is 877-339-2534, 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. ET Monday through Friday and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. ET Saturday and Sunday, per the CPSC notice.

Coverage from 2016, including Consumer Reports and the [NBC News wire story](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/8-million-cuisinart-food-processors-recalled-due-laceration-hazard-n695316), sent readers to a recall page on Cuisinart's website. When checked on October 9, 2026, cuisinart.com/recall returned a not-found error. The CPSC web page, updated August 11, 2026, now lists only the phone line. The saferproducts.gov record still shows the original text pointing to a "Product Recalls" link on cuisinart.com, so the two CPSC sources don't fully agree; the phone number is the route both list.

Only the chopping blade is recalled: CPSC names the "riveted blade," not the base or discs. Don't keep the old blade in service while you wait: CPSC says it "can crack over time" and shed small metal pieces into food, and Conair had 69 reports of blade pieces in food, including 30 mouth lacerations or tooth injuries. We found no reliable source on how long a replacement takes to arrive, so we won't guess.

## Cuisinart 14-029: the 2013 MFP-107 7-cup lid and disc recall

Per [CPSC 14-029](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=14029), about 25,000 MFP-107 7-cup processors were sold October 2012 through June 2013 for about $100. The nine variants are MFP-107, MFP-107BC, MFP-107BCWS, MFP-107BK, MFP-107BKWS, MFP-107DCWS, MFP-107MGSLT, MFP-107MR and MFP-107WS.

The reversible slicing and shredding disc can loosen, strike the cover and break it; one consumer was struck on the cheek, with no medical attention sought. The model number is on the underside of the base, and the remedy is a free replacement lid and disc.

## KitchenAid food processor recall: the only one is from 2000

The KitchenAid food processor recall on record is [CPSC 01-004](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=01004), from October 5, 2000. It covered about 220,000 5- and 7-cup processors sold as the Little Classic, Little Ultra Power and 7-Cup Ultra Power, sold April 1997 through August 2000 for $110 to $160. Model numbers begin KFP300, 4KFP300, RRKFP300, KFP350, 4KFP350, RRKFP350, KFP450 or RRKFP450. Serial numbers begin WJG, WJH, WJI, WJJ or WJK00 through WJK33; both numbers are on the bottom of the unit.

A cap on the blade unit could dislodge into food. KitchenAid estimated 30 incidents, two involving consumers starting to choke.

CPSC's remedy: stop using these models and contact KitchenAid for a free replacement blade unit. The notice lists KitchenAid at 866-444-3574, 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. ET Monday through Friday. That number dates from 2000, and we haven't confirmed it still reaches KitchenAid.

The CPSC database shows no other KitchenAid food processor recall, and the current 13-cup KFP1318 isn't on any list. A January 2023 Facebook claim that KitchenAid recalled mixer attachments for lead was [rated False by Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/), which found no evidence of any recall. That rumor was about stand mixer paddles and hooks, not food processors; our [stand mixer picks](/best/stand-mixers/) cover the mixers themselves.

## Other food processor and chopper recalls on the CPSC list

**GE 14-cup processor, model 169203 (Walmart, 11-227, 2011).** About 255,000 units. The safety interlock could fail and let it run without the lid secured, and it could smoke or catch fire. Walmart had 58 reports: 24 of running without the lid, including 21 fingertip injuries, and 34 of smoking, including 3 fires. Full refund at Walmart.

**Mainstays Electric Mini Chopper, MS14100094536S1 (Walmart, 24-178, 2024).** About 51,750 cordless choppers whose blade can run unexpectedly during assembly. Walmart had five laceration reports, two needing medical attention. Full refund.

**Ultimate Chopper (05-258, 2005)** had a lid interlock that could fail. **Hamilton Beach bébé baby food maker (14-193, 2014)** is a small baby food processor whose lid pin could loosen when the slicing and shredding disc was used with the motor shaft adapter; it isn't a full-size processor.

The CPSC Recall API shows no food processor recall for Ninja or SharkNinja, Breville, Magimix or Hamilton Beach full-size models. SharkNinja's recalls are other appliances: [16-034](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=16034) gave Ninja BL660 blenders new warnings and instructions, and [25-247](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=25247) was Foodi pressure cookers. ## Recall or just a worn blade?

A blade that isn't recalled can still chip or crack. Jim Nanni, then associate director of appliance testing at Consumer Reports, advised inspecting "any food preparation product and its parts before each use for signs of damage or cracking," and replacing the part or the whole appliance if it's damaged. Follow your manufacturer's manual for blade handling and replacement.

For blade types and what each does, see [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).
