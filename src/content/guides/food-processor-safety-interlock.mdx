A food processor safety lock (interlock) keeps the motor off until the bowl is locked to the base and the lid is locked to the bowl. Some models check more. [Breville's BFP810 manual](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf) says the large pusher must be in the chute, and [KitchenAid's 13-cup help page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Operation/Not_Turning_on%2F%2FMalfunction%2F%2FFail_to_Operate_-_Food_Processor) says the feed tube must click down. If one part sits slightly off, nothing happens.

That is deliberate, and it is the first thing Breville's and KitchenAid's troubleshooting checks when a motor won't start. Below is what each brand's lock checks, why a lid won't turn into place, and how to tell a lock that is working from one that has failed. Everything here comes from manufacturer manuals and support pages, CPSC recall records and one patent; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What a food processor safety lock checks, model by model

The lock is not the same on every machine. Breville's BFP810 manual describes a "feed chute safety system" that stops the motor unless the bowl and lid are locked and the large feed pusher is inserted. The small and medium pushers can come out while it runs; pull the large one and the motor stops.

KitchenAid's 13-cup page adds the feed tube and a fill limit. The [Hamilton Beach 70730 guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) mentions only the bowl and lid, and the [Ninja BN600C guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf) (the Canadian sibling of the BN601) signals a missing lock with flashing lights. Neither of those two manuals says the pusher is part of the interlock, so we don't claim it is.

## Food processor lid won't lock: the causes manufacturers list

If the lid won't turn into its locked position, work through these in order. Each comes from the source named.

1. **Start with the bowl.** The Hamilton Beach, Breville and Ninja manuals all have you turn the bowl clockwise until it locks or clicks before the lid goes on. Breville adds that the bowl handle should face the front.
2. **The blade isn't fully seated.** Ninja's BN600C guide says that "if the blade assembly is not fully seated, you will not be able to install and lock the lid in position." The guide also says the blade is not locked in the bowl and should be handled by the top of the shaft.
3. **A disc isn't fully seated on the shaft.** KitchenAid's [Work Bowl Cover Will Not Close](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/Other_Sizes/Operation/Assembly_or_Removal/Work_Bowl_Cover_Will_Not_Close_-_Food_Processor) page (for its other-size processors) says to push down and twist so it drops into place.
4. **The disc isn't on the drive adapter.** KitchenAid's [13-cup disc page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Operation/Work_Bowl_Cover_Won%E2%80%99t_Close_When_Using_the_Disc_-_Food_Processor) says to check it is positioned properly there.
5. **A disc is in the mini bowl.** The same KitchenAid page says mini bowls take only the mini multipurpose blade, not slicing or shredding discs.
6. **The lid seal is stiff.** That page also says some KitchenAid covers have a red leak seal that makes the lid harder to attach, and suggests a little olive oil on the lid, or removing the seal if you aren't processing liquids.
7. **The bowl is the wrong way round.** On two Philips models (HR7628/00 and HR7782/00), [Philips support](https://www.philips.com.au/c-t/XC000006079/why-does-the-lid-not-lock-into-position) says the lid only closes, and the processor only switches on, with the bowl handle on the right. That is specific to those models, but it shows how particular each design is.

If the lid still won't turn, check your model's manual rather than forcing it. Disc and blade types are covered in [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## Food processor won't work with the lid on: check the sequence

A processor that is fully assembled but won't start usually has one step in this order not quite done:

1. Bowl turned clockwise and locked to the base.
2. Lid aligned and locked: turned clockwise on the Hamilton Beach, Ninja and Breville (Breville: line up the ALIGN | LOCK graphics).
3. Pusher or feed tube seated: the large pusher on the Breville BFP810, the feed tube clicked down on the KitchenAid 13-cup.
4. Food in the chute below the max line.

The last step catches people out. KitchenAid says the 13-cup "will not start if food is loaded in the large Feed Tube above the max fill line." Breville marks its chute CHUTE FILL MAX and says an overfilled chute stops the pusher from triggering the safety catch. Breville's own troubleshooting for a motor that won't start checks the bowl, lid and chute fill first, then the cord and outlet.

If all four are right, the next things these troubleshooting sections check are power and overheating, which [food processor won't turn on](/guides/food-processor-wont-turn-on/) covers.

## Why a food processor needs the pusher in place

The pusher keeps hands out of the chute, and on some designs it is wired into the lock. Cuisinart's patent [US4216917](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4216917A/en), filed in 1978 and published in 1980, describes a motor switch that closes only when the cover is locked and the pusher is in the feed tube. Its stated purpose is preventing a user from reaching a hand into the hopper.

The patent notes that a very small hand could fit down the passage, and that the interlock allowed a larger feed tube for whole foods. That is design history, not a description of any current model.

Breville's BFP810 applies the same idea through its feed chute safety system. Where the pusher isn't in the lock, the manuals still require it. Hamilton Beach's safeguards say never to feed food by hand, always use the pushers, and use them to clear the chute; if that isn't possible, unplug and take the unit apart. Ninja's guide says never to add food by hand.

## Never bypass the food processor interlock

All three manuals on this page say the same thing in nearly the same words:

- Hamilton Beach, safeguard 18: "Do not attempt to defeat the lid interlock mechanism."
- Breville: "Never try to override or tamper with lid interlock mechanism."
- Ninja: "DO NOT attempt to defeat the interlock mechanism."

The warranty in Ninja's BN600C guide also excludes "any unit that has been tampered with." We give no workarounds. If the lock won't engage after the checks above, contact the manufacturer; Hamilton Beach's guide says not to attempt repairs yourself and to call its customer service line.

## How to tell if the safety lock has failed

A working lock stops the motor when a part is out of place. A failed one lets it run with the lid off. The [CPSC's 2005 Ultimate Chopper recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2005/CPSC-Ultimate-Chopper-LLC-Announce-Recall-to-Inspect-and-Replace-Food-Processors-) told owners to remove the lid and blade assembly, then see whether the unit runs. If it does, stop using it; that recall offered a free replacement unit.

Lock failures are on record in CPSC recalls:

- **GE 14-cup, model 169203 (CPSC 11-227, 2011):** about 255,000 units sold at Walmart. The CPSC says the [safety interlock system can fail](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2011/Walmart-Recalls-GE-Food-Processors-Due-to-Laceration-and-Fire-Hazard), with 24 reports of the unit running without the lid in place, 21 of them fingertip injuries.
- **Ultimate Chopper (CPSC 05-258, 2005):** about 1.5 million units; 17 injury reports from interlock failure or broken blade assemblies.
- **Mainstays mini chopper (CPSC 24-178, 2024):** not described as an interlock fault, but related: the CPSC says the [blade can operate unexpectedly](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Walmart-Recalls-Mainstays-Electric-Mini-Choppers-Due-to-Laceration-Hazard-Sold-Exclusively-at-Walmart) during assembly or when not enclosed in the container; 5 laceration reports.

When we checked the saferproducts.gov recall API on Oct 9, 2026, the only recall covering any of the six models in our hub was Cuisinart's CPSC 17-050, which concerns cracking riveted blades in older DFP-14 units, not the interlock. Details are in the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Keep the interlock working: storage and washing

Breville's BFP810 manual says never to store the processor with the pusher assembly in the locked position, to avoid a bowl switch malfunction. It also says to store the bowl and lid in position but not locked, because locking them strains the safety interlock. Hamilton Beach's guide also says not to lock the bowl and lid during storage.

Washing matters too. Breville's manual advises against immersing the bowl in water for long periods because of the interlock components. It says to use the dishwasher only occasionally, on the top shelf, because regular washing damages those components. Cleaning steps are in [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/), and part-by-part dishwasher rules are in [are food processor parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).
