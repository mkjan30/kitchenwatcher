The food processor vs blender split is simple. A food processor is for solid prep: chopping, slicing, shredding and dough. A blender is for liquids: smoothies, soups and thin sauces. A processor can stand in for a blender only on thick, cool purées like hummus, pesto or cooled soup. Of the processor manuals I read, Ninja's and Breville's forbid hot liquids, and Ninja's and Hamilton Beach's say not to crush ice.

Everything below comes from four owner's manuals and two independent reviewers, America's Test Kitchen (ATK) and TechGearLab. Nothing here was tried hands-on; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method. For model picks, see the [best food processors](/best/food-processors/) and [best blenders](/best/blenders/).

## Why a wide bowl and a narrow jar behave differently

The difference is the container. ATK's [2023 comparison](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7243-food-processor-vs-blender-do-you-need-both) explains that a blender jar is sealed and narrow, so food forms a vortex that keeps pulling it back into the blades. In a food processor, food travels around a much larger bowl and meets the blades less often, which ATK says gives a coarser purée.

Liquid also behaves badly in a processor bowl. ATK's [full-size food processor review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) (August 2026) says centrifugal force makes the liquid level climb once the motor starts, so a clear maximum-fill line on the bowl matters to avoid leaks.

Blade height matters too. ATK measured gaps as small as 2.9 mm between blade tips and bowl walls, and 2.2 mm to the bowl bottom, on the best models. The worst left 6.1 mm and 5.4 mm.

High blades matter for mayonnaise. ATK says the Ninja Professional XL (a different model from the BN601) couldn't emulsify it because its blades sat too high to reach the egg yolk, and the redesigned blade on the Cuisinart Custom 14 couldn't handle a single batch, only a double one. Blade types are covered in [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## How much liquid a food processor bowl actually holds

Bowl size is not liquid capacity. ATK lists a 3-cup liquid maximum for the 14-cup Cuisinart Custom 14, because liquid climbs the bowl walls while the motor runs.

## Can you put hot soup in a food processor?

It depends on the manual, and two of the three I read say no. Ninja's BN600C guide, warning 29: "DO NOT blend hot liquids," because pressure buildup and steam can burn the user. Breville's BFP810 book (page 4) says not to process hot or boiling liquids and to let them cool to room temperature before they go in the bowl. Its tips table (page 15) repeats that cooked food should cool before puréeing.

Hamilton Beach is softer. Safeguard 28 (p. 3) says to be careful pouring hot liquid into the bowl, because sudden steaming can eject it from the appliance. That is a caution, not a ban, and the same guide's processing chart lists "Puree: soups, sauces" for the chopping blade (p. 9).

So the manuals conflict: Hamilton Beach cautions, while Ninja and Breville prohibit. Follow the manual for your model. If you can't find it, let the soup cool before it goes in. I couldn't read Cuisinart's or KitchenAid's booklets, so this page makes no claim about them.

A blender may be the safer tool, but only if its manual says so. The Vitamix 5200 manual allows hot liquids with caution: lock the lid, never start above variable speed 1, and don't fill to maximum capacity (pp. 5, 11, 12). Not every blender manual agrees; [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/) compares them.

## Can a food processor crush ice?

Two of the three manuals say not to. Ninja's BN600C guide (warning 45) says not to load the bowl with ice cubes for crushing, to reduce the risk of bowl damage or the lid separating. Its manual-programs section adds that the unit "is NOT intended to crush ice."

Hamilton Beach (p. 9) lists crushing ice with grinding grain, coffee beans or spices, slicing frozen meat and kneading dough. Its guide says to avoid all of these "to maintain peak performance." Breville's BFP810 instruction book doesn't mention ice at all.

The sources disagree here. ATK's 2023 article "[7 Appliances a Food Processor Can Replace](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/641-7-appliances-a-food-processor-can-replace)" says to toss in cubes for crushed ice in seconds, without naming a blade or giving any caveat. The manual for your model wins over a general tip, so skip the ice in a Ninja or Hamilton Beach. Blender manuals also differ on ice; see [can a blender crush ice](/guides/can-a-blender-crush-ice/).

## Can you use a food processor as a blender?

Yes for thick, cool purées: hummus, pesto, nut butter, applesauce and cooled soup within the bowl's liquid limit. Expect a coarser result. ATK's full-size review says blenders give liquids a finer, smoother texture, and ATK's 2023 comparison calls processor purées coarser.

ATK's 2023 "7 appliances" article lists hummus, pesto, salsas, nut butters and puréed soups as processor jobs, though ATK's 2026 review prefers a blender or immersion blender for soup. The two ATK pages differ on soup.

For nut butter, TechGearLab timed each processor. The Ninja Professional (TechGearLab lists it as BN600/BN601) took 7 minutes, the Hamilton Beach 10-cup 10 minutes and the Cuisinart Custom 14 19 minutes. Motor size didn't line up with speed: the Hamilton Beach (450 W, per TechGearLab) finished well ahead of the Cuisinart (720 W, per ATK).

Thin drinks are the weak spot, because the small liquid limits above cap the batch. Ninja's guide also says not to run the bowl empty (warning 26) or grind dry ingredients with the chopping blade (warnings 30 and 32).

The reverse swap is weaker still. ATK says blenders can't make dough, and the Vitamix manual says its wet blades can knead but are "not quite as efficient" as its dry-blade container.

## Should you buy a food processor or a blender first?

ATK's 2023 verdict comes down to what you make most:

- **Mostly smoothies, soups and sauces:** a blender.
- **Mostly chopping, slicing, shredding and dough:** a food processor.
- **Liquids and solids equally often:** ATK says you need both.

One hybrid exists. TechGearLab reviewed a Vitamix 12-cup food processor attachment that runs on a Vitamix blender motor base, so it only makes sense if you already own one. If you only chop small amounts, compare [a mini chopper vs a food processor](/guides/food-chopper-vs-food-processor/).
