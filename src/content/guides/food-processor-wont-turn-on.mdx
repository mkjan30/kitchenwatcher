If your food processor won't turn on, check these in order:

1. The bowl and lid are fully locked.
2. Food in the feed chute sits below the MAX line, with the pusher all the way in.
3. The outlet works and the breaker hasn't tripped.
4. The motor isn't cooling after an overload. If it is, wait the brand's cool-down time.
5. Reset it: press Off/Pulse (KitchenAid) or unplug it.
6. If none of that works, call the manufacturer's service line.

KitchenAid's support page puts it plainly: "The most common reason for the food processor not to turn on is improper assembly" ([KitchenAid, Will Not Turn On](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/Other_Sizes/Operation/Will_Not_Turn_On_or_Shuts_Off/Will_Not_Turn_On_-_Food_Processor)). Everything below comes from four brands' manuals and support pages. For Cuisinart, the only readable source was iFixit's student-written wiki ([how we research](/how-we-research/)).

## Step 1: check the bowl, lid and pusher lock

If your food processor won't work with the lid on, check that the bowl, lid and pusher are each fully locked. Every manual below makes the interlock depend on it. Per-brand cues:

1. **Hamilton Beach 70730:** turn the bowl clockwise to lock it, then the lid. The [use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) (p.5) says the unit will not work unless the bowl is locked, and will not work unless the lid is locked.
2. **Ninja BN600C series:** turn the bowl, then the feed chute lid, clockwise until each clicks. Per the [owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf) (p.9 and p.11), the control panel stops flashing once both are seated.
3. **Breville Sous Chef 16 BFP810:** it runs only when the bowl, lid and large pusher are all assembled, with the bowl handle facing the front ([instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf), p.13 and p.19).
4. **KitchenAid 13-cup:** align and lock the bowl and lid, then push the feed tube all the way down until it clicks ([KitchenAid 13-cup support](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Operation/Not_Turning_on%2F%2FMalfunction%2F%2FFail_to_Operate_-_Food_Processor)).
5. **Cuisinart DFP-14:** the work-bowl handle goes at the front, the cover tab starts slightly left of center before you turn it, and the small pusher must be pressed down. This comes from iFixit's student-contributed [DFP-14BCN wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DFP-14BCN_Troubleshooting), not Cuisinart, because no copy of Cuisinart's booklet was readable.

How interlocks work, and what to do if the lid won't seat, is on the [food processor safety interlock](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/) page.

## Step 2: too much food in the feed chute

KitchenAid's 13-cup page says the processor won't start if food in the large feed tube is above the max fill line. Breville's book says the same for the BFP810: food in the chute must stay below the CHUTE FILL MAX line for the pusher to activate the safety catch (p.13, p.15, p.18). An overfilled chute is also the first thing it lists when INSERT PUSHER flashes (p.19). Take a few pieces out and push down again.

## Step 3: power, outlet and breaker

- Hamilton Beach (p.11): plug a working lamp into the same outlet. If it stays dark, you may have blown a fuse or tripped the breaker.
- KitchenAid: plug into a grounded outlet, unplug and re-plug the unit, then check the fuse or breaker. The Other Sizes page adds: try another outlet.
- Breville (p.18): if the motor still won't start, check the power cord and the outlet.

## What the flashing lights mean, by brand

### KitchenAid food processor pulse light flashing

KitchenAid's 13-cup [Pulse Light Flashing](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Operation/Pulse_Light_Flashing) page and 7-cup [Flashing Lights](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/7_Cup/Operation/Flashing_Lights_-_Food_Processor) page describe two patterns. Speed light off and Off/Pulse flashing means it overheated: wait until the Off/Pulse light turns off, which KitchenAid says may take 5 to 15 minutes. Speed light on and Off/Pulse flashing means the motor is locked: press Off/Pulse to reset. If your KitchenAid food processor is not working and neither light is on, go back to steps 1 to 3.

### Ninja food processor blinking lights

In the BN600C owner's guide (p.14), flashing lights on the control panel mean the bowl or feed chute lid isn't installed correctly. Reinstall the bowl, then the lid, until each clicks.

One caveat: that guide is for the Canadian BN600C series (850 W, per the guide), not the US BN601. Ninja's US troubleshooting page returned an error, so whether the BN601 behaves the same is unconfirmed. {/* VERIFY: confirm flashing-panel meaning and 15 min reset apply to the US BN601 */}

### Breville screen icons

INSERT PUSHER flashing: per the book (p.19), check that chute food is below the max line; if the pusher is fully in, check that the bowl and lid are assembled with the bowl handle facing the front. OVERLOAD PROTECTION flashing: press POWER to switch off, unplug and let it cool 30 minutes (p.14, p.19). Separately, continuous operation stops when the timer reaches 20 minutes (19:59) and the unit returns to standby mode (p.14), so a stop at that mark is the timer limit the book describes.

### Hamilton Beach: no lights

The 70730's use and care guide describes a control knob (OFF, 1, 2 and P) and no indicator lights.

## Step 4: it overheated, so how long to wait

The manufacturers' times don't agree, and Hamilton Beach's guide gives two numbers for two situations. After a motor overload, unplug and cool 15 minutes (p.6, p.11). If the unit "stops and will not come back on," turn it OFF, let it stand 3 to 4 minutes and turn it back ON. The [iFixit 70730 wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Hamilton_Beach_70730_Troubleshooting) repeats only the 3 to 4 minutes. For an overload stop, I'd follow the manual's 15.

For Cuisinart, iFixit's wiki says 5 minutes to 1 hour in one place and 5 to 10 minutes in another, and no Cuisinart figure was readable.

Ninja's reset (BN600C guide, p.13): unplug, cool about 15 minutes, remove the lid and blade, empty the bowl and make sure nothing jams the blade. Ninja calls exceeding maximum capacity the most typical cause of overload. Prevention is on the [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/) page.

## Motor hums or lights come on but the blade won't spin

- **Ninja:** after unplugging and cooling, take off the lid and blade, empty the bowl and make sure nothing jams the blade (BN600C guide, p.13).
- **Breville:** if the blades or disks stall, stop the machine and unplug it before clearing the wedged food. If it happens more than once, the book says the bowl may be overloaded; try smaller batches (p.14).
- **Cuisinart (iFixit, third party):** on the DFP-14BCN, a humming motor with a shaft that spins freely by hand points to the capacitor; a stiff shaft points to the drive shaft.
- **Hamilton Beach (iFixit, third party):** iFixit lists damaged switch-to-motor wiring, and blade and scraper fitted in the wrong order (blade first, then scraper).

Hamilton Beach's guide (p.11) covers a unit that stops and won't come back on after its suggestions: do not attempt to repair it. Call Customer Service for the nearest Authorized Service Center. None of these sources supports opening the base.

## Food buildup in the safety mechanism

Dried food in the lid or pusher slots can stop the interlock from engaging. iFixit's Cuisinart wiki (third party) says to unplug, take out the blade and discs, and soak the bowl and cover in warm soapy water. For heavy buildup, it says to soak them 30 minutes in half water and half vinegar, then dislodge food with a small brush, pipe cleaner or cloth-covered chopstick. Before soaking any part, check [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/) and [whether the parts are dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## When to stop and contact the manufacturer

Stop if it still won't start after the bowl, chute, power and cool-down checks.

- **Strong odor or visible smoke:** Hamilton Beach says to unplug immediately and call Customer Service; it says a slight odor during initial use, from manufacturing residue, will go away (p.11).
- **Ninja:** the BN600C guide lists Customer Service at 1-877-646-5288 and a one-year limited warranty (p.13, p.15).
- **Hamilton Beach:** the guide gives a one-year limited warranty for units bought and used in the US and Canada, and sends claims to Hamilton Beach's customer service website, not the store (p.12).
- **KitchenAid:** its support pages list customer service at 1-800-422-1230.
- **Older Cuisinart with a four-rivet blade:** check the [food processor recall](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/) page first.
