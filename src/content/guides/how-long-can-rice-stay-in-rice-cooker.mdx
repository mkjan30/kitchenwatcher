How long rice can stay in a rice cooker depends on your manual, because neither FDA nor FoodSafety.gov gives a maximum time for cooked rice kept hot. FoodSafety.gov says hot food kept for later must stay at 140 °F or above, and leftovers must be refrigerated within 2 hours. The Zojirushi NS-ZCC and NL-BAC models and the Hamilton Beach 37548 allow 12 hours of Keep Warm, the Zojirushi NHS-10/18 allows 5, and the NHS-06 has no Keep Warm. Rice in a cooker that is off or unplugged falls under the 2-hour rule.

## How long rice can stay in a rice cooker, by situation

- **Keep Warm on:** up to your manual's limit, as long as the rice stays at 140 °F or above ([FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety)). Zojirushi's [rice-cooker FAQ](https://store.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers) says 12 hours is the maximum for most of its models, but to check your manual.
- **Cooker switched off or unplugged:** 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90 °F, then refrigerate (FoodSafety.gov).
- **Overnight:** only on Keep Warm, and only if the night is shorter than the manual's limit. See the overnight section below.
- **Brown rice:** Zojirushi's [NHS manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nhs.pdf) says not to use Keep Warm at all, because brown rice "may become dry or spoil in few hours". The [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) says not to keep any food except white rice warm.
- **Small amount:** Zojirushi's [NL-BAC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf) says not to keep a small amount warm for long hours.

## Keep-warm limits in rice cooker manuals

Every limit below is a manufacturer spec unless another source is credited. The manuals frame their limits mostly as quality: drying, odor, discoloring. The NL-BAC manual does add that foul odors or bacterial growth may occur, depending on cleaning, room temperature and how often the lid is opened. Zojirushi's FAQ also says it can't guarantee freshness past the manual's limit.

### Zojirushi's 12-hour rule and Extended Keep Warm

The NS-ZCC manual is direct: "Do not keep rice warm for more than 12 hours." Its Extended Keep Warm mode, which you switch on with its own key during regular Keep Warm, holds rice at approximately 140 °F to reduce dryness, discoloration or odor. It can't be started once regular Keep Warm has run 12 hours, and after 8 hours it switches back to regular Keep Warm on its own.

[CNN Underscored](https://www.cnn.com/cnn-underscored/reviews/best-rice-cooker) reads it differently: it says the normal Keep Warm lasts 12 hours, after which Extended Keep Warm activates for another 8, and that both modes hold about 140 °F. The manual never adds the two together, says Extended can't start after 12 hours of regular Keep Warm, and its 12-hour line has no exception. The sources disagree, so I'd take the manual's 12 hours as the ceiling.

### Rice cookers with shorter or no Keep Warm

The Zojirushi NHS-10 and NHS-18 cap Keep Warm at five hours, "otherwise the rice may become hard and dry", per the NHS manual. The 3-cup NHS-06 has no Keep Warm, so its rice starts cooling as soon as cooking ends. Per [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-rice-cookers), the KitchenAid Grain and Rice Cooker's Keep Warm "maxes out at six hours". The Hamilton Beach 37548 counts up Warm time "for up to 12 hours" and then turns itself off, per its use and care guide. A forgotten pot then sits in an unheated cooker.

## Why 140 °F matters: Bacillus cereus and rice

FDA's [Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download) says the vomiting type of B. cereus illness "most often affects rice and other starchy foods". Symptoms start 30 minutes to 6 hours after eating and usually clear in about a day. The bacteria grow between 4 °C and 48 °C (about 39 to 118 °F), and at warm temperatures they can form spores that grow into more bacteria.

The part most pages skip is the toxin. "Cooking may kill the bacteria, but it might not disable the toxin that causes the vomiting type of illness," the FDA writes. That toxin, cereulide, is still stable after 121 °C for 30 minutes, so reheating may not undo hours spent warm but not hot.

[FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) calls 40 to 140 °F the danger zone, where germs grow rapidly. It says to keep food at 140 °F or above if you aren't serving it right away; below that, its 2-hour refrigeration rule applies.

## Is your rice cooker actually holding 140 °F?

The evidence is thin. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) says its winner, the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10, and its budget winner, the Toshiba 6-Cup (TRCS01), both kept rice at a food-safe temperature for 12 hours. It doesn't publish the temperatures. Its 12-hour test used medium-grain brown rice, which Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says not to keep warm at all. So the test shows heat holding, not what the manual approves.

[Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-rice-cookers) took readings on an Aroma 8-cup cooker: 200 °F when cooking finished, 170 °F at 30 minutes and 154 °F at 60 minutes. That's still above 140 °F, but falling, and it stops at the one-hour mark. I found no published measurement of any cooker at hour 8 or hour 12.

If you rely on Keep Warm for long holds, a food thermometer is the only way to know whether your cooker stays at FoodSafety.gov's 140 °F line.

## Can you leave rice in the rice cooker overnight?

Only with Keep Warm on, and only if the night fits your manual's limit. Rice cooked at 7 p.m. reaches Zojirushi's 12-hour line at 7 a.m. On an NHS-10/18 it hits the 5-hour limit around midnight, and a Hamilton Beach 37548 shuts itself off at the 12-hour mark.

If the cooker is off or unplugged, the answer is no. The rice cools into the 40 to 140 °F zone, and FoodSafety.gov's 2-hour rule applies. The NS-ZCC manual also says not to keep rice that has already cooled, and not to add cold rice to warm rice.

## How to store and reheat leftover rice

1. Refrigerate within 2 hours of cooking, or 1 hour if it's above 90 °F (FoodSafety.gov).
2. Spread it in shallow containers so it chills quickly, with the fridge at 40 °F or below (FoodSafety.gov).
3. Reheat leftovers to 165 °F, per FoodSafety.gov's [safe minimum temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).
4. Don't use a Zojirushi Reheat button on cold rice. The NS-ZCC manual says Reheat works only during Keep Warm, only on white rice, and shouldn't be repeated.

## Why manuals limit Keep Warm even when rice stays hot

The manuals give quality reasons. The NS-ZCC manual warns of bad odors, drying out or discoloring; the NL-BAC manual lists drying, wetness, odor and yellowing. It also says cancelling Keep Warm or unplugging can cause a foul odor, or condensation that makes rice sticky.
