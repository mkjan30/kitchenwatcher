How much water in a pressure cooker depends on the model and pot size. Instant's FAQ says to add thin, water-based liquid: 1 cup for a 3-quart pot, 1½ cups for 6 quarts, 2 cups for 8 quarts and 2½ cups for 10 quarts. Never fill past the PC MAX 2/3 line, or past the 1/2 line for rice, dried beans and dried vegetables. Other brands set different rules, so check your manual.

Ninja's Foodi FD401 guide says at least 1 cup. Breville's BPR700 sets a 1-quart minimum for food and liquid combined. Everything below comes from the manufacturers' manuals and FAQs, and the CPSC, credited where used. We haven't cooked in these pots; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What counts as liquid: the minimum liquid in an Instant Pot

The minimum means thin, water-based liquid. Instant's Rio Plus Wide manual lists broth, stock, soup or juice. It also says that if you use canned, condensed or cream-based soup, you should still add water as directed in its minimum table.

Thick sauces don't count on their own. Instant's FAQ says "thicker sauces will require an additional cup of thin liquid."

Oil doesn't count either. The [Instant Pot Singapore help page](https://help.focusglobalinc.com/IPSG/IPSG_Cooking/358), run by Focus Global Inc., says oils and oil-based sauces "do not have enough water content" to count toward the minimum. Cosori's manual says to avoid recipes with more than ¼ cup (60 mL) of oil or fat.

What about juices released by meat or vegetables? None of the manuals we read counts them toward the minimum. Until a manufacturer says otherwise, add the full minimum of thin liquid yourself.

## Why a pressure cooker needs liquid, and what happens without enough

"Pressure cooking requires liquid in the inner cooking pot to produce the steam needed," says Instant's Rio Plus Wide manual. Too little liquid shows up as pressure and burn errors in Instant's manual and FAQ.

The manual's troubleshooting and error tables name too little liquid as a cause of three problems. They are steam gushing from the vent while set to Seal, C5 (no water in the inner pot) and C7 or NoPr. The C5 and C7 rows repeat the minimums: 1 cup for 3 qt, 1½ cups for 6 qt, 2 cups for 8 qt. Its Food burn row blames high temperature at the pot bottom, such as starch deposits, rather than liquid. Instant's FAQ is the source that lists not enough liquid as a cause of the burn error.

Other brands are blunter. Cosori says running without liquid "will seriously damage the cooker." Breville says to fill at least to MIN "to avoid thermal damage and fire."

If you already see a Burn message, too little liquid is one cause Instant's FAQ lists; our [Instant Pot burn message guide](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/) covers the rest. For C5, C7 and NoPr, see [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/), and for steam leaks, [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/).

## Instant Pot max fill line: PC MAX 2/3 and the 1/2 line

Instant and Cosori both mark a PC MAX 2/3 line, and the CPSC's general advice is to keep the inner pot no more than two-thirds full. The rules from the manuals:

- **Instant:** don't fill above PC MAX 2/3, per Instant's FAQ and Rio Plus Wide manual.
- **Instant, foods that expand:** don't fill above the 1/2 line for rice, dried beans and dried vegetables, per the Rio Plus Wide manual.
- **Instant, foods that foam:** applesauce, cranberries, pearl barley, oatmeal or other cereals, split peas, noodles, macaroni, rhubarb and spaghetti "can foam, froth, sputter, and clog" the steam vent, per the Rio Plus Wide manual, so stay at 1/2.
- **Cosori CMC-CO601:** the same PC MAX 2/3 line, and 1/2 for rice, dehydrated vegetables and foods like applesauce.
- **Ninja FD401:** don't fill past PRESSURE MAX; no more than halfway for dried vegetables, beans, grains and rice.
- **Breville BPR700:** MAX at 4.5 quarts and ½ at 3 quarts (for foods that expand) on its 6-quart bowl. It goes further on foaming foods such as applesauce, oatmeal and split peas: they "should not be cooked in a pressure cooker."

Breville's MAX line is 4.5 of 6 quarts, three-quarters of the bowl, not two-thirds. So "2/3 full" is a common rule, not a universal one: use the lines printed in your own pot.

Instant's FAQ also says the advertised quart size is the inner pot's complete volume, and cooking capacity is lower. That matters if you're [choosing an Instant Pot size](/guides/what-size-instant-pot/). For rice ratios and times, see [how to cook rice in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/).

## Why overfilling is dangerous: the Insignia pressure cooker recall

The clearest evidence comes from CPSC recall [24-013](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Best-Buy-Recalls-Insignia-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard). Best Buy recalled Insignia multi-function pressure cookers NS-MC60SS8, NS-MC60SS9 and NS-MC80SS9, plus replacement inner pots NS-MCRP6NS9 and NS-MCRP6SS9. The CPSC says the inner pots had incorrect volume markings, which can cause owners to overfill the pot. Hot food and liquid can then be ejected on quick release or when the cooker is opened under pressure.

Best Buy received 31 reports of contents expelled under pressure, including 17 reports of burn injuries, some second-degree and severe, per the CPSC record. The remedy was a free replacement inner pot and floating locking valve. The CPSC's general rule: check that the inner pot "is not filled beyond two-thirds capacity," that the lid is fully locked, and that the floating locking valve has dropped before opening the lid.

Instant's Rio Plus Wide manual explains the mechanism: overfilling "may cause a risk of clogging the steam release pipe and developing excess pressure." For other recalls, see [are pressure cookers safe?](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/) and our [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/).

## Where Instant's own sources and other guides disagree

Instant's sources don't all agree. Its US FAQ says 1 cup for a 3-quart pot and 1½ cups for 6 quarts. The Instant Pot Singapore help page says "at least 1.5 – 2.5 cups (355 – 473 ml)" without tying the figure to pot size. Its metric figures don't match its cups either: 2.5 cups is about 590 mL. It also lists cooking sauces among the liquids that count, where the US FAQ says thicker sauces need an extra cup of thin liquid.

Breville counts food and liquid together toward its 1-quart minimum, rather than setting a liquid-only figure. That's a different kind of rule, not just a different number.

Some cooking sites give figures lower than Instant's own for larger pots. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1341751/limit-liquid-pressure-cook-cut-meat) says a pressure cooker needs 1 cup of liquid, "no more, no less," and [CorrieCooks](https://www.corriecooks.com/pressure-cooker-need-water-every-time-everything-cook/) says 1 cup for a 6-quart Instant Pot, where Instant's FAQ says 1½ cups. Neither cites a manufacturer. Follow the manual for your model.

## Quick checklist before you seal the lid

- Thin, water-based liquid at or above your manual's minimum (table above).
- Thick sauce? Add an extra cup of thin liquid, per Instant's FAQ. Condensed or cream soup? Still add the minimum water, per the Rio Plus Wide manual.
- Oil doesn't count toward the minimum.
- Food and liquid together below PC MAX 2/3, or your pot's MAX line.
- Rice, beans, dried vegetables and foaming foods below the 1/2 line.
- After sautéing, deglaze the pot before pressure cooking, per Instant's FAQ.
