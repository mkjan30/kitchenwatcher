To clean a food processor, switch it off and unplug it, then take off the lid, lift out the blade or disc by its hub, shaft or finger holes (never the edge), and remove the bowl. Wash the parts in warm soapy water with a soft sponge and no abrasive pads. Wipe the motor base with a damp cloth and never immerse it. Dry everything before storing. The Hamilton Beach, KitchenAid, Ninja and Breville instructions all follow that outline.

This is a research page. It's built from those four brands' manuals and support pages, plus TechGearLab's cleaning scores and America's Test Kitchen's cleanup notes. Nothing here comes from hands-on use; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Before you start: unplug and take it apart in the right order

Every brand starts the same way: off and unplugged. Hamilton Beach and Ninja also set an order for removing parts.

1. **Switch off, then unplug.** Hamilton Beach's [70730 use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) says to turn the knob to OFF, then unplug, before taking off parts or cleaning. KitchenAid's [13-cup cleaning page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Cleaning_and_Care/How_to_Clean_the_Base_and_Attachments_-_Food_Processor) says press Off/Pulse, then unplug. Ninja's [BN600C owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf) says turn off and unplug the motor base.
2. **Take the lid off before the bowl.** Hamilton Beach says to always remove the lid before the bowl to avoid damaging the appliance.
3. **Lift the blade or disc out.** Hamilton Beach says to carefully remove the blade from the motor shaft. Ninja says to lift its disc out using the finger holes and to grasp the chopping blade by the top of its shaft.
4. **Remove the bowl and separate the rest.** Ninja's cleaning section starts with "Separate all parts."

## How to clean food processor blades safely

The manuals are most specific about the blade:

1. **Hold blades by the hub, never the edge.** Hamilton Beach's safeguards say to insert and remove the blade and disc using the hubs, and to use caution when emptying and cleaning.
2. **Don't wipe the edge with your fingers.** Breville's [BFP810 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf) calls the blades and discs razor sharp and says to clear food with a spatula or the included cleaning brush. Ninja says to use a dishwashing utensil with a handle so your hand doesn't touch the blade.
3. **Don't leave blades in a full sink.** [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/how-to-clean-a-food-processor) says never to leave blades soaking in the sink, where someone can reach in and get cut, and [Homes & Gardens](https://www.homesandgardens.com/kitchens/how-to-clean-a-food-processor) says to keep them out of the sink and wash them one by one. No manual read here says it in those words.
4. **Brush the hollow shaft.** TechRadar recommends a bottle brush for a hole up the blade's center column and a long-handled brush around the edges, and suggests rubber gloves for a better grip on wet blades.
5. **Don't sharpen it.** Ninja's guide says not to attempt to sharpen blades.
6. **Consider hand washing the blade.** [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) says not to put the blade through the dishwasher because it can get dull. That differs from Hamilton Beach, KitchenAid, Ninja and Breville, which all allow dishwasher washing of blades.

If you own an older Cuisinart, look at the blade while it's in your hand. The [CPSC recall 17-050](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) says "Only food processors with four rivets in the blades are included in this recall." The CPSC remedy is to stop using the riveted blade and contact Cuisinart for a free replacement. Our [recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/) covers the model prefixes. For which blade does what, see [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## Bowl, lid and pusher: hand wash vs a quick soapy-water pulse

Breville, Ninja and Hamilton Beach all say the bowl and lid can be washed in warm or hot soapy water (Hamilton Beach and Breville extend that to every removable part). Breville specifies a mild liquid detergent and a non-abrasive sponge, and says to rinse the bowl and lid as soon as possible after use. KitchenAid warns that abrasive cleansers or scouring pads may scratch or cloud the work bowl and cover.

Some guides suggest a shortcut: refit the bowl and blade, add warm water and a few drops of dish soap, and run it. Homes & Gardens says to keep it to a few drops or it foams over. [eHow](https://www.ehow.com/13780297/how-to-clean-a-food-processor-step-by-step-guide) says to fill the bowl about a third full and run it on high for about 30 seconds, or pulse it 5 to 10 times.

TechRadar, by contrast, says food processors have no auto-clean program. No manual read for this page describes the pulse method, so treat it as a pre-rinse, not a wash.

On dishwashers: Hamilton Beach, KitchenAid and Ninja allow the top rack, while Breville says the bowl and lid should go in only occasionally. The details are in [are food processor parts dishwasher safe?](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/)

## How to clean a food processor base

Every brand says never immerse the motor base. The rest varies a little.

- **Hamilton Beach:** disconnect power first, then wipe the base, control knob and cord with a damp cloth or sponge, with a mild nonabrasive cleanser if needed. Don't immerse the cord, plug or base.
- **KitchenAid 13-cup:** wipe the base and cord with a warm damp cloth, then dry with a soft cloth. No abrasive cleaners or scouring pads. The [corded chopper page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Chopper/Corded/Cleaning_and_Maintenance/How_to_Clean_Corded_Food_Chopper) gives the same damp-cloth, no-immersion rule.
- **Ninja BN600C:** a clean damp cloth, with no abrasive cloths, pads or brushes. Ninja also says not to spray the motor base or control panel with any liquid.
- **Breville BFP810:** a soft damp cloth, dry it thoroughly, and wipe food off the cord. No abrasive scouring pads or cleansers.

TechRadar adds: pay attention to the top of the base where the blade system attaches and the area around the buttons, then dry it.

## Food processor smells like garlic (or is stained by carrots)

Of the manuals read for this page, only Breville's gives an odor and stain procedure. It says garlic, fish and carrots may odor or stain the bowl or lid. Its fix:

1. Fill the bowl with warm soapy water to the MAX LIQUID mark.
2. Soak for 5 minutes, without immersing the whole bowl.
3. Wash, rinse and dry.
4. Store it with the lid off.

Breville also warns that oils in citrus rind can damage the bowl, so wash it right after processing rind. [Philips](https://www.philips.ie/c-f/XC000003610/how-can-i-get-rid-of-the-smell-on-my-philips-chopper), writing about its choppers, gives similar advice: wash the bowl and knife with hot water and detergent straight after use, then store without closing the lid.

TechRadar recommends a 1:1 baking soda and water paste left for about 20 minutes. Homes & Gardens suggests an overnight soak in warm soapy water, then baking soda, with baking soda or vinegar for a cloudy bowl. None of the manufacturer sources opened for this page endorses baking soda or vinegar. Breville says not to immerse the bowl for long periods because of its interlock parts, and KitchenAid says abrasives can cloud the bowl, so a gritty paste or an overnight soak may go against those two.

A burning smell from the motor is a different problem; see [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).

## Dry and store it so it doesn't come back smelly

Ninja says to thoroughly rinse and air-dry all parts, and TechRadar warns that putting a food processor away damp can lead to mold and bacteria growth. Storage advice differs by brand:

- **Lid unlocked:** Breville says store the bowl and lid in position but not locked, because locking can put unnecessary strain on the safety interlock. Hamilton Beach says not to lock the bowl and lid in storage.
- **Lid locked:** Ninja says to keep the blade assemblies inside or attached to the bowl with the lid locked. That's a direct conflict with Breville and Hamilton Beach, so follow your own model's manual.
- **Blades and discs:** KitchenAid's 13-cup caddy holds up to two blades, three discs and the drive adapter inside the bowl. Hamilton Beach stores the disc on the motor shaft, slicing side down. Breville says the opposite: never store a blade or disc on the motor shaft, keep accessories in the storage container, and don't keep discs in a drawer with other utensils.

## Which models are easiest to clean, per independent testing

TechGearLab rated the Breville Sous Chef 16 Pro (BFP800) easiest to clean of its group, just ahead of the Magimix, crediting a longer blade shaft and very few nooks in the bowl and lid. It called the Ninja Professional "difficult to clean by hand," with the dual-level blade the harder part. [America's Test Kitchen's August 2026 review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) says the newer Sous Chef 16 (BFP810) has a lid with nooks and crannies that need detailing as you clean, and multi-part blades that are a bit annoying to assemble.
