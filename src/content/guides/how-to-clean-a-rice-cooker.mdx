To clean a rice cooker, unplug it and let it cool. After every use, wash the inner pot, the removable inner lid and the steam cap in warm soapy water with a soft sponge, and empty the dew or condensation collector. Wipe the heating plate and center sensor with a wrung-out damp cloth. Check your manual before using the dishwasher, vinegar or bleach, because brands disagree.

That routine follows [America's Test Kitchen's cleaning guide](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7279-how-to-clean-rice-cooker) and Zojirushi's [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf). Below, I put it next to the manuals for the other cookers on our [rice cooker hub](/best/rice-cookers/), because the brands don't agree.

## How to clean a rice cooker after every use

1. **Unplug it and let it cool.** Every manual I read starts here, as do ATK and the Panasonic and Tiger guides.
2. **Wash the inner pot.** Use warm water, dish soap and a soft sponge. Don't leave it soaking: Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says the top of the pan "may rust if soaked in water too long", and ATK says the same. [Panasonic's support page](https://help.na.panasonic.com/answers/how-to-clean-and-maintain-the-rice-cooker/) allows soaking for a few minutes if food is stuck.
3. **Wash the inner lid and steam cap.** On the NS-ZCC, the inner lid comes off but its gasket can't be removed. Cuckoo's [CR-0655F manual](https://cuckooamerica.com/cdn/shop/files/CR-0655F_User_Manual.pdf) says to pull the steam cap off after every use, separate it into 2 parts, soak them in lukewarm water and wash them.
4. **Empty the dew collector.** Cuckoo says rice water left in the dew dish can cause a bad smell.
5. **Wipe the body, never immerse it.** Use a well-wrung damp cloth. Hamilton Beach's [37548 manual](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840264200.pdf) and Panasonic both say never to put the base in water.
6. **Dry everything before reassembling.** Tiger and Panasonic both say every part should be fully dry first.

## How to clean the rice cooker heating plate

Wipe off stray grains and starchy water right after cooking. ATK says leftover bits can burn onto the plate and stop it working correctly. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says to wipe the plate and center sensor with a cloth soaked in lukewarm water and wrung well, and to lift stuck grains with a bamboo spatula or chopstick.

If grains or other bits are stuck to the plate, Zojirushi says to polish them off lightly with sandpaper of about #320 dipped in water, then wipe with a damp cloth. It also says never to use sandpaper on anything other than the heating plate or center sensor. ATK recommends the same method for burned-on bits on any metal plate and sensor, but says to skip sandpaper on ceramic ones.

The sandpaper step is Zojirushi's instruction, not a universal one. Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual says to wipe debris and moisture from the heating (thermal) plate and to remove anything stuck to the temperature sensor without damaging its metal plate; it gives no sandpaper instruction. A dirty plate is one cause of scorching; the others are in [why a rice cooker burns rice on the bottom](/guides/rice-cooker-burning-rice-bottom/).

## Is the rice cooker pot dishwasher safe?

It depends on the brand. Zojirushi's rice cooker FAQ (Q5) says "rice cooker parts and accessories are not dishwasher safe". Its [NL-BAC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf) says no dishwasher or dish-dryer, because either may cause scratches, deformation or discoloration. The NS-ZCC manual's line "Do not use the Inner Pan for dishwashing" reads more like a ban on washing dishes in the pan, so I rely on the FAQ for the dishwasher answer.

Hamilton Beach goes the other way. Its 37548 manual marks parts DISHWASHER-SAFE but says not to use the SANI setting, whose temperatures could damage the product. The same page says the lid liner may be removed to wash by hand. {/* VERIFY: which numbered 37548 parts are dishwasher-safe; research log says pot, basket and lid liner, but the manual text says the lid liner washes by hand */}

Panasonic's support page says removable parts are "usually" dishwasher safe and to check the Operating Manual. Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual says to wash the pot with dishwashing detergent and a soft cloth or sponge, and its cleaning section never mentions a dishwasher. It's silent, so I won't guess. Look it up in your model's manual, and hand-wash if it says nothing.

## Why does my rice cooker smell, and how do I get rid of it?

The manuals name these causes:

- Rice water left in the dew dish (Cuckoo CR-0655F).
- "Negligent cleaning", especially of the lid, which Cuckoo says can cause a bad smell during keep warm.
- Cooking rice after a steamed dish, which Cuckoo says can leave its smell in the rice.
- Keeping rice warm past 12 hours, keeping anything but white rice warm, adding cold rice, or leaving the spatula in (Zojirushi NS-ZCC).
- How well the unit is cleaned, room temperature and frequent lid opening (Zojirushi NL-BAC).

Zojirushi's NL-BAC procedure, "IF THERE IS AN ODOR":

1. Fill the pan with water to WHITE water level 1. Add nothing else, such as detergent.
2. Close the lid, select QUICK and start it.
3. When it beeps and switches to Keep Warm, press Keep Warm/Cancel.
4. Let it cool, discard the water, and dry the cooker with the lid open in a ventilated place.

Cuckoo's CR-0655F has Auto Clean, which its manual calls "Automatic Sterilization (Steam Cleaning)". Add water to scale 2, close and lock the lid, select Auto Clean from Menu (or press Timer/Auto clean twice in standby), then press Cook/Turbo. For strong odors, the same manual adds a sterilizing boil: water to the 2-person plain-rice line plus a big spoonful of ginger, Cook/Turbo, Cancel 30 to 40 minutes after boiling, then clean the pot.

Cuckoo's blog suggests vinegar or lemon water and a short steam cycle instead. That conflicts with Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual, which says "Do not use vinegar" in the pan, with the NL-BAC manual's "Do not pour vinegar into the Inner Cooking Pan", and with ATK's advice to avoid vinegar on nonstick. Follow your own brand's manual, and don't put vinegar in a Zojirushi pan. For how long rice can safely sit on keep warm, see [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Which cleaners and tools do the manuals forbid?

- **Zojirushi NS-ZCC:** no thinner, benzene, bleach, abrasive cleansers or brushes.
- **Zojirushi NL-BAC:** no melamine sponges, sponges with a nylon surface, or metal or nylon scrubbing brushes.
- **Cuckoo CR-0655F:** no sharp cutlery or metal spoons in the pot, no rough sponge, brush or abrasive, and no benzene or thinner.
- **Tiger:** never the abrasive side of a sponge, don't wash dishes in the pot, and don't dry it upside down.

One conflict stands out. Tiger's guide for its JAX-T series soaks removable parts for 2 to 3 hours in 2 Tbsp chlorine bleach, 2 Tbsp dish soap and hot water. Zojirushi forbids bleach and long soaks. Treat Tiger's soak as Tiger's model-specific instruction, not general advice.

## Is a stained or discolored inner pot normal?

Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says the nonstick coating may discolor after long use without affecting its nonstick quality or sanitary properties. It says to contact service if the inner pan or inner lid is deformed or corroded, and to replace the inner lid or gasket if steam leaks between the lid and the body.

Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual says a starch film on the lid or pot is harmless and some stains don't affect hygiene. It calls a light yellow layer of rice on the bottom "melanoizing" and says it isn't a malfunction. For peeling or scratched coating, see [rice cooker inner pot coating peeling](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/); for safety recalls, see the [rice cooker recall check](/guides/rice-cooker-recall-check/).
