To clean an Instant Pot, unplug it and let every part cool to room temperature first. The inner pot, the lid and its parts, the steam rack and the condensation collector can be hand-washed in hot soapy water or put in the dishwasher, with the lid and accessories on the top rack, per Instant's [Rio Plus Wide](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) and [Rio Wide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf) manuals and its [FAQ](https://instantpot.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions). The cooker base only gets a barely damp cloth. Never immerse it, and never rinse it under a faucet.

We compiled this from Instant's own manuals and FAQ plus independent reviewers' cleaning notes. We did not clean a unit ourselves. [How we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

## Which Instant Pot parts are dishwasher safe?

The inner pot, lid, lid parts, sealing ring, steam rack and condensation collector are all dishwasher safe; the base is not. This table comes only from the Cleaning tables in the two Rio manuals and Instant's FAQ.

| Part | Dishwasher? (per Instant manual) | How to clean it (per Instant manual/FAQ) | How often | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inner cooking pot | Yes (no rack given) | Hot water and mild dish soap; vinegar-dampened sponge for hard-water stains; soak burned residue in hot water for a few hours | After each use | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals; FAQ |
| Lid and lid parts (anti-block shield, sealing ring, steam release valve or vent, silicone cap; the Rio Wide's table also lists the float valve) | Yes, top rack | Remove small parts first; clean inside the steam release pipe; drain the lid by turning it vertically over a sink | After each use (both manuals: clean the cooker after each use) | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals; FAQ |
| Sealing ring | Yes, top rack | Hot soapy water; for odor, 1 cup water plus 1 cup white vinegar, Pressure Cook 5 to 10 minutes, Quick Release | After each use | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals |
| Steam rack | Yes, top rack | Hot water and mild dish soap; no harsh detergents, powders or scouring pads | After each use | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals; FAQ |
| Condensation collector | Yes, top rack | Empty and rinse | After each use | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals |
| Cooker base and control panel | No, never immerse | Barely damp cloth inside, on the condensation rim and outside; air dry | After each use (general rule in both manuals) | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals; FAQ |
| Power cord | No | Barely damp cloth to wipe off particles; the Rio Wide manual says to disconnect it from power first | After each use (general rule in both manuals) | Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals |

Both manuals carry the same footnote on the inner pot: some discoloration may occur after machine washing, but it does not affect the cooker's safety or performance. Part lists differ by model. The Rio Plus Wide manual's Cleaning table lists a steam release vent and a steam release cover, while the Rio Wide's lists a steam release valve and a float valve, so check the manual for your own model.

No Instant source we read says which rack the inner pot goes on. [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/features/how-to-clean-an-instant-pot) puts it upside down on the lower rack. That is TechRadar's practice, not Instant's instruction.

## Before you start: unplug it, let it cool, take it apart

The Rio Plus Wide manual's safeguards and Cleaning section set four rules before any cleaning, and the Rio Wide manual repeats them:

1. Unplug the cooker, and let the appliance cool to room temperature.
2. Never immerse the power cord, plug or appliance in water or other liquid.
3. Never clean the appliance by rinsing it under a faucet.
4. Make sure every surface is dry before you use or store it.

Then lift out the inner pot and steam rack, pull off the condensation collector and take the small parts off the lid.

## How to clean the Instant Pot lid

These steps follow the lid sections of the Rio Plus Wide manual, with the Rio Wide manual's Cleaning table where it agrees.

1. **Remove the sealing ring.** Grip its edge and pull it out from behind the sealing ring rack. Check that the rack is secured, centered and an even height all the way around. If the rack is deformed, the manual says not to try to repair it.
2. **Remove the anti-block shield.** Grip the lid like a steering wheel and press your thumbs against the side of the shield, toward the side of the lid and up, until it pops off the prongs. The manual calls the shield an integral part of product safety, says to clean it frequently and says never to operate the cooker without it.
3. **Clean the steam release pipe.** With the steam release valve and anti-block shield removed, clean the inside of the pipe so it doesn't clog.
4. **Check the float valve and silicone cap.** The Rio Wide manual lists both among the lid parts to remove and wash. Instant's FAQ says to clean food debris off a float valve that stays popped up, and to replace a damaged or missing silicone cap before pressure cooking. If the valve sticks, see [why an Instant Pot float valve won't go up or down](/guides/instant-pot-float-valve/).
5. **Wash, drain and dry.** Hand-wash with hot water and mild dish soap, or use the dishwasher's top rack. Drain it by holding the lid vertically over a sink and turning it all the way around. Instant's FAQ says a submerged lid can trap moisture inside and must dry completely before you cook again.
6. **Reassemble and store.** Press the sealing ring onto its rack firmly with no puckering. It should not fall out when the lid is turned over. Store the lid upside down on the cooker base.

Both Instant manuals tell you to remove the steam release valve to clean the pipe; check the manual for your model before pulling parts off. If the lid leaks after you put it back together, see [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/).

## How to clean the sealing ring

Wash the ring after each use, by hand or on the top rack, and store spare rings somewhere well ventilated to disperse odor, per both Rio manuals. For odor, the manual's cycle is 1 cup of water and 1 cup of white vinegar in the inner pot, Pressure Cook for 5 to 10 minutes, then Quick Release. The Rio Plus Wide manual and Instant's FAQ suggest one ring for sweet dishes and one for savory, and replacing rings every 12 to 18 months. The full odor guide is [Instant Pot sealing ring smell](/guides/instant-pot-sealing-ring-smell/).

## How to clean the inner pot: burned food, hard water and rainbow stains

**Burned food.** Both manuals say to soak tough or burned residue in hot water for a few hours before cleaning. To stop it happening, see [the Instant Pot burn message](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/).

**Hard-water marks.** The manuals say these may need a vinegar-dampened sponge and some scrubbing.

**Rainbow or blue stains.** Instant's FAQ calls this rainbowing and says it can come from high heat, starchy foods such as rice and pasta, or detergent after hand or machine washing. It says this is common and normal in stainless steel cookware. Both manuals say discoloration after machine washing does not affect safety or performance.

### What other guides suggest (not in Instant's manuals)

- [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/how-to/how-to-clean-an-instant-pot) (Dec 2022) tips a cup of white vinegar around the pot so it covers the sides, lets it sit for 5 minutes, and suggests Bar Keepers Friend for marks that remain.
- [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/features/how-to-clean-an-instant-pot) (Aug 2022) soaks a pot with a blue-ish haze from the dishwasher in white vinegar for a few minutes. For burned food, it pressure cooks 2 tablespoons of baking soda with 4 cups of water for 5 minutes, then lets the pressure release naturally.

Neither method appears in the Instant manuals we read. The manuals also warn against harsh chemical detergents, powders and scouring pads on accessories.

Independent reviewers single out the stainless pot as the hard part. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) (May 2025) found that the stainless pots of the Duo Crisp 6.5-quart, Duo Evo Plus and Duo 6- and 8-quart needed some extra scrubbing, though for the Duo Evo Plus it said this "wasn't a huge issue." [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker) lists "Pot can be hard to clean" as a con of the Duo Plus V4, especially after sautéing. Our [Duo Plus review](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/) covers the rest of its findings.

## How to clean the base, rim groove and condensation collector

**Base.** Wipe the inside of the base and the condensation rim with a barely damp cloth and let them air dry. Wipe the outside and control panel with a soft, barely damp cloth or sponge (both Rio manuals and the FAQ). Never put liquid or food in the base without the inner pot.

**Rim groove.** TechGearLab found a narrow groove where the lid seals on every pressure cooker it rated. Crumbs and liquid collect there, and the groove is too narrow to reach easily, which is why none came close to a perfect cleaning score.

TechRadar uses wadded-up kitchen towel with cleaning spray, and says some owners use a soft toothbrush dipped in hot soapy water. These are TechRadar's tips, not Instant's. Put any liquid on the cloth or brush, never into the base.

**Condensation collector.** Empty and rinse it after each use. Pull it away from the base, not down (Rio Plus Wide manual).

## If liquid spills into the base or onto the heating element

Instant's FAQ covers this case directly:

- **Water-based spill:** once the element is completely cool, wipe the inside of the base and the heating element with a dry cloth. Turn the cooker upside down on a flat surface and let it air dry for 72 hours, then run the [water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/).
- **Oil or fat spill:** do not use the appliance. Instant says to discontinue use and dispose of it according to your local regulations.

The FAQ says a spill onto the base can trigger a C6 code. See [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/).

## How often to clean an Instant Pot, and what not to use

Clean it after each use, per both Rio manuals. That includes the inner pot, sealing ring, steam rack and condensation collector every time. Never use harsh chemical detergents, powders or scouring pads on the accessories. Tom's Guide suggests a monthly deep clean if you use the pot daily; that schedule is its own suggestion and does not appear in Instant's manuals.

## Other multicookers are not cleaned the same way

Lid design changes the job. TechGearLab says the Breville Fast Slow Pro's lid must be unscrewed to remove it for cleaning, which kept it from a top cleaning score, and ATK called taking that lid off before washing a tedious task.

ATK found removable lids much easier to clean than hinged ones. ATK also found nonstick pots, such as the Crock-Pot Express, easier to clean than stainless, but says the Cosori's nonstick coating "won't last forever" and finds stainless more durable.
