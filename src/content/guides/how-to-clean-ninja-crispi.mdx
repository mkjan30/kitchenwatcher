To clean a Ninja Crispi, wash everything except the PowerPod in the dishwasher or by hand, per Ninja's FN100-series owner's guide. The glass containers, crisper plates and adapter can go in the dishwasher, and the lids go on the top rack only. Wipe the PowerPod and control panel with a damp cloth and never immerse it. Let the glass cool to room temperature before it touches water, a sink or the dishwasher.

Below, each part in turn, with Ninja's rules kept apart from third-party tips. For whether the Crispi is worth buying, see our [Ninja Crispi review](/reviews/ninja-crispi-fn101/).

## Before you clean: unplug the Crispi and let the glass cool

Ninja's guide says to turn the unit off and unplug it before cleaning, and to let the appliance cool completely. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-crispi) measured the Crispi's glass exterior at about 280°F and says temperatures above 180°F are considered hot enough to potentially cause severe burns.

Heat is also a breakage risk. The guide says sudden temperature changes can break the glassware. Hot glass must not touch cool or wet surfaces, and it should cool before it goes in a sink or gets washed. It tells you to let the glass cool gradually to room temperature before it meets water.

No source we read says how long that takes. Ninja gives 10 minutes for the PowerPod and adapter but no figure for the glass, so don't treat 10 minutes as safe for the container. Leave it on a dry surface until it is back to room temperature; the guide says its heat-protectant base lets it sit on any countertop.

## How to clean the Crispi glass containers

Per Ninja's guide, the glass containers can go in the dishwasher or be washed by hand in hot soapy water. Its safety section adds that non-abrasive cleaners and plastic or nylon pads are fine if scouring is necessary. Air-dry or towel-dry afterward.

For baked-on residue, the independent blog [Ninja Food Tech](https://ninjafoodtech.com/2026/07/15/how-to-clean-a-ninja-crispi-glass-air-fryer-without-scratching-it/) suggests filling the cooled container with warm water and dish soap and soaking it for about 30 minutes. Then wipe with a soft sponge, and soak again if residue remains. The blog argues that several short soaks are safer than one hard scrub. That is a third-party tip, not in Ninja's FN100 Series guide, but it stays within that guide's rules.

What Ninja rules out: abrasive cleaning tools and metal scouring pads, and it says to take care not to scratch the glass with metal utensils and knives. The guide says physical damage makes glass more likely to break from temperature changes. SharkNinja's product page describes the containers as free of PTFE and PFAS, and TechGearLab lists no PTFE cooking-surface coating, so [coating-peel advice for basket fryers](/guides/air-fryer-basket-coating-peeling/) doesn't apply.

### Is baking soda, vinegar or a Magic Eraser OK on Crispi glass?

Ninja's guide names none of them. It bans abrasive cleaning tools and metal scouring pads, and nothing more specific.

[Ninja Food Tech](https://ninjafoodtech.com/2026/07/15/how-to-clean-a-ninja-crispi-glass-air-fryer-without-scratching-it/) advises against melamine sponges such as Magic Erasers and against dry baking soda or baking-soda paste, because they clean by mild abrasion. On vinegar, the same blog says it may help with mineral marks but is usually unnecessary, that dish soap and soaking should come first, and never to mix vinegar with bleach. Ninja's guide doesn't mention vinegar.

The evidence here is thin: one manufacturer rule and one blog's reasoning. The conservative choice is dish soap, soaking and a nylon pad, which is exactly what Ninja allows.

## How to clean the PowerPod and the area around the finger guard

Ninja's instruction is short: wipe the PowerPod and control panel with a damp cloth. Never immerse it in water or any other liquid, never put it in the dishwasher, and never remove the finger guard; the guide says removing it can damage the heating element. The guide also says the PowerPod should be cleaned thoroughly after each use.

That is where Ninja stops. The guide gives no method for grease on the finger guard or the fan behind it. Owner reviewer [Just Average Jen](https://www.justaveragejen.com/ninja-crispi-review-recipes-and-ideas.html) reports that food splashes can collect where the PowerPod meets the dish and need a wipe and dry after cooking.

Until Ninja publishes more, stay inside what the guide allows:

- Work only with the PowerPod off, unplugged and completely cool.
- Use a damp cloth, not a wet one. Ninja Food Tech also says the cloth should be damp, not dripping, and warns against pouring water into vents or spraying cleaner onto the PowerPod.
- Don't push liquid through the finger guard or into openings. The guide says not to immerse the housing in liquid and not to use metal scouring pads, because pieces can break off and touch electrical parts.
- If grease behind the guard won't come off with a damp cloth, contact Ninja customer service rather than removing the guard.

## How to clean the crisper plates, lids and adapter

The crisper plates and the adapter can go in the dishwasher or be washed by hand and dried thoroughly, per Ninja's guide. The guide repeatedly says to handle the crisper plate with care so it doesn't bend. Neither part is microwave or oven safe.

Lids go on the dishwasher's top rack only, or can be washed by hand. Don't seal a lid on a glass container right after cooking, and don't set a hot PowerPod, container or crisper plate on a lid.

The adapter stays hot after cooking, so the guide says to wait 10 minutes before handling it. To separate it from the PowerPod, grip the top of the pod, push down on the adapter's front tab and lift the pod off.

## Cleaning mistakes that can crack Crispi glass

Ninja's owner's guide lists what breaks its glass. During cleanup, that means:

- Rinsing hot glass, or running cold water into it.
- Setting hot glass on a wet counter or cool surface.
- Striking the glass with utensils or knocking it against a hard object.
- Scrubbing with abrasive cleaning tools, which the guide lists under physical damage.
- Keeping a container that has been dropped, chipped, cracked or deeply scratched. The guide says its strength may be compromised and it could shatter.

[Iowa State University Extension](https://blogs.extension.iastate.edu/answerline/2019/03/04/glass-kitchenware-cautions/) explains the mechanism: thermal shock is an abrupt temperature change that fractures glass as it expands or contracts. It cites a 2010 Consumer Reports test in which newer Pyrex glass taken from a hot oven shattered almost instantly on a wet granite countertop. It also relays Consumer Reports' advice to set hot glassware on a dry cloth potholder or towel.

How much margin a given glass has depends on its type. The [American Ceramic Society](https://ceramics.org/ceramic-tech-today/hells-kitchen-thermal-stress-and-glass-cookware-that-shatters/) summarizes research by Bradt and Martens: soda-lime glass resists fracture at temperature differences under about 100°F, borosilicate at about 330°F. Ninja's guide calls the Crispi glass "TempWare" and the product page calls it "CleanCrisp," but neither says which type it is. We found no Ninja source that names the glass type, so assume the smaller margin.

## What to do if a Crispi container chips or cracks while cleaning

Stop using it. Ninja's guide says a chipped, cracked or deeply scratched container should be discarded immediately. Clean up with thick gloves, because pieces may be sharp and hard to find, and put broken glass in the trash, not the recycling. Replace the container before cooking again.

Our [Ninja Crispi review](/reviews/ninja-crispi-fn101/) covers the reported shattering incident and the exterior heat readings.

## How often to clean the Ninja Crispi

After every use. Ninja's guide says the PowerPod should be cleaned thoroughly after each use and that every washed part should be dried. Its one-year limited warranty excludes damage caused by failure to perform required maintenance. The guide also says children must not clean or maintain the appliance.
