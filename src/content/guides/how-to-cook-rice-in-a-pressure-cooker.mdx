To cook rice in an Instant Pot, Instant's cooking time table lists 4 minutes at pressure for white, jasmine and basmati rice and 20 minutes for brown and wild rice. America's Test Kitchen uses 1 cup of rinsed white rice to 1¼ cups water, 3 minutes at high pressure and a 10-minute natural release. Instant's FAQ says not to fill the inner pot over the 1/2 line with foods that expand, such as rice.

This page compiles manufacturer and test-kitchen instructions; we did not cook these batches ourselves. See [how we research](/how-we-research/) for the method.

Every number below is credited. Instant's times are a manufacturer spec from its cooking time tables, which give no water ratio and no release method. ## How to cook white rice in an Instant Pot, step by step

These steps follow ATK's method for long-grain white, jasmine or basmati rice at high pressure. The lid and valve step comes from Instant's FAQ.

1. Rinse the rice in a fine-mesh strainer under cold running water until the water runs clear.
2. Add 1¼ cups water and ½ teaspoon salt (optional) per cup of rice. For each additional cup of rice, add 1 more cup of water and ½ teaspoon salt, so 2 cups of rice take 2¼ cups water (ATK). See the minimum-liquid note below before cooking a single cup in a 6 qt or larger pot.
3. Check the level. Instant's FAQ says not to fill the inner pot over the 1/2 line with foods that expand, such as rice.
4. Lock the lid, set the steam release to sealing (Instant FAQ) and cook at high pressure for 3 minutes (ATK).
5. Turn the unit off and let the pressure release naturally for 10 minutes (ATK).
6. Open the lid and fluff the rice with a fork.

ATK explains why the water is lower than on the stove. In a covered pot, water evaporates through the whole cook, so ATK adds an extra ½ cup. A pressure cooker loses little water before it seals and then prevents evaporation, so it needs only an extra ¼ cup.

## Why Instant Pot rice times differ between sources

Instant says 4 minutes for white rice; ATK says 3. For brown rice, Instant says 20 minutes, while [Well Plated](https://www.wellplated.com/instant-pot-brown-rice/) uses 22 and [Spend With Pennies](https://www.spendwithpennies.com/instant-pot-rice/) gives 22 to 25. Well Plated's page also gives 8 minutes for white rice, double Instant's 4. None of these pages explains the gap, and Instant's table gives no water amount, so the times may assume different ratios.

Water is inconsistent even within one page. For brown rice, Spend With Pennies lists 1 cup plus 2 tablespoons per cup in its ratio section and 1⅓ cups in its recipe notes, without saying which is right. We found no water ratio from Instant for rice in its multicookers.

If your rice is consistently underdone or soft, these are the two dials sources vary: time and water. Start from one source's full method rather than mixing numbers from several.

## Should you natural release or quick release rice?

Instant's FAQ recommends natural release for high-starch foods and says quick release can cause splattering with them. It also says not to use quick release for foods that expand when cooked, such as beans and grains. Release mechanics are covered in [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

ATK and both recipe blogs above use a timed natural release of 10 to 15 minutes. Spend With Pennies then opens the valve to vent what pressure remains. Instant's FAQ does not say whether venting after a partial natural release is acceptable for grains.

Budget for more than the 3 or 4 minutes on the display. Instant says reaching pressure takes 10 to 40 minutes or longer, depending on the amount and temperature of the ingredients. Well Plated estimates 10 to 15 minutes to build pressure for its brown rice, and its recipe card lists 51 minutes in total.

## Can you cook 1 cup of rice without a burn warning?

Instant's FAQ lists the minimum thin liquid to avoid a burn (BRN) error: 1 cup for a 3 qt, 1½ cups for a 6 qt, 2 cups for an 8 qt and 2½ cups for a 10 qt. ATK's single cup of rice with 1¼ cups water sits below the 6 qt and 8 qt figures. Neither source addresses this conflict.

The Rio Plus Wide (7.5 qt) [manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) gives a 2-cup minimum marked "unless otherwise specified in recipe", without saying whether that covers rice. ATK's 2-cup batch uses 2¼ cups water, which clears Instant's 6 qt and 8 qt minimums. General liquid rules are in [how much water in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/), and error troubleshooting is in [the Instant Pot burn message guide](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/).

Well Plated says batches under 2 cups of rice come out slightly mushier and that 2 cups cooks most evenly in a 6-quart pot. One commenter there reported that 22 minutes burned their brown rice. The author asked about steam escaping and pot size, and no resolution is shown.

## Does the Rice button do anything different?

[America's Test Kitchen's multicooker review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) says the Rice and Beans settings on most models are simply shortcuts for pressure-cooking. The recipe pages cited here all use Pressure Cook or Manual instead, which lets you set the time yourself.

Instant's own documents show that programs differ by model. The Rio Plus Wide manual says its Rice program "uses pressurized steam" and lists White Rice at 12 minutes on low pressure and Brown Rice at 30 minutes on high pressure as defaults (manufacturer spec).

Instant's FAQ lists White Rice, Brown Rice, Sushi Rice, Steam and Warm for the Dual Action's 2-quart side pot, with Pressure Cook listed only for its 4-quart main pot, and says the side pot makes up to 8 cups of cooked rice. Check your model's manual for what its Rice program does.

## Pressure cooker vs rice cooker: which makes better rice?

The independent sources disagree, and none of the pages we read publishes a side-by-side rice test of both appliances.

| Question | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Is a multicooker good for rice? | ATK calls a multicooker a way more versatile rice cooker (ATK multicooker review, 2025) |
| Is the rice as good? | Pressure cookers were just slightly inferior to dedicated rice cookers, particularly with brown rice; the review does not say which rice cookers it compared against (TechGearLab, updated Nov 24, 2024) |
| Does it save time? | You won't save a lot of time on steamed rice, per CR test engineer Ginny Lui (Consumer Reports, updated Jan 1, 2026) |
| How long do rice cookers take? | About 30 to 65 minutes for large batches of long-grain white rice, with slower machines making better rice (ATK rice cooker review, Oct 23, 2025) |
| Does pressure help in a rice cooker? | ATK's Cuckoo 6-Cup HP Pressure Rice Cooker made 12 cups of long-grain white rice in 35 minutes, about 15 minutes faster than ATK's favorite, but consistently overcooked long-grain white rice and scorched the bottoms of most batches; its brown rice preset made nicely cooked brown rice (ATK rice cooker review, Oct 23, 2025) |

ATK's [rice cooker review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2328-rice-cookers) named the Zojirushi 5.5-Cup Neuro Fuzzy its winner and found that hot-and-fast cookers ranked lower, especially on brown rice. If rice is most of what you make, that evidence leans toward a dedicated rice cooker; if you want one appliance for rice and everything else, ATK's view favors the multicooker.

## Which pressure cookers made good rice in independent tests

These are findings from others' tests, not a ranking. For rankings, see [the best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/).

- ATK said the [Instant Pot Pro 8-Quart](/reviews/instant-pot-pro-8qt/)'s rice function was excellent.
- ATK said the Instant Pot Rio 6-Quart made decent rice ([Rio vs Duo Plus](/vs/instant-pot-rio-6qt-vs-instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/)) and the Breville Fast Slow Pro made good white rice ([Pro 8-Quart vs Breville](/vs/instant-pot-pro-8qt-vs-breville-bpr700/)).
- [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker) found the Breville's rice a bit fluffier and less sticky than other models, and the [Instant Pot Duo Plus](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/)'s rice just slightly less moist and tender. It called the gap minimal but still noticeable. Elsewhere it says the roughly 5% differences it saw between cookers in pressure-cooking tests are unlikely to be noticed by most people.
