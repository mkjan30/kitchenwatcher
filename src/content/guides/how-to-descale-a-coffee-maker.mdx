To descale a coffee maker, fill the water tank with a coffee-maker descaling solution, or with diluted white vinegar if your manual gives a vinegar recipe. Run the clean cycle, or a normal brew cycle, with no coffee and no paper filter. Then run 1 to 3 water-only cycles, depending on the brand. Repeat when the clean light comes on or on your manual's schedule; harder water means descaling more often.

The manufacturers don't agree on vinegar or on how many rinses to run. The table below shows what four common brands' manuals actually say, so you can follow the one for your machine. I'm working from the manuals and brand guidance only; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Descaling drip coffee maker steps, brand by brand

These steps combine the OXO, Ninja, Cuisinart and Moccamaster instructions. Where they differ, the brand is named.

1. **Empty the machine.** Empty the carafe and brew basket, and remove the paper filter. [Cuisinart's DCC-3200 booklet](https://www.cuisinart.ca/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-master-ca/en_CA/v1726577654996//Instruction%20Booklet/DCC-3200C.pdf) says to take out the charcoal water filter first. [OXO's 8-cup manual](https://www.oxo.com/media/wysiwyg/PDF_Files/Brew_8718800_8CupCoffeeMaker_Manual_M03_Web.pdf) says to remove any filters and the Single Serve Accessory. Moccamaster's guide says no filter is needed for the descaling brew.
2. **Fill the tank.** Use a descaling solution per its label, or vinegar only if your manual gives a recipe. Cuisinart says one-third white vinegar and two-thirds water, filled to capacity. [Ninja's CE251 owner's guide](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/da/da60176b-126f-490a-b1a4-f1bd1654c887.pdf) says 16 oz white vinegar topped up with water to the 12-cup line (60 fl oz). Don't fill past MAX or the 12-cup line.
3. **Put the empty carafe back** under the basket. OXO says to screw its lid on.
4. **Start the clean cycle.** On a machine with no clean button, such as a Moccamaster, run a normal brew cycle with no filter, per Moccamaster's descaling guide. That guide says to dissolve the powder in 32 oz of warm water and never to put hot water in the brewer.
5. **Let it finish, then discard the solution.**
6. **Run water-only rinse cycles.** Cuisinart, Ninja and OXO each say one full cycle of fresh water. Moccamaster's guide says three.
7. **Wash the carafe and brew basket**, and put the Cuisinart water filter back in.

## Descaling instructions by model: OXO, Ninja, Cuisinart, Moccamaster

Every cell below is a manufacturer instruction, not a measurement. No independent lab has timed descaling cycles for these models in anything I found, so there are no third-party numbers here.

The biggest differences are vinegar and rinse count. OXO gives no vinegar ratio at all, only "descaling solution". Moccamaster says not to use vinegar.

Moccamaster's own figures also don't quite agree with each other: its USA guide says to dissolve the descaler in 32 oz of warm water, while the Moccamaster USA method in a [Martha Stewart article republished by Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/way-clean-moccamaster-according-experts-150000764.html), which quotes Moccamaster USA's Will Etherington, says 40 oz of cold water.

## Using the coffee maker clean cycle

The clean button doesn't add anything to the water. It runs the solution you put in the tank through the machine in a pattern the manufacturer chose. What each manual says to do:

- **Cuisinart DCC-3200:** press and hold CLEAN until the LED flashes, then release. The machine beeps five times and shuts off at the end. If the CLEAN light comes on again, repeat with fresh solution; the booklet says one cycle is usually enough.
- **OXO 8-cup:** slide the selector to Carafe mode, press Power, then Clean. A chime sounds at the end. The cycle takes about 25 minutes and varies with how much scale is inside. OXO says to let the cycle finish.
- **Ninja CE251:** make sure Small Batch is not selected, then press CLEAN. It takes about 25 to 35 minutes, and pressing CLEAN or Power cancels it. The unit beeps at the end. Ninja warns that scalding can occur if you open the basket during a brew or clean cycle.
- **Moccamaster:** there is no clean button. Moccamaster's guide says to brew the solution through with no filter.

If a Cuisinart CLEAN light stays on after descaling, see [why the Cuisinart clean light won't turn off](/guides/cuisinart-coffee-maker-clean-light/).

## How often to descale a coffee maker

Start with your machine's own trigger:

- **OXO 8-cup:** the Clean light comes on after every 90 cycles, per its manual. OXO also says how often you need to descale depends on water hardness.
- **Cuisinart DCC-3200:** clean whenever the CLEAN LED lights. The booklet gives no cycle count, only that buildup depends on your tap water's mineral content and how often you brew.
- **Ninja CE251:** clean when a full carafe takes longer than 12 to 15 minutes.
- **Moccamaster:** about every 100 brews or at the end of a box of filters, per its guide. The Martha Stewart article republished by Yahoo gives Moccamaster USA's schedule as every 100 cycles or 3 months, and quotes Etherington suggesting every 50 cycles or 1.5 months with hard water.

For machines without a counter, your water hardness is the main variable. The [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/hardness-water) classes water by milligrams per liter as calcium carbonate: soft is 0 to 60, moderately hard 61 to 120, hard 121 to 180, and very hard over 180. The USGS says heated hard water can form calcium carbonate deposits that can reduce the life of equipment, and that mineral buildup will occur in home coffee makers too.

No manufacturer I read gives a chart that turns a hardness number into a descaling interval. Moccamaster USA's 1.5-month (hard water) and 3-month figures are the only time-based numbers from a manufacturer I found, and they are for Moccamaster brewers. Treat them as a rough range, not a rule, and descale sooner if brewing slows down.

Cuisinart's DCC-3200 booklet (Canadian edition) also says to change the DCC-3200's charcoal water filter every 60 days or 60 uses, more often with hard water.

## Signs your coffee maker needs descaling

- **Slower brewing.** Ninja's CE251 guide uses a 12 to 15 minute full-carafe brew as its cue.
- **The clean light is on** (OXO, Cuisinart).
- **The brew won't start.** Ninja's CE251 guide says that if a brew still won't start, it may be time for a clean cycle. [iFixit's DCC-3200 troubleshooting page](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DDC-3200_Troubleshooting) lists calcium/mineral buildup as a possible cause on the DCC-3200.
- **Louder operation, a bitter or metallic taste, slow brewing, or coffee that isn't hot enough.** [Ratio Coffee](https://ratiocoffee.com/blogs/coffee-guides/signs-you-need-to-descale-your-coffee-maker), a coffee-equipment maker, lists these as signs. That's a manufacturer's claim, not a measurement. Hamilton Beach's Kevin Carter, [quoted by Yahoo Shopping](https://shopping.yahoo.com/home-garden/kitchen/article/how-to-descale-your-coffee-maker-165755117.html), named slower brewing, louder-than-usual operation and inconsistent water flow as signs a machine needs cleaning.

## Should you descale with vinegar or a descaler?

The brands disagree, so follow your own manual. Cuisinart and Ninja give vinegar recipes. OXO's manual calls only for descaling solution and says the warranty is voided if descaling isn't done according to its instructions. Moccamaster's guide says vinegar is not strong enough to break down mineral buildup and can leave a lingering odor and aftertaste.

That last point is a manufacturer statement. I found no measurement comparing how well vinegar and citric-acid descalers remove scale from drip machines, so the evidence is thin either way. Recipes published elsewhere don't match the manuals either: Mr. Coffee's blog uses about half as much vinegar as water, Yahoo Shopping says 1:1, and Ratio mentions vinegar and water with no ratio. For the full comparison, see [vinegar or descaler for a coffee maker](/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/).

## Mistakes that make descaling fail

- **Overfilling.** OXO says not to fill past MAX, and Ninja says not past the 12-cup line.
- **Leaving the charcoal filter in.** Cuisinart says to remove it before descaling.
- **Leaving Small Batch on** (Ninja CE251).
- **Stopping the cycle early.** OXO says to let it finish.
- **Skipping the rinse.** Every manual above ends with at least one water-only cycle.
- **Brewing with distilled water** (OXO). OXO's manual says to use filtered water, not distilled, and that filtered water helps prevent mineral buildup.
- **Using alkaline cleaning agents, abrasive pads or harsh chemicals** when cleaning, which OXO's manual rules out.

## Descaling an espresso machine is different

Espresso machines heat water in a boiler or thermoblock under pressure, and their manuals have their own descale programs, solution amounts and steps. Don't apply the drip-machine ratios above to them.
