If you have toast stuck in the toaster, unplug it at the wall and let it cool completely. Then raise the lift lever if your toaster has one, and ease the food out with wooden tongs or a wooden spoon handle, never a fork or knife. GE's toaster manual says to remove stuck bread carefully without damaging the elements and to "never use a metal object for this purpose."

## How to get stuck toast out, step by step

These steps combine the stuck-food instructions in the [GE G9TMA2SSPSS manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf), the [Breville BTA720/BTA730 booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) and the [Hamilton Beach manual 840216701](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840216701.pdf).

1. **If it's still toasting, press Cancel.** GE says Cancel ejects the contents; Breville says the carriage moves to the raised position.
2. **Unplug it at the wall.** GE and Hamilton Beach print the same line, "Do not attempt to dislodge food when toaster is plugged in," and Breville's is nearly identical.
3. **Let it cool completely.** GE, Breville and Hamilton Beach all say to wait until it has cooled before removing anything.
4. **Raise the carriage as high as it goes.** Breville says its high-lift lever makes small items such as English muffins easier to remove, and Hamilton Beach says to lift up its toast boost for smaller foods.
5. **Ease the food out with something wooden.** [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) suggests wooden tongs or bamboo chopsticks; [Straight Dope](https://www.straightdope.com/21343258/is-poking-a-knife-into-the-toaster-really-dangerous) suggests a wooden spoon handle. GE and Breville both warn you not to damage the heating elements, so work gently.

## Can you put a fork in a toaster?

No, and every manual read for this page agrees. GE says never use a metal object to remove stuck bread. Breville says metal utensils such as a knife can damage the elements and lead to electrocution. Hamilton Beach says not to use a fork or other utensil that could damage the element or cause a fire or electric shock, and Dualit's [safety advice page](https://help.dualit.com/en-US/classic-toaster---be-aware-safety-advice-254925) says it does not recommend a knife or any other object.

[Straight Dope](https://www.straightdope.com/21343258/is-poking-a-knife-into-the-toaster-really-dangerous) explains the risk in two parts. With the toaster unplugged, there is no immediate shock, but metal can pierce the mica insulation around the coils and later cause a short, a blown fuse or a live metal frame. With it plugged in, a slip can hit the lever and power the elements, and if the outlet's hot and neutral wires are reversed, the coils can be live even when the toaster is off.

Straight Dope addresses the common belief that a butter knife is safe once the toaster is off, and still advises against it, as does every manufacturer manual read for this page. Use wood.

## Should you turn the toaster upside down and shake it?

The manuals disagree here, so check yours. Hamilton Beach's manual says that for food lodged in the slots, you unplug, let it cool, then "turn toaster upside down and shake." GE's manual says the opposite: do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove crumbs, bread or other items.

Breville's booklet mentions turning the toaster upside down only for emptying crumbs. For jammed food it says to ease the food out gently.

Follow your own model's manual, and if you own the GE, don't shake it. The crumb side of this disagreement is covered in [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/).

## Toaster jammed and everything is flashing?

On some Breville toasters, that is jam detection working. Breville's [US instruction book for the BTA720 and BTA730](https://assets.breville.com/BTA720/BTA720_BTA730_USCM_IB_F26_LR.pdf) (the Bit More) says that if bread, bagels, English muffins or toaster pastries jam in the slots, all the buttons and the LED toasting progress indicator flash and the elements turn off. Breville then says to unplug, let it cool completely and carefully ease the food out, without metal utensils. The Australian and New Zealand booklet for the same models describes the same feature.

Most basic toasters don't have this. The GE and Hamilton Beach manuals describe no jam detection, so on those you won't get a warning. If your lever won't go down at all afterwards, see [toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).

## How to get a bagel or English muffin out of a toaster

Small and thick items are where testers report retrieval trouble: they don't rise high enough, or they're a tight fit in the slot. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) checks whether English muffins and bagels pop up high enough to grab, and reports these per model (page updated October 22, 2025):

- **GE 2-Slice Toaster (G9TMA2SSPSS, matched by the Amazon listing Reviewed and ATK both link):** the lift lever is needed to raise English muffins high enough to grab safely.
- **Cuisinart CPT-122:** neither bagels nor English muffins popped up high enough without the extra lift, and a fat New York-style bagel is a tight fit.
- **Black+Decker T2569B:** English muffins don't reach the top even with high lift, so watch your fingertips.
- **Revolution InstaGLO R180:** no high lift, which Reviewed says becomes a problem when retrieving English muffins.
- **Breville Toast Select Luxe** (Reviewed gives no model number): half an English muffin was almost impossible to grab despite the high-lift lever.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) lists tricky retrieval as something to avoid: on toasters with no lift lever, its testers had to use a nonmetal utensil or risk burning their fingers. Hamilton Beach's manual says to use toast boost for small items.

Which way the cut side faces is a separate question, covered in [the toaster bagel setting](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Why toast gets stuck, and how to stop it happening again

- **Bread too thick or too long.** Breville says bread should not stick out above the slots or be thick or long enough to jam or wedge the carriage.
- **Small items that don't rise high enough.** Use high lift or toast boost, per Hamilton Beach and Reviewed's findings above.
- **Oversized foods.** GE, Breville and Hamilton Beach all say oversized foods must not go in a toaster. See [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).
- **Walking away.** Breville says not to leave the toaster unattended because toast may jam, and Dualit says a toaster should never run unsupervised.

If retrieval is a constant problem, our [best toasters](/best/toasters/) page and the [Breville BTA720XL review](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) cover lift levers and slot sizes model by model.

## When stuck food starts smoking or catches fire

GE's troubleshooting chart says that if smoke comes out, press Cancel, then empty the crumb tray if it is full; its care section says to unplug the toaster and let it cool before cleaning. If the smoke has another cause, see [why is my toaster smoking](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-smoking/).
