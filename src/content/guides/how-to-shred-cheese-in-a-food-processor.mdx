To shred cheese in a food processor, fit the shredding disc with its shredding side facing up, lock the lid, and feed cold cheese through the feed tube with the pusher and even pressure. Run firm cheese straight from the fridge. Freeze soft cheese like mozzarella first: 30 minutes per Hamilton Beach, 45 per Breville, up to several hours per KitchenAid.

Use the speed your manual gives for the disc. Everything below comes from four manufacturers' manuals and help pages, TechGearLab's shredding scores, two Cook's Country tastings and a Pantry & Larder storage guide ([how we research](/how-we-research/)). Where brands disagree, your machine's manual wins.

## Step by step: how to shred cheese in a food processor

These steps combine the [Hamilton Beach 70730 use and care guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf), the [Breville BFP810 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf), the [Ninja BN600C owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf) (the Canadian sibling of the BN601) and KitchenAid's [Food Processor Tips](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Product_Info/Food_Processor_Tips_and_Tricks/Food_Processor_Tips).

1. **Handle the disc carefully.** Hamilton Beach warns that the disc is very sharp and says to turn the knob to OFF and unplug the unit before fitting it. Ninja's sequence plugs the base in first, then has you hold the disc by its finger holes.
2. **Seat the disc with the shredding side up.** It goes on the motor shaft (Hamilton Beach) or the disc spindle (Ninja, Breville). Hamilton Beach says to run one blade or disc at a time.
3. **Lock the bowl and lid.** Hamilton Beach says the unit won't work unless both are locked. Ninja's control panel keeps flashing until they are. Some models also need the pusher in place; see [why the safety lock needs every part seated](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
4. **Cut the cheese to fit the feed tube.** Breville says to remove the rind from hard cheeses first. Ninja's troubleshooting says to trim ingredients to a uniform size and not to cut them shorter than the feed chute is tall.
5. **Pick the speed.** Ninja allows only the DISC program or LOW with the disc. Hamilton Beach says to select 1, 2 or P (pulse). KitchenAid's general tips say high speed for hard cheeses and low speed for soft ones.
6. **Feed the cheese with the pusher, never your fingers.** Hamilton Beach prints that warning in capitals. Breville says to press the food lightly before starting, then use medium, even pressure for cheese, and never force it, which can damage the disks.

## Shredding disc: which side goes up?

The side you want to use faces up, per both the Hamilton Beach and Ninja manuals. Hamilton Beach adds that the name stamped on the face-up side is the function you get, so its slicing/shredding disc fitted the other way up slices instead of shredding.

Each brand labels the two faces differently:

- **Hamilton Beach 70730:** SLICE and SHRED are stamped on the two faces.
- **Ninja BN600C:** the faces are labeled Slicer and Shredder.
- **Breville BFP810:** a separate reversible shredder has a fine side and a coarse side. The manual's text doesn't say which way up gives which.
- **KitchenAid 9-cup:** its [reversible disc help page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/9_Cup/Operation/Reversible_Slicing%2F%2FShredding_Disc) doesn't say in its text which side goes up.

For what the S-blade, dough blade and slicing disc do, see [food processor blades explained](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).

## How cold should the cheese be? Manufacturer chilling and freezing times

For firm cheese, Hamilton Beach says Cheddar or Swiss chilled at least 30 minutes, processed straight from the fridge. KitchenAid says firm cheese should be "very cold". Breville gives no time.

For soft cheese, the sources disagree. Hamilton Beach says to freeze mozzarella for 30 minutes and Breville says 45 minutes for softer cheeses. KitchenAid's general tips page and its [Shredding Cheese page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Product_Info/Food_Processor_Tips_and_Tricks/Shredding_Cheese) say to freeze soft cheese thoroughly, which "may take several hours". Its 9-cup disc page says 10 to 15 minutes, so KitchenAid disagrees with itself.

Don't overshoot, either: KitchenAid's tips say not to process food frozen so hard that a sharp knife tip can't pierce it.

## How to shred mozzarella in a food processor

Use block mozzarella, not fresh mozzarella. Breville's manual says grating soft white cheeses such as fresh mozzarella is not recommended. For block mozzarella, every brand that gives a figure says to freeze it first, for the times in the table above.

Start with your own manual's time. If the cheese still smears, go colder, but stop short of KitchenAid's knife-tip limit.

Cook's Country's [shredded mozzarella tasting](https://www.cookscountry.com/taste_tests/1789-shredded-mozzarella) (updated April 8, 2024) says the team usually buys block mozzarella and shreds it as needed. Tasters found the pre-shredded strands "gritty and powdery" raw, from the anti-caking agents, though texture mattered less once melted on pizza. We haven't compared them ourselves.

## Why food processor cheese turns gummy or rolls up, and how to fix it

- **Cheese smears, spreads or rolls up on the disc.** Cause: the cheese is too warm. KitchenAid says soft and medium-hard cheese may spread out or roll up on the shredding disc, and to shred only well-chilled cheese. Hamilton Beach's troubleshooting repeats its fridge and 30-minute freezer advice.
- **Shreds are irregular.** Cause: too much cheese or too much force. Hamilton Beach says packing the chute too full or pushing too hard on the pusher gives irregular shreds. Breville says never to force food down the chute.
- **Shreds come out uneven.** Ninja's troubleshooting says to trim ingredients to a uniform size and not shorter than the feed chute is tall.
- **The motor slows down.** Breville calls this normal for heavier loads, naming shredding cheese as an example. It says to cut the food smaller and use a smaller pusher.
- **A chunk is left on top of the disc.** KitchenAid and Breville both say a few pieces left on the disc after shredding is normal. KitchenAid suggests cutting them by hand.

If you want grated rather than shredded cheese, Hamilton Beach's chart lists the chopping blade for grating chilled cheese such as Parmesan. Its chopping-blade maximums are 5 ounces of Parmesan or Romano, or 8 ounces of Cheddar, Jack or mozzarella, cut into 1-inch cubes. Getting the residue off the disc afterward is covered in [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

## Keeping shredded cheese from clumping afterward

Store-bought shreds stay loose because of additives. Cook's Country's [shredded cheddar tasting](https://www.cookscountry.com/taste_tests/2143-shredded-sharp-cheddar-cheese) says pre-shredded cheese is coated with cellulose or cornstarch to stop clumping. Those agents can aid melting but draw moisture out over time, which can then inhibit melting.

For home shreds, [Pantry & Larder](https://pantryandlarder.com/how-to-stop-shredded-cheese-clumping/) suggests tossing them with 1 to 2 tablespoons of cornstarch per block. It also suggests an airtight container rather than a zip bag, so shreds aren't pressed together. For freezing, it says to flash-freeze the shreds on a tray, then keep them in an airtight container, where they last 2 to 3 months before quality starts to drop.

## Warm motor or electrical smell while shredding cheese

KitchenAid's Shredding Cheese page calls grating cheese a high-energy job. The unit may feel warm, the motor shuts off if too warm and resumes once cool, and a slight electrical odor is normal. Colder cheese reduces the odor, and so does cleaning the blade often, which KitchenAid says cuts friction between the blade and the lid.

Hamilton Beach draws the line: for a strong odor or any visible smoke, unplug the unit immediately and call customer service. Cool-down times by brand are in [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).

## Which food processors shred cheese best?

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor) shredded potatoes, carrots and cheese on eight models. The Magimix 4200XL and Breville Sous Chef 16 Pro (BFP800) both scored 8 and left "only one or two small, thin pieces unshredded", and both offer adjustable shred sizes. On cheese alone, the Magimix scored 7 and the Breville 5.

TechGearLab says the Ninja Professional (BN600/BN601) "truly shined in the shredding and slicing tests" with a 7, despite offering a single shred size. It scored the Cuisinart Custom 14 a 6 (4 on cheese) and notes no shredding adjustment without buying more discs. So cheese.com's claim that every food processor has fine and medium shred choices is wrong; the Hamilton Beach 70730 also ships with one shred face.
