To slice vegetables in a food processor, fit the slicing disc on the spindle with the side stamped SLICE ([Hamilton Beach 70730 manual](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf)) or "Slicer" ([Ninja BN600C guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf)) facing up. Lock the lid, cut food to stand upright in the feed tube, pack it snugly, then push with the pusher using even pressure at the speed your manual gives (Ninja: DISC or LOW only).

Below: the steps, how to set thickness on common models, and a fix for each cause of uneven slices, all from manufacturer manuals plus credited third-party tests ([how we research](/how-we-research/)).

## How to slice vegetables in a food processor, step by step

1. **Fit the spindle and disc slicing side up.** Hamilton Beach says to do this with the knob at OFF and the unit unplugged. It also says to operate "only one blade or disc at a time", and its bowl scraper can't be used with the disc. Ninja says to hold the disc by its finger holes when you put it on. Not sure which disc you need? See [which food processor blade to use](/guides/food-processor-blades-explained/).
2. **Lock the bowl and lid.** Hamilton Beach and Ninja both say the unit won't run until they are; that's the [safety interlock](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
3. **Prep the food.** For round produce such as onions and apples, [KitchenAid's attachment tips](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Fresh_Prep/Food_Processor/Food_Processor_Attachment_Tips) say to peel, core and seed, then halve or quarter to fit the tube. For a single carrot or celery stalk, cut a flat end.
4. **Load it standing vertically and packed snug.** KitchenAid's tips say slender foods such as carrots can fall over in the tube, giving an uneven slice, and to pack the tube to prevent it.
5. **Pick the speed.** Ninja: use only the DISC program or LOW. KitchenAid's [13-cup accessories guide](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Disc_Selection/Accessories_Selection_Guide_-_Food_Processor): Low for soft vegetables, potatoes and tomatoes, High for hard vegetables. Hamilton Beach: speed 1, 2 or Pulse.
6. **Push with the pusher, never your fingers.** Breville's [BFP810 manual](https://assets.breville.com/BFP810/BFP810_USCM_IB_A22_LR.pdf) says "Never push food down by hand when slicing or shredding. Always use pusher." Hamilton Beach warns never to push food through the chute "with hands or other objects".
7. **Unplug before removing the lid** (Hamilton Beach and Breville both say so; Ninja says to wait for the disc to stop completely), then lift the disc out by its finger holes (Ninja), and the spindle after it.

## Prep and feed tube: the step that decides slice quality

Most slicing problems start before the motor does: food must stand upright and not wobble. Packed tightly, pieces don't have room to move around, per [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/how-to-prep-vegetables-with-a-food-processor).

- **Match the tube to the food.** Breville says to use the small or medium chute for long, thin produce such as carrots, cucumbers and zucchini so it doesn't tip over.
- **Fill the wide tube properly.** Breville's wide chute is 5.5 in (manufacturer spec). Food should fit snugly enough to stay upright but still slide down, and stay below the MAX mark, or the pusher won't engage the interlock.
- **Stand it on the left.** Breville says to place items vertically on the left side of the chute to minimize tilting.
- **Don't cut pieces too short.** Ninja's troubleshooting says "Do not cut ingredients smaller than the feed chute height."
- **Hard produce goes in a smaller chute.** Breville says sweet potato and pumpkin belong in the small or medium chute, because the large one could damage the disks.
- **Chill watery vegetables.** Hamilton Beach says room-temperature vegetables with high water content are less firm and "do not perform as well".
- **Mind the tube width.** [ATK's review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) says a tube too wide lets food slump; too narrow forces trimming.

## How much pressure and which speed to use

| Food type | Pressure | Speed | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft (tomatoes) | Light, even | Low (KitchenAid) | Breville BFP810 manual; KitchenAid tips and 13-cup guide |
| Firm (potatoes) | Medium, even (Breville); firmer (KitchenAid tips) | Low for potatoes (KitchenAid 13-cup guide); High for potatoes and carrots (KitchenAid attachment tips) | Breville BFP810 manual; KitchenAid 13-cup guide and attachment tips |
| Any, on a Ninja | Guide with the pusher | DISC or LOW only | Ninja BN600C guide |
| Any, on a Hamilton Beach | Don't push too hard | 1, 2 or Pulse | Hamilton Beach 70730 manual |

The brands word this differently (KitchenAid's two pages even split on potatoes), so follow your own manual. Breville says never to force food down the chute because it can damage the disks, and Hamilton Beach says pushing too hard gives irregular slices.

## How to adjust slicing thickness on a food processor

**KitchenAid 13-cup (KFP1318).** Per [KitchenAid's support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Disc_Selection/How_to_Adjust_the_Slicing_Thickness_-_Food_Processor), move the external knob left for thinner slices and right for thicker. The knob "only works with the Externally Adjustable Slicing Disc"; with any other disc or blade fitted, turning it does nothing. The support page gives no millimeter range, number of settings, or whether you can change thickness while the motor runs.

**Breville Sous Chef 16 (BFP810).** Breville's manual says it slices "from 1/8in – 5/16in (0.3mm – 8.0mm)". The units conflict: 1/8 in is about 3.2 mm, not 0.3 mm, so check the markings on your disk. The manual text doesn't describe how to set it.

**Fixed-disc models** (Cuisinart Custom 14, Ninja BN601, Hamilton Beach 70730) slice at one thickness. TechGearLab says Cuisinart sells other thickness discs; it doesn't say the same for the other two. Disc sizes below are from [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor); its Hamilton Beach figure, "one width between 2mm-4mm", doesn't pin down a number.

## Why food processor slices come out uneven or slanted

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slices slanted or uneven | Unevenly cut food or uneven pressure | Load evenly cut food and press the pusher evenly | Breville BFP810 manual |
| Food falls over in the tube | Large chute not full | Pack the large chute full, place items far left, or use a smaller chute; cut a flat end | Breville; KitchenAid attachment tips |
| Irregular slices | Chute overpacked or pusher pushed too hard | Load less and ease off the pusher | Hamilton Beach 70730 manual |
| Unevenly sliced pieces | Pieces of mixed sizes, or cut too short | Trim to a uniform size; don't cut shorter than the chute height | Ninja BN600C guide |
| Not slicing at all | Disc upside down, or vegetables too warm | Flip to the SLICE or "Slicer" side (raised blade face-up, per KitchenAid); refrigerate watery vegetables | Hamilton Beach; Ninja; KitchenAid attachment troubleshooting |
| A few pieces left on the disc | Normal | Nothing to fix | Breville BFP810 manual |

KitchenAid's [slicing troubleshooting page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixer_Attachments/Fresh_Prep/Food_Processor/Food_Processor_Attachment_not_Shredding_or_Slicing_Properly) and its tips page are written for the stand-mixer food processor attachment, so read them as KitchenAid's general advice, not KFP1318 instructions.

## Which vegetables slice well in a food processor, and which don't

Start with firm produce. Hamilton Beach's slicing chart lists cucumbers, apples, mushrooms, carrots, potatoes, cabbage, tomatoes and peppers. ATK says firm ingredients slice best, and suggests rolling greens such as kale into a tight bundle before feeding them in.

Tomatoes vary most by machine. TechGearLab found the Hamilton Beach 70730 "partially destroyed the tomatoes" (4/10), while the Cuisinart scored 9/10 on tomatoes but 5/10 on zucchini. ATK reports juicier, more ragged slices from a few models, and uneven, wedge-shaped ones from one lower-ranked machine. Hamilton Beach says not to slice frozen meats in the 70730.

## Slicing disc tips: handling, storage and the shred side

- Handle the disc carefully: Hamilton Beach's manual carries a laceration warning that it is very sharp, and Ninja says to grasp it by the finger holes. Hamilton Beach says to store its disc slicing side down.
- Don't store disks loose in a utensil drawer, per Breville.
- The other side of a reversible disc shreds. See [how to shred cheese in a food processor](/guides/how-to-shred-cheese-in-a-food-processor/).
