The Instant Pot burn message, shown as "Food burn" in Instant's Duo Plus and Rio Plus Wide manuals, means the cooker detected high temperature at the bottom of the inner pot and lowered the heat on its own. Instant's FAQ names three causes: too little thin liquid, a steam release not set to sealing, or browned bits left after sautéing. To fix it, turn the cooker off, release pressure, inspect and scrape the pot bottom, add thin liquid and restart.

This is a research page and nothing was cooked for it. It is built from two Instant manuals, Instant's FAQ, America's Test Kitchen, Consumer Reports and iFixit; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What the Instant Pot burn message means

Instant's [Duo Plus 60 v4 manual](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/70/70a7238e-060c-4daa-8215-be85436c28dd.pdf) (p.44) and [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) (p.42) give the same cause for Food burn: "High temperature detected at bottom of inner pot; cooker automatically reduces temperature to avoid overheating." Both list the same solution: starch deposits on the bottom may have blocked heat dissipation, so turn the cooker off, release pressure according to the recipe and inspect the bottom of the inner pot.

The name on screen varies. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/3395-your-instant-pot-says-burn-what-should-you-do) says it reads "BURN," "HOT," "OvHt" or "Food Burn" depending on the machine, and Instant's FAQ calls it a "food BRN Error." Only the two manuals above were read for this guide, so older models may describe it differently.

It is a heat-protection response, not proof the food is ruined. Per [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/multi-cookers/instant-pot-duo-vs-instant-pot-max-face-off-a5243964132/), Instant describes it as a safety mechanism that stops heating to prevent food from burning.

## How do you fix burn on an Instant Pot? Seven steps from the manuals and ATK

1. **Turn the cooker off.** That is the first instruction in both manuals' Food burn row.
2. **Release pressure according to the recipe.** That is the manuals' wording. Instant's FAQ says not to quick-release fatty, oily, thick or high-starch foods (its examples: stews, chilis, pasta, congee) or foods that expand, such as beans and grains. Those overlap with the foods the sources tie to Burn (Consumer Reports' bean chili, ATK's beef stew, starchy foods per Instant), so check your recipe before venting; see [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).
3. **Wait for the float valve to drop, and never force the lid.** Both manuals say to open the lid only after the float valve has dropped down, and not to force the lid off the cooker base. If it won't open, see [the float valve guide](/guides/instant-pot-float-valve/).
4. **Inspect the bottom of the inner pot.** This is the manuals' last instruction for Food burn.
5. **Check the parts.** America's Test Kitchen's list: nothing caught between the pot and the burner, the silicone sealing ring in place, the float valve clean and moving freely, and the steam-release switch set correctly.
6. **If food has scorched, scrape and add water.** ATK says to stir, scrape up the browned bits and add a little water; its editor used half a cup. Consumer Reports' testers had to stop, stir, close the lid and wait for pressure again.
7. **Reseal and finish the cook.** ATK says to set the time you had left; if you don't know it, its editor guesses and subtracts a minute or two for the pot coming back up to pressure.

If the alert keeps returning with enough thin liquid, a clean pot bottom and the release set to seal, none of the sources read here offers another fix; contact Instant Consumer Care. Other codes are covered in [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/).

## Why does an Instant Pot say burn? Five causes the sources name

### Not enough thin liquid

Instant's FAQ lists minimums of thin, water-based liquid by pot size: 1 cup for 3 qt, 1½ cups for 6 qt, 2 cups for 8 qt and 2½ cups for 10 qt. The Rio Plus Wide manual gives 2 cups for its 7.5-qt pot. The FAQ adds that "thicker sauces will require an additional cup of thin liquid," and both manuals say to add water when using canned, condensed or cream-based soup. The full fill-line rules are in [how much water in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/).

### Browned bits left after sautéing

The FAQ's third cause is simply "After sautéing, deglaze the inner pot." The Duo Plus manual's deglazing steps are: remove the food, add water, broth or wine, and loosen what's stuck with a wooden spoon or silicone spatula.

### Starch settled on the bottom

This is the cause the manuals themselves name in the Food burn row: starch deposits blocking heat dissipation. Instant also told Consumer Reports the warning can occur with very starchy foods that settle at the bottom. Layering starches or tomato on top without stirring is a common blog tip, but neither manual nor the FAQ mentions it.

### Steam release not sealed

The FAQ says the steam release handle must be set to sealing and pushed down fully on the steam post. If steam keeps escaping, see [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/).

### Something between the pot and the heating element

ATK lists this first among its checks. iFixit's student-written [Duo 80 V2 troubleshooting wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Instant_Pot_IP-DUO80_V2_Troubleshooting) says food "can find its way between the pot and heating element," and also names dairy cooked directly in the pot as a cause of overheating.

## Which foods have set off Food burn in independent reports?

Consumer Reports' March 2019 Duo vs Max face-off is the clearest independent case. Every time testers made chili with dried beans, the Instant Pot Max flashed "Food Burn" as it was reaching pressure. A second Max bought for the test did the same. CR reports the message only for the Max, and says the Max did not show it with beef stew or any other pressure-cooked food.

ATK's Hannah Crowley saw the alert while making beef stew in an Instant Pot Pro (article dated April 15, 2022); she traced it to browning tomato paste and flour too far before sealing. Instant's FAQ separately says to be cautious pressure cooking more than ¼ cup of oil, oil-based sauces, condensed cream-based soups and thick sauces, and to add liquid to thin sauces.

The evidence is thin. No source read here gives a rate, and none says which current models are more sensitive than others.

## How Food burn differs from C5 and C7/NoPr in Instant's manuals

Two other codes share causes with Food burn. Every cell below is manufacturer manual wording, lightly shortened, from the Duo Plus 60 v4 manual (pp.43–44); the Rio Plus Wide manual's rows match. The full table is in [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/).

| Message on display | Cause (manufacturer manual wording) | What the manual says to do | Manual / model it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food burn | High temperature detected at bottom of inner pot; cooker automatically reduces temperature | Turn cooker off, release pressure according to the recipe, inspect bottom of inner pot (starch may block heat dissipation) | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.44; Rio Plus Wide p.42 |
| C5 | Temperature too high because there is no water in inner pot | Check for scorching; add thin liquid (3 qt 1 cup, 6 qt 1½ cups, 8 qt 2 cups) | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.43; Rio Plus Wide p.41 |
| C5 | Temperature too high because inner pot is not placed in cooker base | Press Cancel, let element cool, check for foreign objects, reinsert pot | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.43; Rio Plus Wide p.41 |
| C7 or NoPr | Not enough liquid | Add thin, water-based liquid by pot size | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.43; Rio Plus Wide p.41 |
| C7 or NoPr | Quick release button is in the Vent position | Check the Steam Release switch is set to Seal | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.43; Rio Plus Wide p.41 |
| C7 or NoPr | Heat element has failed | Contact Consumer Care | Duo Plus 60 v4 p.43; Rio Plus Wide p.41 |

## How to prevent the Instant Pot burn message next time

- Meet the minimum thin liquid for your pot size (FAQ, both manuals).
- Add an extra cup of thin liquid for thick sauces (FAQ).
- Deglaze every time after Sauté (FAQ, Duo Plus manual).
- Check the sealing ring and float valve before closing the lid (ATK). Ring condition is covered in [sealing ring smell](/guides/instant-pot-sealing-ring-smell/).
- Don't fill past PC MAX (2/3), or past the ½ line for foods that expand, such as rice (FAQ).
- Check liquid amounts in recipes from elsewhere: ATK notes its own recipes account for it, but other recipes may not.

## Is it safe to keep cooking after a burn warning?

Follow the manual: turn the cooker off, release pressure, inspect. Both manuals say never to force the lid off, and both say that if steam escapes from the sides of the lid, turn the appliance off. No source read here shows a way to override or disable the alert.
