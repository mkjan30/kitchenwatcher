The Instant Pot float valve is the small pin on the lid that rises when the pot reaches pressure and drops when the pressure is gone. Instant's [Duo Plus manual](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/116080-116081-Instant-Pot-Duo-Plus-8qt-6qt-and-3qt-Manual.pdf) says that until it drops, "the lid is locked and cannot be opened," and not to force it. When it won't rise, the same manual's troubleshooting table points to two causes: a lid that isn't fully closed, or too little food or water.

## Float valve vs steam release valve: two different parts

The float valve is a pressure indicator and a lid lock. You don't operate it. The steam release handle (a switch on the Rio models) is the part you set to Seal or Vent.

Instant describes the float valve the same way across its manuals. The Duo Plus manual says "as the cooker heats up and pressure builds, the float valve rises." The [Rio Wide manual](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf) says the "float valve drops into lid when all steam is released." Newer models add an on-screen cue: the [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) says the display shows "OK to Open Lid" once steam is completely released.

## Why is my Instant Pot float valve not rising?

### Is the lid fully closed and locked?

The Duo Plus manual lists "Float valve obstructed by the lid locking pin" as a cause and says to close the lid completely. iFixit's student-written [Duo80 V2 troubleshooting wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Instant_Pot_IP-DUO80_V2_Troubleshooting) gives the same cause. Newer models tell you directly: the Rio Plus Wide displays "Turn to Lock Lid" and won't start pressurizing, and the Rio Wide manual says it "will not start pressurizing unless the lid is locked."

### Is there enough liquid, and is the release on Seal?

The Duo Plus manual says to always add at least 18 fl oz / 500 ml of liquid so enough steam can be generated. Its Getting Started page words the same minimum as "2 cups (~18 fl oz / 500 mL)", though 2 cups is 16 fl oz. Instant's [FAQ](https://instantpot.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions) gives a lower minimum of 1 1/2 cups for a 6-quart pot, so Instant's own documents differ here. The full breakdown by size is in [how much water a pressure cooker needs](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/).

Also check the steam release is on Seal. The Rio Plus Wide manual lists a switch left in Vent as a cause of its C7 and NoPr codes, covered in [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/).

### How long does the float valve take to rise?

A slow-rising Instant Pot float valve is often just a full pot. The Duo Plus manual says preheating can last "from a few minutes to 40 minutes or more," depending on the amount and temperature of the food. Instant's FAQ gives 10 to 40 minutes or longer.

## Instant Pot float valve stuck up or leaking steam

### How to unstick a float valve that stays up after cooking

The Duo Plus manual's instruction is specific: "If the float valve is stuck, move the steam release valve to the Venting position. Once all the steam is released, push the float valve down with a pen or long utensil." Its troubleshooting table adds: press lightly, open the lid cautiously, and clean the float valve and lid before the next use.

The Rio Plus Wide manual warns not to place your face, hands or exposed skin over the steam release valve or float valve while there is residual pressure. Use a utensil, not your fingers.

### Why is steam leaking from the float valve?

The Duo Plus manual lists two causes when steam leaks from the float valve for over 2 minutes: food debris on the float valve silicone seal, and a worn-out silicone ring. Instant's FAQ steam-leak checklist says to ensure the float valve is properly attached and the silicone cap is in good condition, and to "replace the silicone cap if it is damaged or has small gouges before pressure cooking." Leaks from the rim of the lid are a sealing-ring problem, covered in [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/).

## Instant Pot lid won't open: what Instant says to do

Instant's FAQ is direct: if "the float valve is still up and/or the lid is difficult to open, this indicates that the appliance is still pressurized." It says do not force it open, not to open the appliance until it has depressurized, and to contact Customer Care for further assistance.

1. Release pressure the way your recipe directs, keeping your face, hands and skin away from the steam release valve and float valve (Rio Plus Wide manual). Quick release means setting the steam release to Vent. Natural release (switch left on Seal) can take "up to 40 minutes or more" per the Rio Wide manual, or "10 to 40 minutes, or even more" per the Duo Plus manual. The Duo Plus manual allows a wet towel on the metal part of the lid to speed cooling, without covering the steam release; Instant's FAQ also says never to cover the float valve with cloth.
2. Wait for the float valve to drop. If the steam has stopped and it is still up, the Duo Plus manual says to make sure the handle is on Venting, then press the float valve lightly with a long utensil.
3. Once the pin is down (or the display shows "OK to Open Lid" on models that have it), turn the lid counter-clockwise and lift it off.
4. If the pin is down but the inner pot lifts with the lid, that's vacuum. All three manuals say to set the steam release to Vent.

Some advice that ranks for this problem goes further than Instant does. [Paint the Kitchen Red](https://www.paintthekitchenred.com/17-common-instant-pot-problems-and-how-to-solve-them/) says that with the wrong or crooked lid, once everything has cooled completely, the author has had someone hold the base while twisting the lid "with some force." Its lid-won't-close section relays one owner's report of loosening two screws beside the locking pin, flagged "at your own risk." The same page says never to force a lid that is still under pressure.

No Instant document read for this page supports forcing the lid or loosening lid screws; every one says not to force the lid. For release timing, see [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

## How to clean and replace the float valve and silicone cap

The Rio Wide manual lists the float valve and silicone cap among the lid parts. Both Rio manuals say to hand wash lid parts with hot water and mild dish soap or wash them on the dishwasher's top rack, and to "remove all small parts from lid before washing." All three manuals say to check the float valve for clogging before use. Full lid and pot cleaning is in [how to clean an Instant Pot](/guides/how-to-clean-an-instant-pot/).

Replacement parts are model-specific. The Rio Plus Wide manual lists "Float Valve with Silicone Cap" as part 310-0020-01; the Rio Wide manual lists "Float Valve" as 310-1094-01. The Duo Plus manual we read gives no part number. Match the part to your own manual.

## When the float valve itself was a recall remedy

One CPSC recall we found involves this exact part, but it isn't an Instant Pot. In [CPSC recall 24-013](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Best-Buy-Recalls-Insignia-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard) (October 26, 2023), Best Buy recalled Insignia pressure cookers NS-MC60SS8, NS-MC60SS9 and NS-MC80SS9 over incorrect volume markings, after 31 incidents including 17 burn reports.

The remedy was a free replacement inner pot and "floating locking valve for signaling pressure." The notice repeats the rule: make sure the floating locking valve has dropped before opening the lid. Check other models at our [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/), and the wider picture in [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).
