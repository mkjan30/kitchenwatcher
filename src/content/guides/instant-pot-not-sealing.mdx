If your Instant Pot is not sealing, Instant's manuals and FAQ point to a short list of causes. The usual ones are the steam release set to Vent, a sealing ring that is missing, damaged, dirty or not seated behind its rack, or a lid not closed properly. Debris on the float valve and too little thin liquid also stop it sealing. Where the steam escapes tells you which. Instant's rule: turn the cooker off if steam comes from the sides of the lid, or streams steadily from the valve for more than 3 minutes.

Everything below comes from Instant's online FAQ, the troubleshooting tables in the [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) and [Rio Wide manual](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf), and [Wirecutter's pressure cooker testing](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-electric-pressure-cooker/), credited where used. Other models' manuals may word things differently.

## Is some steam normal while an Instant Pot seals?

Yes. Both Rio manuals list "minor steam leaking/hissing from steam release vent during cooking cycle" as normal: the cooker is regulating excess pressure, and no action is needed.

Coming to pressure takes time, and Instant's own sources disagree on how long. Both Rio manuals say it can take 10 to 15 minutes, and longer with frozen food. Instant's FAQ says it "can take anywhere from 10 to 40 minutes or longer", depending on the amount and temperature of the ingredients.

For a third-party benchmark, Wirecutter timed preheating with a pot of beans: 14 min 24 s for the Pro, 14 min 35 s for the Rio, 14 min 48 s for the Duo and 16 min 8 s for the Duo Plus. If yours is well past those numbers and still not pressurized, work through the table below.

## Instant Pot steam leaking from the side of the lid: the ring and rack

Turn the cooker off first. Both Rio manuals say that if steam escapes from the sides of the lid, switch it off, wait until the steam stops and the cooker cools, then check the sealing ring. Then:

1. Pull the ring out from behind the ring rack and clean off any food.
2. Inspect the rack. The Rio Plus Wide manual says it should be secured, centered and an even height all the way around the lid.
3. Place the ring over the rack and press down firmly so there is no puckering. It should be snug and not fall out when you turn the lid over.
4. Instant's FAQ says to turn the inner pot upside down on a flat surface and check that its rim is flush all the way around, and to replace the pot if it looks warped or damaged. The Rio manuals say to contact Consumer Care about a misshapen rim.

The manuals say rings stretch with normal use and should be replaced every 12 to 18 months, or sooner if stretched, deformed or damaged. Instant's FAQ adds to use only authorized Instant Pot rings. Wirecutter notes one clue that a ring is worn: the pot takes noticeably longer to build pressure. For ring odor and buying replacements, see [the sealing ring smell guide](/guides/instant-pot-sealing-ring-smell/).

## Steam coming from the top of the lid? Check the valves

If steam streams from the top of the lid rather than the side, the cause is usually one of these:

- **Steam release on Seal.** On Rio models, the manuals say the Steam Release switch must be set to Seal. Wirecutter reports that Instant Pot models from 2021 on have a steam-release switch and a lid that seals the valve when locked. On older models, you turn the valve to the sealed position yourself. For a steam release handle, Instant's FAQ says it must be seated on the steam post, set to sealing and replaced if loose or damaged. For a vent (quick release) button, the FAQ says it must be in the upward (sealing) position. The FAQ does not say which models have which.
- **Float valve and cap.** Instant's FAQ says to check that the float valve is attached and its silicone cap is in good condition, and to replace a damaged or gouged cap.
- **Clogging.** The Rio Plus Wide manual says to check the steam release valve, steam release pipe, anti-block shield and float valve for clogging before each use. Wirecutter also reports that gunk in the lid valves and rim can stop the pot sealing properly.

For a float valve that won't rise or is stuck, see [the float valve guide](/guides/instant-pot-float-valve/).

## Why won't my Instant Pot pressurize when nothing leaks?

- **Too little thin liquid.** Instant's FAQ gives minimums of 1 cup for a 3 qt, 1½ cups for a 6 qt, 2 cups for an 8 qt and 2½ cups for a 10 qt pot. More in [how much water to use](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/).
- **Food stuck to the bottom after sautéing.** Instant's FAQ lists deglazing the pot after sautéing as a fix for the Burn error, alongside too little liquid and a valve set to venting. If you see a Burn message, see [the burn message guide](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/).
- **A wet lid.** If steam leaks from the lid's plastic molding, Instant's FAQ says moisture may be trapped inside the lid, which can happen if it was submerged; let it dry completely.
- **C7 or NoPr on the display.** The Rio Plus Wide manual's causes are in the table above; other codes are in [the error code guide](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/).

## What not to do when an Instant Pot won't seal

- **Pressing down on the lid.** Instant's manuals and FAQ give no such step; for a lid not closed properly, the Rio manuals say to open it, then close it again.
- **Forcing the lid open.** Instant's FAQ says never force the lid open after pressure cooking, and the Rio Plus Wide manual says only to open it after the float valve has dropped.
- **Repairing the lid yourself.** The Rio Plus Wide manual says not to try to repair a deformed ring rack, and both Rio manuals send a warped rack to Consumer Care.
- **Off-brand parts.** Instant's FAQ says to use only authorized rings, and Wirecutter advises buying rings and parts from the manufacturer to avoid counterfeits.

## Still not sealing? Run the water test, then contact Instant

Run [the water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/) with nothing but water. If steam escapes from the sides of the lid, the Rio manuals say to turn the cooker off. If steam leaks for more than 3 minutes, Instant's FAQ says to record a short video, then turn the cooker off and unplug it.

Per the Rio Plus Wide manual, a warped ring rack, a misshapen pot rim, a pressure sensor failure and a failed heating element all go to Instant Consumer Care. Wirecutter reports that Instant Pots carry a one-year limited warranty.
