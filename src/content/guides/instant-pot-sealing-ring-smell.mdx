An Instant Pot sealing ring smell is food odor held in the silicone: Instant Pot Singapore's help page says the food-grade silicone ring may pick up food odors during cooking. Instant's fix, from its Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals, is 1 cup water plus 1 cup white vinegar in the inner pot, Pressure Cook for 5 to 10 minutes, then Quick Steam Release. Store rings somewhere well ventilated. Replace them every 12 to 18 months, or sooner if stretched, deformed or damaged.

## Why the Instant Pot sealing ring holds onto smells

The sealing ring is food-grade silicone, and [Instant Pot Singapore's help page](https://help.focusglobalinc.com/IPSG/IPSG_Cleaning_and_Maintenance/359) says it may pick up food odors during cooking. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1976321/clean-instant-pot-sealing-ring/) adds that the trapped odor may even carry into your next dish.

Storage makes it worse. [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/instant-pot-smell_l_61522e77e4b03d83bad6efd3) describes the ring as porous and says that if it's loose or stored in the pot while damp, trapped food can feed microorganisms and the smell gets much worse. It quotes Tracy Fadden, vice president of marketing at Instant Brands, recommending cleaning the ring and lid after every use and letting the ring air-dry before storing it. Instant's [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) agrees on the first part: the ring should be cleaned after each use.

## How to get smell out of an Instant Pot ring: Instant's vinegar method

These steps come from Instant's [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) and [Rio Wide manual](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf). Both give the same odor method in their cleaning tables.

1. Remove the sealing ring and wash it with hot water and mild dish soap, or on the top rack of the dishwasher.
2. Press the ring back over the sealing ring rack so it sits snug, with no puckering.
3. Pour 1 cup (250 mL) water and 1 cup (250 mL) white vinegar into the inner pot.
4. Close the lid and run Pressure Cook for 5 to 10 minutes.
5. When it finishes, use Quick Steam Release. The Rio Plus Wide manual's safety section says not to place your face, hands or exposed skin over the steam release valve or float valve while the cooker is operating or still has pressure.
6. Store the ring where air can reach it. The manuals say to store the lid upside down on the cooker base and to store sealing rings in a well-ventilated area to disperse odor; HuffPost's Instant Brands source says to let the ring air-dry first.

None of the other pages we read for this guide gives this method. Instant Pot Singapore, HuffPost and Tasting Table give a 2-minute Steam cycle instead (see the table below).

## Other sealing ring odor methods and who recommends them

Plenty of other fixes circulate. Here is each one, who recommends it, and where it clashes with Instant's manuals.

| Method | What to do (quantities, time) | Who recommends it (source) | Conflicts or cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure cycle with vinegar | 1 cup water + 1 cup white vinegar, Pressure Cook 5-10 min, Quick Steam Release | Instant's Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals | None; this is Instant's own method |
| Steam with vinegar or lemon | 2 cups water or white vinegar plus cut-up lemon rind, Steam 2 min with ring in place, air dry | Instant Pot Singapore help page; Tasting Table (2 cups vinegar or lemon juice, Steam 2 min); HuffPost's writer used 2 cups distilled white vinegar on Steam for 2 min, and HuffPost's Instant Brands source suggested adding lemon slices | Not in the manuals; HuffPost's run is one writer's account, not a measurement |
| Dishwasher, top rack | Top rack; Instant Pot Singapore says high temperature and strong detergent help | Rio manuals (top rack); Instant Pot Singapore says it "will usually remove the majority of smells"; HuffPost quotes Fadden saying the heat helps open the silicone's pores | Owners in The Takeout's report say it doesn't always work |
| Overnight soak | White vinegar, lemon juice, tomato juice or bleach overnight, then dishwasher | Instant Pot Singapore help page | Bleach conflicts with the Rio Plus Wide manual's "never use harsh chemical detergents" on accessories, a list that includes the ring; we don't recommend it |
| Store with an absorber | Keep the ring in a bag with ground coffee or baking soda | Instant Pot Singapore help page | Not published in the manuals |
| Bake in the oven | 250 F for 20-30 min on a baking tray | HuffPost, quoting Fadden on Instant's test kitchens | Rio Plus Wide manual lists the ring among its accessories and says not to use included accessories in a conventional, convection or toaster oven; we follow the manual |
| Sunlight | Not published | Tasting Table says sunlight's UV rays can remove foulness; HuffPost quotes Fadden saying drying in the sun or freezer helps kill bacteria | No measurement given; evidence is thin |

Two of these come from Instant-linked sources but contradict the Rio Plus Wide manual. Nobody we read measured whether the oven bake or the bleach soak works better, and the manual is the document Instant ships with the product, so it wins here. If you try anything beyond the manual method, start with the dishwasher or a Steam cycle, which don't break any manual rule.

## When the smell won't come out: what owners report

Not every ring comes clean. [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com/2022248/instant-pot-silicone-ring-clean-often/) relays members of an Instant Pot Community Facebook group who say the dishwasher doesn't always remove the odor. One member said an overnight zip-top-bag soak in 1/4 cup white vinegar, 1/4 cup lemon juice and water left the rings with no odor; another owner suggested a 3-minute Steam cycle with vinegar, lemon juice and water as a shortcut.

These are individual owner accounts, not measurements, and nobody we read compared methods side by side. If a ring still smells after Instant's pressure-cycle method, the practical answer is a second ring for sweet food rather than more scrubbing.

## Instant Pot ring sweet and savory: why two rings help

Instant's own answer to lingering odor is a second ring. The [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) says to install one ring at a time and use one for sweet dishes and one for savory, and it lists an extra sealing ring in the box alongside the one that comes installed. Instant's FAQ calls the extra ring handy for switching between different dishes.

Only some models include that spare: Instant's FAQ says "some models come with an extra sealing ring", and the Rio Wide manual's box contents list just one. [Instant Pot Singapore's help page](https://help.focusglobalinc.com/IPSG/IPSG_Cleaning_and_Maintenance/359) gives the same savory-and-sweet advice and sells a colored two-pack, which makes the two easy to tell apart. Check your own model's box contents before you buy a spare.

## When to replace the Instant Pot sealing ring: Instant's checklist

Instant's FAQ and both Rio manuals say to replace the sealing ring every 12 to 18 months, and sooner if you notice stretching, deformation or damage.

Before every cook, Instant's guidance amounts to this checklist:

- **Cuts or tears.** The FAQ says to always check for cuts, deformation and correct installation before cooking.
- **Stretching or deformation.** Don't use a stretched, deformed or damaged ring. The FAQ warns that ignoring this may cause food to discharge.
- **Puckering or a loose fit.** The ring should be pressed in with no puckering and shouldn't fall out when you turn the lid over (Rio manuals).
- **Age.** 12 to 18 months since you fitted it.
- **Genuine part.** The Rio manuals' safety sections say to replace it only with an authorized Instant Pot sealing ring. Both list the part as 211-1004-01 for the Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide.

Instant's checklist is about damage and age; odor isn't on it. A damaged or stretched ring is the one Instant says not to use.

One exception: on the Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid, Instant's FAQ says the ring is permanently attached and not designed to be removed. If it's worn or damaged, you replace the whole lid.

## Refitting the ring after cleaning

A ring that goes back on badly can leak, so refit it the way the [Rio Wide manual](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf) describes. Press it over the sealing ring rack with no puckering, then turn the lid over and check it doesn't fall out. Check that the rack is secured, centered and an even height all the way around, and don't try to repair a deformed rack.

If you see steam leaking from the side of the lid on the next cook, the manuals' troubleshooting tables list a missing, damaged or badly installed ring, or food debris on it, as causes; our guide to an [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/) covers the rest. After fitting a new ring, [run a water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/) before cooking food.

Ring fit has caused a recall on another brand. Per the CPSC record, Breville recalled about 35,600 BPR600XL Fast Slow Cookers (batch numbers 1235 to 1529) in December 2015 (recall 16-066). The sealing gasket could be inserted upside down on the lid, allowing an unexpected release of built-up pressure. The remedy was a replacement gasket and updated instructions. Our [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/) lists the others.
