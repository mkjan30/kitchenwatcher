Instant Pot vs Ninja Foodi comes down to the lids, the size and the price, not cooking quality. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker) says its Ninja models "perform almost identically to the Instant Pot" at pressure cooking, and [Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-electric-pressure-cooker/) says the Foodi it tried performed well at pressure cooking and air frying. The other difference is safety: in May 2025 the CPSC announced a recall of about 1.8 million OP300-series Foodi pressure cookers, fixed with a free replacement lid.

## Instant Pot or Ninja Foodi: which to pick

- **You want a plain pressure cooker:** get an Instant Pot. TechGearLab's top-scoring model is the Instant Pot Duo Plus V4, at 86/100 and a $150 list price. See our [Duo Plus review](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/) and the [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) ranking.
- **You want one appliance that pressure cooks and air fries:** either brand will do the job. Wirecutter leans toward the Instant Pot Duo Crisps for their "slightly smaller footprint."
- **You already own an OP300-series Foodi:** check the model number and stop using the pressure function until you have the replacement lid (details below).

Is the Ninja Foodi worth it? For most buyers, the evidence says no, unless air frying in the same pot matters to you. [The evidence is weighed below](#is-the-ninja-foodi-worth-it-what-the-evidence-supports).

## Does the Ninja Foodi pressure cook as well as an Instant Pot?

TechGearLab says the Ninja models "perform almost identically to the Instant Pot" in its cooking tests. The one caveat it gives is that the Smart XL is "a bit inconsistent when it comes to pressure cooking meat." Wirecutter's verdict on the Foodi it tried (named only as the Ninja Foodi Electric Pressure Cooker & Air Fryer) is similar: it performed well at pressure cooking.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) gives the most detail, and both brands had problems. The Ninja OL601 produced great rice and slow-cooked beef stew and baked beans. But it first showed a "NO PRESSURE" error on beef stew, which ATK thought might come from sautéing onions first or using too little liquid. The cooker gave good results after a restart, though its pressure-cooked beans were mushy.

The Instant Pot Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid also made great rice. It gave a C7 error on beef stew, then made stew with tender beef once more liquid was added and the cycle restarted, and its pressure-cooked baked beans were "a little mushy." These are notes on two different machines, not a controlled head-to-head.

## Air frying: where a pressure cooker air fryer combo differs

TechGearLab calls the air frying on its Ninja models "quite effective." ATK found the OL601 cooked unevenly with both racks in and more evenly in a single layer. The Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid made "juicy, golden chicken cutlets" for ATK but "somewhat unevenly cooked french fries."

Other reviewers were harder on the Duo Crisp. [Reviewed](https://reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-electric-multi-cookers-and-instant-pots) found its chicken thighs crisp on top and slightly mushy underneath, and notes it comes with no air fryer basket. Wirecutter calls it "unremarkable as an air fryer." Reviewed describes both its Duo Crisp and the Ninja as pressure cookers first and air fryers second, and points readers who mainly want air frying to dedicated air fryers; see our [best air fryers](/best/air-fryers/) list.

## Lids: one hinged lid, two separate lids, or an attached crisping lid

The three designs work differently day to day:

- **Instant Pot Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid:** Instant's manual says it is "designed to pressure cook and air fry using a single lid." Wirecutter says the hinged lid makes countertop placement tricky and that the sealing ring can't be removed. It also cites reports of the lid jamming.
- **Older Duo Crisps:** Reviewed says the Duo Crisp + Air Fryer (headed "6 Qt" but listed at 8 quarts in its spec line) has separate lids "you can remove and store," something it says you can't do on the Ninja. Wirecutter says the older Duo Crisp 8-quart's lid is detachable.
- **Ninja Foodi FD400:** the owner's guide lists a separate pressure lid and a crisping lid that "is attached to the unit." TechGearLab says the Deluxe XL's crisping lid "can get in the way" and is hard to clean. Wirecutter calls the Foodi's air-fryer lid nondetachable.

Both manuals say the lid stays on until pressure is gone. Instant's says "DO NOT attempt to force the lid off" and not to open the cooker until it has depressurized, and Ninja's FD400 guide says the pressure lid "will not unlock until the unit is completely depressurized." For how each release method works, see [natural vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

## Which is bigger, the Instant Pot or the Ninja Foodi?

The Ninjas in these sources have the bigger pots: 8 quarts for the FD400 and the Smart XL, against 6.5 quarts for the Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid.

By manufacturer spec, the Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid is 6.5 quarts, 1500 W and 23.32 lb, and measures 15.5 x 15.4 x 13.4 inches. Reviewed's spec line for the same Duo Crisp says 27.8 lb and 15.4 inches tall, so the two sources disagree on weight and height. The FD400 has an 8-quart pot and is rated 1760 W, per its owner's guide, which gives no weight. TechGearLab says the Ninja Smart XL weighs 25 lb and measures almost 15 inches tall.

To choose a capacity, see [what size Instant Pot](/guides/what-size-instant-pot/).

## Fill lines and minimum liquid: the two manuals differ

Instant's Duo Crisp with Ultimate Lid manual says not to fill past the "PC MAX — 2/3" line, or past the "1/2" line for foods that expand, such as uncooked beans, applesauce, oatmeal, barley and noodles. It sets a minimum of 2 cups of liquid for the 6.5-quart pot, unless a recipe says otherwise.

Ninja's FD400 guide asks for "at least 1 cup of liquid" and says not to fill past the PRESSURE MAX line. For rice, beans and other foods that expand, it says not to fill the pot more than halfway.

Follow the manual for your own model, and don't carry one brand's minimum over to the other. Our [minimum water guide](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/) covers Instant's sizes.

## The Ninja Foodi OP300 recall: what owners need to do

According to the CPSC record for recall 25-247, dated May 1, 2025, the recall covers "all Ninja Foodi OP300 Series Multi-Function Pressure Cookers," sold in black with a 6.5-quart capacity. The models are OP300, OP301, OP301A, OP302, OP302BRN, OP302HCN, OP302HAQ, OP302HW, OP302HB, OP305, OP305CO and OP350CO, plus OP300-series replacement lids. The model number is printed on a label on the side of the cooker; an extra code after it (the record's example is "OP301 I07") is not part of the model.

About 1,846,400 units were recalled in the US, plus about 184,240 in Canada. The hazard, in the record's words: "The pressure-cooking lid can be opened during use, causing hot contents to escape." SharkNinja received 106 reports of burn injuries, more than 50 of them second- or third-degree burns to the face or body, and 26 lawsuits had been filed.

The remedy: stop using the pressure-cooking function immediately and contact SharkNinja for a free replacement lid. The record says owners "can continue to use the product's air frying and other functions." The cookers sold from January 2019 through March 2025 for about $200, per the record. Its model list includes no OL or FD model numbers.

To check other brands, use our [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/), and see [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

## Error messages: C7, NO PRESSURE and ADD WATER

ATK says it couldn't find C7 in the manual and learned online that it meant the heating element had failed or there wasn't enough liquid. The 140-0068-01 manual copy we read does list it, though: C7 is "Loss of pressure," and the fix is to cancel cooking, turn the cooker off and back on to reset, then contact Consumer Care if that doesn't work. ATK lists the same 140-0068-01 model number, so we can't explain why the two sources disagree.

Ninja's FD400 guide lists "ADD WATER" on the Steam function (the water level is too low) and "ADD WATR" on the Pressure function. For ADD WATR it says to add liquid before restarting and also to check that the pressure release valve is in the SEAL position and the silicone ring is installed correctly. NO PRESSURE comes only from ATK's notes on the OL601; we found no Ninja manual that defines it. For the full list, see [Instant Pot error codes](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/) and the [burn message guide](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/).

## Is the Ninja Foodi worth it? What the evidence supports

On the numbers we have, it's hard to justify for pressure cooking alone. TechGearLab scores the Smart XL 83 and the Deluxe XL 80, at list prices of $350 and $250, against 86 for the Instant Pot Duo Plus V4 at $150 list. Wirecutter calls the Foodi "huge and expensive," and TechGearLab says "Ninja is phasing out some of their Foodi pressure cooker models."

ATK's OL601 entry now sits under Discontinued, and no Ninja made our [pressure cooker ranking](/best/pressure-cookers/). The evidence is thin, though: four reviewers, different Ninja models in each review, and only TechGearLab scoring both brands on the same tests. If you want air frying and pressure cooking in one pot, the Foodi did both well for Wirecutter and TechGearLab. Just budget for the counter space.
