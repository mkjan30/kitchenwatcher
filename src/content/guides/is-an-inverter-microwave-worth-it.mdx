Is an inverter microwave worth it? For most kitchens, no. [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/learn/research/microwave-inverters) compared 7 inverter and 19 non-inverter models and found no gain in heating evenness or in billed energy. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537-the-best-microwave-ovens) found the inverter "didn't provide any measurable benefits" in a Panasonic it rated Not Recommended. Reviewed disagrees, but publishes no comparable measurements.

The one clear inverter win RTINGS found is a tiny, short, low-power job: 2 teaspoons of cold butter at 30%. This page summarizes other people's measurements. Kitchen Watcher tested nothing; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

## What is an inverter microwave?

A regular microwave's magnetron, the tube that makes the microwaves, is either fully on or fully off. Per [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/learn/research/microwave-inverters), an inverter model swaps in a high-frequency switching power supply (MOSFET transistors and a smaller high-frequency transformer). It still switches on and off, but so fast that the wave is effectively on all the time at reduced strength.

[Panasonic](https://www.panasonic.com/ca/consumer/home-appliances-learn/panasonickitchen/welcome-to-the-panasonic-kitchen/technology/30yearsofinverter.html) says it introduced inverter technology in 1988 and that its first inverter model put out 700 W. It markets the feature as "linear power control" that delivers "more even cooking." That is Panasonic's claim, and the page gives no measurements.

You will see the feature under brand names: Panasonic Inverter and Cyclonic Inverter, Toshiba "Origin Inverter Technology", LG "Smart Inverter" (per Reviewed) and Sharp "Inverter Technology".

## Microwave power levels explained: how a regular microwave does 50%

A regular microwave makes "50%" by switching full power on and off. Duty cycle is the share of each cycle the magnetron spends on. PWM (pulse-width modulation) is the name for running power that way.

1. **The bulb test.** [ATK's 2019 power-levels article](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/1843-microwave-cooking-when-to-use-the-power-levels) put an LED bulb in a microwave. Per 1-minute window, it lit for 60 seconds at 100%, about 30 seconds at 50%, about 18 seconds at 30% and about 6 seconds at 10%.
2. **The cycle length.** ATK's 2026 review says a traditional model at 50% runs full power for 15 to 30 seconds, then switches off for 15 to 30 seconds. RTINGS measured the full on-plus-off period at about 30 seconds on every non-inverter model it bought, and found the period doesn't change with cook time.
3. **The gap.** ATK's figures allow a cycle of up to a minute, while RTINGS measured about 30 seconds. Models vary, and neither source claims one figure fits every microwave.
4. **The practical rule.** ATK says it doesn't make sense to use lower power on a traditional microwave for anything shorter than about a minute. Opening the door restarts the on/off cycle.

The rule follows from the numbers. RTINGS puts the "on" window at 30% at about 10 seconds, so a 5-second burst at 30% runs at full power the whole time.

## Inverter vs regular microwave: what the measurements show

| Question | Traditional (PWM) microwave | Inverter microwave | Who measured / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| What happens at 50% power | Full power 15 to 30 s, then off 15 to 30 s | Constant reduced power | ATK 2026 review |
| Below 30% power | Pulses on and off | Also pulses on and off | RTINGS |
| Length of one on/off cycle | About 30 s, fixed regardless of cook time | Very short; wave effectively always on at 30% and above | RTINGS |
| Evenness, chilled mashed potatoes at 50%, 26 probe points | No difference in mean or spread | No difference in mean or spread | RTINGS |
| Billed (active) power, 26 units | Baseline | No lower (higher power factor only) | RTINGS |
| Yearly running cost (Australia) | AUD $7.96 to $18.57 | AUD $12.03 to $17.23 | CHOICE estimate |
| 2 tsp cold butter at 30%, center of turntable | Melted within 10 s | Softened | RTINGS |
| Whipped egg whites at 30% | Overcooked at 3 min (Sharp SMC1162HS) | Overcooked at 2 min (LG LMC0975ST) | RTINGS |

RTINGS heated chilled mashed potatoes at 50% power to the same total microwave energy, 350 kWs, then read 26 probe points in each dish. Inverter models were neither hotter on average nor more even. RTINGS's conclusion is that evenness is set by the food and physics, not the power supply.

[ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537-the-best-microwave-ovens) says traditional and inverter models are "virtually indistinguishable" at full power. Its inverter Panasonic NN-SN67KS landed in Not Recommended: it burned coconut instead of toasting it and overheated and curdled frozen mac and cheese. ATK got better results from its sensor modes than from its inverter.

Why so little difference? Microwaves heat only the outer layer of food, 1 to 3 cm per RTINGS and ¼ to 1 inch per ATK's power-levels article. RTINGS measured turntables at just under 2 RPM, so each spot passes through hot zones for a few seconds at a time. Heat takes minutes to conduct inward, which smooths out a 30-second pulse.

### Delicate foods: butter, chocolate, eggs, defrosting

RTINGS ran each job on a $90 Insignia NS-MW09SS8 (PWM) and a $400 Breville Smooth Wave (inverter). A stick of butter at 30% in 5-second bursts softened in both. The PWM model needed 4 bursts and the inverter 8, because 5 seconds is shorter than the PWM "on" window, so the butter got full power.

Melting 8 oz of chocolate at 30% in 30-second rounds with stirring left it smooth in both models, per RTINGS. Over-easy eggs turned out well in both, and RTINGS rated the scrambled eggs identical. With whipped egg whites at 30%, the inverter LG overcooked them at 2 minutes and the PWM Sharp at 3.

On auto-defrost with 1 lb of ground chuck, the PWM [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS](/reviews/toshiba-em131a5c-bs/) under-defrosted it. The inverter Panasonic NN-SC67NS cooked the edges and used 50% more energy, and RTINGS found its defrost routine pulses too. Entering 1.1 lb on the Toshiba fully defrosted it, with the meat just beginning to cook in one corner. RTINGS concludes that the program design matters more than the inverter.

[CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/home-and-living/kitchen/microwaves/articles/what-is-an-inverter-microwave) found inverter and non-inverter models similar for even heating, reheating, defrosting and cooking time. It is the one source where inverters did better on chocolate. CHOICE credits the inverter models "teamed with auto-cook functionality": they beeped to prompt stirring, or needed no stirring at all, while its regular model didn't beep and produced unusable chocolate.

### Where an inverter does win

RTINGS found one clear case. Two teaspoons of refrigerated butter in the center of the turntable melted within 10 seconds in a PWM model but softened in an inverter. When a cook time is shorter than the PWM "on" window, about 10 seconds at 30%, the average power runs far above the setting. RTINGS recommends an inverter for people who often do tiny, short, low-power jobs, and says the higher price isn't justified for everyone else.

## Does an inverter microwave save electricity?

Not by any measurement I found. RTINGS measured a higher power factor (useful power versus total power drawn from the outlet) on inverter models. It found no lower active power, the figure your electricity bill counts, across its 26 units.

CHOICE estimated yearly running costs in Australian dollars: $7.96 to $18.57 for standard models and $12.03 to $17.23 for inverters, and called them similar. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/2210542/hands-down-microwaves-always-have-feature-inverter/) says an inverter reduces power consumption and electrical costs, but it cites no measurement. The sources disagree, and the measured data shows no saving.

## Why do some reviewers say inverters are better?

[Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/small-appliances/best-right-now/best-countertop-microwaves) (updated Aug 11, 2026) is the main pro-inverter voice. It writes "In our experience, inverter microwaves perform better" and credits its inverter picks with "gentle defrosting". It also says spending more on an inverter is worth it for "consistent, continuous power, better defrosting" and delicate reheating. Tasting Table calls an inverter "essential" and says an inverter model may cost about $50 more, without citing a source for either.

Against that are RTINGS's 26-unit measurements, ATK's Not Recommended inverter model and CHOICE's finding of "minor benefits" that "may not always be noticeable". The difference is method. RTINGS held total energy constant and probed temperatures, while Reviewed publishes no comparable numbers. RTINGS also shows Reddit comments repeating the marketing claims; I don't count forum posts like those as evidence.

## So is an inverter microwave worth it for you?

- **Worth it if** you often soften small amounts of butter, temper tiny portions or do other short low-power jobs, and you won't stir or work in bursts (RTINGS).
- **Not worth it for** reheating, popcorn, beverages or full-power cooking. ATK's power-levels article lists heating water, watery soups or beverages, and making popcorn among the jobs that don't need lower power.
- **What matters more:** CHOICE says to look for "well-designed sensors, auto-cook functions and good visibility" and ease of use. ATK found sensor cooking "helpful but not essential", and got better results from the Panasonic's sensor modes than from its inverter.
- **Same results without one:** use lower power, short bursts, stirring or turning, and standing time, the technique ATK and RTINGS both describe.
