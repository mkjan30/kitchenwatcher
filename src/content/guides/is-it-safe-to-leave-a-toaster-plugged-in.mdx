Is it safe to leave a toaster plugged in? Every manufacturer source I read says to unplug it when you're not using it. GE's manual for the G9TMA2SSPSS and G9TMA4SSPSS says "Unplug from outlet when not in use." Breville's BTA720/BTA730 booklet says to remove the plug if the toaster is not in use, and Dualit says to switch off at the socket. The U.S. Fire Administration says to unplug small appliances when you're not using them.

No source I read puts a number on the risk of an idle toaster starting a fire. CPSC recall records do show specific toasters switching on by themselves, but no source gives a rate for how often it happens.

## Should you unplug your toaster? What manufacturers and fire officials say

Should you unplug your toaster? All four sources below say yes, in plain terms. None of them says leaving it plugged in is fine.

## Can a toaster turn on by itself? What CPSC recalls show

Yes, a toaster can turn on by itself, but the documented cases are rare and tied to specific models. Three CPSC recalls describe a toaster heating without anyone pushing the lever:

- **KitchenAid ProLine toasters, recall 05-142 (March 24, 2005, about 40,000 units).** The [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2005/CPSC-Whirlpool-Announce-Recall-of-KitchenAid-Toasters-) says "An interruption of power to the toaster can cause the heating elements to unexpectedly self-start," which could ignite items left on top. Whirlpool had 3 reports, including one outside the US where items on top of a toaster caught fire; the notice lists no injuries or property damage.
- **Waring Dualit-Plus electronic toasters, recall 95-036 (December 1, 1994, about 2,700 units).** Waring had six reports that the toasters "turned on by themselves after interruptions in the electrical power," per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/1994/cpsc-and-waring-recall-electronic-dualit-plus-toasters). Two involved singeing or scorching next to the toaster; no fires or injuries were reported.
- **Hamilton Beach model 22602, recall 12-057 (December 15, 2011, about 14,000 units).** The heating element "can be energized although the toaster lifter is in the up or off position," per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2012/Toasters-Recalled-by-Hamilton-Beach-Due-to-Fire-Hazard). There were 5 reports of toasters energized when first plugged in.

A fourth recall is different. Hamilton Beach model 22600 (11-265, 2011, about 300,000 units) could keep its element on "indefinitely when an item is placed in the toaster," per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2011/Hamilton-Beach-Recalls-Toasters-Due-to-Fire-Hazard). That is an in-use fault, not an idle one.

There is one field case outside the recall records. In 2016 the Springfield, Mo. Fire Department told [KSMU](https://www.ksmu.org/community-safety/2016-09-01/fire-department-warns-of-potential-danger-of-toasters) that an August 30 apartment fire was believed to have started with a toaster that was plugged in but hadn't been used in several days; investigators found it was the only heat source where the fire started.

Fire Marshal Mark Epps said worn lever springs can let the mechanism sag and turn the element on by itself. That is a general explanation, not a confirmed cause for this fire. KSMU reports $6,000 in damage and minor heat inhalation for the occupant.

No source I read gives a rate for idle self-starts.

## Why an "off" toaster still matters: what's live when it's plugged in

Breville's booklet says its BTA720/BTA730 is electronically controlled and won't operate until plugged in. On plug-in, the LED toasting progress indicator lights up, and the toaster reverts to stand-by after a few minutes if no button is pressed. The booklet never calls stand-by "off": its instruction is "To turn the toaster off, remove the power plug from the power outlet."

GE's manual says the lever won't lock down unless the toaster is connected to the electricity supply. Neither manual explains what stays powered in stand-by.

Neither manual gives a stand-by power figure, so I don't give one either. Some pages claim that "phantom power" can heat the element of a switched-off toaster and ignite crumbs. No source I read supports that, and the documented self-starts above came from specific defects, not normal stand-by power.

## Toaster fire hazard: the risks that apply when it's running

Most toaster fire hazard warnings in the manuals are about the toaster in use, not sitting idle:

- **Curtains and walls.** GE and Breville both say a fire may occur if the toaster is covered or touching flammable material, including curtains, draperies or walls, when in operation. GE recommends 4 in of clearance on all sides (manufacturer spec).
- **Crumbs.** Both manuals say to operate with the crumb tray in place. Breville says failure to clean the crumb tray regularly may result in a fire hazard, and Dualit calls emptying it "vital to avoid a risk of fire." The method is in [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/); note that GE says not to hold the toaster upside down or shake it, while Breville says to turn it upside down over a bin and shake.
- **Walking away.** Breville and Dualit say not to leave the toaster unattended in use. Per the [USFA's cooking fire page](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/), unattended equipment was the leading factor (37%) in nonconfined home cooking fires.
- **Metal.** GE and Breville both say oversized foods, metal foil packages or utensils must not be inserted, and Breville specifically warns against a knife; more in [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).

## When unplugging your toaster matters most

- **Before cleaning or freeing stuck bread.** GE says not to dislodge food while the toaster is plugged in; Breville says to remove the plug first.
- **A damaged cord.** GE says not to operate any appliance with a damaged cord or plug; the USFA says to replace the appliance.
- **An odd smell or sparking.** Dualit says to switch it off, unplug it and contact Dualit.
- **After a power cut, on electronic models.** Both power-interruption recalls (05-142 and 95-036) told owners to unplug immediately.
- **Stored under a cupboard.** Dualit says never plugged in.
- **If your model is recalled.** Check with our [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).
- **Power strips.** Breville's booklet says use with a power strip or extension cord is not recommended and suggests its own outlet; the USFA says not to overload power strips and to use ones with internal overload protection.

## Where the evidence on idle toasters is thin

There is no national statistic for fires started by idle, plugged-in toasters in any source I read. The USFA estimates 170,000 home cooking fires and 24,200 residential building electrical fires in 2021, but neither page breaks out toasters.

The Springfield case is one department's finding on one fire. The self-start recalls are old (1994 to 2011) and model-specific. My bottom line: the risk isn't measured anywhere I could find, the advice from every source I read is to unplug, and unplugging costs nothing.

## If your toaster turned on by itself or won't shut off

Unplug it and stop using it. Then check whether the model is recalled using our [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/); GE's manual also says not to use an appliance after it malfunctions and to contact Consumer Support. If it is burning right now, go to [what to do in a toaster fire](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).
