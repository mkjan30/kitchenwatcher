The KitchenAid dime test checks beater height: put a dime in an empty stainless bowl, attach the flat beater and run the lowest speed. If the dime gets a small nudge on each pass, the clearance is fine. If it never moves, the beater is too high; if it is swept around nonstop, it is too low. Fix it with one adjustment screw, which KitchenAid says turns no more than 1/4 turn either way.

Everything below comes from KitchenAid's support pages and the publications credited in each section; nobody here ran the test. See [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What the KitchenAid dime test checks

KitchenAid's [beater-to-bowl clearance article](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Product_Info/Stand_Mixer_Assembly_and_Operation/Adjust_the_Beater_to_Bowl_Clearance) says the mixer leaves the factory set so the flat beater just clears the bowl bottom. Its [bowl-lift troubleshooting page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Bowl_Lift_Mixer/Beater_Concerns/Beater_Hitting_Sides_or_Bottom_-_Bowl-Lift_Mixer) adds that this setting can change over time.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/2728-keep-your-kitchenaid-mixer-running-smoothly-with-this-simple-fix) (Andrew Janjigian, 2020) puts the right beater to bowl clearance at 1/16 inch and uses a dime as the gauge. A Baker's House also calls a dime about 1/16 inch thick.

That is close but not exact. Per [Wikipedia's dime article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dime_(United_States_coin)), a US dime is 1.35 mm (0.053 in) thick, a little under 1/16 in (0.0625 in). So the dime is a handy stand-in, not a precision feeler gauge. That is why the test watches how the dime moves, not whether it fits.

## Signs your KitchenAid beater height is off

KitchenAid's support pages list these symptoms:

- **Too high:** ingredients at the bottom of the bowl are not mixed in. On a tilt-head, a beater set too high can also stop the head from locking ([head bouncing page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Operation/Head_Bouncing_or_Wobbling_-_Tilt-head_Mixer)).
- **Too low:** the beater hits the bottom, the white coating chips on the underside, the bowl gets scratched and the head may bounce up and down ([tilt-head beater hitting bottom page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Beater_Concerns/Beater_Hitting_the_Bottom_of_the_Bowl_-_Stand_Mixer)).
- **Beater hitting the side on a bowl-lift:** KitchenAid says the bowl may look level without being locked. Press down on the back of the bowl until it snaps before you touch the screw.

Where the chip sits matters. KitchenAid's [coating article](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Beater_Concerns/White_Beater_Coat_Chipping_-_Stand_Mixer) says chips on the bottom mean the beater is too low. Chips on the side come from knocking it against the bowl, and chips on top from forcing it on or off the shaft.

## How to do the KitchenAid dime test, step by step

These steps combine KitchenAid's support pages, ATK and [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/article/the-kitchenaid-mixer-fix-you-need-to-know/):

1. **Use a stainless steel bowl.** KitchenAid and Taste of Home both say the test is not for glass or ceramic bowls.
2. **Lock the bowl in.** On a tilt-head, KitchenAid's [assembly page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Product_Info/Stand_Mixer_Assembly_and_Operation/Assemble_and_Adjust_a_Tilt_Head_Mixer) says to set it on the clamping plate and turn it gently clockwise. On a bowl-lift, press the back of the bowl down until it snaps.
3. **Put the dime flat in the bottom of the empty bowl**, in the beater's path.
4. **Attach the flat beater.** KitchenAid says to slip it onto the shaft, press up and turn it right over the pin. Then lock the head down, or raise the bowl.
5. **Run the lowest speed for a few seconds** and watch the dime. ATK says lowest speed; Taste of Home and A Baker's House say Stir or low.
6. **Turn the speed off and unplug the mixer** before you reach for the dime or the screw.

KitchenAid's [tilt-head stand mixer manual](https://c.searspartsdirect.com/mmh/lis_pdf/OWNM/L0710518.pdf) (part 9709785, 2007) says: "Avoid contacting moving parts. Keep hands, hair, clothing, as well as spatulas and other utensils away from beater during operation." It also says to unplug the mixer before putting on or taking off parts. So never reach in for the dime while the beater is turning.

## How to read the KitchenAid dime test result

ATK and Taste of Home agree on the two failure readings. A dime that never moves means too much clearance, so the beater is too high. A dime pushed around continuously means too little clearance, so the beater is too low.

The target in between is where the sources split. KitchenAid's clearance article gives no distance at all; it only says a correctly adjusted beater will not strike the bottom or side of the bowl.

The reading every source agrees with is slow, small nudges on each pass. Don't chase an exact number nobody official publishes.

## Where the KitchenAid beater height adjustment screw is

### Tilt-head mixers (KSM150PS, K45SS)

Tilt the head back. ATK says the screw sits in the hinge where the top and bottom of the mixer meet, and Taste of Home describes it as where the head meets the stand. [A Baker's House](https://www.abakershouse.com/the-dime-test-for-your-kitchenaid-mixer/) says it is a silver screw and warns that it is not either of the large black screws on the sides of the head.

### Bowl-lift mixers (KSM70SKXX and similar)

KitchenAid's clearance article shows two possible positions: screw A, or on some models a tiny screw at B. ATK places the bowl-lift screw in the neck and says to remove the paddle and bowl first. Taste of Home says it is inside the stand. KitchenAid says mixers made before 1980 have no adjustment screw at all and need an Authorized Service Facility.

For which design suits you, see [tilt-head vs bowl-lift mixers](/guides/tilt-head-vs-bowl-lift-mixer/). Here it only changes where you look.

## Which way to turn the screw, and why sources contradict each other

For tilt-heads, KitchenAid's tilt-head manual is the clearest source: turn the screw "SLIGHTLY" counterclockwise (left) to raise the flat beater, or clockwise (right) to lower it, until the beater just clears the bowl. KitchenAid's Evergreen page agrees, and so does A Baker's House (turn right when the blade is too high).

For bowl-lifts, KitchenAid's clearance article says counterclockwise raises the bowl and clockwise lowers it. Only a slight turn is needed: the screw will not rotate more than 1/4 turn (90 degrees) either way, a full range of 1/2 turn. Some bowl-lift models made after April 2003 turn the opposite way, so if one direction does not help, KitchenAid says to try the other.

The wording is the problem. Raising a bowl brings it closer to the beater, and the same article says the tilt-head screw "is the same". Yet the tilt-head manual says counterclockwise raises the beater, away from the bowl. ATK says clockwise increases clearance, the reverse of KitchenAid's tilt-head manual.

So don't trust a direction from any page, including this one. Unplug, make one small turn, run the dime test again, and repeat. KitchenAid's tilt-head manual warns that over-adjusting the screw may stop the motor head locking when lowered. Its head bouncing page says the same of a beater set too high. A head that will not lock is the sign you have overshot.

## Glass, ceramic and wooden bowls: skip the dime

KitchenAid says the dime test works for stainless bowls only, not glass or ceramic. Because of how glass is made, the clearance is slightly different, so it says to adjust without the dime until mixing works. Its bowl-lift page adds that yeast dough may take longer to come together in a glass bowl.

For the Evergreen wooden-bowl tilt-head, KitchenAid's [Evergreen support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Other/Evergreen_Design_Series_-_Wooden_Bowl/Beater_Hitting_Base_of_Bowl) gives a different, unpowered method. Put a dime in the center of the bowl, attach the flat beater and lower the head until it meets the dime. Turn the neck screw 1/4 turn at a time until the head locks with the beater just touching the dime, then remove the dime.

## If the dime test does not fix your KitchenAid beater hitting the bowl

- **Check the bowl is seated.** A bowl-lift bowl must snap in, per KitchenAid.
- **Check the silver neck pin.** KitchenAid says a loose neck pin can make the head bounce. ATK's 2023 article says that on some models, such as the Classic and Artisan, you tap it back flush with a mallet, then tighten the screw underneath the mixer.
- **Replace a chipped white beater.** KitchenAid says one worn on the bottom from hitting the bowl should be replaced.
- **Contact KitchenAid service** if it still hits, as its bowl-lift page advises.

A little bounce while kneading is normal, and KitchenAid recommends speed 2 for kneading. Grease or motor faults are a different problem: see [KitchenAid mixer leaking oil](/guides/kitchenaid-mixer-leaking-oil/) and [stand mixer overheating](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/).
