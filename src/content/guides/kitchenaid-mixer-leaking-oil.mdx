If your KitchenAid mixer is leaking oil, [KitchenAid's help article](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Other/Noise_and_Pouring_Shield/Oil_Leaking_from_Stand_Mixer) says it is oil separating from the solid gear grease, which is packed in to last the mixer's lifetime. KitchenAid calls the grease food grade and approved for incidental food contact. Its fix: run the empty mixer on speed 10 for 2 minutes, then wipe. For a permanent fix, KitchenAid sends you to a service facility.

This page is research-based: nobody here opened a mixer. It draws on KitchenAid's own documents, iFixit and a repair shop, as explained in [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Why is my KitchenAid mixer leaking oil?

KitchenAid's help article lists three places a KitchenAid mixer leaking oil shows it:

- A few drops in the bottom of the bowl.
- Oil dripping from the KitchenAid beater shaft into a batch.
- Oil leaking from the lock-lever or speed-control slots, if the mixer was turned on its side during shipping or storage.

The mechanism, per KitchenAid: the gearcase is packed with solid grease that lubricates the gears as you mix. Over time some oil can separate from that grease and settle at the lowest point of the motor head. KitchenAid says it happens more often in warm weather and when the mixer sits unused for a while. [iFixit's KSM150PSER troubleshooting wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/KitchenAid_KSM150PSER_Troubleshooting) agrees: if the mixer sits too long or is old, oil may separate from the gear grease and drip out while it runs.

For context on how much grease is in there, KitchenAid's [2005 stand mixer service manual](https://documents.cdn.ifixit.com/SIy6Mpd5TF2trML1.pdf) (LIT4177310-C, hosted by iFixit) says the gearcase holds 6 fluid ounces of Benalene 930-2 grease "or equivalent". It adds that gearcases lubricated with this grease "will not require lubrication for years." The manual covers tilt-head and bowl-lift models from the K45SS to the KSM150 and KSM5.

## Is KitchenAid mixer grease food safe?

KitchenAid says yes, for incidental contact. In its words, "The grease is food grade and is approved for incidental contact with food prepared in the mixer," although it may discolor light-colored frostings or mashed potatoes.

What "food grade" means is less clear than the phrase suggests. KitchenAid's page names no standard or certification. The US rule for this kind of lubricant is FDA regulation [21 CFR 178.3570](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-178/subpart-D/section-178.3570), which covers lubricants with incidental food contact on machinery used to produce, prepare or hold food. It caps mineral oil at 10 parts per million in food and dimethylpolysiloxane (viscosity above 300 centistokes) at 1 part per million, and it requires using the minimum amount needed to do the job.

KitchenAid does not say its grease meets that rule, and I found no source that confirms an NSF H1 rating for it. So I am not claiming either.

In practice, KitchenAid's position is that incidental contact is acceptable. Whether to eat a visibly oily batch of frosting is your call; evidence beyond KitchenAid's own statement is thin.

## How to stop the oil: KitchenAid's own steps

If you are finding KitchenAid mixer grease in the bowl, these are the steps from KitchenAid's help article, in order:

1. If the mixer has not been used for a while, run it on speed 10 with nothing in the bowl for 2 minutes before you use it.
2. Turn it off and wipe the excess oil from around the planetary.
3. Fit the pouring shield while you do this to cut down on splatter.
4. Run the mixer empty now and then to help prevent the oil from building up.
5. Avoid overuse and let the mixer cool completely between uses; KitchenAid says overuse without cooling can also cause the leak. The [stand mixer overheating guide](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/) covers shut-offs and cooling.

KitchenAid's help article also says the leak will not harm the mixer, that adding oil is not part of regular maintenance, and that a mixer that sounds normal and is not grinding can keep being used. Before any work beyond wiping, the service manual's caution applies: always remove the power cord from the outlet before servicing any part of the mixer.

## When a KitchenAid mixer keeps leaking oil: service vs DIY regrease

If the leak continues after the speed-10 routine, KitchenAid says only a service facility will stop it completely. The service removes the old grease, repacks the gearcase with new grease and replaces the gasket.

KitchenAid also says getting it serviced is "entirely up to the consumer". If your mixer is still under warranty, check KitchenAid's warranty terms before you open the case yourself.

For readers weighing DIY, KitchenAid's service manual describes the job. It covers taking apart the gearcase, cleaning out as much of the old grease as possible, refilling with 6 oz of Benalene 930-2, fitting a new gearcase gasket and tightening the four bottom-cover screws to 18-20 in-lb. It warns not to pry near the transmission gasket, because that can stop the gasket sealing. The manual is written for professional service technicians, and this page doesn't give step-by-step disassembly.

iFixit's KSM150PSER wiki gives the same fix: replace the old gear grease and clean out as much of the old grease as you can.

In an [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/812788/Oil+or+grease+leaking+from+bottom+of+stand.) opened in September 2023 about a 15-year-old Artisan leaking from the bottom of the stand, a January 2026 answer suggests a cheaper first step. Remove the drip ring and tighten the Phillips screws around the perimeter. If the screws needed tightening and less than a tablespoon has leaked, that commenter says you are probably fine; otherwise, open it, replace the grease and inspect the gasket. Chapel Hill Forge warns that over-tightened screws can deform the gasket, so tighten, don't crank.

After any regrease, check beater clearance with the [KitchenAid dime test](/guides/kitchenaid-dime-test/).

### Where sources disagree

KitchenAid says the grease lasts the lifetime of the mixer. [Chapel Hill Forge](https://www.chapelhillforge.com/kitchenaid-stand-mixer-repair/), a repair shop, suggests servicing the seals and grease about every 10 years, depending on use. The shop also lists causes KitchenAid's help article doesn't mention: overfilling the bowl, damaged gears, worn gaskets and over-tightened screws that deform the gasket. Treat those as a repair shop's view.

Owners disagree too. In an older [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/197052/Oil+or+grease+leaking+into+mixing+bowl) opened in 2014 about a K45SS leaking into the bowl, the 2014 answer marked as the solution says the lubricant has separated and should be cleaned out and replaced. A 2020 answer calls the leak normal and food grade and advises against opening the mixer unless it grinds.

## Does this mean my mixer is defective or recalled?

No recall covers a KitchenAid mixer leaking oil. On Oct 5, 2026 I searched CPSC's recall database (its SaferProducts.gov API) for "stand mixer" and for "KitchenAid". Neither showed a KitchenAid or Whirlpool stand-mixer recall. The "stand mixer" search returned only a 1990 Sunbeam Mixmaster recall. The KitchenAid-titled recalls cover food processors (2000), toasters and coffeemakers (2005) and electric kettles (2018).

[Snopes rated a viral KitchenAid "lead recall" rumor False](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/) in January 2023. To check your own model, see the [stand mixer recall check](/guides/stand-mixer-recall-check/).
