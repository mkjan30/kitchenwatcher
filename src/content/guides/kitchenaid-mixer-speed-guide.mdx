Here is the KitchenAid mixer speed guide from the 5-qt Artisan's Use & Care manual (W11118281B). Start every recipe on Stir. Knead yeast dough on speed 2 with the dough hook, never higher. Use 4 for cookies and other semi-heavy batters, 6 for creaming, 8 for whipping cream and egg whites, and 10 for small amounts of either. The odd numbers in between are for finer adjustment.

Unless credited otherwise, everything below is quoted or paraphrased from [KitchenAid's Use & Care guide W11118281B](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/KitchenAid-Artisan-Series-5Qt-Tilt-Head-KSM150PS-Use-Care.pdf), which covers the K45 series and the KSM45 to KSM200 series, including the Artisan KSM150. I checked it against KitchenAid's own support pages, and say where they add something.

## KitchenAid stand mixer speed chart: what each setting is for

| Speed | Manual's label | What the manual says to use it for | Attachment (per manual) | Source (manufacturer spec) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stir | Stir | Slow stirring, combining, mashing, starting all mixing; adding flour, dry ingredients and liquids. "Do not use Speed 1" for yeast dough | Flat beater or flex edge beater | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 2 | Slow mixing | Mixing and kneading yeast doughs, heavy batters, candies; starting mashed potatoes; cutting shortening into flour; thin or splashy batters | Dough hook for yeast dough; flat beater or flex edge beater otherwise | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 4 | Mixing, beating | Semi-heavy batters such as cookies; combining sugar and shortening; adding sugar to egg whites for meringue; medium speed for cake mixes | Flat beater, flex edge beater or wire whip | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 6 | Beating, creaming | Medium-fast beating (creaming) or whipping; finishing cake, doughnut and other batters; high speed for cake mixes | Flat beater, flex edge beater or wire whip | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 8 | Fast beating, whipping | Whipping cream, egg whites, boiled frostings | Wire whip | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 10 | Fast whipping | Small amounts of cream or egg whites; final whipping of mashed potatoes | Wire whip | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |
| 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 | Not published | Set between the listed speeds "if a finer adjustment is required" | Not published | Manufacturer spec / KitchenAid manual |

The attachment column comes from the accessory icons in the manual's speed chart (page 6). The dough hook appears only on the speed 2 row. The manual's accessory chart pairs the flat beater with normal to heavy mixtures, the wire whip with mixtures that need air, and the dough hook with yeast doughs. The flex edge beater is sold separately.

Two operating notes from the same manual: start on the lowest speed and increase gradually, and lift the speed lever slightly as you move it if it feels stiff. KitchenAid's [speed control support page for the 3.5-qt Mini](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Mini_3.5_Quart_Tilt-Head_Stand_Mixer/Mixer_Performance/Stand_Mixer_Speed_Control_Guide) adds that all speeds have a Soft Start feature, which starts the mixer at a lower speed and then quickly ramps up to the one you selected. The W11118281B manual doesn't mention Soft Start.

## What speed to knead dough on a KitchenAid

Speed 2, with the dough hook, every time. The manual says: "Do not exceed Speed 2 when preparing yeast doughs as this may cause damage to the Stand Mixer." Its kneading section is stricter still. It says to always use the dough hook and that "use of any other speed creates high potential for unit failure."

The manual also says not to use Speed 1 to mix or knead yeast dough. So the rule on the 5-qt tilt-head is speed 2, not "low".

KitchenAid's [Making Bread support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Product_Info/Stand_Mixer_Tips_and_Tricks/Making_Bread_in_a_KitchenAid_Stand_Mixer) recommends speed 2 as well, but gives no fixed knead time. It says the kneading time in most bread recipes can be cut significantly, and that the time depends on the type of flour, the amount of water and added ingredients such as butter, eggs or sugar. Kneaded dough should be "smooth, slightly tacky (but not sticky), and elastic." Over-kneading makes it tough and tight.

The limit has a cost. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) reports getting good results on speed 2 with doughs that call for higher speeds, but says it took "significantly longer" than on machines without speed restrictions. ATK also reports that KitchenAid used to recommend kneading no more than 4 to 6 minutes at a time, and that in 2024 it dropped that time limit. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) reports the Artisan kneaded a whole-wheat sandwich loaf in 7 minutes. It doesn't say which speed it used.

### KitchenAid speed 2 with the dough hook: how much flour

The manual sets these limits for yeast dough:

- 4.8-L bowl (about 5 qt): no more than 8 cups (1 kg) all-purpose flour or 6 cups (800 g) whole-wheat flour.
- 4.28-L bowl (about 4.5 qt): no more than 7 cups (900 g) all-purpose flour or 6 cups (800 g) whole-wheat flour.

Those are KitchenAid's ceilings, not a measured working volume. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) put the Artisan's usable capacity at about 3 quarts against its 5-qt label. For sizing a bowl to your recipes, see [what size stand mixer you need](/guides/what-size-stand-mixer-do-i-need/). For when the hook beats the paddle, see [dough hook vs paddle](/guides/dough-hook-vs-paddle/).

### Is Stir the same as speed 1?

The manual never says so outright, but its Stir row carries the warning "Do not use Speed 1 to mix or knead yeast doughs." KitchenAid's support chart for the 3.5-qt Mini lays the same row across both Stir and 1, with the same warning, and lists speed 2 separately for yeast dough. Both sources say not to exceed speed 2. Follow the manual for your model.

## What speed to whip cream and egg whites on a KitchenAid

The manual gives thresholds by amount, always with the wire whip and turning up gradually:

- 1 egg white: gradually to 10.
- 2 or more egg whites: gradually to 8.
- Less than 3/4 cup (200 ml) cream: gradually to 10.
- More than 3/4 cup (200 ml) cream: gradually to 8.

It warns that there are "just a few seconds between whipping stages," so watch closely.

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) timed the Artisan whipping 1 cup of cream to stiff peaks in 1 minute 50 seconds. It doesn't publish the speed it used, so treat that as a rough idea of how fast this goes, not a setting.

## What speed for cake batter, cookies and add-ins

From the manual's mixing tips:

1. Start on Speed 1 until the ingredients are blended, then increase gradually to the speed you need.
2. For packaged cake mixes, use Speed 4 for medium and Speed 6 for high, for the time on the package.
3. Mix cookie and other semi-heavy batters on 4 with the flat beater.
4. Fold in nuts, raisins or candied fruit during the last few seconds of mixing on Speed 1.
5. Mix very liquid mixtures on lower speeds to avoid splashing, and speed up only after they thicken.

The manual also says a KitchenAid mixes faster and more thoroughly than most electric mixers, so recipe times should be cut back. Mix until the batter looks the way the recipe describes.

## What are low, medium and high on a KitchenAid?

The manual maps recipe words to numbers in one place only: cake mixes, where 4 is medium and 6 is high. It gives no full low-medium-high chart, so I won't invent one. For anything else, match the task to the speed chart above.

Cuisinart's [SM-50C instruction booklet](https://www.cuisinart.ca/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-master-ca/en_CA/v1726597154045//Instruction%20Booklet/SM-50C.pdf) has 12 speeds and lists kneading bread dough on speeds 3 and 4, and pasta dough on 4. A recipe written for a Cuisinart that says "speed 4" for dough is past KitchenAid's limit. The [Cuisinart SM-50 review](/reviews/cuisinart-sm-50/) and [Cuisinart SM-50 vs KitchenAid KSM150PS](/vs/cuisinart-sm-50-vs-kitchenaid-ksm150ps/) compare the two.

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) says more speeds don't necessarily mean better control. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) found the number of speeds mattered less than whether they stepped up meaningfully.

## If the mixer strains, heats up or stops at speed 2

The manual says the mixer may warm up under heavy loads and that this is normal. If the mixer fails to operate, it says to check the plug and the circuit, turn the mixer off for 10 to 15 seconds and back on, and if it still won't start, let it cool for 30 minutes. KitchenAid's [Mixer Shuts Off page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Bowl_Lift_Mixer/Operation/Mixer_Shuts_Off_-_Stand_Mixer) says some mixers have a thermal overload switch that turns the mixer off if it overheats. It puts the cool-down at about 30 minutes, and says a shutdown from steam on the circuit board clears in about 3. ## Speed settings on the Mini, Classic and bowl-lift models

The Classic K45 is covered by the same W11118281B manual, so the chart above applies. KitchenAid's support chart for the 3.5-qt Mini uses the same Stir-to-10 layout and the same speed 2 limit for yeast dough.

The 7-qt bowl-lift is different. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) says the 7-qt model it tested (KSM70SNDXBM, a sibling of the KSM70SKXX) has 11 speeds, including a "half speed" for folding delicate ingredients. The W11118281B manual doesn't cover that model, so check its own manual for the speed chart.
