A microwave air fryer combo is worth it mainly when counter space is the constraint, because it does not match a dedicated air fryer. [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/microwaves) says the Ninja Crispi MW101 air fries more slowly than most dedicated Ninja air fryers, and its review says it preheats slowly and struggles to hold a steady temperature. [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/best-microwaves,review-6316.html) says the IKEA GÅTEBO "won't replace your air fryer."

I compiled this from independent reviews, manufacturer manuals and a Consumer Reports safety alert. I did not use either unit; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

## Is a microwave air fryer combo worth it? The short verdict

Worth it if:

- You have room for one appliance, not two.
- You reheat leftovers you want crisp. Tom's Guide and TechRadar both praised the MW101's FusionCrisp mode, which microwaves and then air fries in one program.
- You are replacing both a microwave and an air fryer anyway.

Skip it if:

- You already own an air fryer you're happy with. [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/air-fryers/ninja-crispi-microwave-review) says to buy a microwave "a third of the price" instead.
- The spot under your wall cabinets lacks a foot of clearance.
- You mostly just microwave.

Every microwave air fryer combo review I read is a single-unit review or a roundup of other outlets, so treat the verdicts as a few people's judgment, not a consensus.

## Combi microwave vs air fryer: what the reviews show

RTINGS is the only lab I found that has run a combo through its air-fry tests, and its detailed scores are member-only. Its public summary of the MW101 says the unit "produces crispy, well-cooked food, but it preheats slowly and struggles to maintain a steady temperature once it reaches it." Its best-microwaves list adds that it air fries more slowly than most dedicated Ninja air fryers, "but its speed is still reasonable," with fried food that comes out mostly crispy and golden brown.

For the GÅTEBO, Tom's Guide says it did a "decent job" crisping fries and toasting bread when its reviewer put it head-to-head with an actual air fryer. The roundups agree in general terms. [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1878653/are-multifunction-microwave-appliances-worth-it) says the reviews it cites agree that air frying "falls short for combination machines." [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1896167/kitchen-appliances-multi-purpose-microwaves-worth-it) says the TechRadar and CNET reviews it summarizes found the air fryer function can take extra time to cook some foods.

The evidence is thin in one important way. No source I could read published side-by-side cook times for a combo and a standalone air fryer with the same food, so "slower" is reviewer judgment. ## Air fryer microwave clearance: the hidden cost

Combos vent hot air, and that changes where they can go. IKEA's GÅTEBO manual asks for at least 11.8 inches (30 cm) above the oven, 8 inches (20 cm) from any adjacent wall and 8 inches from the front edge of the counter. Tom's Guide says the MW101 needs 11.81 inches above it and should not be wall-mounted or placed in a cabinet. TechRadar says 12 inches, and its reviewer could not put the unit where the old microwave sat under a wall cupboard.

Measure before you buy, especially if the combo will replace a microwave tucked under cabinets.

Weight is the other practical issue. The GÅTEBO weighs 35 lb per its manual, and Tom's Guide lists the MW101 at 41 lb and calls unboxing it "a two-man job," with a glass dish that is heavy when full. For chamber sizes and what fits on a counter, see [what size microwave do I need](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Microwave performance: the part you'll use most

A combo is still mostly a microwave, so its microwave mode matters more than its air fryer. [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/ikea/gatebo-countertop) says the GÅTEBO's 900 W makes it "not the fastest microwave," and that some microwaves with the same power heat food more quickly, though "it's not a major difference." It also notes a small chamber, a membrane touch panel where complex tasks take many more steps, and no steam sensor. Tom's Guide says it takes over three minutes to heat soup to the middle of the bowl.

Sources disagree on one GÅTEBO feature. Tom's Guide lists sensor cooking, while RTINGS says it lacks a steam sensor and the IKEA manual describes none.

RTINGS calls the MW101 a good everyday microwave that heats food quickly with straightforward controls, but with a medium-sized chamber. Its best-microwaves list says it cooks "a tiny bit faster" than the Breville Smooth Wave, a 1,200 W model, and that it has a steam sensor. RTINGS is inconsistent on noise: the review says it operates quietly, while the list says it is louder than the other picks there. The MW101 has no inverter, per RTINGS.

## Ninja Crispi microwave: what reviewers agree on and where they differ

The Ninja Crispi microwave (MW101) is a different product from Ninja's Crispi portable glass air fryer. RTINGS calls the MW101 a "good everyday microwave" with a medium chamber and slow, unsteady air-fry preheat. TechRadar, which reviewed the UK model, calls it an "excellent all-rounder" if you have the counter space. It lists a dim interior light, a fingerprint-prone front and no steam cooking function.

[Tom's Guide's Camilla Sharman](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/kitchen-dining/ive-been-testing-ninjas-microwave-air-fryer-hybrid-for-2-weeks-now-i-dont-want-it-to-leave-my-kitchen) (2026-10-10) says she couldn't always find FusionCrisp timings in the guide, so she looked some up online. She followed the guide's recipe for a whole 1.5 kg chicken without flipping it; the top tested done, but the underside was not at a safe temperature when she checked it with a thermometer. Her fix next time is to turn the chicken halfway through. She also found the glass dish heavy and tricky to remove from the top position.

All three are single-unit reviews, and they disagree mainly on how much the size and headroom matter.

## Accessory rules that differ from a normal microwave

Combos come with metal accessories, and the rules for them change by mode. IKEA's GÅTEBO manual says not to place the air fry basket or grill tray inside the oven when the mode is microwave only. It also says to always set them on the glass tray when cooking with them.

Tom's Guide says the MW101's glass dish and crisper plate mustn't be used with the standard microwave function. The dish goes in an upper or lower position depending on the mode.

Consumer Reports' safety alert quotes one owner report in which a metal convection rack was inside a Toshiba combo when it started on its own, and the unit was "sparking with flames." CR's advice for owners of the affected Toshiba models includes not storing items inside the microwave. ## Safety reports on Toshiba combo models

[Consumer Reports' safety alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) of 2026-06-25 covers 28 incidents reported on SaferProducts.gov between January 2023 and April 2026 of Toshiba microwaves starting on their own. All but four involve the ML2-EC10SA or ML2-EC10SA(BS) air fryer-microwave, and two involve the ML2-EC09SA combo. CR advises owners to monitor these units while they run and leave them unplugged when not in use, and it has asked the CPSC to investigate.

Midea, which makes Toshiba-brand microwaves, told CR that "a limited number of units" of the ML2-EC10SA have a power button that can stick and trigger the 30-second add-time function. It said it found "no evidence that this issue poses a safety risk," and that owners who've had the problem can call 866-646-4332 for evaluation and free service. The CPSC recall database lists no recall for Toshiba or Midea microwaves as of 2026-11-27, so this is a safety alert, not a recall. The full story is in [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/), and you can check any model with the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## Price: one combo vs two appliances

TechRadar lists the MW101 at $449.99 and has seen about $50 off in the US. Tom's Guide says it launched at $449. On Nov 27, 2026, during Black Friday week, Tom's Guide's price widget showed it at $349.99 at Amazon, Best Buy and SharkNinja, a sale price marked down from $449.99. The GÅTEBO was $199.00 on IKEA's product page the same day, with no markdown shown; Tom's Guide says "$200."

Whether that is a saving depends on what you already own. If you have a working air fryer, a combo replaces only the microwave, and TechRadar's advice to buy a cheaper plain microwave applies.
