What to do in a microwave fire: keep the door closed and turn the oven off. Then unplug it, or shut off power at the fuse or circuit breaker panel. That is the shared instruction in the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS, Breville BMO650 and IKEA GÅTEBO manuals. If the flames don't die out, or smoke is spreading, alert others, leave and call 911 from a mobile or neighbor's phone, as the U.S. Fire Administration advises.

Everything below comes from manufacturer manuals, U.S. fire and safety agencies, CPSC recall records, Consumer Reports and two secondary sources named where used. Nobody here has handled a microwave fire for this page; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What to do if your microwave catches fire, in order

1. **Keep the door closed.** The [NIH Division of the Fire Marshal](https://news2use.ors.nih.gov/Pages/Fire-Safety-Tips-for-Using-Microwave-Ovens.aspx) says never to open it until you are absolutely certain the fire is out.
2. **Turn it off.** NIH says this stops the fan, so it won't feed oxygen to the flames.
3. **Unplug it, or shut off power at the fuse or breaker panel.** All three manuals say this. [Highspeed Training](https://www.highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/microwave-safety/), a UK safety-training site, says to turn it off and unplug it only if it is safe to do so.
4. **Wait.** NIH says to "simply wait until the fire suffocates."
5. **If it's still burning or smoke is spreading, leave.** The [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/) says to alert others, leave the building and call 911 from a mobile or neighbor's phone.

None of the sources read for this page suggests water or carrying the oven outside. What they all say is keep the door closed and cut the power.

## Microwave smoking but no flames: switch off, unplug, door shut

Breville's [BMO650 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BMO650/BMO650_USCM_IB_L24_LR.pdf) covers smoke on its own: "If any smoke is observed, switch off and unplug the microwave oven at the power outlet. Keep the door closed to stifle any flames." Toshiba's [EM131A5C-BS booklet](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf) says not to overcook food, and IKEA's GÅTEBO manual says food severely overcooked can smoke or ignite.

If the smoke came with sparks or flashes, see [why is my microwave sparking](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/). If it started with nobody using the oven, see [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/).

## Should you open the door or use a fire extinguisher?

Every source read for this page says keep the door closed: the three manuals, NIH, and Highspeed Training, which says opening it gives the fire more oxygen. None of them mentions an extinguisher for a fire inside a microwave.

The USFA's general rule is to use an extinguisher only if all six of its checklist answers are yes, including that the fire is small and contained in a single object, you are safe from its smoke, and you have a clear escape route. Otherwise, leave and call 911. For plugged-in appliances it says Class C applies: an agent that doesn't conduct electricity.

Here is the conflict the sources don't resolve: using an extinguisher on a fire inside the oven means opening the door, and every microwave-specific source says not to. My reading is that the closed door is the first tool, and the extinguisher is for a fire that has escaped the oven. Which type to own is covered in [what fire extinguisher for a kitchen](/guides/what-fire-extinguisher-for-kitchen/), and the steps in [how to use a fire extinguisher](/guides/how-to-use-a-fire-extinguisher/).

## When to leave and call 911

- **Flames are still visible after the power is off.** NIH: "If in doubt, call the fire department."
- **Fire or smoke is outside the oven, or reaching the cabinets above.** The USFA's checklist requires a fire small and contained in a single object. CPSC records for over-the-range models (below) include fires involving surrounding cabinets.
- **You can't reach the plug or breaker without passing the fire.** Highspeed Training says to unplug only if it is safe to do so.
- **Smoke is filling the room.** The USFA checklist asks whether you are safe from the fire's toxic smoke; if not, leave and call 911.

## After the microwave fire is out

Leave it unplugged and keep the door shut until you're certain the fire is out (NIH). Juan Alberto Arguello, CR's test program leader for product safety, said in the Toshiba alert below that overheating plastic and insulation can produce hazardous fumes, including formaldehyde or other toxic compounds.

Don't run it again to see if it still works. Toshiba says not to operate the oven if it is not working properly or has been damaged, and to contact the nearest authorized service facility for examination, repair or adjustment. Breville and IKEA say their ovens should be serviced only by qualified personnel.

Residue matters too. Breville says food residue, grease or fat left in the cavity could make it overheat and may cause the oven to smoke or catch fire. For how to clean it, see [how to clean a microwave](/guides/how-to-clean-a-microwave/).

## Is it safe to use a microwave after a fire inside?

Not until you're sure it isn't damaged, and the sources lean toward having it checked. The manuals say not to operate a damaged oven and to send it to authorized or qualified service. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) says there is little cause for concern about leakage unless the door hinges, latch or seals are damaged, and to contact the manufacturer if they are. It also says to stop using an oven that keeps running with the door open.

The secondary sources disagree. Highspeed Training says to have the oven checked and tested by a qualified person after a fire, because there may be damage you can't see. [Real Homes](https://www.realhomes.com/advice/clean-microwave-caught-fire) is less cautious: if it smells burnt because food burned but the oven itself isn't damaged, it says it should be safe to use; after a fire, it says to check the manufacturer's safety instructions or call the manufacturer. Real Homes cites no safety authority, so I'd weight the manuals and Highspeed Training above it.

Breville's BMO650 manual lists the damage to check for: a misaligned or bent door, damaged door seals and sealing surface, broken or loose door hinges and latches, and dents inside the cavity or on the door. If there is any, it says not to operate the oven and to contact a Breville service centre. FDA adds not to use an oven whose door doesn't close firmly or is bent, warped or otherwise damaged. Beyond those, my reading of the manuals' "not working properly" would also cover melted plastic inside the cavity, or an oven that sparks or won't heat after the fire.

Don't open the outer cabinet yourself; the manuals restrict that to qualified service. More on door and seal leakage is in [is microwave radiation safe](/guides/is-microwave-radiation-safe/).

## What usually starts microwave fires, per the manuals and the CPSC

The manuals name these causes:

- **Overcooking**, and leaving paper, plastic or other combustibles unattended inside (all three manuals)
- **Wire twist-ties left on paper or plastic bags** (all three; Toshiba adds metal handles)
- **Heating oil or fat** (Breville)
- **Recycled paper** (Breville; IKEA lists recycled paper towels with metal pieces as an arcing cause; NIH says only if approved for microwave use)
- **Popcorn left unattended** (NIH); Breville says popping time should not exceed 3 minutes
- **Using the cavity for storage** (all three manuals)
- **Food residue and grease** (Breville)

The CPSC also records defects. In [recall 16-253](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=16253) (2016), about 15,200 Whirlpool microwave hood combinations were recalled because internal arcing can ignite an internal plastic component. Whirlpool received five reports, including one home fire and two fires involving the surrounding cabinets. In [recall 02-018](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=02018) (2001), about 1.8 million Whirlpool, KitchenAid and Kenmore hood combinations could overheat and catch fire, with seven fire reports. That record says countertop microwaves are not included.

## Can a microwave catch fire on its own? The Toshiba alert

A [Consumer Reports safety alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) (June 25, 2026) cites 28 incidents filed on SaferProducts.gov of Toshiba-branded microwaves auto-starting or adding cooking time on their own, resulting in overheating, sparks, smoke and fire. CR advises monitoring the oven while it runs, not leaving it unattended, not storing items inside, and unplugging it after use.

Midea, which makes Toshiba-brand microwaves, told CR it was aware of the issue but that the products are not unsafe. It is a safety alert, not a recall. Affected models and what to do are in [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/).

## Report a microwave fire to the CPSC and the manufacturer

Consumer Reports says to report the incident to the CPSC through SaferProducts.gov and to contact the manufacturer. Then check whether your model is already recalled with our [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).
