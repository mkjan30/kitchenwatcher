If your microwave is not heating but the light, fan and turntable still work, makers' support pages point to a few things you can check yourself first. [GE](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=110), [Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10007304) and [Whirlpool](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Cooking/Microwaves/Countertop_Microwave/Operation/Not_Heating/Microwave_is_Not_Heating_-_Countertop_Microwave) name demo mode, a door that isn't fully latched, the Kitchen Timer or a low power level used by mistake, and the power supply. If none of those is it, GE says one of many internal components may not be working, and it gives no do-it-yourself advice because the capacitor can hold a charge after you unplug the oven. Call service.

**The 7 checks, in order:**

First, confirm it's really cold with the 1-cup water test. Then:

1. Demo mode: does the timer race or show "d"?
2. Door: is it fully latched and clean?
3. Kitchen Timer, Delay Start or a low power level set by mistake.
4. Power: wall outlet, breaker, not a generator.
5. Vents and clearance.
6. Reset the power once.
7. Read your model's own troubleshooting table.

Still cold? It's a repair, not a reset.

## First, confirm the microwave runs but doesn't heat: the water test

Whirlpool's countertop help page gives the simplest check: put 1 cup (250 mL) of water on the turntable and run it for 1 minute on HI (100%). If the water's temperature rises, the oven is heating.

For a stricter version, [Panasonic's NN-S554MF service manual](https://www.e-service.css.panasonic.co.jp/Panasonic_ServiceManual(Global%202011)/OT/NN-S554MF/SIMP/s0302000000.html) heats exactly 1 liter of tap water at High for exactly 1 minute, stirring and reading the temperature before and after. Panasonic's minimum rise is 8.6 °C for a 1,000 W rated oven and 9.4 °C for 1,100 W. Those are Panasonic's figures for its own ovens, not a universal pass mark, and Panasonic notes that low line voltage lowers magnetron output.

Handle the cup carefully. The [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS manual](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf) warns that liquids can superheat past boiling without bubbling.

## Check 1: Is your microwave in demo mode?

Demo mode lets you press buttons without heating anything. Whirlpool's countertop page says the light comes on, the fan runs and the turntable turns, but the magnetron isn't turned on. Its [over-the-range demo-mode page](https://producthelp.whirlpool.com/Cooking/Microwaves/Product_Info/Microwave_Product_Information/How_to_Set_Demo_Mode_for_an_Over_the_Range_Microwave) says the mode is meant for learning and retail displays. That page contradicts itself: one sentence says the fan does not turn on, and a later one says the fan will run.

| Brand | How to spot it | How to exit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Samsung | Timer counts down much faster than usual | Models with an Options or My Settings button: option 6, set to 2; other models, see the manual | [Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10007304) |
| Whirlpool (keypad) | Display counts down but food stays cold; "d" or "Demo" may show | With oven and timer off, hold Timer Off or the 3 pad for 3 to 5 seconds until two tones; repeat to clear | Whirlpool demo-mode page |
| Whirlpool (touchscreen) | Same | OPTIONS/CLOCK submenu, or Tools then INFO then Store Demo Mode OFF | Whirlpool demo-mode page |
| Any Whirlpool still stuck | Same | Unplug or switch off the breaker for 60 seconds | Whirlpool demo-mode page |
| Toshiba EM131A5C-BS | Not applicable | The instruction booklet describes no demo mode | Toshiba manual |

If Samsung's timer races but your manual doesn't mention demo mode, Samsung says to request service.

## Check 2: Is the door fully latched?

A microwave with the light on but not heating may simply not be sensing a closed door. Breville's BMO650 instruction book says to check the door is fully closed before calling service. Samsung says a door that isn't completely shut may run but not heat, and to wipe the door and frame with a damp cloth because debris could stop it closing properly. GE says to open the door and close it firmly so its safety mechanisms engage.

Amana's support page says to look for food debris or cracks on the latches, close the door firmly and listen for a distinct click that means the switches have engaged.

[FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) explains why the door matters: the federal standard requires two independent interlocks that stop microwave production when the door opens. FDA says not to use an oven whose door doesn't close firmly or is bent, warped or damaged, and to contact the manufacturer about damaged hinges, latches or seals. The Toshiba manual says never to defeat or tamper with the interlocks.

## Check 3: Did you set the Kitchen Timer or a low power level by mistake?

GE and Samsung both say to make sure the Timer or Kitchen Timer wasn't used instead of a cook cycle. The Toshiba EM131A5C-BS has a separate Kitchen Timer pad, and its manual says no cooking function can run while the Kitchen Timer is on. Its troubleshooting table lists an unsuitable cooking time or power level among the causes of undercooked food.

Whirlpool says lower power levels take longer to heat food. Breville's book says the oven light can dim at low power levels, which is normal. [Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/microwave-not-heating/) adds Delay Start and control lock to its list. Whirlpool says "LOC" or "L" on the display means the control lock is on.

## Check 4: Is the outlet, breaker or power source the problem?

Whirlpool says that on an inverter or generator, the power may run the lights and controls but not the heating, so plug into a wall outlet and try again. Amana suggests testing the outlet with a lamp and checking for a tripped breaker or blown house fuse. Breville says to make sure the plug is secure (if not, unplug, wait 10 seconds and plug it back in), check for a blown fuse or tripped breaker, and test the outlet with another appliance.

Panasonic's service manual says to check line voltage under load, because low voltage lowers output. The Toshiba manual says to use the voltage on the rating label and covers grounding and extension cords.

## Check 5: Are the vents blocked or the clearance too tight?

Samsung says that without enough clearance the oven can't vent properly and will either not heat or have weak heat; required clearances vary by model. The Toshiba EM131A5C-BS manual asks for at least 3 inches between the oven and adjacent walls, with one side open, and 12 inches above, and says not to install it over a range cooktop. Its troubleshooting table lists restricted ventilation ports as a cause of undercooked food.

## Check 6: Reset the power once

Whirlpool says to unplug the microwave or switch off its breaker for 60 seconds, then restore power. Bob Vila says unplugging for a few minutes may clear Delay Start and demo mode, and cancels control lock on most models but not all. Do it once. If the water test still fails afterward, a reset won't fix it.

## Check 7: Look up your model's own troubleshooting table

Whirlpool's demo-mode page says its model number is on the inside door frame. Search the maker's support site for that model's manual and read its troubleshooting table.

|---|---|---|

| Breville BMO650 instruction book | Cord, fuse or breaker, outlet; door not fully closed | Door not closed properly |

## Still cold? Why a microwave not heating is a repair, not a DIY job

Repair sources name the usual suspects. Bob Vila lists a failed door switch and, for people with high-voltage repair experience, the high-voltage diode, capacitor and magnetron; it recommends a professional replace a magnetron. A [community guide on iFixit](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/3+-+Microwave+Oven+-+Common+Faults/186290), not written by iFixit staff, also covers the glass fuse, door microswitches and thermal cutouts. No source read says how often each part fails, so this page doesn't rank them.

The makers draw a hard line here. GE offers no do-it-yourself repair advice because the internal high-voltage capacitor can hold a charge even after the oven is unplugged, and diagnosis generally needs special equipment. Amana says not to open the outer cabinet or replace the magnetron, diode or capacitor yourself. The Toshiba and Breville manuals say only qualified service personnel should adjust or repair the oven.

The sources conflict. Bob Vila says the CPSC and major makers strongly discourage DIY repair, yet describes discharging the capacitor and testing parts. The iFixit guide warns the capacitor can hold close to 4,000 volts unplugged, then gives discharge steps. Kitchen Watcher follows the manufacturers and gives no opening or testing steps.

For the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS, the manual lists a one-year limited warranty from the date of purchase and Midea America Corp customer assistance at 800-842-1289. Breville says to contact its Service Center and not to try to adjust or repair the oven yourself.

## Repair or replace a microwave that stopped heating?

Check the warranty first: the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS manual gives one year from purchase. Bob Vila's rule of thumb is that microwaves last about 7 years, units under 3 years old are often worth repairing, and it generally doesn't make sense to repair one older than 7. That's one source's rule of thumb, not a measured figure.

Before paying for a repair, run the model through our [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/). If you're replacing it, start with [the best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) and [what size microwave you need](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/).

## When to stop using the microwave now

FDA says that if an oven keeps running with the door open, stop using it. Don't use one whose door is bent, warped or doesn't close firmly. You can report suspected radiation problems to FDA on its Accidental Radiation Occurrence Report form; see [is microwave radiation safe](/guides/is-microwave-radiation-safe/) for more.

Other symptoms have their own pages. If it sparks, see [why is my microwave sparking](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/). If it starts on its own, see [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/). If there's smoke or fire, see [microwave fire: what to do](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/).
