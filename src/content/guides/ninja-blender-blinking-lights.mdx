Ninja blender blinking lights on a corded model usually mean the safety system is keeping the motor off. Either the pitcher or cup isn't locked onto the base with the lid locked on top, or the motor shut itself off after an overload or overheating. To fix it, reseat the container and lid with the triangles or arrows lined up, or unplug the blender and let it cool. Ninja's [BN700 Series owner's guide](https://files.bbystatic.com/m1NH7lSdseYGtt3pTyhjEg==/b6d70423-79ae-4911-96a8-afa98a22e926) gives about 15 minutes for an overload reset and about 30 minutes for a thermal reset.

## Cause 1: the pitcher isn't seated or the lid isn't locked (safety interlock)

All three model families have an interlock that keeps the motor off until the container and lid are locked.

### Ninja blender lid won't lock: Professional Plus (BN701)

Per the BN700 owner's guide:

1. Lower the pitcher onto the base with the handle slightly to the right and the LOCK symbols showing, then rotate it clockwise until it clicks.
2. Drop the Stacked Blade Assembly onto the drive gear. Ninja notes it sits loosely. If it isn't fully seated, the lid won't lock.
3. Put the lid on with the triangle on the lid lined up with the triangle on the handle. Press the handle down until it clicks. Ninja says the triangles "MUST be aligned for the lid to lock and the unit to operate."
4. Press Power. The available program buttons light up when the blender is ready.

### Older Professional (BL610)

The BL610 guide tells you to make sure the lid is securely on the container "in the correct position (align arrows)."

### BlendBOSS

Per SharkNinja's DB300 Series FAQ:

1. Thread the blade assembly fully onto the cup so it sits flush with the cup's lip and leaves no gap.
2. Line up the cup handle with the tab on the blade assembly while you screw it on. SharkNinja's FAQ notes "there is no audible click" when the blade is on correctly.
3. Set the cup on the base and rotate it clockwise until it clicks.

## Cause 2: overload or overheating shut the motor off

The BN700 guide describes two separate protections with two different wait times.

**Overload reset.** The guide's "Resetting the motor" steps are:

1. Unplug the unit.
2. Let it cool for "approximately 15 minutes."
3. Remove the lid and blade assembly, empty the pitcher and make sure nothing is jamming the blades.

Ninja says exceeding the maximum capacity "is the most typical cause of appliance overload." The BN701's 72 oz pitcher has a 64 oz max liquid capacity (manufacturer spec).

**Thermal switch.** Warning 38 in the same guide says overheating trips a thermal switch that disables the motor. To reset it, unplug the blender and let it cool "for approximately 30 minutes."

The guide doesn't say which lights show for each case. If you can't tell whether it was an overload or overheating, wait the full 30 minutes, which covers both of the manual's wait times. The BL610 guide gives about 15 minutes for both an overload and an overheat.

If the motor gave off a smell before it stopped, see [why a blender smells like burning](/guides/blender-smells-like-burning/).

## Ninja BlendBOSS flashing lights: the progress bar explained

SharkNinja's DB300 Series FAQ, dated March 20, 2026, explains three patterns:

- **All 5 lights flashing:** the base is plugged in with no container. Place the cup and rotate it clockwise until it clicks.
- **One red light:** "The unit needs to be unplugged to reset, this will cancel the runtime protection mode." Next time, use enough liquid, don't overfill, and check that nothing is stuck in the blades.
- **Your selected program flashing:** the blades may be stuck or struggling with a large or frozen ingredient. Clean any debris out of the base area, make sure the blades aren't blocked, and add liquid.

The FAQ lists the DB300 and DB301 variants only and doesn't name the DB351. The DB351 is also sold under the BlendBOSS name, but none of the Ninja documents read for this page say whether its lights mean the same things. Our [Ninja BlendBOSS DB351 review](/reviews/ninja-db351/) covers that model.

## Ninja blender won't start and the lights keep blinking after you've reseated it

If your Ninja blender won't start after a careful reseat and a full cool-down, the interlock part itself may be worn or broken. The owner reports we found are in an [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/262834/Why+is+the+power+light+blinking) about a 1100 W Professional blender, model BL663CO, a different model from the BN701.

- The highest-scored answer (score 23, October 2015) says to line up the arrow on the lid with the arrow on the handle and to check you're using the correct lid.
- One owner (score 7, February 2017) reports that the black plastic part on the handle that presses down when the jar locks can slip out of position.
- Another (score 5, May 2018) reports that the part of the lid that presses a small button at the handle had broken off.

Some answers in that thread describe ways to defeat the switch. We won't repeat them. Ninja's BN700 guide says "DO NOT attempt to defeat the interlock mechanism," and its warranty excludes any unit that has been tampered with. The BL663CO is also one of the 12 models in [CPSC recall 16-034](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2016/Laceration-Injuries-Prompt-SharkNinja-to-Recall-Ninja-BL660-Blenders) (November 12, 2015), a laceration recall for the loose stacked blade assembly, so check [whether your blender is recalled](/guides/blender-recall-check/).

The fix for a broken lid tab or pitcher is a replacement part, or a call to Ninja Customer Service at 1-877-646-5288 (from the BN700 guide). The guide's one-year limited warranty covers the original owner only. It excludes normal wear and tear of wearable parts such as blending vessels and lids, so a worn lid may not be covered.

No lights at all is a different problem: see [what to check when a blender won't turn on](/guides/blender-wont-turn-on/).

## What blinking does NOT mean on a corded Ninja

Some troubleshooting pages mix up product lines. [SlashGear's guide](https://www.slashgear.com/1925511/ninja-blender-blinking-red-light-why-how-to-fix-kitchen-appliance/) covers red and green battery and charger codes for the cordless Blast Max BC200 and BC250 series, plus white, orange and purple codes for the Blast and Blast Max portables. Those are battery-powered models, so the charging codes don't apply to a corded blender.

None of the Ninja documents read for this page assigns meanings to colors such as orange, purple or white. Match the pattern you see to your model's own guide.

## How to stop it happening again

All of these come from Ninja's BN700 guide unless noted:

- Fill no higher than the MAX LIQUID line.
- If ingredients keep getting stuck, use the Auto-iQ programs. Their pulses and pauses let food settle toward the blades. Adding some liquid usually helps too.
- For ice, use ice straight from the freezer, not ice that has started to melt.
- Keep the counter and the blender's feet clean and dry so the base doesn't move while blending. SharkNinja's BlendBOSS FAQ gives the same advice.
- Never blend hot or carbonated liquids in the BN701. Warning 6 says it "may result in pressure buildup and steam." More in [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/).
