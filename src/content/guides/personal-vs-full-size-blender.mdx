Personal blender vs full size blender comes down to what you blend. A personal blender, a sealed 24- to 32-oz cup that screws onto the blade, suits single cold smoothies and shakes. A full-size blender, a 36- to 72-oz pitcher, handles batches, hot soup and nut butter. Nutribullet's and Ninja's personal-cup manuals forbid hot liquids in sealed cups.

## Do I need a full size blender? Pick by what you blend, not by watts

If you're asking whether you need a full size blender, start with your recipes:

- **One or two cold smoothies or shakes a day:** a personal blender. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-blender/) calls its personal pick, the Ninja BlendBOSS, "ideal for single servings only."
- **Batches, hot soup or nut butter:** a full-size blender. Only the pitcher formats in the manuals below allow hot liquids at all.
- **Both:** a combo. nutribullet's NBF50500 user guide lists a pitcher with a vented lid cap plus a 24-oz handled cup, a 32-oz cup and two to-go lids.

Wattage is a weak guide here. The personal, combo and budget full-size models in the table below are all rated between 1,000 and 1,200 W.

## How a personal blender works, and what it's built for

nutribullet's guide has you add ingredients to the cup, twist the blade on until sealed and set it on the motor base. CNET describes unscrewing the BlendBOSS blade top after blending and swapping in a drinking lid. CNET says the BlendBOSS isn't the pick for large batches, soups, almond butter or dips, because the tumbler is "geared toward single servings" of 26 oz or less.

Cup shape is the real limit. Tom's Guide explains that personal cups taper toward the base, so there's nothing to pull a thick mixture toward the blade, and that pulsing helps. TechGearLab gave its personal pick, the 16-oz Ninja Fit Compact, 2.8 out of 10 for purée and says it doesn't recommend that unit, "or any personal blender," for puréeing.

### Is a personal blender only for smoothies?

Mostly smoothies, shakes and frozen drinks, with small dips possible. Tom's Guide made hummus in the BlendBOSS without stopping to stir, while TechGearLab's Ninja Fit made grainy nut butter.

## Can a personal blender take hot liquid? The manuals say no

Hot liquid is where the formats split. Here is what each manual says:

- nutribullet's [NBF50500 user guide](https://nbmedia.imgix.net/NBF50500_BlenderCombo_UserGuide_English_LoRes_PROOF_F20221010.pdf?dl=) says to "never blend hot ingredients or liquid in any sealed nutribullet cup." The reason given is that heat can build pressure, separate the cup from the blade or violently expel hot contents. Cup blending should start with room-temperature or refrigerated ingredients (70°F or cooler). Hot liquids go only in the pitcher, with the pitcher lid and vented lid cap locked, below the MAX lines, starting on LOW.
- Ninja's [BN400 Nutri-Blender Pro owner's guide](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/70/70cc8fb2-37d6-472d-aa56-6c5535a40f14.pdf), a personal-cup model, says: "DO NOT blend hot liquids," citing pressure buildup and steam that can burn.
- The [Vitamix 5200 manual](https://www.vitamix.com/media/catalog/product/file/Vitamix-5200-Owners-Manual.pdf) allows hot liquids. It says not to fill the container to maximum capacity, to lock the lid and to "Never Start on Speeds Above 1 with Hot Liquids."

CNET's buying tips call personal blenders "great for soups." That contradicts the nutribullet and Ninja manuals, and this page follows the manuals. Full steps are in [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/).

## What a full-size blender does that a cup can't

**Batches.** CNET puts full-size jars at up to 72 oz, and Serious Eats' favorite full-size models range from 36 to 64 oz.

**Soup from friction.** TechGearLab heated soup to 152.4°F in the Vitamix 5200 by friction alone. The Ninja BL610 reached 73°F and the BN701 74°F, so not every full-size blender heats soup. The [Vitamix 5200 vs Ninja BN701](/vs/vitamix-5200-vs-ninja-bn701/) comparison covers that gap.

**Nut butter.** CNET's Vitamix VX1 turned 3 cups of almonds into almond butter in 1 minute 13 seconds. ATK says the Ninja Professional Plus (BN701) struggled with almond butter, and that blenders rated under 750 W often struggled with tough tasks like nut butters. The Vitamix manual says to stop nut butters no more than one minute after the mixture starts circulating.

**Jar shape.** ATK found that tall, narrow jars about 4.5 in. across kept food near the blades and blended without extra air.

**The downsides.** ATK lists the Vitamix 5200 at 20.25 in. tall, too tall for a standard 18-in. cabinet gap. Its manual says the container, lid and lid plug don't go in the dishwasher.

## Single serve vs countertop blender for ice and frozen fruit

On ice, a single serve vs countertop blender is closer than you'd expect. In CNET's timed test, the BlendBOSS cleared ice in 13 seconds on Ice Crush with no chunks and the Ninja Professional took 7 seconds. The Vitamix VX1 took 26 seconds and left several large cubes. Serious Eats says personal blenders "usually can't crush ice" and that many personal models it reviewed carried a warning against it, with only the Ninja BN401 producing a satisfying slush. CNET's BlendBOSS result contradicts the general rule.

Format isn't the whole story. nutribullet's Combo guide says that model "is not intended to be used without liquids, or as an ice crusher," and TechGearLab says it struggles to fully crush ice. Yet Consumer Reports, per The Daily Meal, gave the NBF50500 its highest marks for crushing ice, so the evidence on this model conflicts. Details are in [can a blender crush ice](/guides/can-a-blender-crush-ice/).

On power, CNET's tips put personal blenders "between 900 and 1,000 watts," yet CNET lists its own personal pick at 1,100 W. [Blender wattage explained](/guides/blender-wattage-explained/) covers what the number means.

## Is a personal blender quieter or easier to clean?

Smaller isn't quieter. CNET logged 92 dB for the BlendBOSS on smoothie mode and 87 dB for the full-size Ninja Professional BL610 on medium. Tom's Guide describes single-serve motors as "harsh and whiny" next to the rumble of bigger machines.

For cleaning, Tom's Guide suggests adding dish soap and water to the cup or pitcher and pulsing for a few seconds. nutribullet's guide says the pitcher is dishwasher safe and the cups are top-rack safe, but never on the sanitize cycle, which can warp the plastic. See [how to clean a blender](/guides/how-to-clean-a-blender/) and [is a blender jar dishwasher safe](/guides/is-a-blender-jar-dishwasher-safe/).

Full-size pitchers with a loose stacked blade need care when pouring. CPSC recall [16-034](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2016/Laceration-Injuries-Prompt-SharkNinja-to-Recall-Ninja-BL660-Blenders) covered Ninja BL660, BL663 and BL665 blenders after 53 laceration reports. The remedy says to empty the pitcher "through the locked lid's pour spout or by removing both the lid and the stacked blade assembly" before pouring. The BL610 in the table isn't among the 12 recalled model numbers CPSC lists.

## Combo blenders: the middle option

A combo pairs a pitcher with personal cups on one motor base. TechGearLab gave the nutribullet Combo 75 out of 100 and named it its budget pick. Per [The Daily Meal](https://www.thedailymeal.com/2242713/best-blender-brand-2026-consumer-reports/), Consumer Reports' 2026 ratings gave the NBF50500 top marks for ice, purée and smoothies but lower scores for brand reliability and owner satisfaction.

Tom's Guide lists the Ninja Detect Power Pro at 72 oz plus a 24-oz single-serve cup. The trade-off is more parts to store. See [nutribullet NBF50500 vs Ninja BN701](/vs/nutribullet-nbf50500-vs-ninja-bn701/).

## Which should you buy? A five-question checklist

1. **How many servings at a time?** One or two points to a personal cup; more points to a pitcher.
2. **Hot soup?** Only a pitcher format, per every manual above.
3. **Nut butter or thick dips?** A full-size blender. TechGearLab doesn't recommend any personal blender for puréeing.
4. **Cabinet clearance?** Measure it, as CNET advises. The Vitamix 5200 is 20.25 in. per ATK; the BlendBOSS is 12.91 in. per CNET.
5. **Both habits?** A combo, if you have room for the extra cups.
