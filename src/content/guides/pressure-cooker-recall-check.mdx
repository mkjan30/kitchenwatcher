As of Nov 13, 2026, CPSC pressure cooker recall records cover the Ninja Foodi OP300 series and the Crock-Pot 6-qt Express (some date codes). They also cover Insignia, Bella/Crux/Cooks, Ambiano and older Breville and Instant Pot models. Two more cookers carry CPSC stop-use warnings with no remedy: the Power Quick Pot Y6D-36 and the Gourmia GPC625. Find your model number and match it in the table below.

## How to check your pressure cooker in 3 steps

1. **Find the model number.** Per CPSC, it is on the side for the Ninja OP300 series and on the back for the Power Quick Pot. Crock-Pot, Ambiano, Breville and the Instant Gem 65 have it on the bottom. SharkNinja's [recall registration page](https://www.rqa-inc.com/client/SharkNinja/) says the Ninja rating label is on the back near the power cord, so check both places. For Ninja, CPSC says a code after the model number is not part of it: "OP301 I07" is model OP301.
2. **Check the date, batch or serial code where it matters.** Only some codes are covered for the Crock-Pot SCCPPC600-V1, the Breville BPR600XL, the Instant Gem 65 and the Instant Pot Smart. A matching model with a code outside the listed range is not in the recall.
3. **Search CPSC and the maker.** Search the model at cpsc.gov/Recalls or SaferProducts.gov, then check the company's recall page. No listing does not prove a unit is safe; it means no CPSC action was on record when you looked.

## Ninja Foodi recall: OP300 series, CPSC 25-247

[CPSC 25-247](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/SharkNinja-Recalls-1-8-Million-Foodi-Multi-Function-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard-Serious-Burn-Injuries-Reported) covers every Ninja Foodi OP300-series 6.5-qt black multicooker in the table, plus OP300 replacement lids bought separately. About 1,846,400 units were sold in the US and about 184,240 in Canada, from January 2019 to March 2025, for about $200. CPSC counts 106 burn reports, more than 50 of them second or third degree, and 26 lawsuits.

The remedy is a free replacement pressure lid. CPSC says to stop using the pressure-cooking function now; air frying and the other functions can still be used. SharkNinja's [registration page](https://www.rqa-inc.com/client/SharkNinja/) says to remove and discard the old pressure lid and that you don't need to send the cooker back; replacements are handled through its online form.

SharkNinja's page and the CPSC notice differ in two places. SharkNinja's page, which also covers the Health Canada recall, lists OP300C, OP301C and OP305CCO. Those are not in the CPSC text, and the page doesn't say which market they were sold in. It also puts the rating label on the back near the power cord, where CPSC says side (both checked Nov 13, 2026).

The OL-series SmartLid models are not listed by CPSC in this recall. For how the Foodi compares with Instant's lineup as a purchase, see [Instant Pot vs Ninja Foodi](/guides/instant-pot-vs-ninja-foodi/).

## Instant Pot recall history: two CPSC actions, none for current models

Only two CPSC actions name Instant Pot. [CPSC 15-741](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2015/Instant-Pot-Pressure-Cookers-Recalled-by-Double-Insight) (July 2015) covered about 1,000 Instant Pot Smart and Smart-60 Bluetooth cookers, serials 1410–1503, whose thermal probe could conduct electricity. Three shocks were reported, and owners got a free replacement base.

[CPSC 18-110](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/Double-Insight-Recalls-Multicookers-Due-to-Fire-Hazard-Sold-Exclusively-at-Walmart) (March 2018) covered about 104,000 Gem 65 8-in-1 multicookers sold only at Walmart. CPSC calls them multicookers, its list of their functions does not include pressure cooking, and the hazard was overheating and melting underneath, not a lid or pressure failure. There were 107 overheating reports, five with minor property damage, and no injuries.

As of Nov 13, 2026, the SaferProducts.gov Recall API shows no recall for the Instant Pot Duo, Duo Plus, Rio or Pro lines. See our [Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-qt review](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/) and [Instant Pot Pro 8-qt review](/reviews/instant-pot-pro-8qt/).

## Crock-Pot pressure cooker recall: Express Crock 6-qt, CPSC 21-035

[CPSC 21-035](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2021/Crock-Pot-6-Quart-Express-Crock-Multi-Cookers-Recalled-by-Sunbeam-Products-Due-to-Burn-Hazard) covers the Crock-Pot 6-Quart Express Crock Multi-Cooker, model SCCPPC600-V1, made July 2017 to October 2018. Only date codes K196JN–K365JN and L001JN–L273JN are covered; the code is engraved on one prong of the plug and on the bottom of the base. About 914,430 units were sold in the US and 28,330 in Canada, from July 2017 to November 2020, for $70–$100.

CPSC says the cooker can pressurize when the lid is not fully locked, and the lid can then detach. It counts 119 lid detachments and 99 burns, from first to third degree.

The remedy is a free replacement lid, and slow cook and sauté can still be used. CPSC says owners who keep using pressure mode while waiting "should be certain the lid is securely turned to the fully locked position by aligning the arrow on the lid with the lock symbol on the base."

The 8-qt Express Crock XL is not in this notice; CPSC lists only the 6-qt model.

## Stop-use warnings with no recall: Power Quick Pot and Gourmia

In both warnings below, CPSC tells owners to stop using the cooker because the company refused to recall it, so there is no refund or repair.

**Power Quick Pot Y6D-36.** [CPSC warning 27-030](https://www.cpsc.gov/Warnings/2027/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Immediately-Stop-Using-Tristar-Products-6-Quart-Power-Quick-Pot-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Burn-Hazard) covers about 393,000 6-quart units sold from about July 2018 to November 2023 for $44–$160. CPSC says the lid can open while pressurized and counts 24 reports of hot contents expelled, with serious burns. It says importer Tristar Products refused to recall it or offer a remedy. CPSC says to stop using it immediately, dispose of it, and not sell or give it away. Some headlines, such as [one on local12](https://local12.com/news/nation-world/nearly-400000-pressure-cookers-recalled-over-burn-hazard), call this a recall; CPSC does not.

**Gourmia GPC625.** [CPSC warning 26-280](https://cpsc.gov/Warnings/2026/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Immediately-Stop-Using-Gourmia-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Serious-Burn-Hazard-Multiple-Burn-Injuries-Reported-Majority-Sold-at-Best-Buy) covers about 43,500 6-qt digital cookers sold 2017–2020, mostly at Best Buy, for $50–$80. CPSC says the lid can open while still pressurized, the float valve sits inside the handle and is hard to see, and the inner pot has incorrect volume markings. It cites 5 reports of contents expelled under pressure, 4 with severe burns, and at least 2 lawsuits. CPSC says Gourmia (importer Steelstone Group) and Best Buy refused an acceptable recall, and gives the same stop, dispose and don't-resell instruction.

## Other recalled brands: Insignia, Bella/Crux/Cooks, Ambiano, Breville BPR600XL

### Insignia (CPSC 24-013)

CPSC says incorrect volume markings on the inner pot can cause owners to overfill it, so hot contents can be ejected on quick release or if the lid is opened under pressure. Best Buy received 31 incident reports, including 17 burns. Covered owners get a free replacement inner pot and floating locking valve.

### Bella, Bella Pro, Crux and Cooks (CPSC 23-256)

Sensio's recall covers 6-, 8- and 10-qt electric models and three Bella stovetop cookers, because the lid can unlock and be removed during use. The remedy is a refund.

### Ambiano at ALDI (CPSC 26-007)

The lid can be opened before pressure is released. CPSC says to return the intact cooker to ALDI for a full refund. The other route is to email Tempo a photo of the cooker with its unplugged power cord cut, plus a photo of the model number and date code. [Fox6's write-up](https://www.fox6now.com/news/ambiano-pressure-cooker-recall-aldi) gives the unit count and sale dates but no model numbers.

### Breville BPR600XL (CPSC 16-066)

The sealing gasket can be inserted upside down, causing unexpected pressure release. Owners get a replacement gasket and updated instructions. The Breville Fast Slow Pro BPR700 is a different model number and is not in this recall; we compare it in [Instant Pot Pro 8-qt vs Breville BPR700](/vs/instant-pot-pro-8qt-vs-breville-bpr700/).

The Recall API also returns smaller or older pressure-cooker recalls, such as Rena Ware Nutrex 2153 (18-752, about 700 units). Search your brand if it isn't above.

## What the pressure cooker recalls have in common

Six of these actions (Ninja, Power Quick Pot, Gourmia, Ambiano, Bella/Crux/Cooks and Crock-Pot) involve a lid that opens or detaches while the cooker is pressurized or before pressure is released. Insignia's recall and part of the Gourmia warning are about wrong fill markings, Breville's is about a gasket, and the two Instant actions are a shock hazard and an overheating base. For why lids fail and how rare it is, see [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

## What to do if your pressure cooker is recalled or warned

- **Ninja OP300 series:** stop using the pressure function and register for the free lid (CPSC 25-247).
- **Crock-Pot SCCPPC600-V1 in the code range:** stop using pressure mode and register for the free lid; slow cook and sauté are allowed (CPSC 21-035).
- **Ambiano:** stop using it and take the refund route CPSC lists (CPSC 26-007).
- **Power Quick Pot Y6D-36 or Gourmia GPC625:** stop using it and dispose of it; don't sell or give it away (CPSC 27-030, 26-280).
- **Any burn, lid or steam incident:** report it at SaferProducts.gov.
