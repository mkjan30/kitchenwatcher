The difference in a pressurized vs non-pressurized basket is where the resistance comes from. A pressurized (dual wall) basket has a perforated inner wall over an outer wall with one small exit hole, which creates back-pressure so coarse or stale coffee still makes a shot. A non-pressurized (single wall) basket gets nearly all its resistance from the coffee puck, so it needs a fine, fresh grind. Breville's [BES870](https://www.breville.com/content/dam/breville/us/assets/miscellaneous/instruction-manual/espresso/BES870-instruction-manual.pdf) and [BES500](https://assets.breville.com/BES500/BES500_USCM_IB_T23_LR.pdf) manuals give the rule: dual wall for pre-ground, single wall for freshly ground beans.

## Pressurized portafilter explained: how each basket works

Breville's own explainer describes the dual wall layout. The inner wall has many holes, and the outer wall has "a single, small hole at the base." Some have a few small holes, but always far fewer than the inner wall. Forcing water through that hole creates back-pressure that, in Breville's words, "compensates for inconsistencies" such as uneven tamping or the wrong grind size.

Not every pressurized basket is built the same way. [Seattle Coffee Gear](https://www.seattlecoffeegear.com/blogs/scg-blog/pressurized-vs-unpressurized-whats-the-difference) says some use "a small chamber with offset holes" and others a smaller set of holes centered in the basket. [Coffeeblog.co.uk](https://coffeeblog.co.uk/pressurised-portafilter-baskets/) describes the usual layout as a varying number of holes on the first layer and a single small hole on the bottom layer.

A single wall basket has no restriction of its own. "In an unpressurized basket, nearly all of the resistance is created by the coffee, not by the basket," Seattle Coffee Gear writes.

The pump's bar rating is a separate topic; see [espresso machine bar pressure explained](/guides/espresso-machine-bar-pressure-explained/).

## What the manufacturers say about each basket

Breville's BES870 manual says it plainly: "Use Dual Wall filter baskets if using pre-ground coffee." Single wall baskets are for "grinding fresh whole coffee beans."

The same manual has a useful note on volume. In the 2 cup basket, pre-ground coffee is about 3 level scoops and fresh ground about 4, because "pre-ground coffee is often coarser and hence occupies more volume." So the scoop count changes when you switch, even though the target weight doesn't.

The BES500 manual for the Bambino Plus adds a freshness cutoff. Single wall baskets are for beans "roasted less than 30 days," and dual wall baskets for pre-ground coffee and "older beans that are past the 30 day roast date."

De'Longhi's [EC260 manual](https://static.pcrichard.com/docs/EC260BK_Owners-Manual_01.pdf) for the Stilosa lists a one-shot filter "suitable for ESE pod" and a two-shot filter. Its troubleshooting section says to "use only pre-ground coffee for espresso coffee makers." The manual never calls its filters pressurized, so that detail comes from Coffee Chronicler below.

## Do I need a non-pressurized basket?

- **No grinder, or you buy pre-ground:** stay with the dual wall basket. Both Breville manuals say so, and [Tom's Guide's Erin Bashford](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/coffee-makers/single-vs-dual-walled-baskets-whats-best-for-your-espresso-at-home) says pressurized baskets are for people without a grinder that can grind espresso-fine.
- **Beans past 30 days off roast:** dual wall, per the BES500 manual, even if you grind them fresh.
- **A burr grinder that reaches espresso-fine, and beans under 30 days off roast:** switch to single wall, per the BES500 manual and Bashford.

Enthusiast writers go further. Coffee Chronicler's Asser Christensen says pressurized portafilters "should be avoided if you're serious about learning the barista craft," and Bashford takes the same line for anyone getting serious about espresso. That is an informed opinion, not a measured result.

## Does a pressurized basket ruin the shot? What the measurements show

The evidence is thin. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-espresso-machine) measured extraction yield with a refractometer: 20.7% on the CASABREWS CM5418, which it called "virtually perfect," and 12% on the De'Longhi Stilosa in its comparison table. TechGearLab gives 18 to 22 percent as the ideal yield. The CM5418 ships only with pressurized baskets, per TechRadar.

TechGearLab doesn't say which basket it used for each shot. Neither result can be pinned on the basket type, and no source we read published measurements comparing the two basket types on the same machine.

The "fake crema" claim is opinion too. Coffeeblog.co.uk says pressurized baskets "ensure the illusion of good extraction by creating artificial crema," though it finds the Bambino Plus pressurized basket less synthetic-looking than some. Seattle Coffee Gear says they "create less perfect espresso" but are less finicky.

## Before you buy an upgrade basket: size and fit

1. **Match the diameter.** The CM5418 is 51 mm (TechRadar), the Stilosa 51 mm (Coffee Chronicler), Breville's Bambino Plus and Barista Express 54 mm (Breville manuals), and the Gaggia Classic 58 mm (Tom's Guide).
2. **Check how the basket is held.** A spring clip is the usual method, per TechRadar; the CM5418 uses a notch instead, and TechRadar couldn't find a compatible single wall basket even on Casabrews' own site.
3. **Expect to dial in again.** Breville's BES870 manual says a finer grind in single wall baskets may need a higher grind amount to dose the basket correctly.
4. **A bottomless portafilter is a separate purchase.** It replaces the whole portafilter, so the same size and fit points apply.
