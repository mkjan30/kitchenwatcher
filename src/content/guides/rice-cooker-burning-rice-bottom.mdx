A rice cooker burning rice on the bottom isn't always a fault. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC and NL-BAC manuals both say the bottom layer may be slightly browned, depending on cooking conditions. A hard, dark or burnt layer is different. The manuals and outside tests trace it to starchy water or bran, debris on the heating plate or sensor, a bent pot, custom heat settings or reheating, or a cooker that heats too hard.

If your rice is wet or hard rather than browned, that's a texture problem; see [why rice cooker rice turns out mushy](/guides/rice-cooker-rice-mushy/).

## Is a slightly brown bottom layer normal?

The [Zojirushi NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) (p. 12) puts this in its usage notes: "the rice on the bottom of the Inner Pan may be slightly browned." The [NL-BAC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf) says the same for that model.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) (ATK) draws the line. In the IMUSA 6-Cup (GAU-00011), "a thin, brown overcooked film appeared on the bottom of the rice on more than one occasion," which ATK read as a sign "the heat source was too intense." It contrasts that with the golden-brown crust you might want for tahdig.

Some cookers brown on purpose. ATK notes the Cuckoo 6-Cup HP pressure cooker (CRP-P0609S) has a nurungji preset, and the [Cuckoo CR-0655F manual](https://cuckooamerica.com/cdn/shop/files/CR-0655F_User_Manual.pdf) names Nu Rung Ji among the menus its customized-taste setting applies to.

Why a cooker usually stops short of burning: [Zojirushi's FAQ](https://www.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers) (Q23) says a basic cooker has a thermostat that detects when the temperature "rises above 212°F and turns the heater off." [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-rice-cookers) makes the same point from the other side: on a stove, too much time and the rice "will absorb all of the water and start to burn," while a cooker's sensors can stop the cooking for you.

## Cause 1: starchy, cloudy water and rice cooker scorching

Zojirushi's warnings here sit in the NS-ZCC manual's Pre-Washed rice instructions. Cooking in thick white water "may cause rice to scorch" (p. 13), and if the water looks too thick, change it and rinse the rice. Adjusting the water without stirring "may cause the finished rice to get hard or cause scorching."

For timer cooks, the manual says starch "settles to the bottom" and may scorch, so change the water a couple of times and rinse (p. 17). On p. 19: "If you do not like scorching, rinse just before cooking."

For regular rice, the NL-BAC manual's troubleshooting entry for scorched rice says that after a long soak or a timer cook, "the bran may collect at the bottom," and that rice not washed well enough leaves too much bran on the grain. Zojirushi's FAQ (Q17) adds that rinsing "helps prevent paper-thin layers of starch from forming inside the pan." How much water to add is a separate question, covered in the [rice cooker water ratio guide](/guides/rice-cooker-water-ratio/).

## Cause 2: grains or debris on the heating plate or center sensor

The NL-BAC manual's first listed cause of scorched rice is "foreign matter such as rice" stuck to the outside of the pan, the inside of the main body, the center sensor or the heating plate.

The NS-ZCC manual (p. 18) says to remove stuck grains with a bamboo spatula or chopstick. If grains are stuck to the heating plate, "lightly polish them off with Sand paper (of about #320) dipped in water," then wipe with a damp cloth. Sandpaper goes on nothing but the heating plate or center sensor.

The CR-0655F manual says to make sure the heating plate and magnetic switch "are clean and dry whenever putting the inner pot into the cooker." ATK's [cleaning article](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7279-how-to-clean-rice-cooker) says burned bits "can impede the heating plate from working correctly," and to skip sandpaper on a ceramic plate or sensor.

The full method is in [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).

## Cause 3: a bent, warped or deformed pot

The NL-BAC manual ends its scorched-rice checklist with "Make sure the Inner Cooking Pan has not deformed."

The [Hamilton Beach 37548 manual](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840264200.pdf) says twice: "Make sure bottom of cooking pot is not bent or warped, or it will not conduct heat correctly." Its troubleshooting table files that warning under rice that isn't cooked, not under scorching, and says to call customer assistance to replace the pot.

The CR-0655F manual says not to use a deformed inner pot, and to contact customer service if the pot was dropped or is deformed. The NS-ZCC manual (p. 18) says to contact service when the pan or inner lid is deformed or corroded.

## Cause 4: custom heat settings and reheating

Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual is direct about its My Mode settings: "'MY MODE' function may influence scorching," and "Scorching of rice may occur by longer 'HEAT' Time." It also says a longer HEAT time makes rice "tasty" and that "melanizing will occur," which is browning by design. For custom cooking stages, it says rice "may be scorched in the high cooking stage." The defaults are SOAK 0 and HEAT 0, so going back to them is the first fix.

Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual (p. 15) says not to use Reheat on anything but white rice, not to repeat it, and not to use it when the rice is cold or the pan too cool. It gives scorching as a risk for each. It also says to loosen and level the kept-warm rice before reheating "to avoid scorching or rice becoming hard."

## Cause 5: does a long keep warm scorch the bottom?

No source we could read ties ordinary keep warm to a scorched bottom. The NS-ZCC manual (p. 14) says not to keep rice warm for more than 12 hours, not to add cold rice, not to keep anything but white rice warm and not to leave the spatula in. It links those to "bad odors, drying out or discoloring." The NL-BAC manual gives a similar list, adding small amounts held for long hours, to keep rice from turning dry or yellow.

ATK ran a 12-hour keep-warm test on its top three picks and reported on food-safe temperatures, not browning. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual says WARM runs for up to 12 hours, then the cooker turns off.

How long cooked rice is safe to hold is a separate question; see [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Cause 6: a rice cooker that heats too hard and fast

ATK found that machines which "quickly heated the water and cooked the rice for shorter periods" produced rice that "consistently ranked lower," especially brown rice. The IMUSA film above is its example. Two other ATK notes report bottom browning: the GreenLife 6-Cup, where "the bottom of the rice browned" in larger batches, and the Cuckoo 6-Cup HP pressure cooker (CRP-P0609S), which cooked a full batch about 15 minutes faster than ATK's winner but "scorched the bottoms of most batches" of long-grain white rice.

Reviewed says its Hamilton Beach Rice & Hot Cereal Cooker "had trouble cooking a single cup of rice without overcooking the bottom in some areas," and the rice tended to stick to the bottom of the pot.

Each finding is one outlet on one model, so the evidence is thin. If the right water, a clean plate and a flat pot don't help, the machine may be the problem. Compare models on the [best rice cookers hub](/best/rice-cookers/).

## Rice stuck to the bottom of the rice cooker: getting it out safely

ATK says rice stuck to the bottom of the IMUSA's bowl "several times," which made it hard to clean. Per the NS-ZCC manual (p. 18):

1. Soak the pan in warm or cold water and wash it with a sponge.
2. Don't leave it soaking long; the top of the pan may rust.
3. Don't use brushes, abrasive cleansers or vinegar on it.

ATK adds: skip the abrasive side of the sponge on nonstick. If the coating is already damaged, read [rice cooker inner pot coating peeling](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/).

## When is the rice cooker itself the problem?

Suspect the machine when the pot is deformed, when scorching continues after the fixes above, or when testers have reported it on your model (ATK on the IMUSA and Cuckoo CRP-P0609S; Reviewed on the Hamilton Beach Rice & Hot Cereal Cooker). Zojirushi, Cuckoo and Hamilton Beach all point to customer service for a deformed pot.

ATK's browning and scorching notes name the IMUSA, GreenLife and Cuckoo CRP-P0609S, not the [Zojirushi NS-ZCC10](/reviews/zojirushi-ns-zcc10/) or [Cuckoo CR-0655F](/reviews/cuckoo-cr-0655f/). That isn't the same as saying those two never scorch.
