A rice cooker cup holds about 180 ml (6 oz), roughly 3/4 of a US cup. That's per Zojirushi's rice cooker FAQ ("about 6-ounces, or about 3/4 of a U.S. measuring cup") and the [Hamilton Beach 37548 manual](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840264200.pdf) (6 oz, 177 ml). Zojirushi states its cookers' capacity in that cup, so measure rice and water with the cup that came with your cooker: both makers' water lines and charts assume it.

Below: the rice cooker cup size each manual and tester gives, a conversion table to US cups, why the water lines depend on the cup, and what to do if you lose it.

## How much is a rice cooker cup? What each maker and tester says

The answer is close to 180 ml for the two makers that state a figure, but they don't match to the millilitre, and Cuckoo's capacity table implies a smaller cup. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC10 manual says approx. 180 ml; Hamilton Beach says 6 oz (177 ml), which is 6 fluid ounces at [NIST's 29.57 mL per fl oz](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/approximate-conversions-us-customary-measures-metric). I haven't averaged them.

ATK's figure is the broadest check: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) says "the scoops of all the models in our lineup held approximately ¾ cup." It doesn't list them in millilitres. Cuckoo's [CR-0655F manual](https://cuckooamerica.com/cdn/shop/files/CR-0655F_User_Manual.pdf) never states its cup size; the roughly 170 ml figure is my division of its capacity table (0.34 L for 2 cups, 1.0 L for 6), so treat it as approximate. For Aroma I couldn't open a source that states a figure, so I haven't given one.

## Rice cooker cup to regular cup: the conversion

To convert a rice cooker cup to a regular US cup, multiply by 0.75. That comes from dividing Zojirushi's approx. 180 ml by about 240 ml, which is [NIST's approximate conversion](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/approximate-conversions-us-customary-measures-metric) for 1 cup (0.24 L). Going the other way, a US cup holds about a third more than a rice cooker cup: 240 divided by 180 is about 1.33.

Watch the direction: a rice cooker cup is about 25% smaller than a US cup (60 ml is a quarter of 240), but a US cup is about 33% bigger than a rice cooker cup. The second number is how much extra rice you add if you scoop with a US cup.

These are calculations from two approximate figures, not measurements. With Hamilton Beach's 177 ml cup each row comes out a little smaller, and with Cuckoo's implied 170 ml cup smaller again. I've left out grams on purpose: none of the sources gives a weight for a cup of rice, and it depends on the grain.

## Why a rice cooker cup is 180 ml

The size comes from Japan. Zojirushi's FAQ says its cup is based on a traditional Japanese measuring vessel called a masu. The matching unit is the gō, which [Wikipedia's page on Japanese units of measurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_units_of_measurement) puts at about 180.4 mL and describes as "reckoned as the appropriate size of a serving of rice or sake."

Hamilton Beach, a US brand, uses nearly the same size and says in its 37548 manual that the cup's "size is standard in the rice industry." I can't confirm that every brand follows it. ATK's finding that every scoop it tested held about 3/4 cup suggests most come close, but Cuckoo's capacity table implies about 170 ml, and Aroma doesn't state a figure in any source I could open.

## The water lines in the pot assume the included cup

The numbered lines inside the inner pot are scaled to the included cup, not to a US cup. [ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) describes the usual method: measure with the provided scoop, then fill water to the matching line, for example "the '2' line for two scoops of rice."

Both manuals say this directly:

- Zojirushi's [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) says to measure rice with the provided cup and level it off, and that "using anything other than the provided measuring cup to measure rice may produce slight variations in the finished cooked rice."
- Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual says its "chart is based on measuring rice and water with the rice measuring cup provided," and tells owners not to throw that cup away.

In practice, if you scoop rice with a US cup and fill to the line, you're cooking about a third more rice than the line expects, with the same water. Ratios by grain are on the [rice cooker water ratio guide](/guides/rice-cooker-water-ratio/), and if your rice keeps coming out soft, see [why rice cooker rice turns mushy](/guides/rice-cooker-rice-mushy/).

## Rice cooker cup size vs. capacity: cooked or uncooked?

The cup count in a rice cooker's name can mean uncooked or cooked rice, depending on the brand. Zojirushi's FAQ says capacity "is stated in these 6-ounce cups of uncooked short grain white rice," and that one cup of raw rice doubles once cooked. The NS-ZCC10 spec matches: regular rice 0.18 to 1.0 L, which is 1 to approx. 5.5 cups. Cuckoo's CR-0655F manual also counts raw rice, 2 to 6 cups.

Hamilton Beach's 37548 goes the other way: the "2–14 cup" on its manual cover is cooked rice, since the chart caps raw rice at 7 rice-cooker cups and gives the yield as 14+ cups measured in a US cup.

[Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/home/kitchen-tech/best-rice-cooker-120015478.html) lists the Cuckoo CR-0655F as 6 cups uncooked and 12 cooked. It contradicts itself on its Hamilton Beach budget pick, a different model from the 37548. The spec line says 8 cups uncooked, while the text says the "8-cup designation here is for cooked rice." When sources disagree like that, check the box or manual. For choosing a size, see [what size rice cooker you need](/guides/what-size-rice-cooker-do-i-need/).

## Lost your rice cooker measuring cup? What to do

1. **Check the packaging.** Zojirushi's FAQ says that on a new NS-TSC10/18 the clear cup sits in a hole in the Styrofoam on top of the cooker and can be hard to see. That answer is written for one model, but it's worth checking any box before you order.
2. **Order the brand's own replacement.** Zojirushi's FAQ says measuring cups can be ordered from its Parts and Accessories page or by calling Customer Service at 800-733-6270, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. PST, with the model number ready. For other brands, check the manual or the maker's parts page.
3. **Use a 3/4 US cup measure in the meantime.** This is arithmetic, not a maker's instruction: 3/4 of NIST's approximate 240 ml cup is 180 ml, and Hamilton Beach equates its 177 ml cup to approximately 3/4 US cup. Use the same measure for every scoop so the lines stay in proportion, and remember that Zojirushi warns any other cup may cause slight variations.

### Zojirushi's white and green cups

The NS-ZCC10 ships with two cups, per its manual. The white cup (approx. 180 ml) is for white, semi-brown, brown and sweet rice. The green cup (approx. 171 ml) is for Pre-Washed rice, also called musenmai.

Don't mix them up: the green one holds a little less. If the green cup goes missing, the manual says to use the white cup and fill water slightly above the scale line.
