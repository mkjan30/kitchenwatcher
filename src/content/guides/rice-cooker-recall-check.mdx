As of Nov 18, 2026, the only rice cooker recall we found in CPSC's recall database is 17-017. It covers Sherwood Marketing's 3 Squares Tim3 Machin3 and Mini Tim3 Machin3 rice and slow cookers, announced Oct 18, 2016. Searches for Zojirushi, Aroma and Cuckoo returned no rice cooker, and neither did Tiger, Toshiba, Hamilton Beach or KitchenAid. We searched CPSC's public Recall API by product name and brand ([how we research](/how-we-research/)). Below is how to check any model number yourself.

## The one U.S. rice cooker recall: 3 Squares Tim3 Machin3 (CPSC 17-017)

Everything in this section comes from [CPSC's API record for recall 17-017](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=17017), titled "Sherwood Marketing Recalls 3 Squares Rice and Slow Cookers Due to Fire, Electric Shock Hazards" and dated Oct 18, 2016.

- **What's covered:** 3 Squares-branded Tim3 Machin3 and Mini Tim3 Machin3 programmable rice and slow cookers with fixed power cords, in 20-cup/4-quart and 8-cup/1.7-quart sizes. They are stainless steel with black plastic, have a keypad on the front and measure about 11 inches across and 12 inches tall.
- **Model numbers:** start with 3RC and end in 3010S, 3020S, 3434, 5020 or 9010S. CPSC says the model number is printed on a label on the side of the cooker.
- **Hazard:** improperly installed wiring can cause electrical shorting, a fire and electric shock hazard.
- **Incidents:** three reports, including a cooker sparking when turned on and a cooker turning itself on, plus one shock injury.
- **How many:** about 175,000 units, plus about 100 sold in Canada.
- **Where and when:** sold December 2015 through July 2016 for $30 to $70 at Amazon, Bed Bath & Beyond, Best Buy, Costco, Home Depot, Kohl's, Target, Wayfair, the 3 Squares website and other retailers.

[Mic's Oct 19, 2016 article](https://www.mic.com/articles/157087/slow-cooker-recall-full-list-of-recalled-model-numbers-and-what-to-know-about-fire-hazard) calls this a "full list" of model numbers, but it gives the same 3RC prefix and five endings. That pattern is the full list CPSC publishes.

### Replacement or discard? The sources disagree

CPSC's current record says: "The remedy is not available. Consumers should immediately stop using the recalled rice/slow cookers. Destroy and discard this product." That is not what readers were told in 2016. [WPTV's NBC repost](https://www.wptv.com/money/consumer/3-squares-brand-rice-slow-cookers-recalled-for-fire-shock-hazard) and Mic both said to contact 3 Squares for a free replacement cooker.

[Health Canada's 2016 notice](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/sherwood-marketing-llc-recalls-3-squares-rice-and-slow-cookers) also promised a replacement, with a contact number of 1-800-390-0249. It lists only the 20-cup Tim3 Machin3, in two models, 3RC-3010S and 3RC-9010S, and about 96 units sold in Canada from December 2015 to August 2016, where CPSC says 100.

My advice is to follow the current CPSC record: stop using the cooker and discard it. Don't assume a replacement is still offered.

## How do I check if my rice cooker is recalled?

1. **Find the model number.** It's on the rating label, usually on the side or base. CPSC says the 3 Squares label is on the side.
2. **Search CPSC by brand and by model number.** Use CPSC's recall search on cpsc.gov or the public [Recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=rice), and try both the brand and the full model number.
3. **Search the seller or importer name too, and try spelling variants.** Recalls are titled by the firm that announces them: 17-017 is Sherwood Marketing's recall. A title search for "3 Squares" finds it, but "3Squares" written as one word, as on the cooker's logo, returned zero records on Nov 18, 2026.
4. **In Canada, also check Health Canada's** [recalls and safety alerts site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en), which can list a different set of models than CPSC, as it did for 17-017 (below).

Search several ways, because CPSC's search is literal. A product-name search for [rice](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=rice) returned 29 records on Nov 18, 2026, and only 17-017 was a cooker. Most of the rest matched the letters "rice" inside names such as Fisher-Price, Price Master and Monoprice.

A search for the product name ["rice cooker"](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=rice%20cooker) returned zero records, even though 17-017 exists. So a zero on one search proves little. Try the brand, the model number and the seller before you conclude there's no recall.

## Outside the U.S.: UK Rice Robot recall and border rejection

These are UK actions, not U.S. CPSC recalls. The UK's Office for Product Safety and Standards published [report 2507-0031](https://www.gov.uk/product-safety-alerts-reports-recalls/product-safety-report-rice-robot-ready-set-rice-cooker-sold-via-amazon-2507-0031) on Sep 15, 2025, about the Rice Robot Ready Set rice cooker, 1.2 litres, model 6464-UK, sold via Amazon. It rated the fire risk high: the thermal fuse was not in contact with the heating bowl, so it was unlikely to protect against overheating, and the cooker had non-insulated live metal parts. The import was rejected at the border.

On Dec 16, 2025, OPSS published [recall 2510-0169](https://www.gov.uk/product-safety-alerts-reports-recalls/product-recall-rice-robot-sold-by-spree-tv-2510-0169) for a Rice Robot sold by Spree TV, with the same barcode and the same thermal-fuse fault. Spree TV recalled it from buyers, who are told to stop using it and register for a free replacement.

## Recalls that get mixed up with rice cookers

Multicookers and slow cookers are separate products, even when they have a rice setting. CPSC recall [18-110](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=18110) (Mar 1, 2018) covered Instant Pot Gem 65 8-in-1 multicookers from Double Insight, sold only at Walmart, for a fire hazard. CPSC says to return them to Walmart for a free replacement. For pressure-cooker and multicooker recalls, see our [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/), [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) and [rice cooker vs Instant Pot](/guides/rice-cooker-vs-instant-pot/).

Viral recall posts are the other mix-up. In January 2023 a Facebook post claimed KitchenAid had recalled stand-mixer attachments for lead, and [Snopes rated it False](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/) after finding no recall. If a post names a rice cooker recall, look for a CPSC recall number before acting on it.

A peeling nonstick pot is [a complaint, not a recall](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/), and so is a code on the display ([Zojirushi error codes](/guides/zojirushi-rice-cooker-error-codes/)).

## What if a rice cooker that isn't recalled sparks or turns itself on?

Sparking when turned on and turning itself on are two of the incidents CPSC listed for the 3 Squares cookers. If any rice cooker does either, recalled or not, unplug it, stop using it and contact the manufacturer.

Then report it to CPSC through [SaferProducts.gov](https://www.saferproducts.gov/IncidentReporting). Health Canada's 17-017 notice likewise asks Canadians to report incidents through its Consumer Product Incident Report Form.
