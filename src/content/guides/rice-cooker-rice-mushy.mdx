Rice cooker rice that comes out mushy almost always had too much water for that rice and that setting. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual says "Too much liquid added to cooking pot. Reduce liquid," and Tiger's April 2026 guide says "The primary cause of mushy rice is using too much water." Hard or half-cooked rice usually means too little water or too little soaking, per Tiger, or rice and water that weren't measured correctly, per Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual.

Sticky rice comes from too much water, per Tiger, and from leftover surface starch, per Zojirushi's FAQ. Find your symptom in the table below.

## Why is my rice cooker rice mushy?

### Too much water for the line you used

Tiger calls too much water the primary cause of mushy rice. Tiger says to keep any adjustment to a few millimeters above or below the pot's line; going further gives rice that is undercooked or overly sticky. The lines for each rice are in the [rice cooker water ratio guide](/guides/rice-cooker-water-ratio/).

### The wrong menu

The NS-ZCC manual lists "the correct menu was not selected" as a cause of rice that is too soft, too hard or half-cooked. ATK found long-grain white rice cooks best on the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10's mixed-rice setting, not white, which it said "wasn't obvious at first." ATK reported the same for the Toshiba TRSH01 and the Zojirushi Induction NW-QAC10.

### Parboiled or pre-soaked rice

Zojirushi's FAQ (Q6) says parboiled or converted rice "will result in mushy and overcooked rice" in a rice cooker. The NS-ZCC manual says rice pre-soaked in water "may turn out slightly softer."

### Large batches and machines that run wet

Some machines run wet even at the brand's own line. ATK found the Aroma ARC-914SBD's large batch "gummy and mushy," and the Tiger JBV-A10U "consistently gave us a mushy mass of short-grain white rice." ATK also found the Cuckoo CR-0655F's short-grain and brown rice "a little wet" at Cuckoo's ratios, and found medium-grain brown rice "often too wet" at the recommended ratios. See our [Cuckoo CR-0655F review](/reviews/cuckoo-cr-0655f/) and [brown rice in a rice cooker](/guides/brown-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/).

## Rice cooker rice undercooked or hard in the center

Undercooked rice usually means too little water, a pot that isn't seated, or no rest at the end. Hamilton Beach's 37548 troubleshooting table says to add enough water (follow package directions) and seat the pot securely. A bent or warped pot "will not conduct heat correctly," it says. It also says to rest the rice 10 to 15 minutes after the cooker switches to WARM.

The NS-ZCC manual's matrix also lists a deformed pan or inner lid, objects on the heating plate or center sensor, and the unit being unplugged or Reset pressed during cooking. H04 on the display means the pan isn't placed securely ([Zojirushi error codes](/guides/zojirushi-rice-cooker-error-codes/)). Other causes:

- **Not enough soak.** Tiger names "inadequate soaking time" and suggests soaking 15 to 30 minutes before Quick Cook. For normal cycles, Tiger says the soak is built into the program.
- **Quick menus.** The NS-ZCC manual says Quick Cooking rice "may be slightly harder than normal."
- **Lid opened.** "Do not open the Outer Lid while cooking rice. Rice will not cook properly," per the NS-ZCC manual.
- **Rinse-free rice.** Zojirushi's FAQ (Q28) says unadjusted rinse-free rice "may cook up too hard"; on models without a rinse-free setting, remove 1 heaping teaspoon of rice per cup. The NS-ZCC manual adds that adjusting the water for Pre-Washed rice without stirring "may cause the finished rice to get hard."

## Rice cooker rice hard but not undercooked: texture settings and old rice

The NS-ZCC manual says to decrease the water for new-crop rice and increase it for old-crop rice. It describes its SOFTER menu as "Glutinous, soft and full" and HARDER as "Crisp elasticity." For rice softer than SOFTER, it allows filling up to half a scale above the line, with the warning that "if too much water is used it may boil over." Tiger adds that hard water, high in calcium and magnesium, "tends to make the rice hard."

Rice also firms up the longer it sits warm, per Tiger. For hold times, see [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Why is my rice cooker rice sticky?

Tiger ties stickiness to too much water. Zojirushi's FAQ (Q7) says the film on cooked rice is excess starch, prevented by rinsing until the water runs clear, and Q17 says rinsing makes the rice "fluffier." The NS-ZCC manual says to make the first wash quick, then change the water and wash 4 to 5 times. In its Pre-Washed rice section, it warns that cooking in thick white water "may cause rice to scorch."

The sources disagree on how to rinse. Tiger warns that washing too vigorously scrapes the grains and leaches starch, making rice stickier, and says to skip the colander so grains don't break. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual tells you to rinse in its steam basket under a slow-flowing faucet.

Water matters too: the NS-ZCC manual says rice washed in high-alkaline water "may seem yellow or glutinous."

Resting can help. [Engadget's reviewer](https://www.engadget.com/home/kitchen-tech/best-rice-cooker-120015478.html) found a Hamilton Beach Digital Programmable cooker's white rice "very sticky" when the cycle ended; a half-hour on warm "drastically improved the texture." Engadget doesn't give a model number, so don't assume it is the 37548. The same reviewer liked the Cuckoo CR-0655F's white rice most after half an hour on warm.

## Why does my rice cooker boil over or leak starchy water?

The NS-ZCC manual's matrix links boiling over to water and rice not measured correctly, rice not washed thoroughly, an inner lid that isn't attached, an outer lid not closed firmly and the wrong menu. ATK saw starchy water leak from two spots on the Aroma ARC-914SBD during brown rice, and starchy water escape the Zojirushi Induction NW-QAC10's steam vent cap. To clear dried starch from a vent or lid, see [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).

## Can you fix rice that's already mushy or hard?

No manual we read gives a rescue method for a finished batch. The tricks on other pages are common kitchen advice with no manufacturer behind them. [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/2227211/why-is-my-rice-turning-out-mushy/) suggests baking mushy rice at 350 °F for up to 10 minutes or laying a slice of bread over it. [Hungry Huy](https://www.hungryhuy.com/how-to-fix-mushy-rice/) suggests chilling it for about 20 minutes or drying it at 325 °F in 5-minute steps. [Success Rice](https://successrice.com/the-kitchen/how-to-fix-mushy-rice/) says to drain any water left in a rice cooker's pot and keep cooking on low.

None cites a test. The only manufacturer-backed step is Hamilton Beach's: rest undercooked rice 10 to 15 minutes on WARM. We don't suggest adding water and restarting, because no manual we read covers it; fix the next batch instead.

## Check the measuring cup and the water line first

Zojirushi's FAQ (Q22) says its cup is "about 6-ounces, or about 3/4 of a U.S. measuring cup." Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual gives 6 oz (177 ml), a manufacturer spec. ATK says the scoops of every model it tested held about ¾ cup.

Our arithmetic, not a measurement: a US cup holds about 237 ml. Scoop rice with a US cup and fill water to the cooker's line, and you get more rice than the line expects, which pushes rice toward hard. The full explanation is in [rice cooker cup size](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/).

ATK found the water lines on the Cuckoo CR-0655F, the Aroma ARC-914SBD and the GreenLife 6-cup hard to read, while the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10's white markings were easy.

## When the rice cooker itself is at fault

ATK says not every fuzzy-logic cooker it tested made excellent rice, but every one that did had fuzzy logic. If a pot is bent or warped, Hamilton Beach says to call its customer line for a replacement; Zojirushi says to contact service if the pan or inner lid is deformed or corroded.
