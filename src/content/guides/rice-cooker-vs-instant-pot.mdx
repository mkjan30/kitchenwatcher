The rice cooker vs Instant Pot question has no single answer from the independent testers, because they disagree. [Consumer Reports](https://www.yahoo.com/news/best-rice-cookers-consumer-reports-135809338.html) (2022) quoted its lead tester saying even its bottom-rated rice cookers "still make rice better than a multi-cooker." [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) (2025) rated rice from the Instant Pot Pro 8Qt excellent.

My read of that evidence: if rice is a several-times-a-week staple, or you need it held warm for hours, a fuzzy-logic rice cooker earns its place. If you already own an Instant Pot and make rice now and then, ATK's results say you don't need a second appliance. Everything here comes from published tests and manufacturer documents, not our own cooking ([how we research](/how-we-research/)).

## The short answer: which one should you buy?

Do you need a rice cooker if you have an Instant Pot? Only in some cases:

- **Get a rice cooker if** you cook rice most days or want it held for a long time. Zojirushi's [rice cooker FAQ](https://store.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers) says 12 hours is the keep-warm maximum for most of its cookers (manufacturer spec).
- **Keep using the Instant Pot if** rice is occasional and your batches are modest. Instant's FAQ says never to fill above the 1/2 line for rice and other foods that expand (manufacturer spec).
- **Own both if** you want rice and a pressure-cooked main at the same meal. A single-pot Instant Pot cooks one thing at a time, and Instant says reaching pressure alone takes 10 to 40 minutes or longer.

## Instant Pot rice vs rice cooker rice: what independent testers found

On rice quality, the evidence splits. No tester has published one method that runs the same rice through both machines.

### Where tests favor the rice cooker

Consumer Reports' 2022 roundup says its tasters compared rice-cooker rice with multi-cooker rice. Lead tester Larry Ciufo said even the models "at the bottom of the ratings still make rice better than a multi-cooker." CR doesn't name the multi-cooker, its test covered small 3- to 4-cup cookers, and it dates from May 2022.

ATK's [rice cooker review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) (Oct 23, 2025, 50 batches) found rice quality "superior in the machines with longer cooking cycles." Its fastest cookers made a large batch of long-grain white in about 30 minutes and its slowest took about 65. It tested no Instant Pot. Its one pressure rice cooker, the Cuckoo 6-Cup HP (CRP-P0609S), made 12 cups of long-grain white in 35 minutes, but ATK says it consistently overcooked long-grain white and scorched the bottoms of most batches.

[Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/rice-cooker-vs-instant-pot) editor Cynthia Lawrence (July 28, 2024) writes that her rice-cooker rice was consistently better. She found the Instant Pot Pro good but short of a rice cooker, and a few other Instant Pot models inconsistent. That is one editor's home use, not a controlled test.

### Where tests say the Instant Pot is fine

ATK's [multicooker review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) (May 14, 2025) called the Instant Pot Pro 8Qt's rice function excellent. It said the Pro 6Qt did "an excellent job" cooking rice and the Duo Evo Plus made excellent white rice. The Instant Pot Rio made "decent rice."

ATK also explains why: the Rice setting on most models is "simply shortcuts for pressure-cooking," so a cooker that pressure-cooks well tends to make good rice.

### Why they disagree

The testers used different rice, different ratios and different judges, and CR's multi-cooker is unnamed. Even on speed they split: ATK favors long cycles, while [Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-rice-cookers) heads one finding "The Best Rice Cookers Were Fast," though it ranks good rice ahead of speed. On the published evidence, neither side wins outright.

## Rice cooker vs Instant Pot speed: is the Instant Pot really faster?

Not by as much as the label suggests, and no tester has published a timed head-to-head. The 4-minute figure is not the real time. Instant's time table lists white, jasmine and basmati rice at 4 minutes and brown at 20 (manufacturer spec). Its FAQ says the cooker "must first come to pressure before the countdown begins," which takes 10 to 40 minutes or longer, and release time comes after that.

Rice cooker cycles include steps the Instant number leaves out. Zojirushi's FAQ says rice soaks about 30 minutes and steams about 15, and that QUICK cycles on advanced models cut "up to 20 minutes" (manufacturer claim). Serious Eats timed real batches: around 30 minutes for a large batch in the Hamilton Beach 37548 (not counting quick-cook modes) and 26 minutes for 3 cups of jasmine in the Zojirushi NHS-06.

That is why two common claims don't hold. Tom's Guide's "six minutes" vs "20 minutes" ignores pressure build, and Digital Trends' "up to 30 minutes" saving (updated Apr 21, 2022) cites no test.

For pressure-cooker times, ratios and release methods, see [how to cook rice in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/).

## Which keeps rice warm longer, and which holds more?

Zojirushi's FAQ says "for most rice cookers, 12 hours is the maximum," and to check your manual. Instant's FAQ says its Keep Warm runs "up to 10 hours or until turned off" (manufacturer spec), without stating a temperature.

Serious Eats measured the Aroma Housewares 8-Cup Digital Cool-Touch rice cooker at 200 °F when done, 170 °F at 30 minutes and 154 °F at 60 minutes. It found the rice in the Instant Pot 20-Cup Multigrain Cooker suffered minimal deterioration on keep warm, even after 90 minutes. [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) says to keep cooked food at 140 °F or above and refrigerate perishables within 2 hours. It gives no maximum hold time, so neither do I; see [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

Capacity is counted differently too. Instant says never to fill above the pot's 1/2 line for rice, while Zojirushi rates its cookers in uncooked rice using a cup of about 3/4 US cup. See [rice cooker cup size](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/) and [what size rice cooker you need](/guides/what-size-rice-cooker-do-i-need/).

## Instant also sells non-pressure rice cookers

Instant sells non-pressure rice cookers as well. Its own table for its Rice and Grain Cooker lists white rice at 32 minutes and brown at 52 minutes (manufacturer spec).

Serious Eats named the Instant Pot 20-Cup Multigrain Cooker best for meal prep: 10 cups uncooked, 19.9 pounds, with nearly perfect short-grain rice but brown rice with frayed ends twice. It eliminated the Instant Pot Zest for "very wet white rice."

Consumer Reports went the other way on the Zest in 2022, naming the 8-cup its budget pick and noting it "is actually a 4-cup rice cooker." CR rated it Very Good for rice but said the white rice often came out too sticky. So sources disagree on the Zest, and a search for an "Instant Pot rice cooker" can mean either machine.

## Is a rice cooker worth it?

On the evidence above, a rice cooker is worth it for people who eat rice most days or need rice held warm for hours. It is harder to justify for occasional rice cooks who already own a multicooker that ATK rates well for rice.

Price spans a wide range. Serious Eats' top pick, the Hamilton Beach 37548, was $67 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026, and ATK's winner, the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10, was $239 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026, per the links on those reviews. Our price tracker doesn't hold current prices for these models yet.
