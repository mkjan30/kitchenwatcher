For the rice to water ratio in a rice cooker, measure the rice with the cup that came with the cooker and fill water to the numbered line for that many cups. Zojirushi's FAQ gives one white-rice ratio: 1 1/4 cups water per cup of long-grain white rice, on the Mixed setting, and not for its conventional cookers. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual puts long grain, jasmine and basmati on the same line.

The included cup is not a US cup. Zojirushi's FAQ says it holds about 3/4 of a US measuring cup, so a ratio measured in US cups will not match the pan lines. I compiled this page from the manufacturers' manuals and FAQ, America's Test Kitchen's rice cooker review and three recipe sites. Nothing here was cooked by Kitchen Watcher.

Why does long grain get extra water or its own line? Zojirushi's rice cooker FAQ (Q19) says its inner pans have water lines designed for short/medium-grain white rice. Long grain needs a different setting or a little more water to match.

## How the water lines on the inner pot work

The numbered line is the ratio. Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual gives the rule plainly: 3 level rice cups of rice means water to the "3" line, and never past the 7-cup maximum line. Zojirushi's [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) uses the same rule; its example, drawn on the 1.8-liter pan, is 6 cups of rice with water up to scale 6 of the White rice scale.

Two details from the manuals matter:

- **Set the pan on a level surface.** The NS-ZCC manual says so for accurate measurement, and Cuckoo's [CR-0655F manual](https://cuckooamerica.com/cdn/shop/files/CR-0655F_User_Manual.pdf) says to place the pot on a flat surface before filling.
- **Use the scale for your menu.** The NS-ZCC pan has separate scales for sushi, sweet, porridge, semi-brown and brown rice; its Mixed menu uses the White rice scale. The Cuckoo CR-0655F has separate lines for Glutinous (the manual's name for its white-rice menu, up to 6 cups), Mixed/Brown and GABA (up to 4) and Porridge (up to 1.5).

The cup is about 180 ml per the NS-ZCC manual, and 6 ounces (177 ml) per Hamilton Beach. For the conversion to US cups, see [how big a rice cooker cup is](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/).

## How much water does jasmine rice need in a rice cooker?

Hamilton Beach's 37548 chart is the only manual I read with a jasmine row. It says to use the White Rice setting and the line that matches your rice cups, with a 7-cup batch taking 46–48 minutes (manufacturer spec).

Zojirushi's FAQ (Q20) lists Jasmine as a setting on some of its cookers but gives no jasmine water ratio. Reviewed's [2020 guide](https://reviewed.com/cooking/features/how-to-use-a-rice-cooker) says 1 3/4 cups of water to 1 cup of jasmine, cooked in about 15 minutes. It never says which cup it means or mentions pan lines. [IzzyCooking](https://izzycooking.com/rice-to-water-ratio/) says 1 1/4 cups of water per cup for long-grain white, and that the ratio suits jasmine and basmati.

Those answers disagree. Reviewed calls jasmine a long-grain rice, yet its 1 3/4 ratio is well above the 1 1/4 Zojirushi gives for long grain. Hamilton Beach's line method is tied to the cooker you're using, so start there.

## Basmati rice cooker ratio: why the evidence is thin

Evidence here is thin. Hamilton Beach's 37548 chart treats basmati like other white rice: White Rice setting, fill to the matching line, 48–50 minutes for 7 cups (manufacturer spec). Reviewed's guide says 1 3/4 cups water per cup, about 15 minutes, and IzzyCooking says 1 1/4.

[Bite My Bun](https://www.bitemybun.com/rice-to-water-ratio-in-a-rice-cooker/) calls basmati the tricky one: its writers report 3/4 cup of water per cup of rice in one cooker and 1 1/4 in another. Zojirushi's FAQ does not mention basmati, and none of the other manuals I read has a basmati line. America's Test Kitchen's review covers long-grain, short-grain and brown rice but not basmati. I found no independent kitchen that measured basmati water in a rice cooker.

## Long grain rice in a rice cooker: Mixed setting or its own line

Zojirushi's FAQ (Q19) gives the clearest long-grain rule: 1 1/4 cups of water per cup of rice, on the Mixed setting, with both measured in the included cup. The same answer says its conventional cookers are built mainly for short/medium-grain white rice and these ratios will not work on them.

Some Zojirushi models handle long grain with a line instead. The [NL-BAC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf) says not to use long-grain rice on the White, Sushi or Quick menus, and to use the LONG GRAIN WHITE menu and line. It warns long grain may still come out slightly harder than normal, depending on the brand. To soften it, fill slightly above the line, within 1/2 of a scale, which may boil over.

Hamilton Beach's 37548 uses the White Rice setting and the ordinary line, 50–52 minutes for 7 cups. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) cooked 50 batches across its lineup and reports that, on most machines, the long-grain white ratios "worked perfectly the first time." It also notes its winner, the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10, cooks long grain on the mixed-rice setting, which it found not obvious at first.

## When to add or cut water: what the manuals say

Each manual has its own adjust rule, and they mostly agree on direction:

- **Zojirushi NS-ZCC manual:** decrease the water for new-crop rice and increase it for old-crop rice. For rice softer than the SOFTER menu, fill slightly above the line, within 1/2 of a scale, on SOFTER; too much water may boil over. Rice soaked beforehand may turn out slightly softer.
- **Cuckoo CR-0655F manual:** pour more water than the line for sticky rice or old rice, and less when the rice has soaked well.
- **Zojirushi NL-BAC manual:** if rice is too hard or too soft, raise or lower the water 1–2 mm from the line. A tilted surface, the rice brand, where it was harvested, how long it was stored, and room and water temperature all change texture.

Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual adds that rice package directions may call for different amounts of rice and water than its chart, and following them may vary results. ATK said that "with some small adjustments" to the rice-to-water ratio it could cook rice to its preferences in most machines. If your rice still comes out soft or wet, see [why rice cooker rice turns mushy](/guides/rice-cooker-rice-mushy/).

## Rinse first, and skip parboiled rice

Zojirushi's FAQ (Q17) says rinsing removes excess starch and makes rice fluffier and whiter. Its Q18 says to fill the pan with plenty of cold water and drain immediately, stir the rice, then rinse and drain again, repeating 2–3 times. Its NS-RPC and NHS models should be rinsed in a separate bowl instead of the pan. The Cuckoo CR-0655F manual says to rinse in a separate container until the water runs clear.

Rinse-free rice needs less: Zojirushi's FAQ (Q28) says to remove a heaping teaspoon of rice per cup on models without a rinse-free setting.

Parboiled or converted rice is the one white rice Zojirushi warns against. Its FAQ (Q6) says it comes out mushy and overcooked in a rice cooker and is not recommended.

## Brown rice, quinoa and pressure cooker ratios

Brown rice has its own lines and menus. ATK found the makers' recommended ratios for medium-grain brown rice often too wet and preferred the long-grain white water amount. Timing and settings are on [brown rice in a rice cooker](/guides/brown-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/), and other grains on [quinoa in a rice cooker](/guides/can-you-cook-quinoa-in-a-rice-cooker/). ## Where jasmine and long-grain ratios disagree

For jasmine alone, the sources I read range from 1:1 (Bite My Bun, which says to use the same cup for rice and water) to 1 1/4 (IzzyCooking, matching Zojirushi's long-grain rule) to 1 3/4 (Reviewed). The top of that range uses 75% more water than the bottom.

Part of the gap is the cooker: Zojirushi says its ratio does not carry over to its own conventional cookers. Cup size matters too. Only Zojirushi (about 180 ml) and Hamilton Beach (177 ml) state theirs, and a ratio only holds if the same cup measures both rice and water.

Independent results don't settle it either. ATK found Cuckoo's recommended ratios a little wet for short-grain white and brown rice in the CR-0655F. My rule: follow your pan's lines and your manual's menu chart first, then adjust in the small steps the manuals give.
