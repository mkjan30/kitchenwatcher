There is no KitchenAid mixer recall. As of October 4, 2026, the CPSC recall database lists no KitchenAid (Whirlpool) or Cuisinart (Conair) stand mixer recall in any year. The viral January 2023 post saying KitchenAid recalled its white mixer attachments "for lead" was rated False by Snopes. The only stand mixer recall the CPSC database returns is a 1990 Sunbeam.

Checked against the CPSC/SaferProducts.gov recall API on 2026-10-04. This page is research-only; nobody here handled these mixers.

## Is there a KitchenAid stand mixer recall? Not in the CPSC record

I queried the [SaferProducts.gov recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=mixer), which serves the CPSC's recall records, on 2026-10-04. A title search for "KitchenAid" returns four recalls, and none is a stand mixer: food processors (2000, choking hazard), ProLine toasters (2005, about 40,000 units; elements could self-start after a power cut), coffeemakers (2005, fire hazard) and 1.7 L electric kettles (2018, burn hazard). Details for three of them are in the table below.

So the CPSC database shows no stand mixer recall in 2026, for KitchenAid or anyone else. The newest recall a product-name search for "mixer" returns is from 2008, and it is a blending-cup "mixer," not a stand mixer. Whirlpool-titled recalls add KitchenAid-brand microwave-hood combinations (2001) and glass cooktops (2019), also not mixers. The CPSC covers the US only. [Health Canada's recall site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/search/site?search_api_fulltext=kitchenaid) lists the 2018 kettle and a 2019 KitchenAid cooktop recall, and no mixer.

## The KitchenAid lead recall rumor, and what is actually disputed

The rumor is a Facebook post from January 22, 2023, telling owners of mixers with a white paddle and hook to stop using them because of "extreme amounts of lead." [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/) reported it had more than 30,000 shares by January 24 and rated the recall claim False. Snopes found no Whirlpool recall of mixer components "due to lead or anything else."

KitchenAid's statement, as quoted by Snopes, says its aluminum alloy beaters, dough hooks and whisks are tested by an independent third-party lab for lead content. It adds that all current aluminum accessories have a food-safe coating tested for lead content and lead migration. A KitchenAid spokesperson told [Green Matters](https://www.greenmatters.com/small-changes/was-the-kitchenaid-mixer-recalled) that aluminum alloy "can contain small levels of impurities."

The other side comes from Tamara Rubin of [Lead Safe Mama](https://tamararubin.com/2022/12/december-2022-kitchenaid-update-re-lead-contaminated-cast-aluminum-kitchenaid-paddles-whisk-dough-hooks), whose posts, per Green Matters, started the concern. She reports XRF readings such as 333 ppm lead in a c.2014 paddle, 445 ppm in a burnished paddle and 698 ppm in a c.2006 dough hook. She says the issue matters if a coating chips or the part is bare burnished metal.

The two sides measure different things. Rubin's XRF readings report what a part contains. KitchenAid says it tests migration, meaning how much reaches food. [Krazy Coupon Lady](https://thekrazycouponlady.com/tips/at-home/kitchenaid-mixer-attachment-recall) set a 2,434 ppm reading against the CPSC's 100 ppm limit, but KCL's own wording says that limit applies to products with parts accessible to children, and KCL does not say it applies to kitchen tools.

Sources disagree on risk, and no regulator has acted: there is still no recall in the CPSC record. Green Matters notes the FDA limits trace lead in some utensils rather than requiring zero.

### Did KitchenAid replace attachments for free?

Some owners say so, but it was never a recall program. In December 2022, Rubin [wrote on Lead Safe Mama](https://tamararubin.com/2022/12/december-2022-kitchenaid-update-re-lead-contaminated-cast-aluminum-kitchenaid-paddles-whisk-dough-hooks) that several readers got free stainless steel replacements, then said in a reply that the swaps had stopped; commenters there describe refusals or a 20% discount instead. [Krazy Coupon Lady](https://thekrazycouponlady.com/tips/at-home/kitchenaid-mixer-attachment-recall) quotes one owner who was first offered 20% off, then got a stainless flat beater as a replacement after a supervisor approved it. These are individual accounts, and I found no KitchenAid policy either way.

## Coated beaters that flake: wear, not a recall

Per KitchenAid's support page on [grayish residue on beaters](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Bowl_Lift_Mixer/Beater_Concerns/Grayish_Residue_on_Beater_-_Stand_Mixer), shiny burnished aluminum beaters are not dishwasher safe. Dishwashing or soaking oxidizes them into a gray residue that KitchenAid calls "not harmful" but says may discolor food. KitchenAid sells white-coated beaters for all models as the dishwasher-safe option.

A chipped coating is a wear problem, not a recall. If the coating on a white beater is chipping, retiring it is the conservative choice given the dispute above. Care details are in [are stand mixer parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-stand-mixer-parts-dishwasher-safe/) and [how to clean a stand mixer](/guides/how-to-clean-a-stand-mixer/).

## Cuisinart stand mixer recall: none on record

The CPSC has no Cuisinart stand mixer recall. A title search for "Cuisinart" on 2026-10-04 returns six recalls (Cuisinart is a Conair brand), and none is a mixer:

- Food processors in Nov 2013 (14-029) and Dec 2016 (17-050), both for laceration hazards.
- The CBJ-450 compact blender and juice extractor combo, Dec 1, 2022 (23-056): about 66,000 units, where the nut holding the blade could loosen.
- Metal wire bristle grill brushes, Jul 2, 2026 (26-601), expanded Aug 27, 2026 (26-717).
- Propel+ four-burner 3-in-1 gas grills, Jul 9, 2026 (26-605).

The 2026 brush and grill recalls are recent, but none of them is a mixer. [Health Canada's Cuisinart results](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/search/site?search_api_fulltext=cuisinart%20mixer) show the same blender, brush and grill recalls plus a 2019 Cuisinart vertical waffle iron recall, and no mixer. For the mixer itself, see the [Cuisinart SM-50 review](/reviews/cuisinart-sm-50/).

## How to check your own mixer in 3 minutes

1. **Find the model and serial number** on the mixer's nameplate. Write both down.
2. **Search [cpsc.gov/Recalls](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls)** for the brand, then for the word "mixer."
3. **In Canada, also search [Health Canada's recall site](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en)**, which lists Canadian sales separately.
4. **Check Whirlpool's product recall page** (repair.whirlpoolcorp.com/product-recall, the page Snopes cites) for KitchenAid. It did not load for me on 2026-10-04 (connection refused) or 2026-10-05, so the CPSC search is the dependable step.
5. **Treat any post that gives a phone number but no CPSC link as unverified.** The 2023 rumor did exactly that.
6. **Report a problem** at [SaferProducts.gov](https://www.saferproducts.gov/), the CPSC's public incident database.

A real recall names models and serial or date-code ranges, as every record in the table does. If your model and serial number aren't in a recall's list, that recall doesn't cover your mixer.
