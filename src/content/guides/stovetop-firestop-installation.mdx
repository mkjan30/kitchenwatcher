To install a StoveTop FireStop Rangehood, confirm the underside of your hood is 27 to 35 inches above the cooktop, clean and dry a spot on each side centered between the front and back burners, and lift each canister up until its magnet holds. If your hood is non-magnetic stainless, fit the maker's $2.75 adhesive washer kit first. That's the whole job, per the maker's manual.

Everything below follows StoveTop FireStop's own documents: the Rangehood User Manual (675-3-117 Rev B, February 2017), the company's FAQ, and the Rangehood/STFS Plus Adhesive Mounting Kit insert (675-123). For whether it's worth buying, see the [StoveTop FireStop review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/); for how it compares with other options, see the [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/). ## Before you start

- **Check the stove.** The manual says the Rangehood is not recommended for gas stoves, because the powder may put out the burner flame. It's also designed for no more than 3/8 inch of oil before food is added.
- **Check the date code.** The CPSC recalled Rangehood units made November 7 to 13, 2016; they have date code 4516 stamped under the magnet assembly ([CPSC recall 20-037](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires)). Also note the replace-by date on the label; the maker gives a six-year effective life.
- **Don't drop it.** The manual says to discard a unit that has been dropped or damaged.
- **You'll need:** a tape measure, a pencil or painter's tape, a clean dry cloth, and a fridge magnet to test your hood.

## Step 1: Measure the hood height

Measure from the cooking surface up to the underside of the hood where the magnet will sit. The manual's range for the Rangehood is **27 to 35 inches (68 to 88 cm)**.

If a microwave or cabinet sits 15 to 27 inches above the cooktop instead, the maker's FAQ says you need its Microhood, which attaches to the back wall with the included drywall anchors and screws. Above 35 inches, the Rangehood is outside the maker's stated range.

## Step 2: Mark the mounting points

Per the manual, position each canister at the midpoint between the center of the front burner and the center of the back burner, one on each side. Mark both spots.

## Step 3: Test the magnet

Hold a fridge magnet to each marked spot. If it sticks, go to step 5. If not, the maker's FAQ says to order its alternative mounting kit, as long as the hood height is within 27 to 35 inches. The kit page says it's for stainless steel or other non-magnetic hoods.

## Step 4 (non-magnetic hoods only): Fit the adhesive washers

The kit contains two metal washers, two adhesive discs and an alcohol prep pad; the maker's page says the adhesive was developed by 3M. Per the kit's insert:

1. Use the prep pad to clean the spots you marked, and make sure each is completely dry and free of grease.
2. Peel the backing from one side of an adhesive disc and stick it to a metal washer.
3. Peel the remaining backing and press the washer assembly firmly onto the marked spot.
4. Wait 60 minutes. {/* VERIFY: position of the kit's 60-minute wait — insert text says "Wait 60 minutes" but extracted step order is ambiguous */}
5. Once the washers are firmly secured, attach each canister's magnet directly to its washer.

The insert warns that not following its instructions could affect product performance.

## Step 5: Clean, dry and hang

The manual says to clean and dry the areas where the magnets will attach, then raise each canister to the spot directly above. The 2017 FAQ adds that the canisters may tilt slightly depending on the hood. Finally, the manual says to put the enclosed decal where it can be seen in the ventilation hood.

## Keeping it working

The maker's FAQ says the canisters need no maintenance, inspections or repairs during their six-year life, and that grease build-up can be gently wiped with a damp cloth, keeping liquid off the fuse and the label. The 2017 manual is stricter and says simply "do not clean units", so if in doubt, leave them alone. The manual warns that missing the replace-by date could reduce effectiveness, so write the date somewhere you'll see it. {/* VERIFY: current manual revision (product page lists Rev G; Rev B read) — confirm height range and cleaning guidance */}

## Safety notes

- **Don't test it by lighting a fire.** It's single use: the manual says to discard it after activation, and Reviewed.com lists single use as a con ([Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/home-outdoors/best-right-now/the-best-fire-extinguishers-for-homeowners)).
- **Know its limits.** The manual says it may not suppress all stovetop fires and that reignition is possible. If it activates, the manual says to turn the burner off right away if it's safe to.
- **It isn't UL-listed.** StoveTop FireStop's FAQ says it doesn't have UL certification. The [review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/) covers what that means.
- **It doesn't replace the basics.** The U.S. Fire Administration says to stand by your pan and turn the burner off if you leave the kitchen ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA's [cooking safety tip sheet](https://sanfordfl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/CookingSafety.pdf) says to smother a small pan fire with a lid and turn off the burner, and if you have any doubt, get out and call 9-1-1 from outside.
- **Keep an extinguisher by the exit.** See the [Kidde FA110G review](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/), including how to check whether yours is on the 2017 recall.
