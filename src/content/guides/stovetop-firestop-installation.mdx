To install a StoveTop FireStop Rangehood, check that the underside of your hood is 27 to 35 inches above the cooking surface, mark a point on each side centered between the front and back burners, clean and dry those spots, and let the magnets hold each canister there. On a stainless hood that won't hold a magnet, use the maker's adhesive washer kit first. {/* MEASURE: total install time, minutes, magnet method */}

Everything below follows StoveTop FireStop's own installation FAQ and the adhesive kit insert. For whether it works, see my [StoveTop FireStop review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/); for how it compares with other options, see the [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/).

## Before you start

- **Check the date code.** The CPSC recalled Rangehood units made November 7 to 13, 2016; they have date code 4516 stamped into the top of the can under the magnet assembly ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires)). Also check the replace-by date: the maker gives a six-year effective life. {/* MEASURE: photo — date code and replace-by label before hanging */}
- **Keep liquid off the fuse.** The maker says no liquids should touch the fuse on the underside or the label.
- **You'll need:** a tape measure, a pencil or painter's tape, a clean dry cloth, and a fridge magnet to test your hood.

## Step 1: Measure the hood height

Measure from the cooking surface up to the underside of the hood where the magnet will sit. The maker's range for the Rangehood is **27 to 35 inches**. {/* MEASURE: photo — hero: tape measure from cooktop to hood underside */}

If you have a microwave or cabinet above the stove instead, 15 to 27 inches up, the maker says you need its Microhood model, which mounts differently. If your hood is higher than 35 inches, the Rangehood isn't specified for it.

## Step 2: Mark the mounting points

Per the maker, center each canister at the midpoint between the front and back burners on each side. One pair covers a typical four-burner stove, the maker says. Mark both spots. {/* MEASURE: photo — marking the midpoint between front and back burners */}

## Step 3: Test the magnet

Hold a fridge magnet to each marked spot. If it sticks, skip to step 5. If not, you need the adhesive kit. The maker says it made the kit because many kitchens have stainless steel hoods and stainless steel is non-magnetic. {/* MEASURE: photo — magnet test on a stainless hood */}

## Step 4 (stainless hoods only): Fit the adhesive washers

The maker's Rangehood adhesive kit costs $2.75 and contains two metal washers, two adhesive discs and an alcohol prep pad. Per the kit's insert:

1. Clean each marked spot with the prep pad and make sure it's completely dry.
2. Peel the backing from one side of an adhesive disc and stick it to a metal washer.
3. Peel the other backing and press the washer firmly onto the marked spot.
4. The insert includes a 60-minute wait before the canisters go on.
5. Attach each canister's magnet directly to its washer.

{/* MEASURE: hands-on install time for the adhesive method, minutes, excluding the wait */} {/* MEASURE: photo — adhesive disc on washer, then washer on hood */}

The insert warns that not following the instructions could hurt product performance.

## Step 5: Clean, dry and hang

Clean and thoroughly dry each mounting point so the magnet holds, then raise each canister to the underside of the hood by its magnet. The maker says the canisters may tilt slightly depending on the hood. {/* MEASURE: canister tilt after hanging, degrees */} {/* MEASURE: photo — both canisters hung, from below */}

## Keeping it working

The maker says the canisters need no maintenance, inspection or repair during their six-year life. If grease builds up, wipe it gently with a damp cloth and keep liquid off the fuse and label. Write the replace-by date somewhere you'll see it. {/* MEASURE: grease wipe after 2 weeks of cooking left fuse and label dry, pass/fail */}

## Safety notes

- **Don't test it by lighting a fire.** Reviewed.com lists it as not reusable, so an activation spends the canister ([Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/home-outdoors/best-right-now/the-best-fire-extinguishers-for-homeowners)).
- **It isn't UL-listed.** StoveTop FireStop's own FAQ says it doesn't have UL certification. I cover what that means in the [review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/).
- **It doesn't replace the basics.** The U.S. Fire Administration's advice for a pan fire is to cover it with a lid and turn off the burner, and never use water on grease ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA found unattended cooking was the leading factor in home cooking fires ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). If a fire spreads, get out and call 911.
- **Keep an extinguisher by the exit.** I'm testing the [Kidde FA110G](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/), including how to check whether yours is on the 2017 recall.
