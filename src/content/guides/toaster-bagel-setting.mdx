Put the halves in with the cut sides facing the middle of the toaster, then press Bagel. That is the order [GE's 2- and 4-slice toaster manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) gives, and Breville's [BTA720/730 booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) says its Crumpet setting adds heat to the middle elements. But a toaster bagel setting doesn't work the same on every model: Cuisinart's CPT-2000 manual says the cut side faces right, and Reviewed says the Haden Dorset wants it facing out. Check the icon or manual first.

## Which way to put a bagel in the toaster

Here are the steps from GE's owner's manual for its 2- and 4-slice toasters (manual 49-9000001). The copy Lowe's hosts names the G9TMA2SSPSS and G9TMA4SSPSS on its cover, and [GE's own G9TMA2SSPSS page](https://www.geappliances.com/appliance/GE-2-Slice-Toaster-G9TMA2SSPSS) links a newer revision of the same manual with the same bagel steps:

1. Slice the bagel and put the halves in the slots "with cut side toward the inside of the toaster."
2. Leave the browning control at your usual setting.
3. Lower the lever, then press the Bagel button.
4. The bagel pops up when it's ready.

GE's manual also says the lever won't lock down unless the toaster is plugged in, so a lever that springs back up isn't a bagel problem. For other causes, see [why a toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).

KitchenAid's [Pro Line help page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Other_Countertop_Appliances/Toasters/Pro_Line_Toaster/Operation/Toasting_Bagels_in_Pro_Line_Toaster) reverses the order. You set the shade, press Bagel first, then insert the halves "with the cut sides facing in," and the toaster lowers them by itself. It raises the bagel and beeps when done, and switches to Keep Warm if you don't take it out within 45 seconds.

## What the bagel button does on different toasters

Three manuals describe three different mechanisms:

- **More heat in the middle.** Breville's BTA720/730 booklet (the Australian and New Zealand edition) says its Crumpet setting "activates additional heat to the middle heating elements" to toast the inside without burning the outside. It adds that it's normal for the outside elements to look off or low while the setting runs. Breville's [US instruction book for the BTA720 and BTA730](https://assets.breville.com/BTA720/BTA720_BTA730_USCM_IB_F26_LR.pdf) says the same thing word for word about its Bagel button, and tells you to load the cut bagel with the inside facing inwards, per the bagel markings above the middle elements.
- **Less heat on one side.** Cuisinart's [CPT-2000 manual](https://auto.manualsonline.com/manuals/mfg/cuisinart/cpt2000_1.html?p=4) says "the bagel feature reduces heat on one side of the toasting slot."
- **More time only.** Cuisinart's [CPT-180 manual](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/62580/cuisinart-cpt-180.html?page=3) describes its Bagel button as "Adds extra time to toasting cycle." It says nothing about the elements.

The sources disagree on time. [The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com/1773954/what-does-toaster-bagel-button-do/) says the bagel button heats the inner coils more and shortens toasting time, and it cites no manual. Cuisinart's CPT-180 manual says the opposite about time. Both can be right for different designs, so your own toaster's manual is the one to trust.

## When the cut side should face out, or to one side

Cut side in is the common rule, not a universal one. Reviewed says that to toast bagels in the Haden Dorset, "you insert them with the cut side facing out rather than in," and lists "unintuitive toasting bagels" as a con. Haden's own manual wasn't among the sources read, so that rule rests on Reviewed alone.

Cuisinart's CPT-2000 has an embossed icon on top of the toaster, above the control panel, that shows how to load a bagel. The manual says: "Looking straight at the toaster the cut side should always face to the right."

America's Test Kitchen found that the Smeg 4-slice's bagel function heated the outer slots rather than the inner ones. Its testers said they wasted a bagel, and that the toaster stayed stuck on bagel mode for a few cycles. Before your first bagel, look for a bagel icon or markings on top of the toaster, or read the bagel section of the manual.

## How to toast a bagel without a bagel setting

Reviewed's advice for a toaster without a bagel setting is to put the soft side facing in, so the bagel doesn't toast unevenly or burn. It also notes that bagels are dense and need more toasting time than a slice of bread.

[Food Republic](https://www.foodrepublic.com/1461742/which-way-face-bagels-toaster-crispy) suggests using a lower overall setting so the outside doesn't overcook. It gives no source or test for that, so treat it as a starting point and watch the first one. For what each browning number means, see [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/).

Food Republic also suggests dunking stale bagels in water before toasting. None of the manuals read for this page support that. Breville's booklet warns never to add any food substance to a bagel before toasting, such as butter, jelly or marmalade, because it may cause a fire or electrocution.

## Does the bagel setting work? What independent testers found

TechGearLab weights bagels at 35% of its overall toaster score and says its testers made almost 250 bagels. It wants the cut side crispy and golden and the crust gently warmed. Of the six models above, the Breville BTA720XL scored highest at 7.0, and TechGearLab says its bagel setting manages to toast the cut side. The GE scored 6.5, the BTA820XL 6.3, the CPT-180 6.0, the T2569B 5.3 and the CPT-122 4.5.

America's Test Kitchen found the GE toasted bagels relatively evenly in under 2 minutes. On the BLACK+DECKER 2-slice, ATK says "bagels especially had entire sections" that looked untoasted.

The testers disagree on two models. Reviewed says the T2569B's bagel setting, "unlike most," toasts both sides, while TechGearLab lists "lackluster bagel performance" as a con. Black+Decker's own T2569C/BC manual describes the usual design instead: crispy on the cut side, the outside gently warmed.

Reviewed says the CPT-122's bagel setting "toasted bagels beautifully," while TechGearLab says the CPT-122 "left a lot to be desired" and scored it lowest of the six. If bagels matter, see the [toasters ranked by bagel and bread results](/best/toasters/), or the [Breville BTA720XL review](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) and [Cuisinart CPT-180 review](/reviews/cuisinart-cpt-180/). If your bagels come out pale in patches, that's covered in [why a toaster toasts unevenly](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-toasting-unevenly/).

## Fat bagels, frozen bagels and slot fit

Reviewed says a fat New York-style bagel is a tight fit in the Cuisinart CPT-122, and that bagels didn't pop up high enough to grab without lifting the lever. America's Test Kitchen found that bagels stuck out of the shallow slots of an Oster 4-slice long-slot toaster and came out with untoasted strips.

GE's manual warns that "oversized foods, metal foil packages, or utensils must not be inserted in a toaster, as they may create a risk of fire or electric shock." Breville's booklet says to make sure items aren't too large or too thick to fit the slots. If a bagel half doesn't slide in freely, see [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).

For frozen bagels, KitchenAid's Pro Line bagel help page doesn't cover a frozen step. GE's manual gives the Frozen button its own steps for frozen bread and doesn't say whether to combine it with Bagel.

## If a bagel gets stuck in the toaster

GE's manual says to unplug the toaster and let it cool, then remove the bread carefully, and to "never use a metal object for this purpose." Breville's booklet says metal utensils such as a knife "should not be inserted into the toaster to remove jammed toast, bagels or any other items," because it can damage the elements and lead to electrocution.

For the full procedure, see [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/). If you see flames, go to [what to do in a toaster fire](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).
