Toaster fire, what to do, in order:

1. **Cut the power, but only if you can reach the plug safely.** The University of York's toaster notice says to isolate the power if safe to do so. Breville's BTA720/BTA730 booklet says its toaster is turned off by removing the plug.
2. **Never use water.** York says never use water on electrical fires.
3. **Use an extinguisher only if the fire is small and in one object,** the fire department has been called and you have a clear way out, per the U.S. Fire Administration.
4. **Otherwise get everyone out,** closing doors if it's safe (York), and call 911 from a mobile or a neighbor's phone (USFA).

Everything below is sourced to manufacturer manuals and fire-safety bodies; nobody here has put out a toaster fire for this page. See [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Step 1: Cut the power to the toaster if you can do it safely

Pull the plug if it's within reach and away from the toaster flames. That is how the manuals say to turn a toaster off. Breville's Australian/New Zealand [BTA720/BTA730 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) says "To turn the toaster off, remove the power plug from the power outlet." GE's [2 and 4 slice toaster manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf), which covers the G9TMA2SSPSS, says to unplug the toaster before performing any service.

The [University of York notice](https://www.york.ac.uk/staff/essentials/health-and-safety/news/2026/safety-alert-fire-safety-notice-toasters/) adds the key condition: isolate the power only if it's safe. Don't reach across or over the flames for the cord. [Paul Davis](https://pauldavis.com/blog/fast-action-against-toaster-fires/), a restoration company, suggests using the fuse box to cut power when unplugging isn't safe. That is the only source I found for the breaker step, and it doesn't say when walking to the panel would itself be unsafe.

What about the Cancel button? Breville says Cancel stops toasting and moves the carriage to the raised position. GE says Cancel ejects any contents, and its troubleshooting chart says to press Cancel when smoke comes out. Neither manual says anything about pressing Cancel when there are flames, and ejecting the contents brings burning bread up out of the slots. The step the sources do support is cutting the power.

## Step 2: How to put out a toaster fire, or when to get out

The U.S. Fire Administration's [extinguisher page](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/) says to use an extinguisher only if you can answer yes to six questions. They ask whether others are alerted, the fire department has been called, you're physically able, the fire is small and contained in a single object, you're safe from the smoke, and you have a clear escape route. If any answer is no, leave and call 911. It also says young children and older adults should not use extinguishers.

For the extinguisher itself, the USFA says Class C covers electrical equipment plugged into an outlet and its agent doesn't conduct electricity. Multipurpose units labeled B-C or A-B-C cover most home fires. Which one to buy is covered in [what fire extinguisher for a kitchen](/guides/what-fire-extinguisher-for-kitchen/), and the PASS steps are in [how to use a fire extinguisher](/guides/how-to-use-a-fire-extinguisher/).

A fire blanket is the other option. Paul Davis suggests covering the toaster with one, and York lists an extinguisher or fire blanket, but only if you're trained. York's notice is written for UK university staff kitchens, not US homes; the USFA also stresses training and says fire departments can help with it.

## What not to do in a toaster fire: water, knives, flour

- **No water.** York says never use water on electrical fires, and GE says not to immerse the toaster, cord or plug in water.
- **No knives, forks or tongs in the slots.** GE says never use a metal object to free bread, and Breville says metal utensils such as a knife should not be inserted.
- **No flour.** Paul Davis warns flour could cause an explosion (it suggests baking soda or salt instead). For flour, baking soda and salt on cooking fires, see [how to put out a grease fire](/guides/how-to-put-out-a-grease-fire/).
- **No wet towel.** York's rule is no water on electrical fires, and none of the sources read for this page suggests a wet towel.
- **Don't fan it.** Paul Davis says fanning gives the fire more oxygen.

## Why did my toaster catch fire? What the manuals and recalls say

Breville and Dualit tie crumbs directly to fire. Breville says failing to clean the crumb tray regularly may result in a fire hazard. [Dualit's safety advice](https://help.dualit.com/en-US/classic-toaster---be-aware-safety-advice-254925) calls emptying it "vital to avoid a risk of fire." GE says to operate the toaster with the crumb tray in place to reduce the risk of fire.

Placement is next. GE and Breville both say a fire may occur if the toaster is covered or touching curtains, draperies or walls, and Dualit says not to keep a toaster under cupboards or shelves. GE and Breville also warn that oversized foods, metal foil packages and utensils may create a risk of fire or electric shock. Dualit says jam-filled tarts and sugar-coated items should not be toasted. The full list is in [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).

Unattended use matters too. Breville and Dualit both say not to leave a toaster running unattended. The USFA's [cooking fire page](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/) says unattended equipment was the leading factor, at 37%, in nonconfined home cooking fires; that figure covers cooking fires generally, not toasters alone.

Sometimes the toaster itself is the fault. CPSC recall [08-413](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2008/Wal-Mart-Recalls-General-Electric-Toasters-Due-to-Fire-and-Shock-Hazards) covered about 210,000 GE-branded toasters, models 169115 and 169116, sold at Wal-Mart from September 2007 to July 2008, for a possible short circuit between the heating element and bread cage. Wal-Mart had 140 reports of fires or sparks, or of the toasters tripping the circuit breaker, and no injuries.

The remedy was to stop using them and return them to Wal-Mart. The current GE G9TMA2SSPSS is not one of those model numbers. Check yours in the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

## Can you use a toaster again after it caught fire?

No, not without the maker checking it. GE's manual says not to operate any appliance after it malfunctions or has been damaged in any manner and to contact Consumer Support. Breville says to cease use immediately and call Breville Consumer Service if the appliance becomes damaged in any way. Dualit says a toaster showing any sign of damage should be unplugged and not used.

Don't open it up. GE's manual says the outer cover has a tamper-resistant screw, there are no user-serviceable parts inside, and repair should be done only by authorized service personnel.

If the maker clears it for use, note that both manuals start cleaning with unplugging and letting it cool. Then they split: GE says "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove the crumbs," while Breville says to turn the toaster upside down over a bin and shake crumbs free. Follow your own model's manual; [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) covers both.

Then check whether your model has been recalled with the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/), and tell the maker what happened when you contact them, as GE and Breville both ask after a malfunction.

## Is it a toaster fire or just smoke?

Smoke without flames is a different problem. GE's troubleshooting chart says that when smoke comes out, push Cancel to stop toasting and empty the crumb tray if it's full. Breville says a thin smoke or odor on first use is normal. If that's what you're seeing, read [why is my toaster smoking](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-smoking/) instead.
