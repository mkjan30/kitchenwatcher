The CPSC recall database lists no pop-up toaster recall newer than Bodum's Bistro recall of July 2019. Every Hamilton Beach, KitchenAid, Black+Decker and GE toaster recall it lists dates from 2011 or earlier, and none of the six toasters on our [best toasters](/best/toasters/) list is covered. I checked on November 9, 2026, through the CPSC's saferproducts.gov Recall API, which returned 24 entries for "toaster". If you are looking for a toaster recall, match your model and code below. How I check sources is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## How to check your toaster against a recall in 3 steps

1. **Turn the toaster over and read the label.** For the Hamilton Beach, Proctor-Silex, KitchenAid, GE, Bodum and Black & Decker pop-up recalls in the table, the CPSC records say the model number (and the series code or serial number where one applies) is on the bottom. A few older entries differ: the Black & Decker Spacemaker T1000 has its model stamped on the back, and the Wolfgang Puck units have it on a label on the back.
2. **Match both the model and the code in the table below.** A model match alone is not enough for most Hamilton Beach recalls. Recall 11-265 covers model 22600 only with series codes C or D 0190 to 5290 ending in BI, and 08-212 covers 17 models only with specific series codes that the CPSC page does not list.
3. **If it matches, unplug it, stop using it and contact the company.** If the model number does not match any recall here, the toaster is not under these recalls.

### Hamilton Beach toaster recall (and Proctor-Silex)

Hamilton Beach and its Proctor-Silex brand account for four of the toaster recalls in the CPSC list. Recall 12-057 covers model 22602, about 14,000 units sold from August to November 2011, whose element could turn on when first plugged in even with the lifter up. The CPSC page says 5 reports and no injuries, and that some 22602s went out as replacements for recalled 22600s. So a replacement toaster can itself be recalled.

Recall 11-265 covers model 22600 with C or D series codes, about 300,000 units sold from February 2008 to June 2011, after 15 reports of toasters not popping up. Recall 08-212 covers 17 Hamilton Beach and Proctor-Silex models, about 482,000 units, after at least 63 reports of toasters staying on after pop-up. For Proctor-Silex models 24205 and 24208, Proctor-Silex's own 2000 recall page lists the series codes in the table.

The phone numbers conflict. The CPSC pages for 12-057 and 11-265 list 800-851-8900, with a note that the firm's recall hotline changed to that number on May 1, 2024. The CPSC's API records for the same recalls still show (800) 672-5872.

Hamilton Beach's own recall page for the 22600 gives 1-800-672-5872. Proctor-Silex's recall page for the 2000 recall gives (800) 992-4616, while the CPSC's API record for that recall gives 800-851-8900. The May 2024 CPSC note is the most recent dated change, so 800-851-8900 is the number to try first. I re-read all four sources on November 9, 2026 and they still disagree. I haven't phoned the numbers, so if one doesn't help, try the next.

Hamilton Beach says it sends the 22600 replacement in about 2 weeks, asks you to return the old toaster's plug in a prepaid envelope, and says not to sell or give the recalled toaster away.

### KitchenAid toaster recall: the real one and the rumor

The KitchenAid toaster recall is real. On March 24, 2005, the CPSC and Whirlpool recalled about 40,000 ProLine 2- and 4-slice toasters, models KPTT780 and KPTT890 in the PM, ER, BU and TG finishes, with serial numbers beginning with the letters WTP or WTR. They sold from August 2003 to January 2005 for $250 to $300.

After a power interruption the elements could start by themselves and ignite items left on top. The [CPSC page](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2005/CPSC-Whirlpool-Announce-Recall-of-KitchenAid-Toasters-) reports 3 incidents, including 1 fire outside the US, and no injuries. The remedy is a free replacement from KitchenAid at (800) 874-0608. The CPSC says not to return the toaster to the store.

The viral "KitchenAid recall" posts from January 2023 were about something else. They claimed stand-mixer paddles and hooks had been recalled for lead, and [Snopes rated that claim False](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/). It had nothing to do with toasters.

### Black and Decker toaster recall

Two Black & Decker pop-up recalls are in the CPSC list. VersaToast recall 02-099 is the largest toaster recall on this page, at about 2.1 million wide-slot T1200, T1250, T1400 and T1450 toasters sold from June 1999 to January 2002. The CPSC reported 9 fires after a period of non-use, because the element could keep operating, and the remedy was a refund.

Recall 08-111 covers about 70,000 Infrawave ST2000 toasters sold from March to November 2007, where an electrical component could ignite the circuit board. The CPSC reported 2 incidents, one of which damaged a countertop and cabinets, and offered a refund.

A third entry, 98-097, is a 1998 settlement over horizontal Spacemaker Optima T1000 Type 1 toasters. Per the CPSC API record, Black & Decker agreed to replace the toasters for free instead of offering a coupon. The T2569B on our hub is not covered by any of these; see [GE vs Black+Decker T2569B](/vs/ge-g9tma2sspss-vs-black-decker-t2569b/).

### GE toaster recall (sold at Walmart)

Recall 08-413 covers about 210,000 GE-brand toasters sold only at Wal-Mart from September 2007 to July 2008, models 169115 and 169116 (2-slice and 4-slice versions; the CPSC page does not clearly say which number is which). A short between the element and the bread cage led to 140 reports of fires, sparks or tripped breakers, with no injuries, per the CPSC. Wal-Mart offered a refund or replacement. The current GE G9TMA2SSPSS on our hub is a different model and is not covered.

## Toaster recall or toaster oven recall?

The newest entry in the CPSC's toaster results is a toaster oven, not a pop-up toaster. Recall 26-618 from July 16, 2026, covers about 11,480 Panasonic NB-G200 electric toaster ovens in the US, where the power cord insulation can be insufficient because a protective fiberglass sleeve does not cover it adequately, per the CPSC. The remedy is a full refund.

[CTV News reported](https://www.ctvnews.ca/business/article/nearly-14-million-toaster-ovens-recalled-in-canada-and-us-over-burn-hazard/) in September 2025 on a recall of Oster French door countertop ovens in Canada and the US. Check toaster ovens directly with the CPSC. Kitchen Watcher does not cover them yet.

## Is an old toaster recall still open?

The evidence here is thin. Neither the CPSC pages nor the Hamilton Beach and Proctor-Silex recall pages give an end date, so call the company with your model and code. The CPSC's own record shows owners often miss these: its 1991 second notice for Toastmaster D126T toasters says only 7 percent had been returned after a year.

If there is no remedy left, stop using the toaster anyway, because every recall above tells owners to stop using it. Don't sell it or give it away; Hamilton Beach's recall page says so directly.

## What the recall database shows for our current toaster picks

The CPSC API shows no recall for Breville, Cuisinart, Smeg, Zwilling, Magimix, Revolution or Mueller toasters as of November 9, 2026. None of the six picks on [best toasters](/best/toasters/) is covered, including the [Breville BTA720XL](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) and [Cuisinart CPT-180](/reviews/cuisinart-cpt-180/). That is a statement about the recall record only, not a safety rating.

## How to hear about future toaster recalls

The CPSC release reprinted on Proctor-Silex's recall page says recall information is available at cpsc.gov and through the CPSC's free email newsletters. To report a toaster problem yourself, Proctor-Silex's recall page points to SaferProducts.gov and lists the CPSC hotline as 800-638-2772.

The self-start and stay-on faults above are why unplugging matters; [is it safe to leave a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/) covers that habit. If a toaster is already burning, go to [toaster fire: what to do](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).
