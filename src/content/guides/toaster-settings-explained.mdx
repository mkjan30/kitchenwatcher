Toaster settings explained in one line: the numbers on the dial are a relative browning scale, not minutes. Breville's booklet for the BTA720 and BTA730 calls 1 the lightest and 5 the darkest, and suggests starting on 3 the first time you toast. From there, move one step up or down until the color is right. Frozen, Bagel and A Bit More add time or shift heat on top of that setting; KitchenAid's Reheat overrides it; Cancel stops the cycle.

## Toaster numbers meaning: browning level, not minutes

The printed numbers aren't the only stops. Breville's BTA720/730 booklet says its slide has 12 browning settings between 1 and 5, so the half-steps count too.

The dial sets when the toaster switches off, and two mechanisms do that. Older toasters use a bimetallic strip, two metals bonded together that bend at different rates as they heat; the dial sets how far the strip must bend before it releases the toast. [IFLScience](https://www.iflscience.com/what-do-the-numbers-on-your-toaster-really-mean-81324) reports Tom Scott's 2014 video, in which four toasters all set to "two" popped up at different times.

Newer toasters use a capacitor circuit. Per University College London's Daniil Nikitichev, quoted by IFLScience and [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1896471/toaster-numbers-meaning-explained-kitchen-appliance), the dial changes the resistance, and the circuit cuts off once the capacitor reaches a set voltage.

[The Spinoff](https://thespinoff.co.nz/kai/21-04-2019/what-the-numbers-on-your-toaster-really-mean) timed two toasters, one from The Warehouse and a Sunbeam 2-slice. Setting 1 popped at 1:30 and 1:29; setting 7 at 3:55 and 3:14. The steps between settings were uneven, from 10 to 30 seconds. That is two toasters in one 2019 article with no stated method, so read it as an illustration, not a rule.

## What toaster setting for toast? Start in the middle

Start on the middle of the dial. Breville's [BTA720/730 instruction booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) suggests setting 3 the first time, and says raisin toast and light white bread may need a lighter setting while heavy rye may need a darker one. KitchenAid's [toaster support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Other_Countertop_Appliances/Toasters/2_and_4_Slice_Toaster/Operation/Using_the_Toaster_and_Different_Functions) adds that dry bread browns faster than moist bread and needs a lighter setting. It also says to toast only one type and thickness of bread at a time for even results.

Don't carry a number from your old toaster to a new one. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) found the Breville BTA720XL barely toasted on its lowest setting, while the BTA730XL, retested after reader complaints, ran hot, turning medium dark and high to charcoal. ATK also found the Dash's medium too light and said the Magimix runs hot past mid-dial.

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) found the Black+Decker T2569B's dial ran hotter than labeled, and its testers got good control once they adjusted a setting or two lower. ATK says finding ideal doneness takes "some tinkering" on every machine. So: start mid-dial, change one step at a time, and note the number that works for your usual bread.

## Does the second batch come out darker?

The evidence is mixed. [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1896471/toaster-numbers-meaning-explained-kitchen-appliance), citing HowStuffWorks, says heat left over from one batch makes the next one toast faster on bimetallic-strip toasters. Lab consecutive-batch results point the other way for the models tested. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) got 10 identical slices from five medium batches in a row on the Cuisinart CPT-122, and found minimal variation across five batches on the Black+Decker T2569B.

ATK points the other way again on one model: its KitchenAid long-slot KMT3115ER sometimes needed an extra cycle to reach the desired shade, "particularly if the toaster is cold." The residual-heat claim is specifically about strip toasters, and SlashGear says your manual should tell you which type you have. If your second batch comes out darker, drop the dial a step for it.

## What does the frozen setting on a toaster do?

The Frozen (or Defrost) button adds time so the bread thaws before it browns. GE's [owner's manual for the G9TMA2SSPSS and G9TMA4SSPSS](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) gives the order: leave the browning control at your preferred setting, insert the frozen bread, lower the lever, then press Frozen. Breville's booklet says FROZEN activates the additional toasting time frozen bread needs, and that you press it again afterward to deselect it. KitchenAid says Defrost adds time to thaw frozen items before toasting them.

That last step matters. GE's troubleshooting chart, under "Toast is too light/too dark," says to use a higher setting if your toast is too light and to "check that the Frozen button is not selected."

You don't always need the button. TechGearLab says the Black+Decker T2569B lacks a dedicated frozen setting yet handled frozen waffles well on medium-high. The sources disagree on that point: TechGearLab's own spec table lists a Frozen function, and ATK and Reviewed both list a frozen button for the T2569B. TechGearLab also found the A Bit More button on the Breville BTA720XL useful for extra crispness on frozen items.

## What do the Bagel, Crumpet and Fruit Bread buttons do?

These buttons change how the cut side is heated. GE says to put the bagel in with the cut side toward the inside, lower the lever, then press Bagel. KitchenAid says its bagel setting toasts the inside of a cut bagel without burning the outside, and that Bagel and Defrost can be combined.

On the ANZ Breville BTA720/730 the button is Crumpet/Fruit Bread instead. Crumpet adds heat to the middle elements, and Breville says the outer elements may look off or low while it runs; a second press selects Fruit Bread.

For the US BTA720XL, ATK's and TechGearLab's feature lists both name a Bagel button instead; we could not open the US booklet to confirm what it does on that model. Not every bagel mode behaves as labeled: ATK found the Smeg 4-slice's bagel mode heated the outer slots. For which way to load a bagel and when to skip the button, see the [toaster bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Reheat, A Bit More, Lift & Look and Cancel

**Reheat** warms food that is already toasted. KitchenAid says it overrides whatever shade level you have set, is for dry foods only, and that food with butter or other toppings should not go in the toaster. Not every brand's Reheat works that way: ATK says the Dash's reheat button warms cooled toast or adds a bit more browning, and likened the Smeg 4-slice's reheat to the A Bit More setting.

**A Bit More** is Breville's: one press adds a little extra toasting time, before, during or after a cycle, and it works with the Crumpet/Fruit Bread and Frozen functions. Reviewed describes the same button on the BTA820XL.

**Lift & Look** raises the carriage so you can check progress without interrupting the cycle, per Breville.

**Cancel** stops toasting. GE says it ejects the contents, KitchenAid says the toast pops up and the toaster turns off, and Breville says it stops the cycle at any time and raises the carriage.

## When changing the setting won't fix it

GE's troubleshooting chart covers three setting-adjacent problems. Toast too light or too dark: change the setting and check Frozen. Bread pops up almost immediately, untoasted: push the lever all the way down. Smoke: press Cancel, then empty and clean the crumb tray if it is full.

If one side or one slot comes out darker, that is the elements, not the dial; see [why your toaster toasts unevenly](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-toasting-unevenly/). A new toaster's first-use smell is normal: GE and Breville both say to run it empty first, Breville on maximum. More in [why is my toaster smoking](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-smoking/).
