If your toaster won't stay down, check power first. Many modern toasters hold the lever with an electromagnet, so they only latch when plugged in. GE's G9TMA2SSPSS manual says the lever won't lock down unless the toaster is connected to the electricity supply, and Breville's BTA720/730 booklet says its electronic toaster won't operate until plugged in and switched on. Then push the lever fully down, and only then deal with crumbs.

## Why a toaster lever needs power to stay down

In many modern toasters, the lever doesn't lock with a hook. When you press it, a metal tab on the carriage meets an electromagnet, and the magnet holds it there while the elements heat. The [Restarters repair wiki](https://wiki.restarters.net/Toasters) explains that a timer then cuts the magnet's current, and the springs lift the toast. [iFixit's troubleshooting page](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Toaster_Will_Not_Stay_Down) describes the same magnet and lever tab.

That's why a toaster with no power springs straight back up: there's nothing holding the carriage. Restarters notes that older toasters used a mechanical catch instead, so this explanation applies to electronic models. Restarters also calls a lever that won't stay down a common fault. In modern toasters, it says, the likely culprit is the electronic timer, the electromagnet or the tap on the element that supplies the timer's low voltage, all of which sit inside the case.

## Step 1: Check the power, from plug to outlet

1. Push the plug fully into the wall. GE's fix for "the toaster does not work" is to make sure the plug is properly inserted, per its [G9TMA2SSPSS owner's manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf).
2. Plug it straight into a wall outlet. Breville's [BTA720/730 booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) (the Australia and New Zealand edition) and Breville's [US instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BTA720/BTA720_BTA730_USCM_IB_F26_LR.pdf) both say to plug it into its own outlet on a dedicated circuit. It warns that on an overloaded circuit the toaster may not function properly, and it doesn't recommend power strips or extension cords.
3. Make sure the outlet itself has power by plugging in another small appliance.

Philips' support page for this exact problem names only one cause for its toasters: a toaster not connected to household power. Hamilton Beach's 22614-SAU manual says the same in one line: the bread lifter will not latch down unless the toaster is plugged in.

## Step 2: Push the lever all the way down

GE's troubleshooting table covers the problem directly: if the bread pops up almost immediately without toasting, push the toasting lever down all the way. GE Appliances' online support for its 2- and 4-slice toasters repeats that answer. Breville's booklet says to press the lever down until it locks into place.

Some models are known for levers that don't catch the first time. In its [4-slice toaster roundup](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/best-4-slice-toasters) (updated September 19, 2023), Reviewed said the Cuisinart CPT-180P1 it tested had a "flimsy control panel, with levers that often required multiple pushes." If you own that model, see our [Cuisinart CPT-180 review](/reviews/cuisinart-cpt-180/) for the rest of what testers and owners report.

## Step 3: Make sure the bread isn't wedging the carriage

Breville's booklet warns against bread that is too thick or long, because it can jam or wedge the bread carriage. On the BTA720 and BTA730, a jam makes all the buttons and the LED flash, and the elements switch off. That's the toaster reacting to a jam, not a latch fault. The [Breville BTA720XL review](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) covers that model's slot size.

Breville and GE give the same instructions for a jam. Unplug the toaster, let it cool, and ease the bread out without metal utensils. Breville names a knife specifically. If something is stuck, follow [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/) instead of prying.

## Step 4: Clear crumbs with the toaster unplugged

iFixit and Restarters both say crumbs can stop the latch, and iFixit says a crumb lodged between the magnet and the lever tab is enough. Do this unplugged and cold. GE's manual says to unplug the toaster when it isn't in use and before cleaning, and to operate it only with the crumb tray in place.

1. Unplug the toaster and let it cool fully.
2. Slide out the crumb tray, empty it and refit it.
3. Never put anything metal into the slots (GE and Breville). Restarters adds that you should never poke anything into the slots while the toaster is plugged in.

The manuals disagree on the next step. Breville's booklet says to turn the toaster upside down over a bin and shake the crumbs free. Hamilton Beach's 22614-SAU manual also says to turn it upside down and shake to free lodged food. GE's manual has a caution not to hold the appliance upside down and not to shake it.

iFixit and Hunker tell every reader to shake, without mentioning that some makers say not to. Follow your own model's manual. For the full method, see [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/).

## Step 5: If the lever only stays down once the toaster is warm

There's one odd pattern with little evidence behind it. In an [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/224421/Toast+Won't+Stay+Down+Until+Toaster+Warms+Up), an owner of a Waring combination toaster oven and toaster wrote in April 2015 that the lever only held if they held it down for about 10 seconds while the elements warmed up. Cleaning out crumbs and adjusting the browning dial didn't fix it for long. The question was closed without a confirmed fix.

iFixit's wiki doesn't address this warm-only pattern. The nearest cause it lists is a contact carbonized by arcing, which leaves the electromagnet too little current to hold; that's our reading, not iFixit's diagnosis, and it's a repair inside the case. Don't hold, tape or prop the lever down. The one reply in that thread says holding it down risks electric shock, and GE says not to operate an appliance after it malfunctions.

## When to stop: don't open the case, use the warranty

If the steps above don't fix it, stop. GE's manual says a tamper-resistant screw keeps the outer cover from being removed, that there are no user-serviceable parts inside, and that only authorized service personnel should repair it. Breville's booklet says to stop using the toaster if the cord, plug or appliance is damaged, and to call Breville Consumer Service.

iFixit and Hunker describe opening the case to clean springs or file contacts, and Restarters covers repairs to the timer and magnet. We don't recommend any of it. GE's manual says not to remove the cover, Hamilton Beach's 22614-SAU manual says repairs must be done by the manufacturer, its service agent or similarly qualified persons, and Restarters itself warns that live mains may be exposed when the cover is off.

Warranty lengths for the toasters we cover:

- GE G9TMA2SSPSS: 1-year limited warranty, per GE's manual.
- Cuisinart CPT-122 and CPT-180: 3 years, per [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) (updated August 14, 2025).
- Black+Decker T2569B: 2 years, per TechGearLab.
- Breville BTA720XL and BTA820XL: 1 year, per TechGearLab.

If it's out of warranty, a community repair event (Restarters lists groups and events) is one option. Otherwise it may be time to replace it. Our [best toasters](/best/toasters/) hub ranks the options by weight of evidence.

## Is a toaster that won't stay down dangerous?

None of the sources we read rates a lever that won't latch as a hazard on its own, but one iFixit reply says toasters are designed to pop up when they short-circuit, so don't force it. Stop using it if it sparks or smells of burning, if lights flicker when you wiggle the cord (iFixit's sign of a frayed plug wire), or if the cord is damaged. GE says not to operate an appliance with a damaged cord or after it malfunctions, and Breville says to stop using it if the cord, plug or appliance is damaged. See [is it safe to leave a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/), and use the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/) to search CPSC records for your model.
