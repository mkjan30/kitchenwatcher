A Toshiba microwave turning on by itself is a reported problem. Consumer Reports' June 25, 2026 safety alert counts 28 SaferProducts.gov reports from January 2023 to April 2026, 24 of them on the ML2-EC10SA or ML2-EC10SA(BS) air fryer-microwave. Midea, which makes Toshiba microwaves, blames a power button that can stick and trigger the 30-second add-time function. There is no CPSC recall as of November 27, 2026. Unplug it when you're not using it and call Midea at 866-646-4332.

## What to do right now if your Toshiba microwave starts itself

1. **Watch it, empty it, unplug it.** Juan Alberto Arguello, Consumer Reports' test program leader for product safety, says to [monitor it while it runs](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) and never leave it unattended. Store nothing inside it, and unplug it after each use.
2. **If it is running on its own right now, unplug it.** Samsung's support page, written for its own microwaves, says one that turns on by itself is a fire hazard: [unplug it and do not use it](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10007188), then request service.
3. **If it runs with the door open, stop using it.** That is the [FDA's instruction](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) for any microwave. The FDA also says not to run one whose door is bent, warped or doesn't close firmly.
4. **Call Midea at 866-646-4332.** In its statement to Consumer Reports, Midea offered ML2-EC10SA owners an evaluation and free service.
5. **Report it on SaferProducts.gov.** Gabe Knight, a senior policy analyst on CR's safety policy team, says owners should report the incident there as well as to the maker.

> **If there is fire inside:** keep the door closed and turn it off. The U.S. Fire Administration says to [turn it off immediately and never open the door](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/gallery/pictographs/pictograph36.html) until the fire is out; its pictograph also shows the oven being unplugged. Then follow our [microwave fire steps](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/).

Don't open the cabinet to look for the cause. GE says it gives [no do-it-yourself repair advice](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=110) because an internal high-voltage capacitor can hold a charge after the microwave is unplugged.

## Is there a Toshiba microwave recall?

No. This is a safety alert, not a recall, which is how Consumer Reports frames it.

We queried the CPSC's public [Recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Toshiba) on November 27, 2026. A search for "Toshiba" in recall titles returns eight recalls: laptops, batteries, AC adapters, a DVD system and a TV. A search for "Midea" in recall titles and as the manufacturer returns dehumidifiers, window air conditioners and a beverage dispenser. None is a microwave. Product-name searches for ML2-EC10SA, ML2-EC09SA, EM131A5C, EM925A5A and "Toshiba microwave" return nothing.

CR says it is asking the CPSC to investigate and, if needed, secure a recall. We found no public CPSC response. {/* VERIFY: re-run the Recall API queries and check for any CPSC action since 2026-11-27 on publish day. */} To re-check yourself, see [how to check if your microwave is recalled](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## What Midea says, and where the owner reports disagree

Midea told Consumer Reports it knows of "a limited number of units" of the ML2-EC10SA whose power button can stick and start the 30-second add-time function. It said it has found "no evidence that this issue poses a safety risk" and that any effect stays inside the unit. Per CR, Midea did not comment on the reports of melting parts and smoke.

The owner reports CR quotes describe more than added time. They mention smoke, sparks and melting. One owner said a fire inside melted the glass, which later shattered. Another said the unit added time on its own with the metal convection rack inside and was sparking with flames when they reached it.

In six of the ML2-EC10SA reports, per CR, owners say they contacted the maker and were not given a remedy. The maker and the owner reports don't agree on whether this is a hazard, and the reports are user-submitted complaints, not test results. Hunker's [rewrite of the alert](https://www.hunker.com/2211090/consumer-reports-toshiba-microwaves-starting-on-own-safety-hazard/) adds a Reddit owner's account of waking in the night to find an ML2-EC10SA running with nothing inside.

## Why a microwave turns on by itself: the causes sources name

Only one cause has come from the maker: Midea's sticking power button on some ML2-EC10SA units, which triggers the 30-second add-time function.

Samsung's [troubleshooting page](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/troubleshoot/TSG10007188) is about its own microwaves. If the light comes on after the door closes, it says to clean the door and latch and check the latch for damage. A microwave that starts with no interaction should be unplugged and serviced, Samsung says.

In an [iFixit owner thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/388474/Microwave+when+door+is+shut+is+turning+on+by+itself+without+me+asking) about a Kenmore under-cabinet microwave that started on its own, answerers suspected the control board or a stuck relay. That is owner speculation, unconfirmed. A later reply said that in its case, slamming the door had knocked a micro switch arm out of place. The original owner unplugged the unit and replaced it.

None of these is a repair to try yourself. Because of the capacitor charge GE warns about, this is a call-service job.

## Is it heating, or is it just the fan?

GE's support page for its [over-the-range microwaves](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=23937) gives a one-minute test. Put an ice cube, or cool or room-temperature water, in a microwave-safe glass bowl or mug, close the door and let the microwave run on its own for 1 minute. If the ice melts or the water warms, the magnetron is running: GE says to unplug it and contact service. If nothing changes, only the fan is running.

On its over-the-range models, GE calls that normal. Heat from the cooktop or oven below, or from repeated or long microwave use, switches the fan on, and it can run up to 2 hours. GE's page is written for its over-the-range models, not a countertop Toshiba combo. For the Toshiba models in CR's alert, CR's advice is to watch it while it runs and unplug it after use.

## Does this affect the Toshiba EM131A5C?

One of the 28 reports names the EM131A5C, per Consumer Reports. Midea's statement covers only the ML2-EC10SA, so the maker hasn't said whether the sticking-button explanation applies to it.

CR says it has not tested the models in the reports and has not seen the problem in the Toshiba microwaves it has tested. One report doesn't show a pattern, and it doesn't rule one out. The EM131A5C is on our [best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) list; the evidence behind it is in our [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS review](/reviews/toshiba-em131a5c-bs/).
