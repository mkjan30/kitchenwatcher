Don't microwave eggs in the shell, sealed containers or closed jars, paper bags, metal (foil trays, twist ties, gold trim, cartons with metal handles), plastic foam or plastic not labeled microwave-safe. Plain water heated too long, oil or fat on its own, and an empty oven are on the list too. That is what not to put in a microwave according to the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS and Breville BMO650 manuals, the FDA and USDA FSIS. Each item below names its source.

Nobody here tested these items. This list is compiled from two manufacturers' manuals, agency guidance and CPSC recall records, as described in [how we research](/how-we-research/). Where those sources disagree, the page says so.

## What not to put in a microwave: the quick list

The table pulls from Toshiba's ["materials to be avoided" table and warnings](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf) and Breville's BMO650 instruction book. It also draws on the [FDA's microwave oven page](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) (current as of Oct 12, 2023), the [USDA FSIS fact sheet](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402/pdf/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402.pdf) "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven" (June 2011 archive copy; the live FSIS page wouldn't load) and the FAQ on [America's Test Kitchen's best-microwaves review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537).

## Can you microwave eggs?

No, not in the shell. Breville's BMO650 manual says eggs in the shell and whole hard-boiled eggs should not be cooked or heated in the microwave. They may explode during cooking, at the end of cooking, or after you take them out. Toshiba's EM131A5C-BS manual says whole eggs "are able to explode and should not be heated."

Out of the shell, neither manual bans eggs, but Breville says to pierce any food surrounded by a membrane, egg yolks included, several times before cooking, to let steam escape.

For dishes made with eggs, the [USDA FSIS fact sheet](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402/pdf/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402.pdf) says to cook egg dishes and casseroles to 160 °F and allow standing time, because microwaves heat unevenly and leave cold spots. The fact sheet doesn't address eggs in the shell; for that, the manuals are the source.

If you want to reheat a hard-boiled egg, Breville's wording covers it: whole hard-boiled eggs are on the do-not-heat list. Slicing it first isn't addressed by either manual.

## Superheated water: why a plain mug of water can erupt

A cup of plain water can heat past boiling without bubbling, then erupt when you move it or drop in a spoon. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) says superheating "occurs when water is heated by itself in a clean cup." It may not look like it is boiling, and a small disturbance can make it boil over violently.

The FDA's fix is simple: adding instant coffee or sugar before heating "greatly reduces this risk." It also says not to exceed recommended heating times.

Both manuals give the same five steps for water, coffee, tea and other liquids (Toshiba's warning 20 and Breville's "Superheated liquids" section):

1. Don't overheat the liquid.
2. Stir it before heating and again halfway through.
3. Don't use straight-sided containers with narrow necks.
4. After heating, let the container stand in the oven for a short time.
5. Use extreme care when inserting a spoon or other utensil.

The FDA says most microwave-related injuries are heat burns, not radiation injuries: burns from hot containers, overheated foods or exploding liquids. For the radiation question, see [is microwave radiation safe](/guides/is-microwave-radiation-safe/).

## Can you microwave paper bags?

No, not brown paper or grocery bags. Toshiba's "materials to be avoided" table says paper bags may cause a fire. The [USDA FSIS fact sheet](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402/pdf/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402.pdf) says: "Never use thin plastic storage bags, brown paper or plastic grocery bags, newspapers, or aluminum foil in the microwave oven." [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537) also calls recycled paper bags a possible fire hazard.

Bags made for the microwave are a different case. Breville says not to heat food in paper or plastic bags unless the maker says they are suitable. It also says to watch the oven when heating in paper because it may ignite, and it allows popcorn only if commercially packaged for the microwave. Toshiba's list of usable materials includes oven cooking bags, slit to let steam escape and not closed with a metal tie.

Even microwave popcorn bags have failed: [CPSC recall 20-118](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/Fully-Popped-Recalls-Poppin-Cobs-10-Pack-Microwave-Popcorn-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-Sold-Exclusively-at-Uncommon-Good-Stores) covered about 4,900 Poppin' Cobs 10-packs after five reports of the brown paper bags catching fire. No injuries were reported.

What the sources do allow differs. USDA says white microwave-safe paper towels, wax paper, parchment and cooking bags should be safe. Toshiba allows paper plates and towels for short-term warming only, under supervision.

Breville says not to use recycled paper or paper towels when cooking, since they may contain small metal impurities that can spark or start a fire. The manuals disagree, so follow the one for your oven.

## Metal, foil and metallic trim

Toshiba's table says aluminum trays, food cartons with metal handles and metal twist ties can arc, and twist ties could cause a fire. Breville says no metal containers or utensils, and that food-grade foil should not be used. The FDA says "generally" metal pans and foil should not be used, since they reflect microwaves and can damage the oven.

The manuals disagree on small pieces of foil: Toshiba allows small smooth pieces for shielding, at least 1 inch from the walls. The details are in [can you put aluminum foil in the microwave](/guides/can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-the-microwave/), and sparks with no metal inside are covered in [why is my microwave sparking](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/).

Metallic trim counts too. [CPSC recall 16-112](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2016/Illume-Recalls-Valentines-Day-Themed-Ceramic-Mugs) covered about 12,300 Illume ceramic mugs, sold at Target, that were mislabeled as microwave-safe; their metallic print could spark, with three reports.

## Plastic, foam and takeout containers

USDA FSIS says margarine tubs, takeout containers, whipped-topping bowls and other one-time containers should not be used, because they can warp or melt and chemicals may migrate into food. It also says foam trays and plastic wraps used for defrosting are not heat stable. Toshiba says to use only plastic labeled "Microwave Safe," and that plastic foam may melt.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/8073-which-takeout-containers-can-you-microwave) goes further: it gives every takeout material it covers a "No" verdict and says "we don't recommend microwaving any takeout container." It says Styrofoam will melt and metal fasteners on Chinese takeout boxes can spark. The sources disagree on labeled plastic; [is it safe to microwave plastic](/guides/is-it-safe-to-microwave-plastic/) covers the evidence.

## Foods that pop, burst or burn

Foods with a skin or membrane can burst. Breville's manual lists potatoes, chicken liver, egg yolks and oysters, and says to pierce them several times before cooking.

Breville also says not to heat oil or fat, because the oven or the oil may overheat or ignite. For popcorn, it allows only commercially packaged microwave popcorn, with no added oil unless the maker recommends it, and says popping should not exceed 3 minutes. Popcorn accessories have caught fire: [CPSC recall 13-193](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2013/Avon-Recalls-Microwave-Popcorn-Maker) covered about 54,700 Avon microwave popcorn makers after 20 reports of overheating, including two fires that damaged microwaves.

Grapes are a special case. No manual read for this page mentions them, but the sparking is real: a [2019 PNAS study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30782800/) by Khattak, Bianucci and Slepkov explains it as hotspots between grape-sized, water-rich spheres. It explains the effect; it doesn't measure fire risk.

Some competitor lists add more, such as [Homes & Gardens'](https://www.homesandgardens.com/kitchens/things-you-shouldnt-put-in-a-microwave) hot peppers (capsaicin fumes). No primary source read for this page addresses that, so it's left out.

## Glass jars and dishes: how to test if one is microwave-safe

Toshiba's manual says to use glass jars only for warming, always with the lid off, because most are not heat resistant and may break. It also says not to use cracked or chipped dishes, or glassware with metallic trim. America's Test Kitchen says glass jars and bowls, including Mason jars, can overheat and shatter at high temperatures, though some newer Mason jars are labeled microwave-safe.

If you aren't sure a dish is safe, Toshiba's manual gives a test:

1. Fill a microwave-safe container with 1 cup (250 ml) of cold water and put it in the oven along with the dish in question.
2. Heat at full power for 1 minute.
3. If the empty dish is warm, don't use it in the microwave.
4. Don't run the test for more than 1 minute.

## Baby bottles and baby food

Breville's manual says to remove lids and caps from feeding bottles and baby food jars, then stir or shake the contents and check the temperature before serving. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537) lists baby bottles among items to avoid. ## Don't run it empty, and don't store things inside

Toshiba's manual says not to operate the oven empty, and its troubleshooting list names an empty oven as a cause of arcing. Breville's manual says never to use the oven without food or water inside, because running it empty could damage it.

Both manuals say not to use the cavity for storage or leave paper products, utensils or food inside. The FDA says some ovens should not be operated empty, so check your manual. America's Test Kitchen reports its testers shattered a small etched glass bowl after running it empty for several seconds.

## Microwave accessories that have been recalled

Some accessories sold for the microwave belong on the list of what not to put in a microwave. These come only from CPSC records:

- [CPSC 27-050](https://cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Whatever-Works-Recalls-Microwave-Grills-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fire-Hazard) (Oct 29, 2026): about 8,709 Whatever Works microwave grills that can overheat and ignite; three reports of fires, no injuries.
- [CPSC 13-193](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2013/Avon-Recalls-Microwave-Popcorn-Maker) (2013): about 54,700 Avon popcorn makers.
- [CPSC 20-118](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/Fully-Popped-Recalls-Poppin-Cobs-10-Pack-Microwave-Popcorn-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-Sold-Exclusively-at-Uncommon-Good-Stores) (2020): about 4,900 Poppin' Cobs popcorn packs.
- [CPSC 16-112](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2016/Illume-Recalls-Valentines-Day-Themed-Ceramic-Mugs) (2016): about 12,300 Illume mugs.

For recalled ovens, see the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## If something sparks or catches fire

Toshiba's manual says to keep the door closed, turn the oven off, and unplug it or shut off power at the fuse or breaker. Breville says to press STOP/CLEAR if you see sparks, and if there's smoke, switch off, unplug and keep the door closed. The full steps are in [microwave fire: what to do](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/). If the door, hinges or seals are damaged, both manuals say not to use the oven: Breville says to call Breville Consumer Support, and Toshiba says to contact an authorized service facility.
