What not to put in a toaster, according to the manuals: GE, Breville and Hamilton Beach all say oversized foods, metal foil packages and utensils must not be inserted, because they risk fire or electric shock. Breville and Hamilton Beach also ban anything spread with butter or jelly before toasting. Toaster pastries such as Pop-Tarts are where the manufacturers disagree.

This page is about pop-up (slot) toasters only, not toaster ovens. Everything below comes from three owner's manuals, Dualit's safety page, the US Fire Administration and [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters). Where a source says nothing about an item, the table says so rather than guessing.

## Can you put foil in a toaster?

No. GE's manual says: "Oversized foods, metal foil packages, or utensils must not be inserted in a toaster, as they may create a risk of fire or electric shock." Hamilton Beach's booklet uses almost the same sentence, and Breville's booklet says these items "may cause a fire or risk of electric shock."

That is the whole of what the manuals say about foil. They don't explain the physics, so we won't either.

Toaster bags are a related question. No toaster manual we read approves them. Hamilton Beach bans "non-bread items" and "accessory attachments not recommended by the appliance manufacturer," and GE says to use the toaster "only for its intended purpose." We are not recommending them.

## Pop-Tarts in a toaster: fire risk and where manuals disagree

This is where the sources conflict, so check your own manual. Breville's BTA720/730 booklet says the toaster "is for toasting bread, bagels, English muffins and toaster pastries only." For Breville, a toaster pastry is an intended food.

Hamilton Beach says the opposite. Its booklet says "Do not cook, warm, or toast... toaster pastries" and "Toaster pastries should be heated in a toaster oven broiler." Its toasting chart softens this slightly. If you must use the toaster, it says to always use the lowest shade, never heat cracked, broken or warped pastries, and never leave them unattended.

Dualit explains the fire risk. Its safety advice says "Jam filled tarts, or anything with sugar coating should not be toasted," because these ingredients "can reach extremely high temperatures and risk starting a fire." GE's manual doesn't mention toaster pastries at all.

If your manual allows them, Hamilton Beach's precautions are the most specific any of these sources give: lowest shade, no cracked, broken or warped pastries, and never unattended. Breville's booklet also says not to leave the toaster unattended when in use.

## Butter, jam, frosting and cheese: toast first, top after

Breville's booklet warns: never add any food substance to the bread "prior to toasting (such as butter, jelly or marmalade)," because "This may cause a fire as well as lead to electrocution." Hamilton Beach says not to heat any food "spread or iced with margarine, butter, jelly, frosting, syrup, or other spread."

Cheese is the same problem. America's Test Kitchen says slot toasters "should not be used to toast anything wet or melty," naming foods with sauce or cheese such as pizza bagels. Toast the bread or bagel plain, then add the topping. For bagel orientation, see our [bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Oversized, thick or odd-shaped foods and toaster hacks

Breville says bread must be "the correct size for the toaster" and must not "protrude above the toasting slots," or be "too thick or long that it will jam or wedge the bread carriage." Hamilton Beach adds rice cakes, fried foods and non-bread items to its banned list.

Two common hacks run into Breville's warnings. The booklet says "Never operate the toaster on its side," which rules out sideways grilled cheese. It also says not to place toast on top of the slots to warm it, as this "may prevent correct air circulation and damage the appliance."

Frozen bread is an expected use: GE's manual has a Frozen button for it. Hamilton Beach lists frozen waffles on the DEFROST setting, but only frozen foods "made to be placed in a toaster," and its warning box bans "frozen prepared foods."

## Never put metal in a toaster, even to free stuck toast

GE says to unplug, let it cool and "Never use a metal object" to free stuck bread. Breville says a knife "can damage the heating elements as well as lead to electrocution." ATK suggests wooden tongs or bamboo chopsticks instead.

The manuals disagree on turning the toaster over. GE's manual says: "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove the crumbs, bread or other toasting articles." Breville's booklet tells owners to "turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake crumbs free" when cleaning, and Hamilton Beach says to unplug, let it cool, then "Turn toaster upside down and shake" to remove lodged food.

Follow the manual for your model, and unplug it first either way. For the full steps, see [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/).

## What to keep away from the outside of the toaster

GE warns that "A fire may occur if the toaster is covered or touching flammable material, including curtains, draperies or walls." Its manual says not to store or use paper, plastic, pot holders or linens near the toaster opening, and to operate with the crumb tray in place. GE recommends 4 inches of clearance on all sides.

Dualit says toasters shouldn't sit under cupboards or shelves while in use, and should be at least 1.5 metres from the sink where possible. It calls emptying the crumb tray "vital to avoid a risk of fire." See [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) and our wider guide to [preventing kitchen fires](/guides/how-to-prevent-kitchen-fires/).

## If something in the toaster catches fire

Don't reach in. Breville says the way to turn its toaster off is to remove the plug from the outlet. Our [toaster fire guide](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/) covers what to do next, and [which extinguisher to keep in a kitchen](/guides/what-fire-extinguisher-for-kitchen/) covers the equipment. Staying nearby matters: the [US Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/) says unattended equipment (37%) was the leading factor contributing to ignition in nonconfined home cooking fires.
