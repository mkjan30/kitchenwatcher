What size food processor do I need? Sources split: America's Test Kitchen says at least 11 cups is most useful for most home cooks, while CNET says 8 to 12 cups serves most people most of the time. The bigger catch is that the rated bowl size is not what you can process. The Cuisinart 14-cup's liquid max is 3 cups, per ATK.

So the cup number on the box answers only one of three questions. The other two are how much liquid stays in the bowl and how much food the manual lets you chop at once.

## Why food processor size tiers don't match from source to source

There is no standard, and CNET says so outright. Here is how three sources divide the sizes:

| Tier | Breville size blog | CNET size section | CNET FAQ (same page) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 1-4 cups | 3-6 cups | 3-6 cups |
| Medium | 5-9 cups | 7-10 cups | 7-10 cups |
| Large | 10+ cups | 10-13 cups | 11-14 cups |

CNET's own page gives two different large ranges. A 10-cup machine is large to Breville, medium or large to CNET depending on which section you read.

On this page, mini means 3 to 4 cups, mid means 9 to 11 and large means 13 to 16. Treat that as a reading aid, not a fact.

## Liquid capacity vs bowl size: why a 14-cup bowl can hold 3 cups

ATK explains the gap: when the machine runs, centrifugal force makes the liquid level climb, so clear max-fill markings matter to avoid leaks. ATK notes a few models have sealed workbowls for larger volumes of liquid, and it credits a gasket for the 8 cups a now-discontinued Cuisinart 13-cup could process.

The numbers don't follow bowl size. Per ATK, the Cuisinart 14-cup holds 3 cups of liquid, the KitchenAid 13-cup holds 9, the Breville 16-cup holds 5 and the Cuisinart 11-cup holds 2.25. The KitchenAid 13 holds three times the Cuisinart 14's liquid, with a smaller bowl.

If you purée soups, sauces or batters, compare the max-liquid figure, not the bowl cups. If yours leaks below its line, [our leak guide](/guides/food-processor-leaking/) covers the causes.

Hot liquid is a separate limit. Ninja's BN600C guide says do not blend hot liquids, because pressure and steam can burn you. Breville's BFP810 manual says not to process hot or boiling liquids and to cool them to room temperature first. Hamilton Beach cautions that hot liquid poured into the bowl can be ejected by sudden steaming.

The same issue comes up with blenders in [can you put hot liquid in a blender](/guides/can-you-put-hot-liquid-in-a-blender/).

## How much food can you chop at once?

The Hamilton Beach 70730 manual's processing chart is the clearest example. For best results with the chopping blade, it says not to exceed:

- 2 to 3 cups (500-750 ml) of fruit or vegetables in 1-inch cubes
- 0.7 lb (350 g) of meat
- 5 oz (140 g) of Parmesan or Romano
- 8 oz (226 g) of cheddar, Jack or mozzarella
- 10 cups (2.3 L) of crackers, which the chart says can fill the bowl and lid up into the chute

It also says to leave room in the bowl for food to be tossed around. With the discs, it says not to process past the MAX LEVEL on the bowl. For disc technique, see [how to slice vegetables in a food processor](/guides/how-to-slice-vegetables-in-a-food-processor/).

Other manuals point the same way. Ninja's BN600C guide says exceeding maximum capacity is the most typical cause of overload. Breville's manual says not to fill above the maximum fill line for each type of ingredient. Overfilling is also one of the triggers covered in [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).

## 9 cup vs 14 cup food processor: compare by job

A 9-cup and a 14-cup machine can differ by less than the labels suggest. Ninja's BN600C guide treats 9 cups as a hard fill line: do not fill past it. On liquid, ATK lists the 14-cup Cuisinart at 3 cups, while it found a now-discontinued KitchenAid 9-cup (KFP0919) held about 5 cups. Dough is where the bigger bowl showed up in ATK's results: that KitchenAid 9-cup maxed out at 3 cups of flour and couldn't take a double batch, while the 14-cup and 11-cup Cuisinarts handled a double batch with 4 cups of flour.

Weight varies a lot among large machines. ATK lists the Cuisinart 14's base at 12.45 lb and the Breville 16's at 15.25 lb, against 5.5 lb for the KitchenAid 13. CNET gives 18 pounds for the same Cuisinart, so the two sources disagree on that figure.

CNET says heavier-duty models run 15 lb or more and lighter ones, 5 to 10 lb, tend to have suction feet. ATK found lighter models didn't stay put on the counter, and CNET says the Cuisinart's heft doesn't encourage pulling it out of the cabinet.

For a model-by-model look, see [Ninja BN601 vs Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY](/vs/ninja-bn601-vs-cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/), the [Ninja BN601 review](/reviews/ninja-bn601/) and the [Cuisinart DFP-14BCNY review](/reviews/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/).

## How much dough can each size handle?

Dough capacity is a flour weight, and only some manuals give one. Breville's BFP810 manual sets a recommended maximum of 2.0 lb (900 g) of flour in the US and Canada. If the motor slows while kneading, its troubleshooting table says the amount of dough must not exceed the recipe's maximum, so remove half and process two batches (it also lists dough that is too dry as a cause).

ATK used double batches of sticky pizza dough as its capacity and power test. A 10-cup bowl doesn't guarantee dough at all: the Hamilton Beach 70730 manual says not to use it for kneading dough, to maintain peak performance. Technique is in [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/).

## Is a 3- to 4-cup mini chopper enough?

For garlic, herbs, nuts and small dips, a 3- to 4-cup chopper may cover it. CNET's 3.5-cup KitchenAid KFC3516 made a full pint of smooth hummus, but it has no discs and the lowest wattage in CNET's lineup. ATK says a mini bowl add-on is worth buying only if you'll use it often, given the cost, storage and cleanup. The full decision is in [mini chopper vs food processor](/guides/food-chopper-vs-food-processor/).

## Pick your size: a short checklist

If you're still asking what size food processor you need, start from what you make:

- **Soups, sauces, batters:** compare the max-liquid figure, which ATK lists from 2.25 to 9 cups across 11- to 16-cup bowls.
- **Shredding and slicing:** check the disc MAX line and feed tube. Hamilton Beach's manual says not to process past MAX LEVEL, and Breville's chute has a CHUTE FILL MAX mark.
- **Dough:** check the flour maximum (Breville: 2.0 lb) and whether the manual allows kneading at all (Hamilton Beach says not to).
- **Small household or tight storage:** CNET's view is that less is probably more, and 8 to 12 cups serves most people.
- **Big batches:** ATK says processors of at least 11 cups are most useful for most home cooks.
