What size Instant Pot do I need? For one or two people, a 3-quart is enough, per Reviewed and Instant's guidance as relayed by CNET. For bigger households the choice is 6 vs 8 quarts, and the testers split: Wirecutter calls 6 quarts the most useful size, while America's Test Kitchen says 8 quarts suits most cooks. Whatever you buy, Instant's FAQ says the quart number is the full pot volume, and the manuals cap pressure cooking at the PC MAX 2/3 line (the 1/2 line for rice, beans and foods that foam).

We research these cookers from manuals, independent testers and owner reports; we didn't use them ourselves. [How we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

## Why a 6-quart Instant Pot doesn't hold 6 quarts of food

The number on the box is not the amount of food it cooks. Instant's FAQ says it advertises the quart size as the complete volume of the inner pot, and that the volume when cooking will be lower. The fill line, covered next, is what you're really buying.

The [Rio Plus Wide user manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) sets the max fill. Don't fill the inner pot past the line labeled "PC MAX 2/3". For food that expands, such as rice, dried beans or dried vegetables, stop at the 1/2 line. The manual adds that applesauce, cranberries, pearl barley, oatmeal and other cereals, split peas, noodles, macaroni, rhubarb and spaghetti can foam and clog the steam vent, so those also stay under the 1/2 line.

The reason is safety, not taste. The manual says overfilling may clog the steam release pipe and build excess pressure.

A recall from another brand shows what happens when the fill lines are wrong: [CPSC recall 24-013](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=24013) covered Best Buy's Insignia 6- and 8-quart pressure cookers, whose inner pots had incorrect volume markings that could lead to overfilling. Best Buy received 31 reports of contents expelled under pressure, including 17 burn injuries. That was Insignia, not Instant.

Fill lines and minimum liquid have their own guide: [how much water to put in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/). For the wider safety picture, see [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

## 3 quart Instant Pot (and the 4-quart Rio Mini): who it suits

A small pot fits a small household, and the sources that address it agree. Reviewed says that if you typically cook for one or two, a 3- or 6-quart model should suit you fine. CNET relays Instant's recommendation of a 3-quart model for one or two people. Reviewed describes the 4-quart Instant Pot Rio Mini as just right for two to four servings and designed for people cooking for one or two.

Reviewed also says the Rio Mini doesn't have enough capacity to be your primary pot if you cook for a crowd.

There's a catch: the Rio Mini's footprint and weight aren't much smaller than larger models; it's only about a pound lighter than the 6-quart, Reviewed says. A small pot saves less counter space than you might expect.

The small size still needs liquid. Instant's FAQ lists 1 cup of thin, water-based liquid as the minimum for a 3-quart pot. We didn't find a published minimum for the 4-quart Rio Mini in the sources we read.

This isn't a pick for a particular model. To compare current models, see [the best electric pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/).

## 6 quart vs 8 quart Instant Pot

This is where the independent sources disagree, so we'll say so rather than pick a side.

America's Test Kitchen says models with a capacity of 8 quarts are the best size for most home cooks, because they let you cook meals big and small. It calls a 6-quart model a good choice if you want to save money or storage and counter space. Wirecutter takes the other side: it says the 6-quart Rio is the most useful size for home cooks, and that the 8-quart Pro is great if you cook for large groups or regularly make stock or broth.

CNET, in a page last updated in February 2025, relays Instant's guidance that families of six or fewer will likely be happy with a 6-quart and large households should consider an 8-quart. Reviewed draws the line lower: 3 or 6 quarts for one or two people, and likely 8 quarts for larger crowds, though its own Duo Plus write-up says a 6-quart has plenty of space for a family-sized meal.

The clearest measured difference is the floor of the pot. ATK found every multicooker it tested narrower than a Dutch oven, about 7 to 8 inches across instead of 8 to 9, and it preferred broader surfaces because they gave more room to sauté or spread food in a single layer. Its spec listings give the Instant Pot Pro 8-quart an 8.25-inch cooking surface and the Pro 6-quart 7.75 inches. ATK also says the Instant Pot Duo 6-quart's smaller cooking surface meant browning meat in smaller batches.

Going bigger has costs, per Instant's figures:

- More minimum liquid: 2 cups for 8 quarts, versus 1 1/2 cups for 6 quarts (Instant FAQ).
- More food takes longer. Instant's FAQ says coming to pressure can take 10 to 40 minutes or longer, depending on the amount and temperature of the food, and that larger volumes need more time. None of the sources we read timed an 8-quart pot against a 6-quart one with the same load. The one size comparison we found runs the other way: Wirecutter timed the 7.5-quart Rio Wide Plus at 11 minutes 46 seconds to pressurize a pot of beans, faster than the Pro, Rio, Duo and Duo Plus it also timed (14 minutes 24 seconds to 16 minutes 8 seconds).

For one example of each size, see our research on the [Instant Pot Duo Plus 6-quart](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/) and the [Instant Pot Pro 8-quart](/reviews/instant-pot-pro-8qt/).

## Instant Pot size for a family of 4, 5 or 6

No source we read tested how many people each size feeds. The headcounts that exist are guidance, not measurements, and they don't line up. CNET says a 6-quart is ideal for families of three to five, and relays Instant's guidance of six or fewer. Reviewed reserves 6 quarts for one or two people in its size advice while calling a 6-quart family-sized in its Duo Plus write-up.

Treat it as a decision about how you cook, not only how many people you feed. These push toward 8 quarts:

- Cooking for large groups often (Wirecutter, Instant via CNET).
- Making stock or broth regularly (Wirecutter).
- Searing meat in fewer batches, given ATK's wider-surface finding.

These push toward 6 quarts:

- Limited counter or cabinet space (ATK names storage as a reason to go smaller).
- Mostly small batches, which need less minimum liquid in a 6-quart pot (Instant FAQ).
- A smaller budget (ATK).

## Wide 7.5-quart models: a different shape, not just a bigger pot

Instant's Rio Wide and Rio Plus Wide are 7.5-quart pots. Wirecutter describes the Rio Wide Plus as squatter and wider than most electric pressure cookers, with lower walls, similar to a large Dutch oven or slow cooker.

Both manuals list a 2-cup minimum liquid for pressure cooking. The [Rio Plus Wide manual](https://www.qvc.com/footers/cd/pdf/K89432_Manual.pdf) lists model DPPC801GM at 1400 W, 17.86 lb and 15.35 L x 16.34 W x 11.73 H inches (manufacturer spec). The [Rio Wide manual](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0439809/0439809-1-76932d79-d779-4ab7-9028-c556b46ae86c.pdf) lists model DUPC801BK at 1200 W and gives no weight or dimensions.

ATK didn't list a cooking-surface diameter for either wide model. Its general finding still applies: a broader surface lets you sear more food in a single layer. Wirecutter warns that the Rio Wide Plus is really big and some people may not have the counter or storage space for it. At more than 16 inches wide per its manual, measure before you buy.

## Check your space first: footprint and weight

Dimensions are the one number most size guides copy without a source. Of the manuals we read, only the Rio Plus Wide's spec table lists weight and dimensions: 17.86 lb and 15.35 x 16.34 x 11.73 inches. The Rio Wide manual lists wattage only.

For 3-, 6- and 8-quart models, check the specifications table in the manual or on the box before buying. Remember that Reviewed found the 4-quart Rio Mini's footprint isn't much smaller than larger models, so measure the space you have whichever size you choose.

## Before you buy: what changes with size

- **Minimum liquid** rises with size: 1 cup for 3 quarts, 1 1/2 cups for 6, 2 cups for 7.5 and 8, and 2 1/2 cups for 10 (Instant FAQ and manuals).
- **Rice and beans** stay under the 1/2 line in every size. See [how to cook rice in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-pressure-cooker/).
- **First use**: the Rio Plus Wide and Rio Wide manuals suggest a test run before your first meal. See the [Instant Pot water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/).
- **Recalls**: check any used or older cooker in the [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/).

## Which Instant Pot size the evidence points to

The sources agree that a 3- or 4-quart pot suits one or two people and that an 8-quart pot suits large groups and big batches. The 6-quart is where they split: Wirecutter calls it the most useful size, Instant (via CNET) says it covers families of up to six, and ATK still prefers 8 quarts for most cooks. Whatever size you buy, the usable amount is the PC MAX 2/3 line, not the quart number. To compare models, see [the best electric pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/).
