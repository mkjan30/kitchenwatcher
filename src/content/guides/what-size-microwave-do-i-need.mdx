What size microwave do I need? For most people, between about 0.8 and 1.2 cu ft. America's Test Kitchen found 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft "perfectly adequate", and even its smallest turntable, 9⅝ inches, fit a standard dinner plate. Food Network's guide calls 0.8 to 1.2 cu ft sufficient for most people.

Rated cubic feet overstate the space you can use, though, so check three things before you buy: your largest dish, the turntable diameter, and your counter space plus the clearance the manual requires.

This guide covers countertop microwaves only. Over-the-range, built-in and drawer models are sized to a cabinet opening, so their sizing works differently and is outside what I cover here.

## Microwave cubic feet guide: the size bands sources actually give

No independent tester sets a cubic-foot cutoff by household size. Here is what each source says, in its own terms:

| Source | What it says about size |
|---|---|
| [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537) (Oct 2, 2026) | About 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft was perfectly adequate unless you routinely microwave or defrost very large amounts; a 0.7 cu ft oven could be adequate for small households; bigger models hogged counter space without much more usable room |
| [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-microwaves) (Sharon Franke, updated Feb 5, 2026) | 0.8 to 1.2 cu ft is sufficient for most people |
| [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/microwave-ovens/buying-guide/) (updated May 1, 2025) | Groups countertop models as small, midsized and large; gives no cubic-foot cutoffs on its buying guide |
| [LG Canada](https://www.lg.com/ca_en/lg-experience/helpful-hints/microwave-size-and-buying-guide/) (brand guide) | Compact up to 0.9 cu ft, standard 1.1 to 1.5, family 1.8 to 2.1; elsewhere on the same page, 1.1 cu ft and up is family size, and 25 to 32 litres suits a family of four |
| [Top Ten Reviews](https://www.toptenreviews.com/how-to-choose-a-microwave) (July 28, 2024, quoting David Rees, director of the retailer HomeSupply) | 15 to 20 liters is often the standard capacity and best for households of two to four people |

The brand bands don't agree with each other, and LG's page doesn't agree with itself. Its bulleted bands also skip 0.9 to 1.1 cu ft, though its text calls anything over 0.9 cu ft standard. Treat household labels as marketing and use the measurements below.

## Why the cubic-foot rating overstates what fits

Consumer Reports measures usable capacity "because manufacturers often count space you can't use, like the corners around a turntable." Its buying guide puts usable capacity at about 50 percent of the claimed figure across tested microwaves, and about 50 to 60 percent for most countertop models it tested.

Manufacturers say the same in their fine print. Sharp's [SMC2266KS spec sheet](https://static.pcrichard.com/docs/SMC2266KS_Specifications-Sheet_01.pdf) says internal capacity is calculated by measuring maximum width, depth and height, and that actual capacity for holding food is less. IKEA's GATEBO manual carries the same footnote.

[RTINGS's best-microwaves list](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/microwaves) gives a measured chamber volume of 1.06 cu ft for the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS (rated 1.2 cu ft in Toshiba's manual) and 2.08 cu ft for the Sharp SMC2266KS (rated 2.2 cu ft on Sharp's spec sheet).

Those sources disagree on how big the gap is. CR says roughly half; RTINGS's chamber figures sit close to the rating. They measure different things: CR measures usable capacity, RTINGS the chamber volume. I can't tell you which matters more for your dishes, which is why the turntable is the better number to check.

## Microwave turntable size: the number that decides whether your plate fits

Food Network and [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/best-microwaves,review-6316.html) both say it's more important to measure the turntable than to go by cubic feet, because its diameter sets how big a dish the oven can hold while rotating. ATK's tested turntables ran from 9⅝ inches (Galanz, 0.7 cu ft) to 14⅜ inches (Samsung MS14K6000AS, 1.4 cu ft), and even the smallest easily fit a standard dinner plate.

Cubic feet don't predict turntable size. Among ATK's 0.9 cu ft ovens, the Breville Compact Wave has a 10⅝-inch turntable, the Toshiba ML-EM09PA(SS) a 10⅗-inch one and the GE GCST09N1WSS a 13³⁄₁₀-inch one.

To pick the right size microwave for your dishes:

1. Measure your largest plate or dish across its widest point, handles included. Food Network and Tom's Guide both suggest measuring a favorite baking dish from handle end to handle end.
2. For a square or rectangular dish, measure corner to corner, because the dish has to clear the walls as the turntable rotates it.
3. Find the turntable diameter in the manual or spec sheet and compare.
4. If you cook large casseroles, look at ovens with a rectangular tray. CR notes that some models use a tray that slides from side to side instead of a turntable, which might work better for large casseroles or platters.

## Will it fit your counter? Footprint plus the manual's clearance

The outside dimensions are only half the space a microwave needs. Each manual asks for open space around the oven for its vents, and the three I read differ a lot:

- **Toshiba EM131A5C-BS** ([manual](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf)): at least 3.0 inches (7.5 cm) to adjacent walls, one side open, and 12 inches (30 cm) above. Don't remove the legs.
- **Breville BMO650** (instruction book): the installation steps ask for at least 4 inches (10 cm) to adjacent walls and 8 inches (20 cm) above, and say not to remove the legs or place the oven in a cabinet. The safety notes go further, recommending a minimum space of 8 inches (20 cm) all around.
- **IKEA GATEBO** (manual): at least 8 inches (20 cm) to adjacent walls and 11.8 inches (30 cm) above, the back against a wall, 8 inches from the oven front to the counter edge, and a minimum installation height of 36 inches.

Consumer Reports says midsized countertop models are typically a little over 20 inches wide and up to 18 inches deep, with large ones an inch or two larger. It adds that the standard spacing between counter and cabinets is 18 inches, so a countertop model should fit unless your cabinets are very low.

Set that against the manuals before you decide. Toshiba's manual lists the EM131A5C-BS at 12.8 inches tall and asks for 12 inches of space above it. Compare both numbers with the gap under your own cabinets, and follow the manual for your exact model; where it and a general guide differ, the manual governs.

Door swing matters too. ATK noted that the Breville Compact Wave's door sits nearly 1¼ inches above the counter, so it can open over a potholder or other low item.

## 0.9 vs 1.2 cu ft microwave: what actually changes

In the models above, the step from 0.9 to 1.2 cu ft buys a wider turntable and a bigger box. The Breville BMO650 (0.9 cu ft) and BLACK+DECKER EM925AB9 (0.9 cu ft, per Tom's Guide) both have 10.6-inch turntables. The Toshiba EM131A5C-BS (1.2 cu ft) has a 12.4-inch turntable, and its manual lists it at 20.5 x 17.2 x 12.8 inches, against the Breville's 19.1 wide x 16.4 deep x 11.5 tall.

ATK found that bigger ovens didn't offer much more usable room than its 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft picks. If your largest dish fits a 10.6-inch turntable, the smaller oven may be enough. For which of these two to buy, see [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS vs BLACK+DECKER EM925AB9](/vs/toshiba-em131a5c-bs-vs-black-decker-em925ab9/).

Food Network adds that, as a general rule, larger ovens have higher wattage. Wattage is its own question: see [what wattage microwave do I need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).

## When a 1.6 to 2.2 cu ft microwave makes sense

A large oven earns its space if you heat big platters. Sharp's spec sheet pitches the 2.2 cu ft SMC2266KS for larger portions and platters, with a 16.5-inch turntable. [RTINGS's best-countertop list](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/countertop) says its internal capacity rivals that of many over-the-range models. Toshiba lists the ML-EM45P(BS) at 1.6 cu ft with a 13.6-inch turntable, and says it fits a 13-inch pizza.

The trade-off is footprint. Sharp lists the SMC2266KS at 24.4 inches wide, against CR's "a little over 20 inches" for a typical midsized model. The two are compared in [Toshiba ML-EM45P vs Sharp SMC2266KS](/vs/toshiba-ml-em45p-bs-vs-sharp-smc2266ks/).

## Combination microwaves can give up cooking space

[Top Ten Reviews](https://www.toptenreviews.com/how-to-choose-a-microwave) says combination microwaves may sacrifice cooking capacity to fit heating elements and steam functions. The same article then quotes HomeSupply's David Rees saying grill and combination microwaves often have at least 21 to 30 liters. Neither is a measurement, so treat both as weak evidence.

On one model there is a direct comparison: [RTINGS's best-small list](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/small) says the IKEA GATEBO, a microwave and air fryer combo, has a smaller cooking chamber than the Breville Smooth Wave and is best suited to reheating personal portions. Whether a combo is worth it is covered in [is a microwave air fryer combo worth it](/guides/microwave-air-fryer-combo-worth-it/).
