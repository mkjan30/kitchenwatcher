What size rice cooker do I need? For one or two people a 3-cup cooker is usually enough, for three to five people a 5.5-cup, and for six or more or weekly batch cooking a 10-cup. That's our reading of the sources below, not a measured rule, and CNET sizes more cautiously. The cups are 180 ml cups of uncooked rice, per Zojirushi's manuals, and each roughly doubles once cooked.

That doubling comes from [Zojirushi's rice-cooker FAQ](https://store.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers), and it lines up with [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/equipment_reviews/1596-rice-cookers), which puts the 5.5-cup Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 at roughly 2 to 11 cups of cooked rice. Below is what each size actually holds, by rice type, from the manuals.

## 3-cup vs 5.5-cup vs 10-cup rice cooker: capacity at a glance

Uncooked figures are manufacturer specs from each model's manual, in the cup supplied with the cooker. Cooked ranges are credited to whoever published them. Which size fits which household is covered below the table, because the sources don't agree on it.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3-cup | Zojirushi NL-BAC05 | 0.5–3, [NL-BAC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/nl-bac_en.pdf) | 0.5–2 | No porridge menu (steel-cut oatmeal 0.5–1.5) | About 1–6 (ATK) |
| 5.5-cup | Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 | 1 to about 5.5, [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) | 1–4 | 0.5–1.5 | About 2–11 (ATK) |

ATK's ranges are approximate, and ATK says so. Hamilton Beach's cup is 6 oz (177 ml) per its manual, slightly smaller than Zojirushi's 180 ml.

## Is rice cooker size measured in cooked or uncooked rice?

The rated number counts cups of uncooked rice, measured in the cup that comes with the cooker. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC and NL-BAC manuals put that cup at about 180 ml, and its FAQ (Q22) calls it about 6 ounces, or about ¾ of a US measuring cup. ATK found every scoop in its lineup held about ¾ cup. If you measure with a US cup instead, you'll overfill; our [rice cooker cup size](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/) guide covers how to measure.

Not every brand counts the same way. [Consumer Reports](https://news.yahoo.com/best-rice-cookers-consumer-reports-135809338.html) points out that the Instant Zest "8-cup" is really a 4-cup rice cooker, because its number refers to cooked rice while most rice cookers go by dry measure. Hamilton Beach sells the 37548 as a 14-cup cooker, also counted in cooked rice. Check whether the box says uncooked or cooked before you compare.

## What size rice cooker for a family? Sizes by household

Size by how much uncooked rice you cook in one go, not by headcount alone. The only serving counts we found come from Zojirushi's own recipes: the NS-ZCC manual lists 3 cups of rice as 4 to 5 servings in most of its recipes, and the NL-BAC manual's hand-rolled sushi uses 2 cups for 2 to 3 servings. Those are dishes, not plain bowls of rice, so treat them as a rough guide.

A 5.5-cup cooker holds at most about 5.5 × 180 ml, roughly 990 ml, of raw short-grain white rice, which matches the 1.0 L maximum in Zojirushi's NS-ZCC10 specs. Measure what you cook on a typical night and on your biggest night, then pick the size that covers the bigger one.

### 1–2 people: 3-cup

A 3-cup cooker holds up to 3 cups of raw white rice, about 6 cooked by Zojirushi's doubling rule. ATK puts the NL-BAC05 at roughly 1 to 6 cups cooked. If two people want leftovers or cook brown rice, check the brown-rice and porridge limits below before settling on 3 cups.

### 3–5 people: 5.5-cup

By Zojirushi's recipe servings, the 5.5-cup NS-ZCC10's maximum covers a family of three to five with room to spare. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-rice-cooker/) is more conservative, saying a standard cooker of about 5 or 6 cups is "plenty to feed two or three people at once." The sources disagree, and none measured servings, so treat both as rough guides.

### 6+ people or meal prep: 10-cup

A 10-cup model like the NS-ZCC18 holds up to 10 cups of raw white rice, about 20 cooked per ATK. CNET suggests a larger model for bigger groups or heavy weekly meal prep. At the other end, Consumer Reports' 2022 roundup tested only 3- to 4-cup models because its research found that size the most popular.

## Why the headline capacity shrinks for brown rice, porridge and mixed rice

The number on the box is for white rice. Zojirushi's [NS-ZCC manual](https://store.zojirushi.com/cdn/shop/files/ns-zcc_en.pdf) lists the 5.5-cup NS-ZCC10 at 4 cups for brown rice, 4 for mixed rice and just 1.5 for porridge. The 10-cup NS-ZCC18 tops out at 8 cups of brown, 6 mixed and 2.5 porridge.

Smaller models shrink too. The NHS manual caps the 3-cup NHS-06 at 2 cups of brown rice and the 10-cup NHS-18 at 6. The NL-BAC manual allows up to 2 cups of brown rice in the 3-cup NL-BAC05.

If you cook mostly brown rice, porridge or congee, size up one class. A household that would fill a 5.5-cup with white rice gets only 4 cups of brown from it. Method and water for whole grains are in our guide to [cooking brown rice in a rice cooker](/guides/brown-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/).

## Can a big cooker make a small batch? Minimum amounts

Each Zojirushi manual lists a minimum, and it varies by model and rice type (Hamilton Beach's 37548 manual gives a maximum only):

- **NL-BAC05 (3-cup):** 0.5 cup of white or brown rice.
- **NS-ZCC10 (5.5-cup):** 1 cup of white rice.
- **NS-ZCC18 (10-cup):** 1 cup of white rice, but 2 cups for brown, mixed and sweet rice.
- **NHS-18 (10-cup):** 2 cups (360 cc).

ATK says the NL-BAC05 handled as little as 1 cup of cooked rice, the smallest amount of any model in its lineup. ATK also says the 10-cup NS-ZCC "performed as well as" its 5.5-cup winner. But no source we read measured a 1-cup batch in a 10-cup pot, so how good that small batch turns out is unconfirmed. You'll see a "fill it at least one-third" rule on other sizing pages; none of them cites a source, and the manuals don't say it.

Going very small has its own catch. CNET reports that the mini 2- to 3-cup cookers it tried made decent small white batches but all struggled with brown and mixed rice.

## 10 cup rice cooker: who actually needs one

A 10-cup rice cooker makes sense for weekly batch cooking, households of six or more, or regular entertaining. The trade-offs show up in Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual specs:

| Spec (manufacturer spec) | NS-ZCC10 (5.5-cup) | NS-ZCC18 (10-cup) |
|---|---|---|
| Power rating | 680 W | 1000 W |
| Weight | About 6.4 lb | About 8.2 lb |
| Dimensions (W × D × H) | About 10 × 13 × 8.1 in | About 11 × 14.2 × 9.4 in |
| Brown rice maximum | 4 cups | 8 cups |
| Brown rice minimum | 1 cup | 2 cups |

A bigger pot doesn't buy longer keep-warm time. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says "Do not keep rice warm for more than 12 hours," for either size, and [FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/4-steps-to-food-safety) says to keep cooked food at 140 °F or above and refrigerate perishable food within 2 hours; see [how long rice can stay on keep warm](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/) for the details.

## Counter space and the KitchenAid exception

Physical size doesn't always track capacity. ATK calls the KitchenAid KGC3155BM grain and rice cooker "about the size of a mini toaster oven," yet it makes only about 8 cups of cooked rice, against about 11 for the Zojirushi 5.5-cup NS-ZCC10. If counter depth is tight, compare the cooked-rice range, not the box size. The [KitchenAid vs Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 comparison](/vs/kitchenaid-kgc3155bm-vs-zojirushi-ns-zcc10/) goes further.

## How to pick your rice cooker size: a 6-point checklist

- **Uncooked cups per meal:** measure your usual and biggest batch in 180 ml cups.
- **Rice type:** brown rice, mixed rice or porridge lowers the maximum, so size up if you cook those most.
- **Leftovers or meal prep:** plan for your biggest weekly batch, not your average dinner.
- **Small batches:** check the manual's minimum for the rice you cook.
- **Counter depth:** compare dimensions, not just cup counts.
- **The label:** confirm whether the number is uncooked or cooked rice.
