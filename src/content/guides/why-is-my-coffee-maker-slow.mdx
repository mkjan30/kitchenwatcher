Why is my coffee maker so slow? Most often, mineral scale is narrowing the water path. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2461-best-coffee-makers-automatic-drip) says scale "gradually narrows the tubes," and Moccamaster's KBG Select manual warns it can slow and even stop the flow. Before you descale, check that your machine is really slow for its model. Moccamaster's KBG Select manual claims a full carafe in 4 to 6 minutes (manufacturer spec), while Ninja's CE251 owner's guide says about 12 to 15 minutes (manufacturer spec).

## How long should a drip coffee maker take?

The common benchmark is the Specialty Coffee Association's home-brewer standard. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-coffee-maker/) writes that, per the SCA, contact time should be no less than 4 minutes and no more than 8. Moccamaster's own blog gives the same 4 to 8 minutes at full capacity and says anything over 8 disqualifies a brewer. The SCA's program page doesn't publish the number, and its standard PDFs didn't load, so treat the figure as secondhand. For what the badge checks beyond time, see [what SCA certification checks](/guides/what-is-sca-certified-coffee-maker/).

Here is what each source reports for a full pot. These numbers come from different methods, so compare a model with its own row, not across rows.

| Moccamaster (KBG Select manual) | 4 to 6 min | Moccamaster KBG Select manual (manufacturer spec) | Full carafe; a different model from the KBT |
| [OXO Brew 8-Cup](/reviews/oxo-brew-8-cup/) | 4 min 35 s | CNET | CNET's "time to brew 8 cups" |

Even manufacturer and lab figures differ: ATK timed the Moccamaster KBT at 6 min 36 s, above the 4 to 6 minutes the KBG Select manual claims (a different model in the same line). The OXO reads 4:35 at CNET and 6:26 at ATK. CNET timed 8 cups, while ATK reports an average brewing time, so the two aren't the same measurement and shouldn't be averaged. The sources also disagree on the SCA temperature range: ATK cites 194–205°F, while CNET and Moccamaster give 197–205°F.

### Slow vs. slower than it used to be

The useful test is whether your machine got slower, not whether it is slower than someone else's. Ninja's [CE251 owner's guide](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/da/da60176b-126f-490a-b1a4-f1bd1654c887.pdf) has a troubleshooting entry, "Brew cycle is too slow," saying a full carafe takes about 12 to 15 minutes, and that you may need to descale if it takes longer.

So time a full pot and compare it with your model's manual figure or a published lab time. CNET timed the Cuisinart DCC-3200 at 12 minutes and said it was one that "took the longest to brew a full pot" in its group. A slow time on a model like that may be normal for it. For a side-by-side of those two, see [Ninja vs Cuisinart brew speed](/vs/ninja-ce251-vs-cuisinart-dcc-3200/).

## How does mineral buildup slow a coffee maker?

Hard-water minerals "can build up in (and clog) the water tubes," per [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2461-best-coffee-makers-automatic-drip). Moccamaster's KBG Select manual says scale in the boiling element can slow or stop flow and also affect brewing temperature and saturation time. So scale can change how the coffee brews, not just how long it takes.

How fast scale builds depends on your water and your habits. Cuisinart's DCC-3200C instruction booklet (the Canadian edition) says local tap-water mineral content and how often you brew set the rate of calcium buildup, which can affect how the coffeemaker works.

The sources give different descaling intervals:

- America's Test Kitchen: about once every 100 pots, or whenever the machine "seems to be struggling or brewing more slowly than usual."
- Moccamaster KBG Select manual: every 100 cycles or at least every 3 months.
- Cuisinart DCC-3200C booklet: whenever the CLEAN light comes on (see the [Cuisinart CLEAN light](/guides/cuisinart-coffee-maker-clean-light/) guide).
- Ninja CE251 owner's guide: when a full carafe takes longer than 12 to 15 minutes.

Skipping it can cost more than time. The Moccamaster KBG Select manual says its warranty excludes defects caused by lack of descaling.

For the procedure, follow our [step-by-step descaling](/guides/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/) guide. The manuals disagree on vinegar: Ninja and Cuisinart allow it, Moccamaster's manual recommends against it, and ATK says vinegar can be corrosive. Our [vinegar or descaler](/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/) guide covers which to use.

## Coffee maker taking forever to brew but already descaled? Other documented causes

If a descale didn't help, the slowdown may be a blockage or an assembly problem. These causes come from two iFixit owner threads and iFixit's community-written DCC-3200 troubleshooting page. Each thread covers one brand, so treat them as things to check, not failure rates.

### Clogged one-way valve

On iFixit's [Mr. Coffee BVMC-SJX33GT thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/226608/Why+does+it+take+so+long+for+my+coffee+to+brew), the chosen answer (score 9) names mineral buildup first, then a valve clogged by trapped grounds, which it says to remove and wash out. The top answer on iFixit's [Braun KF440 thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/200022/Why+is+my+coffee+maker+is+brewing+slowly) (score 1) says that if cleaning doesn't fix it, the one-way valve can clog with residue, and sometimes has to be replaced if it fails completely.

### Debris in the intake hose

In the same Mr. Coffee thread, one owner (score 7) took the base partway off and found a piece of paper towel stuck in the intake hose where the valve sits; removing it fixed the slow brew. That is a single owner report, not a common failure.

### Dirty drip-stop valve or unseated basket

If the filter basket overflows instead of draining, iFixit's [DCC-3200 troubleshooting page](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DDC-3200_Troubleshooting) says to make sure the drip valve on the bottom of the cone is clean and the basket and carafe are seated properly.

### Wrong assembly or warming-plate position

The same iFixit page says that if water poured into the cone doesn't drip through, the DCC-3200 is probably assembled wrong: specifically, a warming plate that isn't seated properly leaves too large a gap between the plate and the filter cone.

## When can a slow coffee maker not be fixed?

Scale doesn't always come out. America's Test Kitchen says that once the blockage is substantial, the machine "breaks and can't be fixed." Moccamaster's KBG Select manual says a brewer that isn't descaled regularly can be permanently damaged.

If a descale and a check of the basket, drip-stop and warming plate don't restore your model's normal time, the remaining fixes (valve, intake hose) mean opening the base, and the manuals warn against that.

Moccamaster's KBG Select manual says removing the bottom panel voids the warranty and there are no user-serviceable parts inside. Ninja's CE251 owner's guide says servicing must be done by an authorized provider and its warranty excludes tampered units. Cuisinart's DCC-3200C booklet (Canadian edition) says repair should be done by authorized service personnel and its warranty excludes unauthorized repairs. If the machine is under warranty, contact the maker first.
