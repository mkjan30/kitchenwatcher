Microwave sparking usually comes from metal in the oven, spilled food left in the cavity, or running the oven with nothing inside. Those are the three causes in the arcing entry of the [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS instruction booklet](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf)'s troubleshooting table. IKEA's GÅTEBO manual lists the first and last, calls arcing "the microwave term for sparks in the oven," and warns that food residue on the waveguide cover can cause it.

Stop the cycle first: Breville's BMO650 instruction book says to press STOP/CLEAR, and IKEA's says to press Cancel. Then remove the cause and check the cavity before you run it again.

Most causes the manuals list are things you can take out or clean yourself. A few signs, covered below, mean the oven needs a technician instead.

## What to do the moment you see sparks

1. Stop the cycle. Breville's instruction book says to press STOP/CLEAR if you see sparks, arcing or flashing, and IKEA's GÅTEBO manual says to press Cancel and correct the problem.
2. If something inside has caught fire, the Toshiba booklet says to keep the oven door closed, turn the oven off and unplug it, or shut off power at the fuse or circuit breaker panel. For everything after that, follow [what to do in a microwave fire](/guides/microwave-fire-what-to-do/).
3. Unplug the oven before you look inside or clean it. Breville's book says to press STOP/CLEAR and unplug it before cleaning.
4. Don't run it again until you know what caused the microwave sparking. Breville's book says to correct the problem or contact Breville Consumer Support.

## Sparks from metal: foil, trim, twist-ties and racks

[Samsung's support page](https://www.samsung.com/ae/support/home-appliances/what-to-do-if-my-samsung-microwave-is-sparking-during-operation) says sparking is usually caused by conductive materials, and that foil, twist-ties and dishes with gold paint reflect microwaves. IKEA's GÅTEBO manual lists foil touching the oven side, foil not molded to food (its "upturned edges act like antennas"), twist-ties, poultry pins and gold-rimmed dishes.

The Toshiba booklet's table of materials to avoid says aluminum trays and food cartons with metal handles may cause arcing. It adds that metal twist-ties may cause arcing and could cause a fire in the oven. Toshiba also says glassware must have no metallic trim, and to remove wire twist-ties and metal handles before heating.

Racks matter on models that have them. [GE Appliances](https://products.geappliances.com/appliance/gea-support-search-content?contentId=16605) says to make sure a metal rack is firmly seated and not upside down, and that a two-level shelf left in without food on it can arc. [LG](https://www.lg.com/us/support/help-library/arcing-sparking-microwave-oven--1430169363778) says to seat the rack on all four plastic support brackets, because a rack touching the wall will spark.

The manuals disagree on foil. Toshiba allows small, smooth pieces for shielding at least 1 inch from the oven walls, and IKEA says shielding foil must stay at least 1 inch from the walls, ceiling and door. Breville says food-grade foil should not be used in its oven at all. Our [aluminum foil guide](/guides/can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-the-microwave/) covers the full rules.

Metallic decoration really does spark. CPSC recall [15-161](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=15-161) covered about 10,000 Tri-Vista Designs monogram mugs with metallic gold accents; the firm had one report of a mug that began to spark. Recall [16-112](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallNumber=16-112) covered about 12,300 Illume mugs that were mislabeled microwave safe and whose gold metallic print could spark; Target had three reports of mugs sparking.

A "microwave safe" label can be wrong, so check the dish. Toshiba's test: put 1 cup (250 ml) of cold water in a microwave-safe container along with the dish in question and run it at maximum power for 1 minute, no longer. If the empty dish is warm, don't use it.

## Why are there sparks in the microwave with no metal?

If you took out everything metal and still saw flashes, the sources name five other causes.

1. Running it empty. Toshiba and IKEA list operating the oven with nothing inside as a cause of arcing. LG explains that food or water absorbs the energy, and without it the cavity can scorch. Breville says never to run its oven without food or water, and the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens) says some ovens should not be operated empty, so check your manual.
2. Spilled food left in the cavity. Toshiba lists it as a cause and says to clean the cavity with a wet towel. LG says crumbs and spillovers can overcook and spark.
3. Recycled paper towels. Breville and IKEA both say recycled paper can contain small metal pieces, and Breville says they could cause sparking or fire.
4. Vegetables. GE says green beans and carrots can spark from the food itself, more so when uncovered, seasoned with bacon grease or onions, or cooked with little or no water. GE says to cover them or add water, and calls this "not a problem with the microwave."
5. Damage inside the cavity. LG says a damaged cavity interior can cause arcing, and to stop using the oven and schedule repair service. Samsung says to inspect for cracks or exposed wires and stop using it if you find damage.

For an oven that was run empty, LG gives a check: put an 8 oz cup of water inside and run a timed cook for 30 seconds. If no sparking or arcing occurs, LG says it is okay to keep using it. LG publishes no test for the other causes.

## The microwave waveguide cover: what it is and why it sparks

The microwave waveguide cover is a small plate over the opening where microwaves leave the magnetron and enter the cooking chamber, per Engineer Fix. Where it sits depends on the model. Breville's BMO650 book calls it the "microwave guide cover" and places it on the right panel inside the cavity, while IKEA's GÅTEBO manual puts it on the ceiling. Toshiba and IKEA also describe a light brown mica cover that protects the magnetron.

Food residue on this cover is a no-metal cause the manuals name. IKEA's manual says to keep the waveguide cover clean, because food residue can cause arcing or fires. Samsung lists a damaged waveguide cover among its sparking causes. In an [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/233130/Panasonic+Microwave+Sparking+Permanent+Replacement), a poster says a friend's Panasonic started sparking and suspects its mica waveguide cover.

Clean it the way the FDA describes for the oven cavity: water and a mild detergent, with no scouring pads, steel wool or other abrasives. IKEA adds that metal scouring pads can shed pieces that touch electrical parts. Step-by-step cleaning is in our [microwave cleaning guide](/guides/how-to-clean-a-microwave/).

Sources disagree on replacing the cover. Toshiba, IKEA and Breville all say not to remove it, and Breville says it must stay in place for safe operation. Repair content, including that iFixit reply and [Engineer Fix](https://engineerfix.com/why-is-my-microwave-sparking-without-metal/), describes cutting a replacement from sheet mica yourself. We follow the manuals: if the cover is burned, pitted or cracked, stop using the oven and have an authorized service technician replace it.

## When sparking means stop using it and call service

Stop using the oven and contact the manufacturer's service if any of these apply:

- Sparking continues after you remove all metal and clean the cavity and waveguide cover. Samsung says to unplug it and contact service if sparking persists.
- You see burn marks, cracks or other damage inside the cavity (LG, Samsung).
- The door is bent, or the hinges, latches or seals are damaged. The Toshiba booklet and the FDA both say not to operate an oven in that state. Our page on [microwave radiation safety](/guides/is-microwave-radiation-safe/) covers door damage.
- The sparks seem to come from behind a panel rather than from anything in the cavity. Engineer Fix says that points to internal high-voltage parts and a professional technician.

The Toshiba, Breville and IKEA manuals all say only qualified service personnel should adjust or repair the oven, and Samsung says not to repair internal faults yourself. This page gives no advice on opening the cabinet. The cabinet holds a high-voltage capacitor that Engineer Fix says must be discharged before any internal work, and Engineer Fix recommends a professional. Engineer Fix also names a failing magnetron or high-voltage diode as possible causes, which are for a technician to check.

Internal arcing has caused oven recalls. Per the [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=microwave), Whirlpool recall 16-253 covered about 15,200 microwave hood combinations whose internal arcing could ignite a plastic part, and Sunbeam recall 07-562 covered about 300 over-the-range models. Nothing in those records involves the models on our [best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) list. Use our [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/) to look up yours.

## How to stop microwave sparking happening again

- Use microwave-safe dishes with no metallic trim, and run Toshiba's 1-minute water test when unsure.
- Take off twist-ties and metal handles before heating (Toshiba).
- Don't run the oven empty (Toshiba, IKEA, Breville, LG).
- Wipe spills and the waveguide cover after splatters (Toshiba, IKEA, LG).
- Keep racks seated on their supports (GE, LG), and take out a two-level shelf you aren't using with food (GE).
- Skip recycled paper towels (Breville, IKEA, GE).
