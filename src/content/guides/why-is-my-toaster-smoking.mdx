Why is your toaster smoking? If it is new, thin smoke or a distinct smell on the first use is normal, per Breville's instruction book, and GE says to run a new toaster empty on maximum to cure the elements. On an older toaster, GE and Philips point to crumbs or too high a setting: press Cancel, then empty the crumb tray (GE). If it smells odd when switched on, Dualit says turn it off, unplug it and contact them.

This page covers pop-up toasters, not toaster ovens. If you can see flames, go straight to [what to do in a toaster fire](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/). Everything below comes from manufacturer manuals and support pages, each named where it's used; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains why we don't test hands-on.

## Smoking right now? Do this first

1. **Press Cancel.** GE's troubleshooting chart says to push the cancel button to stop the toasting process when smoke comes out, and Breville's booklet says its CANCEL button stops the cycle at any time.
2. **If you see flames,** stop reading here and follow [our toaster fire steps](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).
3. **Unplug it, let it cool, then empty the crumb tray.** GE says to unplug and let the toaster cool before cleaning or removing stuck toast, and its smoke fix is to empty the crumb tray if it is full and clean it if needed.
4. **Don't reach in with a knife or fork.** GE says never to use a metal object to free stuck bread, and Breville says metal utensils such as a knife should not be inserted to remove jammed toast.

## Is it normal for a new toaster to smoke?

Yes, within limits. Breville and Philips call first-use smoke or smell normal, and GE says the first empty run may produce a slight odor.

Breville's [Lift & Look Pro BTA720/BTA730 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) (the Australian and New Zealand edition, not the US BTA720XL manual) suggests toasting on the maximum setting without bread the first time. It says the toaster "may emit a thin smoke or distinct odour on initial use," and that this is normal and not detrimental to performance.

GE's [owner's manual for the G9TMA2SSPSS](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) says to set the browning level to maximum before first use and operate the toaster empty in a well-ventilated room to cure the new elements, which "may produce a slight odor."

Philips support says [dust may have built up on the heating elements](https://www.philips.com.au/c-t/XC000006201/my-philips-toaster-smells-during-first-use) and cause a smell, which it calls normal. Its fix is a few toasting cycles without bread on the highest setting in a properly ventilated room.

The evidence on how long this lasts is thin. No maker gives a number of cycles: Breville and GE describe an empty run on maximum without a count, and Philips says "a few." We won't invent one.

## Why is my older toaster smoking?

### Crumbs in the tray or slots

This is the cause GE's troubleshooting chart names: empty the crumb tray if it is full and clean it if needed. Breville says failing to clean the crumb tray regularly may result in a fire hazard, and [Dualit's Classic toaster safety advice](https://help.dualit.com/en-US/classic-toaster---be-aware-safety-advice-254925) calls emptying it vital to avoid a risk of fire. The full method is in [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/).

### Setting too high for the bread

[Philips support](https://www.philips.com.au/c-t/XC000006032/smoke-comes-out-of-my-philips-toaster) lists a browning setting that is too high as the first cause of smoke. Its fix is to press stop and choose a lower setting. [Toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/) covers what the dial actually controls.

### Loose bits like raisins or seeds

Philips names loose pieces of food, such as raisins or bits from whole-wheat bread, as the second cause. Philips says bits dropping or sticking in the toaster chamber can cause smoke or flame, and to remove loose bits before you put the slices in.

### Spreads, jam and sugary items

Breville says never to add butter, jelly or marmalade before toasting, warning it may cause a fire as well as electrocution. Dualit says jam-filled tarts or anything with a sugar coating should not be toasted, because these ingredients can reach extremely high temperatures and risk starting a fire. The longer list is in [what not to put in a toaster](/guides/what-not-to-put-in-a-toaster/).

### Bread stuck or jammed

If a slice is stuck, GE says to unplug the toaster, let it cool, remove the bread carefully without damaging the elements, and never use a metal object. More on that in [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/).

## When smoke means stop using it

Some smoke has a fix; some means the toaster is done until the maker looks at it.

- **An odd smell or sparking when it's switched on.** Dualit says don't ignore it: turn it off, unplug it and contact them.
- **Any sign of damage.** Dualit says to unplug and not use a toaster with any sign of damage, such as a split or kinked cord, cracks or dents. GE says not to operate a toaster with a damaged cord or plug, or after it malfunctions, and to contact Consumer Support; Breville's booklet says to cease use immediately and call Breville Consumer Service if the cord, plug or toaster is damaged.
- **Smoke that won't stop.** Philips says to contact it if smoke continues after lowering the setting and removing loose bits. No manual we read calls persistent smoke "normal" once the first empty run is done and the crumb tray is empty, so contact the manufacturer rather than trying it again.

Breville also says never to operate the toaster on its side, and not to use it near or below combustible material such as curtains, because bread may burn. To see whether your model has been recalled, use our [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

## Getting crumbs out: the manuals disagree

This is where you need your own model's manual. Breville's ANZ BTA720/BTA730 booklet says to turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake the crumbs free. GE's G9TMA2SSPSS manual says the opposite: do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove crumbs.

Both agree on the basics: unplug it, let it cool, and empty the slide-out crumb tray. Whether you also shake it depends on which manual covers your toaster. Our [toaster cleaning guide](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) walks through both methods.

## How to stop your toaster smoking again

- **Give it room.** GE recommends 4 inches of clearance on all sides (manufacturer spec); Breville's ANZ booklet says 10 cm from the sides and 20 cm above (manufacturer spec). GE and Breville both warn that a fire may occur if the toaster touches curtains or walls, and Dualit recommends not keeping a toaster under cupboards or shelves, and never leaving it plugged in if it is stored there.
- **Keep the crumb tray in.** GE says to operate the toaster with the crumb tray in place, and Breville says not to use it without the tray.
- **Stay with it.** Dualit says toasters sold in Europe and North America are classified as attended appliances and should not be left in operation unsupervised. The [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/) lists unattended equipment as the leading factor, at 37%, in nonconfined home cooking fires; that figure covers all home cooking, not toasters specifically.
- **Unplug when you're done.** GE says to unplug when not in use, and Dualit calls it the golden rule. Our page on [leaving a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/) goes further.
