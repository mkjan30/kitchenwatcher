If your toaster is toasting unevenly, the cause is usually one of four things: a bagel or crumpet mode that heats one side by design, toasting a single slice, bread that is uneven or too big for the slot, or a heating element that no longer glows along its full length. Some models also brown unevenly when nothing is wrong: lab reviewers flagged uneven browning in several current toasters.

So before you blame your toaster, rule out the settings and the bread. If one side or one slot stays cold every time, the toaster has a fault. If it browns unevenly in a patchy, random way, it may simply be how that model is built, and the table further down shows which popular models reviewers flagged.

This page covers uneven and one-sided browning only. If the lever won't stay down, see [toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/); if it smokes, see [why is my toaster smoking](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-smoking/).

## Quick check: is uneven toast normal or a fault?

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do next |
|---|---|---|
| Bagel or crumpet light is on | Mode heats the inside elements more or only (KitchenAid, Breville) | Turn the mode off for regular bread |
| One slice, or two slices in a 4-slot toaster | Normal variation (Philips); two 4-slice models less consistent half-loaded (TechGearLab) | Accept slight variation, or load the toaster fully |
| Same slot or same side pale every time | Element section not heating (iFixit wiki) | Watch the elements glow from above, then contact the maker |
| A section of element wire that stays dark | Break in the nichrome wire (iFixit wiki) | Stop using it; warranty or service, not DIY repair |

## Cause 1: the bagel or crumpet setting is on

Check this before anything else. KitchenAid's support article, [Only Toasts on One Side](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Other_Countertop_Appliances/Toasters/2_and_4_Slice_Toaster/Toasting_Performance/Only_Toasts_on_One_Side_-_Toaster), gives one possible solution. Its bagel setting toasts using only the inside heating elements, so if the outside of the bread isn't toasting, make sure the setting is off.

Breville's [BTA720/BTA730 instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) (the Australian 220-240 V edition) says its Crumpet setting adds heat to the middle heating elements. It also says it is normal for the outside elements to appear off or run at lower power on that setting.

GE's [G9TMA2SSPSS owner's manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf), which also covers the 4-slice G9TMA4SSPSS, says to insert bagels cut side toward the inside of the toaster before pressing Bagel. Not every brand works the same way: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) found the Smeg 4-slice bagel function "worked in reverse," heating the outer slots instead of the inner ones.

For loading direction by brand, see our [toaster bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/). For what every dial number and button does, see [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/).

## Cause 2: one slice, or a half-empty 4-slot toaster

Philips support, in [The browning from my Philips toaster is uneven](https://www.philips.com.au/c-t/XC000016871/the-browning-from-my-philips-toaster-is-uneven) (written for its HD2628/20 toaster), says that when you toast one slice only, browning may vary slightly from one side to the other. It calls this normal and says nothing is wrong with the toaster.

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) reported a related problem in two 4-slice models: the Mueller UltraToast 4-slice and the Black+Decker TR1478BD struggled somewhat with consistency when toasting just two slices, and performed better fully loaded.

The evidence here is thin: one manufacturer support page and one lab observation about two models. No source opened for this page measured how much difference a half-empty toaster makes. If you mostly toast one or two slices, our [2-slice vs 4-slice toaster guide](/guides/2-slice-vs-4-slice-toaster/) covers which design suits you.

## Cause 3: the bread itself

The same Philips page lists three bread causes. Heat absorption varies with the thickness of the slice. A deformed slice may not toast evenly, so don't store loaves stacked on top of each other. A rough cut surface browns unevenly too; Philips says pre-sliced bread from a shop or bakery browns more evenly, or slice your own with a bread knife and keep the surface even.

Bread type matters as well. Breville's booklet says raisin toast and light-textured white breads may need a lighter setting, while heavier rye may need a darker one. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) makes the same point: different types of bread require different settings to toast evenly.

Slot size is the last bread problem. TechGearLab found two regular slices overlapped slightly in the Mueller's long slot when it was used at full 4-slice capacity, and the overlapped parts stayed much paler than the rest.

## Cause 4: crumbs (probably not) and a cold first cycle

No source opened for this page says crumbs cause uneven browning, so don't expect cleaning to fix it. Crumbs matter for safety. GE's troubleshooting chart says that if smoke comes out, push Cancel and empty the crumb tray if it is full. Breville's booklet warns that failing to clean the crumb tray regularly may result in a fire hazard. Our [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) guide covers the steps.

Note the manuals disagree on one cleaning step. Breville's booklet says to turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake crumbs free; GE's manual says not to hold the appliance upside down and not to shake it to remove crumbs. Follow your own model's manual.

A cold toaster may need an extra cycle. America's Test Kitchen says a KitchenAid long-slot model (now discontinued) sometimes needed an extra cycle to reach the desired shade, particularly when the toaster was cold. In the same review, the Zwilling Enfinigy 2 Long Slot browned a bit unevenly and nearly always took retoasting.

## Cause 5: an element section or a whole side not heating

If one side or slot is pale every time with every bread, look at the elements. iFixit's [Toaster Troubleshooting](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Toaster_Troubleshooting) wiki, a community page started by a Cal Poly student team member and edited by nine other contributors, says to run the toaster without bread: if some sections of the nichrome wire glow red and part of the array does not heat, there's a break in the wire somewhere.

An [iFixit Answers thread](https://www.ifixit.com/Answers/View/866523/Why+is+my+toaster+toasting+the+sides+of+bread+unevenly) shows how hard this is to diagnose. The owner of a DCLINA TA1741-UL 2-slice toaster, toasting a single slice in each slot, said one side came out one level lighter than the other, though all four elements seemed to work and the crumb tray was empty. One commenter suggested watching whether both sides of the element reach full glow evenly at startup. Another named the element or a PWM regulator as possible causes.

The owner then reported the center elements heating faster than the outer ones. The first commenter said a 2-slot toaster they had did the same, and wasn't sure there was a fix or whether some toasters are just poorly designed. The thread has no posted answers, only comments.

To check safely, run the toaster empty and look down into the slots from above. Touch nothing and insert nothing. Unplug it and let it cool before you remove anything. GE's manual says never to use a metal object to remove stuck bread. Breville's booklet says metal utensils such as a knife should not be inserted, because this can damage the heating elements as well as lead to electrocution.

## Cause 6: uneven browning is built into some models

Sometimes nothing is broken. America's Test Kitchen calls toasters "notoriously finicky and prone to failure." It counted heating wires and found the best wire-element model had 15 wires on each side of its slots, while lower-rated models had as few as six or eight. It also says quartz heats and cools much more quickly than metal, and that it liked the even, golden toast the quartz models produced.

The three main reviewers don't always agree on the same toaster. On the Breville BTA820XL, Reviewed praises its even toasting, while ATK says it consistently made toast with pale spots. On the Black+Decker T2569B, Reviewed says it "really shines at toasting evenly," but ATK says it didn't toast evenly no matter what settings it tried. Where reviewers split like this, one sample or one bread may explain the gap, so treat any single verdict with caution.

## Fix it, work around it, or replace it?

If the toaster is working but patchy, work around it:

1. Turn off bagel, crumpet and frozen modes for plain bread. GE's troubleshooting chart says to check the Frozen button isn't selected when toast comes out too light or too dark.
2. Match the setting to the bread, per Breville's booklet, and use bread that fits the slot.
3. On a 4-slice model, load it fully if you can; TechGearLab found two 4-slice models more consistent that way.
4. On Breville models, the booklet says one press of A Bit More adds a little extra toasting time, and the Lift & Look lever raises the carriage so you can check progress without interrupting the cycle.

If an element section stays dark, don't repair it yourself. The iFixit wiki suggests twisting the broken nichrome ends together or using a high-temperature crimp, but no manufacturer source opened for this page recommends that. GE's manual says not to remove the outer cover, that there are no user-serviceable parts inside, and that repair should be done only by authorized service personnel. Contact the maker under warranty, or replace the toaster; our [best toasters](/best/toasters/) page ranks current models by weight of evidence.

Uneven browning is not, on its own, a recall sign. None of the toaster recalls in the CPSC's recall database (searched November 6, 2026) names uneven or pale toast as the hazard; they cite fire or shock hazards, such as heating elements that stayed energized or switched on by themselves.
