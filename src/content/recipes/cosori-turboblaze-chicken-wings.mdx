## Why this model

Most basket air fryers stop at 400°F. The TurboBlaze goes to 450°F and has a 6-quart basket (manufacturer spec, per COSORI's product page). The extra heat is the reason to make wings in it. My full test is in the [COSORI TurboBlaze review](/reviews/cosori-turboblaze/), it's ranked on [the best air fryers page](/best/air-fryers/), and it's compared head to head in [COSORI TurboBlaze vs Ninja AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/).

## 450°F vs COSORI's own recipe

COSORI's own dry-rubbed wings recipe cooks at 395°F for 16 minutes with no flip, and isn't written for the 450°F setting. [The Air Fryer Insider](https://theairfryerinsider.com/reviews/cosori-turboblaze-air-fryer-review/), citing Tom's Guide's testing, reports wings blistered at 450°F in 18 minutes with the fat fully rendered. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze) measured the TurboBlaze averaging 395.7°F at a 400°F setting over 20 minutes, so the dial runs close to true.

## Food safety

Chicken is safe at 165°F, per the [USDA FSIS safe minimum internal temperature chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart). Wings are small and bony, so probe the thickest part of the biggest drumette and keep the tip off the bone.

## Tips

- Dry wings blister; wet wings steam. Pat them dry before the rub goes on.
- There is no oil in this rub. Wing skin has enough fat of its own.
- One layer only. Stacked wings stay pale where they touch.
- Watch for smoke at 450°F as the fat renders onto the plate. {/* MEASURE: smoke or error code at 450°F, yes or no and which code */}
