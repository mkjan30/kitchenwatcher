This Breville Bambino Plus review comes down to one point: it is a compact espresso machine that steams milk for you, and the published evidence backs it for beginners making milk drinks. TechGearLab measured an 18.4% extraction yield and a 25-second average shot, and gave it 9.1 of 10 for ease of use, tied for the top mark on its comparison table. [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-espresso-machine/) names it the best compact espresso machine. The trade-offs are a small drip tray, a light, tippy body, a wand that barely adjusts and the need for a capable grinder.

My verdict: buy it if you want lattes and cappuccinos with the milk steamed for you in a small kitchen. Skip it if you want bone-dry foam, a built-in grinder, a pressure gauge or a machine that is easy to get repaired. It sits in our [best espresso machines](/best/espresso-machines/) ranking alongside cheaper and grinder-equipped options.

## What the measurements say about Bambino Plus espresso

TechGearLab's lab pulled 19 g of coffee to 34 g of espresso with a 25-second average brew time. Its refractometer readings put extraction yield at 18.4%, near the bottom of the 18 to 22% range it calls the industry standard. It called the crema "more average," with larger bubbles that faded fast, and noted harsher notes at times. Its espresso sub-score was 6.5 of 10.

Other reviewers timed shorter shots. [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/breville-bambino-plus) timed a one-cup shot at 21 seconds and recorded 53 dB while brewing and 51 dB while frothing. [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/article/breville-bambino-plus-review/)'s editor, an owner since January 2021, reports 21 seconds for a single and 25 for a double. These are separate tests with different doses and settings, not one dataset, so the shot-time gap isn't a contradiction.

[Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/breville-sage-bambino-plus) says the default pre-infusion runs 8 seconds and can be changed by holding the extraction button. It also lists consistent shots as a pro. Breville's troubleshooting table blames shots that run too fast on a grind that is too coarse; our guide to [pressurized vs non-pressurized baskets](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/) explains which basket forgives a weaker grinder.

## Automatic milk texturing: fast microfoam, weak dry foam

Breville's manual lists three milk temperatures (Warm, Ideal, Hot) and three foam levels (Low, Medium, High). The jug rests on a temperature sensor on the drip tray, texturing stops at the selected temperature, and the wand purges itself when lowered. In manual mode, both auto texturing and auto shut-off are disabled. The manual warns to use only the supplied jug.

TechGearLab got latte-ready milk and microfoam in under a minute, scoring milk steaming 7.7 of 10. It struggled with dry foam and saw scalding at the highest temperature setting, so it recommends medium or lower. Taste of Home's editor reports about one minute for 9 oz of milk on the auto sensor.

Tom's Guide says the jug is too wide for small drinks like a flat white, because the MIN line sits too high. TechGearLab says the wand pivots back and forth but not side to side, and Coffee Chronicler says it can't swivel to the sides.

## Daily use: 3-second heat-up, no pressure gauge

Breville claims the ThermoJet heater reaches extraction temperature in 3 seconds; TechRadar and Coffee Chronicler repeat the figure. The manual describes 1 CUP and 2 CUP buttons with preset or programmable volumes, PID temperature control and low-pressure pre-infusion. Hot water comes through the wand when you press 1 CUP and MILK FOAM together.

TechGearLab gave ease of use 9.1 of 10 and timed setup at 9 minutes and a cappuccino at 4 minutes 3 seconds. It found no pressure gauge, which made dialling in slower, and no storage for accessories. Coffee Chronicler notes there is room under the spout for a scale, and that its author used a third-party 54 mm bottomless portafilter.

## Bambino Plus problems, ranked by how many sources report them

1. **Small drip tray.** Four sources report it. TechGearLab says it needs emptying with each use, and TechRadar says it needs a clean after every use because of the auto purge. Coffee Chronicler says almost every day, and Tom's Guide nearly every other day at about four milk drinks a day.
2. **Light, tippy body.** Four sources report it. CNET calls it "light and a little tippy," and Tom's Guide had to steady it when inserting the portafilter, especially with a part-full tank. Coffee Chronicler says you have to hold it in place, and Coffeeness calls it a little too lightweight.
3. **Steam wand limits.** Two sources (TechGearLab, Coffee Chronicler) note the wand can't swing side to side. Weak dry foam and scalding on the hottest setting come from TechGearLab alone.
4. **Loud pump.** CNET says the vibration pump is louder than those in more expensive models. The manual calls a pulsing pump sound normal, and TechRadar's 53 dB is the only reading we found.
5. **Steam error lights.** Breville's troubleshooting table says the froth and temperature lights flashing together mean a steam function error or an empty tank. Each light pattern and its fix is covered in our guide to [Bambino Plus flashing lights](/guides/breville-bambino-plus-flashing-lights/).
6. **Repairability.** One source: Coffee Chronicler says Breville products in general are hard to repair, with parts often sold out, high repair fees, and units usually replaced rather than repaired. Evidence here is thin and not specific to the Bambino Plus, but no source we opened contradicts it.
7. **Forced maintenance.** TechGearLab notes cleaning and descale alerts can't be overridden, a back-flush is due after 200 shots, and descaling took about 8.5 minutes with someone present. For the general method, see [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## How long a Bambino Plus lasts: anecdotes, not failure rates

Coffee Chronicler's author reports about five years of near-daily use, and says the author's mother has run hers for four years with no real problems. Taste of Home's editor used hers nearly every morning from January 2021 until upgrading to an Oracle Jet. These are single-owner reports. I found no published failure-rate data for the Bambino Plus, so they can't tell you how a typical unit holds up.

## What's in the box: sources disagree on baskets

The manual (Korean/English edition) lists single-wall and dual-wall baskets in 1 and 2 cup sizes, plus a 54 mm tamper and the Razor trimming tool. It also lists a milk jug, a steam wand tip cleaning tool and a cleaning disc. Tom's Guide and Taste of Home also list two single-wall and two dual-wall baskets, and TechGearLab and TechRadar list four baskets. Coffee Chronicler, who bought the European Sage version, says US units lack the single-wall basket, so you'd have to buy one. Check the retailer's box listing at purchase.

## Is the Bambino Plus worth it against the under-$500 field?

TechGearLab's list prices put it between the [Casabrews CM5418](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/) ($140) and the [Breville Barista Express](/reviews/breville-barista-express/) ($700), at $500; the price box on this page shows the current tracked price. Tom's Guide and TechRadar quoted a $499.95 list price in 2021, which is out of date. What the extra money buys over the Casabrews is automatic milk texturing. It does not buy a grinder, which TechGearLab flags as a drawback.

The cheaper Breville Bambino (TechGearLab list price $300) shares the compact format but lacks automatic milk steaming, per TechGearLab and Coffee Chronicler. Coffee Chronicler also says the Plus has a larger tank, better tamper and better pitcher. Our [Bambino vs Bambino Plus](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/) page covers the differences in detail.

## Who should buy the Bambino Plus, and who should skip it

**Buy it if you:**
- Drink mostly lattes or cappuccinos and want the milk steamed for you (TechGearLab, Breville manual)
- Have a small kitchen; TechGearLab measured it 7.6 in wide
- Are new to espresso; TechGearLab named it Best for Novices

**Skip it if you:**
- Want bone-dry cappuccino foam (TechGearLab)
- Want a built-in grinder: the [Breville Barista Express](/reviews/breville-barista-express/) has one
- Want to spend less: look at the [Casabrews CM5418](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/) or [De'Longhi Stilosa](/reviews/delonghi-stilosa/)
- Want a pressure gauge or easy repairs (TechGearLab, Coffee Chronicler)
