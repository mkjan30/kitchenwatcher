My Breville Bambino review in one line: it is Breville's cheapest espresso machine, per Tom's Guide, and a good one if you add a grinder. The BES450 manual lists ThermoJet heating that reaches extraction temperature in 3 seconds, PID control, low-pressure pre-infusion and a manual steam wand. TechGearLab named it Best Espresso Machine on a Budget but scored its espresso 6.0 of 10; Tom's Guide gave it Editor's Choice and listed no cons.

The weak spots reviewers name are a grinder you must buy separately to use the single-wall baskets well ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/espresso-machine/breville-bambino), [Home Coffee Expert](https://homecoffeeexpert.com/breville-bambino-review/)) and a light build with many plastic parts. It ranks on our [best espresso machines](/best/espresso-machines/) hub by weight of evidence, not by commission.

## Is the Breville Bambino worth it for you?

The Breville Bambino is worth it if you want lattes or flat whites from a small footprint and will steam milk yourself. It is a poor buy if you want the machine to do the work.

**It suits:**

- Small counters: 6.3 in wide, narrower than the Bambino Plus at 7 in, per Tom's Guide.
- Milk-drink makers happy to learn a manual wand; TechGearLab scored milk steaming 8.6 of 10.
- Buyers who will budget for a burr grinder.

**Skip it if:**

- You want automatic milk texturing. That is the Bambino Plus; our [Bambino vs Bambino Plus comparison](/vs/breville-bambino-vs-breville-bambino-plus/) covers the trade-off.
- You want a grinder built in. Look at the [Breville Barista Express](/reviews/breville-barista-express/), TechGearLab's top scorer at 77.
- Repairability matters most. Tom's Guide says Breville machines are harder to fix than all-metal boiler-fed machines like the Gaggia Classic; see our [Gaggia Classic E24 review](/reviews/gaggia-classic-e24/).

## Espresso: TechGearLab's score against its own words

**TechGearLab's numbers are middling, its text is not.** Espresso quality, weighted 40% of its score, came in at 6.0 of 10, tied with customization as the Bambino's lowest marks. Yet its review says its testers found the espresso almost as good as a cafe's.

It measured an 18% extraction yield with a refractometer, inside the 18 to 22% range it calls ideal, against 18.4% for the Bambino Plus and 19.4% for the Barista Express in the same [roundup](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-espresso-machine). TechGearLab also notes you can't control water temperature.

**Tom's Guide is glowing.** Its reviewer reports an 18 g in, 36 g out shot in 28 seconds and says every shot had a rich, thick crema with no bitterness or sourness. Those taste notes are Tom's Guide's, not ours. Tom's Guide also says the PID is factory-set to 200°F; the BES450 manual says only "Electronic PID temperature control" and gives no figure.

The two scores are hard to compare. TechGearLab rates the Bambino against 10 other machines, including the Barista Express and Rancilio Silvia; Tom's Guide frames its verdict around value for money. Neither publishes shot-to-shot temperature data.

**Pre-infusion is manual, and sources disagree on whether it is customizable.** Breville's manual says to hold the 1 Cup or 2 Cup button for more than a second to run pre-infusion pressure, then release to extract. Tom's Guide and Home Coffee Expert call this customizable or adjustable pre-infusion; TechGearLab says you can't customize it. The manual describes no way to save a pre-infusion time, only shot volumes.

The manual's timing guide: espresso should start flowing after 8 to 12 seconds, and flow in under 7 seconds means an under-extracted shot.

### Grinder and basket choice

The manual says to use the single-wall baskets with freshly ground beans and the dual-wall baskets with pre-ground coffee. Home Coffee Expert's view is blunt: pre-ground coffee and the Bambino are a frustrating combination. If you are unsure which basket fits your setup, see [pressurized vs non-pressurized baskets](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/). For what the 15 bar pump and 9 bar extraction figures mean, see [espresso machine bar pressure explained](/guides/espresso-machine-bar-pressure-explained/).

## Milk steaming on the Bambino: strong wand, slow stop

The manual describes manual milk texturing, with milk at the right temperature, 140-149°F (60-65°C), when the jug is hot to the touch. It also warns that steam can't stop immediately after you press the button and needs a few seconds. TechGearLab put that wind-down at about 10 seconds before you can remove the pitcher without spraying milk, yet still scored milk steaming 8.6 of 10, second only to the Barista Express in its roundup.

Tom's Guide says the switch from espresso to steam takes four seconds and praises the one-hole tip on a ball joint. Home Coffee Expert reports under 15 seconds from brew to steam. Reviewed's expert roundup lists "steam wand not great" among the Bambino's cons, though the clogging complaint in its text sits next to its discussion of the Bambino Plus ([Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/content/breville-bambino-best-espresso-machine-according-to-experts)). The [Bambino Plus](/reviews/breville-bambino-plus/) adds automatic milk texturing.

## Bambino espresso machine problems reviewers and owners report

No source I found publishes a failure rate for the BES450. What exists is reviewer cons and one summary of Amazon reviews, so treat this as a list of patterns, not odds.

1. **Needs a grinder.** TechGearLab and Home Coffee Expert both list it; the manual pairs pre-ground coffee with the dual-wall baskets.
2. **Plastic tamper.** TechGearLab lists a "dinky tamp" as a con and wishes the machine came with a heftier one. Breville's manual lists a 54 mm tamper and calls it a plastic tamp in its cleaning warnings.
3. **Wet, messy pucks.** Home Coffee Expert says there is no 3-way solenoid valve, so removing the portafilter right after a shot gives a wet, messy puck.
4. **Sour or lukewarm first shots.** Home Coffee Expert recommends one or two blank shots to pre-heat. That matches the manual, which says to run a short flow through the group head before inserting the portafilter to stabilize temperature.
5. **Light build.** Home Coffee Expert says it is so light it feels fragile, though it calls the stainless body and group head solid; Reviewed notes many plastic parts. TechGearLab calls it well-built.
6. **Problem units from Amazon.** Reviewed, writing in May 2025, says the Bambino had a 4.2-star Amazon average with an unusually high share of 1-star reviews, about half of them about receiving a used or dirty product. The other half cite insufficient water temperature and pressure, or components breaking early.
7. **Steam wand blockages.** The manual recommends running steam through the wand after milk texturing to reduce blockages, and says a blocked wand stops operating.

Sources also conflict on one point. Home Coffee Expert says the Bambino has no low-water sensor, but the BES450 manual says the LEDs flash 6 times for 3 seconds when no water is detected in the tank.

## Bambino lights, alerts and the manual's fix for each

Every row below comes from the [BES450 manual](https://assets.breville.com/BES450/BES450_USCM_IB_M26_LR.pdf). Bambino Plus owners see different signals; those are on [Bambino Plus flashing lights](/guides/breville-bambino-plus-flashing-lights/).

| What you see or hear | What it means | What the manual says to do |
|---|---|---|
| 2 Cup button flashing continuously | Clean and descale due | Start descaling with 2 Cup and Steam held for 5 s |
| All LEDs flash 6 times for 3 s | No water detected | Fill the tank to MAX and press it down until it sits flush |
| Loud noise, no water comes through | Tank empty | Fill tank |
| Pulsing or pumping sound | Normal pump operation, or tank empty | No action needed; if the tank is empty, fill it |
| Machine on but stops working | Safety thermal cut-out, possibly from pump overheating | 1 Cup and Hot Water for 0.5 s to switch off, unplug, cool 30-60 min |
| You want the factory shot volumes back | Custom volumes saved | From standby, hold 1 Cup and 2 Cup for 10 s to reset factory volumes |

The manual also says the machine enters standby after 10 minutes idle. For the full descale procedure, see [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## Is any part of the Bambino dishwasher safe?

No. The BES450 manual says not to clean any parts or accessories in the dishwasher, and marks the 54 mm portafilter "NOT dishwasher safe." It says to rinse the baskets and portafilter under hot water right after use, and to empty the drip tray after each use or when its indicator rises through the grid. The manual also warns that hot steam may be released during descaling, so make sure the drip tray is inserted first.

Tom's Guide suggests a Breville water tank filter, replaced every three months, while the manual advises against highly filtered, demineralized or distilled water.

## Breville Bambino verdict: worth it with a grinder

Yes, for first-time buyers who drink milk drinks and will buy a grinder. Ease of use at 9.1 of 10 ties for the top mark on TechGearLab's board and milk steaming at 8.6 is second only to the Barista Express, and Breville rates the heat-up at 3 seconds. Against that: a 6.0 espresso score at TechGearLab, a grinder you still have to buy, and a light build. Breville's product page lists a 1-year limited warranty.

[CPSC's recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=breville) showed no Bambino or Breville espresso machine recall when I searched on Oct 4, 2026; its only Breville recall is a December 2015 pressure-cooker recall.
