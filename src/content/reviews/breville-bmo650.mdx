The Breville Compact Wave Soft Close (BMO650) is worth it if you want a small, quiet, easy-to-use microwave and don't mind paying for it. America's Test Kitchen named it its favorite microwave for intuitive controls, the soft-close door and smooth melting. It is a basic 900 W turntable model with no inverter or sensor, at $249.95 at Breville's US store on Nov 27, 2026, with no sale price shown. Owners on Breville's site report latch and interior-paint failures.

The ranked comparison is on the [best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) hub. The evidence here is thin: one independent ranked recommendation, and the two other testers keep their scores behind a paywall.

## Is the Breville soft close microwave worth it? Who it suits and who should skip it

**It suits you if:**

- Your kitchen is small. It holds 0.9 cu ft, and America's Test Kitchen found 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft adequate.
- You want a quiet kitchen. It has a mute button and a soft-close door.
- You mostly melt, soften and reheat. America's Test Kitchen found it melted butter and chocolate smoothly.

**Skip it if:**

- You want sensor cooking or an inverter. It has neither.
- You want more than 900 W of output. See [what wattage microwave you need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).
- You need more than 0.9 cu ft of cavity.
- You want a cheap microwave. The [best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) hub lists a budget pick.

## Breville BMO650 specs, and where the numbers disagree

The manual, America's Test Kitchen, Consumer Reports and CHOICE mostly agree, with four exceptions.

**Weight.** The manual says about 27.5 lb (12.49 kg). Breville's US product page says 29.76 lb (13.5 kg).

**Depth and turntable.** The manual gives 16.4 in deep with the handle, 15.2 in without. [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/microwave-ovens/breville-compact-wave-bmo650sil1buc1/m405094/) lists 16.5 in, and [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2537) lists 16¾ in. The manual and Consumer Reports put the turntable at 10.6 in (270 mm); America's Test Kitchen says 10⅝ in.

**Watts.** The manual rates input at 1,350 W and output at 900 W. Output is the number that matters for cooking times. The Australian BMO650 that [CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/products/home-and-living/kitchen/microwaves/breville-the-compact-wave-soft-close-bmo650) lists is rated 750 to 800 W, so the Australian and US versions are not the same unit on paper. CHOICE also marks it discontinued in Australia.

**Warranty.** Breville's US product page and America's Test Kitchen both list a 1-year limited warranty. The manual gives no length and points to Breville's website. One owner review on Breville's product page says the Best Buy receipt showed two years, but Breville said one year and that rust and paint are not covered. Check the warranty in writing when you buy.

## What America's Test Kitchen found, the only ranked recommendation

America's Test Kitchen's "The Best Microwave Ovens," by Lisa McManus and Valerie Sizhe Li (published 2026-10-02), names the BMO650 its Winner and rates it Highly Recommended. Its tests covered melting and softening butter, melting chocolate, toasting coconut, defrosting ground beef, reheating mac and cheese and cooking bacon. Models were rated on performance, ease of use and cleanup, and ATK focused on models costing about $500 or less.

The testers found the buttons and dial gave distinct feedback, and the large vertical handle was easy to grab. Adjusting time and power was simple. Butter and chocolate melted smoothly, "with less fuss than other models," in America's Test Kitchen's words. The brushed finish showed no fingerprints, and the door's bottom edge has nearly 1¼ in of clearance above the counter, so it opens over a potholder.

Its one listed con is that there's no separate timer. ATK also rated Breville's larger Smooth Wave BMO850 Highly Recommended but still preferred the Compact Wave.

That is the whole independent case. Consumer Reports and CHOICE test the BMO650, but their scores are members-only, so I don't quote them. I found no readable long-term durability data for this model; Consumer Reports' brand reliability scores are members-only.

## The soft-close door: what Breville claims

Breville says the soft-close door is "80% quieter." That is a manufacturer claim. Its footnote says the door measured 81.5 dBA closing, against 99.6 dBA for Breville's older BMO735, in independent lab testing performed on 2018-06-21.

So it is Breville's comparison against its own older model, not a third-party measurement. No independent reviewer I found has published a door-closing decibel reading. Three of the eight owner reviews shown on Breville's product page praise the soft-close door or call the oven quiet.

## Controls and cooking functions, from the instruction book

**Manual cooking.** Set the time with the TIME dial and pick one of 10 power levels, from P100 down to P10 in 10% steps. The +30 s Instant Start button adds 30 seconds at 100% power with each press. In manual mode you can change both power and time mid-cycle; presets allow only time changes, per the manual.

**One-touch presets.** Defrost works by weight (4 to 48 oz, 115 g to 1.36 kg) or by time. Reheat uses lower power. Popcorn has three bag sizes (1.2, 3.2 and 3.5 oz). Beverage heats 1 to 4 cups (8 fl oz each) to about 140 to 150°F (60 to 65°C), per the manual. The Food Menu covers Potatoes (F-1, 1 to 3), Pizza (F-2, 1 to 4 slices) and Frozen Vegetables (F-3, 1 to 4 cups).

**Sound and locks.** The mute button silences the beeps, and the screen shows OFF; otherwise the end of a cycle beeps three times. Holding STOP/CLEAR for 3 seconds sets the child lock. The separate door lock works with a Door Unlock button, and you switch the lock function on or off by pressing and holding door lock, then clock setting, then mute, in that order.

**What it lacks.** There is no sensor and no inverter. The manual says the food menu runs on pre-set times and power levels, and America's Test Kitchen explains that non-inverter ovens reach low power levels by cycling the magnetron on and off. The [inverter guide](/guides/is-an-inverter-microwave-worth-it/) explains when that matters. One owner review on Breville's page notes that above 2 minutes you can't set time in steps finer than 30 seconds.

## Size and wattage in practice: 0.9 cu ft and 900 W

At 0.9 cu ft (25 L), this is a small microwave. America's Test Kitchen found 0.9 to 1.0 cu ft adequate. One owner review on Breville's page says an 11-in dinner plate fits. The manual says dishes should not be wider than the 10.6-in turntable, so the plate reported by that owner is over the manual's guideline. Measure your largest dish; the [microwave size guide](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/) explains how.

At 900 W output, America's Test Kitchen says a model of about 900 to 1,000 W can use full power for the time a recipe or package suggests. It recommends adding time only on lower-wattage ovens, like the 700 W one it tested. The [wattage guide](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/) covers input versus output watts.

## Breville microwave problems owners report: latch, door and paint

Breville's US product page showed 3.9 of 5 from 181 reviews on 2026-11-27. The counts below are from the eight reviews shown on that page, not failure rates.

1. **Soft-close latch and door.** One owner's latch broke at 5 months, and another's soft-close latch broke after about a year. In the second case, the replacement arrived with a misaligned door. Breville replaced units under warranty, and those owners praised its customer service.
2. **Interior paint flaking and rust.** Three of the eight reviews shown report the cavity paint peeling, flaking or rusting: one at about 2 years, one in under 2 years, and one owner who bought in September 2021 and stopped using it about a year ago. One owner says Breville told them the warranty doesn't cover it.
3. **Damaged on arrival.** One owner says the unit arrived with a broken handle.

The manual says not to operate the oven if the door, hinges, latches or seals are damaged. If the door won't close properly, stop using it and contact Breville.

**Recalls:** No CPSC recall was found for any Breville microwave in a check of the CPSC Recall API on 2026-11-27. To check any model yourself, see the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## Care, safety and what the BMO650 manual tells you not to do

- **No metal, no foil.** No metal containers and no recycled paper or paper towels, which may contain metal. The manual says food-grade foil should not be used either. See [can you put aluminum foil in the microwave](/guides/can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-the-microwave/).
- **Food rules.** Pop only commercially bagged popcorn, for no more than 3 minutes. Pierce membranes on foods like potatoes and egg yolks. No eggs in the shell or whole hard-boiled eggs. Don't heat oil or fat, and never run it empty.
- **Sparks or smoke.** For sparks, press STOP/CLEAR; the [sparking guide](/guides/why-is-my-microwave-sparking/) covers the causes. For smoke or fire, switch it off, unplug it and keep the door closed.
- **Leave the cover alone.** Never remove the waveguide cover on the right-hand wall.
- **Cleaning.** Use a damp cloth and mild detergent, with no sprays, harsh cleaners or abrasives on the door. To deodorize, heat a cup of water with the juice of one lemon at 100% for 5 minutes. More in [how to clean a microwave](/guides/how-to-clean-a-microwave/).
- **If it won't start or heat.** Unplug it, wait 10 seconds and plug it back in. Check the fuse or breaker, and check that the door is fully closed. If that fails, the manual says to contact Breville Service and not to repair it yourself. See [microwave not heating](/guides/microwave-not-heating/).
