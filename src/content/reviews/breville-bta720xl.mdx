This Breville Bit More toaster review comes down to one trade-off: two outlets recommend it, but it is slow and not perfectly even. America's Test Kitchen lists the Breville BTA720XL as Recommended among conventional toasters, and TechGearLab names it Best 2-Slice Toaster for Most People at 69/100. ATK timed it at 2 min 32 s to medium, and TechGearLab saw one corner of each slice come out lighter.

So it is a pick for its features and predictable results, not for edge-to-edge evenness. If evenness or speed matters more to you, the [best toasters](/best/toasters/) hub ranks the alternatives by weight of evidence.

## What the Breville Bit More 2-slice does

Everything in this section comes from Breville's US and Canada instruction book for the BTA720XL and BTA730XL ([2012 US manual PDF](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0131/2381/3434/files/BTA720-instruction-manual.pdf), a third-party copy), checked against the current edition on [Breville's own site](https://assets.breville.com/BTA720/BTA720_BTA730_USCM_IB_F26_LR.pdf) (Issue F26), which says the same. It is an electronically controlled 110/120 V toaster with a stainless steel body and these controls:

- **A Bit More.** Breville says one press "adds a little extra toasting time." It works before, during or after a cycle, and with Bagel and Frozen. The manual gives no seconds figure, so treat any exact number you see online as unsourced.
- **Bagel.** Sends extra heat to the middle elements to toast the cut side. Breville says it is normal for the outer elements to look off or low on this setting.
- **Frozen.** Adds the time needed to defrost, then toast.
- **Cancel.** Stops the cycle and raises the carriage.
- **Lift & Look lever** lets you check progress without cancelling. A high-lift position raises the carriage about half an inch for small items like English muffins.
- **12 browning settings** on a slide marked 1 (lightest) to 5 (darkest), with an LED progress indicator. Breville suggests starting at 3.
- **Toast-ready alert** set to High, Low or Mute: hold Cancel for 2 seconds, then pick a level.

One regional note: per Breville's [ANZ booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf), the Australia/New Zealand BTA720 is sold as "the 'Lift & Look' Pro" and runs on 220-240 V. It has a Crumpet/Fruit Bread button where the US model has Bagel. If your manual mentions crumpets, you are reading the ANZ version.

## How fast and how even the Bit More toasts

**Speed.** America's Test Kitchen averaged 2 min 32 s to medium, the slowest of the four toasters it recommends. Its Best Overall, the Magimix Vision, averaged 1 min 25 s, and its Best Conventional pick, the GE 2-slice, averaged 1 min 39 s. TechGearLab lists "average toasting speed" as the Bit More's one con.

**Shade range.** ATK says the Bit More made nice medium and dark toast but "hardly toasted on its lowest setting." If you like very light toast, expect to start higher on the slide.

**Evenness.** TechGearLab noticed that one corner of each slice stayed a bit lighter than the rest throughout its testing. It scored bread toasting 6.0 of 10, while the cheaper Cuisinart CPT-122 and BLACK+DECKER T2569B both scored 8.0.

**Where the sources diverge.** TechGearLab still gave the Bit More its 2-slice award, for predictable results and dedicated bagel and frozen settings. Its lead tester put it as "a consistent job of inconsistently toasting": the pale corner shows up the same way every time, which you can live with or not.

## Bagels, frozen waffles and thick bread

The Bit More does better on bagels and frozen food than on plain bread. TechGearLab scored it 7.0 for bagels and 8.0 for frozen waffles, both above its bread score. TechGearLab also lists its wide slots (1.5 x 5.7 in in its spec table) as able to accommodate artisan breads.

Bagel mode works by heating the middle elements, per Breville's manual, so the cut sides face inward toward them. The outer elements looking dim during a bagel cycle is normal, not a fault. For which way to load a bagel in other toasters, see the [toaster bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Breville Bit More problems testers report

- **Lighter corner.** One corner of each slice stays lighter, per TechGearLab. This is the most specific tester-found flaw.
- **Weak low end and a warm case.** ATK says it hardly toasted on its lowest setting and the exterior "did get a little warm during toasting."
- **Wattage is only in the newer manual.** The 2012 US manual gives no wattage, but Breville's current US instruction book (Issue F26) rates the BTA720 at 120 V, 900 W (manufacturer spec).
- **Warranty.** TechGearLab lists 1 year, shorter than the 3 years it lists for the CPT-122.
- **Durability evidence is thin.** Neither ATK nor TechGearLab reports a long-term failure rate for this model. ATK does warn that toasters in general are "notoriously finicky and prone to failure." I have no verified owner-review data for this page, so there is no failure rate to report.

## Breville's safety and cleaning instructions for the Bit More

Breville's US manual is specific about the crumb tray. It says not to use the toaster "without the slide out crumb tray in place," and that failing to clean the tray regularly "may result in a fire hazard." Metal utensils such as a knife "should not be inserted" to remove jammed food. The manual also says: "To turn the toaster off, remove the power plug from the power outlet," and to unplug it whenever it is not in use.

If bread jams, the manual says all the buttons and the LED indicator flash and the elements turn off. Unplug it and let it cool before carefully easing the bread out. Breville also says not to leave the toaster unattended when in use. More on this in [is it safe to leave a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/) and [toaster fire: what to do](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).

For cleaning, Breville says to unplug the toaster and let it cool completely, empty the crumb tray (on the left side, opposite the lever), then "turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake crumbs free."

GE's [manual for its G9TMA2SSPSS and G9TMA4SSPSS toasters](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) says the opposite: "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove the crumbs." Follow your own model's manual; [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) covers both.

Recall status: a search of the [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=toaster) for toasters on November 6, 2026 returned 24 records and none named Breville. That means no recall was found as of that date, not that one can never happen. See the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/) for how to look up your own model.

## Who the Bit More suits, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you want bagel, frozen, Lift & Look and A Bit More in a stainless 2-slice toaster with wide slots. You also need to live with average speed and a slightly pale corner. Two recommending outlets and a consistent result make it a predictable choice. It was $110 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026, per ATK's price link.

**Skip it** if edge-to-edge evenness or speed matters most. TechGearLab scored the cheaper BLACK+DECKER T2569B and Cuisinart CPT-122 at 8.0 for bread against the Bit More's 6.0, and ATK timed the T2569B at 2 min 5 s. See the [best toasters](/best/toasters/) ranking for how the sources disagree on those. If you're unsure on size, read [2-slice vs 4-slice toasters](/guides/2-slice-vs-4-slice-toaster/).
