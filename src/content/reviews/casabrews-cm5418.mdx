This Casabrews CM5418 review comes down to one answer: it is a budget thermoblock espresso machine ($139.99 at Casabrews on Oct 3, 2026) that most independent reviewers say makes decent espresso for the price. TechGearLab measured a 20.7% extraction yield, inside the 18 to 22% range it calls standard. The catch is an overheat lock after steaming, plus pressurized baskets only. It suits beginners who mostly drink lattes and have little counter space.

It sits as the budget pick on our [best espresso machines](/best/espresso-machines/) ranking. I did not use the machine; this page combines six independent reviews, the manufacturer's page and the CASABREWS manual, and credits each claim.

## How good is the espresso from the Casabrews CM5418?

Good enough for milk drinks; not exciting on its own. That is where most Casabrews espresso machine reviews land, but the details split. In its November 2024 review, TechGearLab measured 20.7% extraction (rounded to 21% in its spec table) and gave espresso quality 7.0 of 10, with an overall score of 69, sixth of the 11 machines it compared.

[TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/coffee-machines/casabrews-cm5418-review)'s reviewer found the shots consistent and well extracted, with noticeable crema, though "a tad boring on its own." [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/coffee-makers/casabrews-cm5418-review), re-reviewing an updated unit in October 2025, got bitter, flat espresso from the stock baskets, even with specialty beans and a Eureka Mignon Specialita grinder. With Casabrews' bottomless portafilter, its reviewer says the espresso tasted much better. [Coffeeness](https://www.coffeeness.de/en/casabrews-espresso-machine-review/) calls the CM5418 a better-than-expected machine for beginners.

Why the split? The baskets. The [CASABREWS manual](https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/58/5813c2fe-09de-4675-9a65-b6fdef428dc3.pdf) says its dual-wall filters regulate the pressure and help extraction "regardless of the grind, dose or freshness." TechRadar says the trade-off is that you won't reach the same peak in flavor, and Tom's Guide blames the stock baskets for its flat shots. Our [pressurized vs non-pressurized basket guide](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/) explains the trade-off.

The 20-bar figure is the pump's rating, a manufacturer spec, not the pressure at the coffee; see [espresso bar pressure explained](/guides/espresso-machine-bar-pressure-explained/). Tom's Guide reports that Casabrews told it the brew temperature was raised by 50°F on revised units and a flush program was added; no other source we read confirms the temperature change. Casabrews' product page now lists an automatic purge after steaming. No source explains how to tell a revised unit from an older one.

## How well does the Casabrews CM5418 steam milk?

Most reviewers say it makes good microfoam slowly, though sources disagree here more than anywhere else. TechRadar praises the wand: it moves freely and makes velvety microfoam, unlike the panarello wands on some rivals. Coffeeness also got silky microfoam. Tom's Guide calls it a typical low-power budget wand that needs care to avoid large bubbles.

TechGearLab blames low steam pressure for slow steaming, scored milk steaming 6.7 of 10, and timed a full cappuccino at 2 min 58 s. [Homes & Gardens](https://www.homesandgardens.com/living/casabrews-espresso-machine-review) found frothing slow and clunky. TechRadar adds that you can't brew and steam at the same time.

The manual says to release steam for 10 seconds before frothing and to start with about 120 ml (4 oz) of cold whole milk. If nothing else happens after 180 seconds of frothing, the pump stops on its own. No milk jug is included, per Tom's Guide and TechRadar.

## Casabrews CM5418 problems owners and reviewers report

### The overheat lock after steaming

After you steam, the 1-cup and 2-cup lights flash together if you try to brew straight away. The manual says the machine is in over-heating protection and that running hot water from the wand for 8 seconds cools it down. Tom's Guide says it happened after every steaming session, not occasionally; TechGearLab says the protection "may prevent" a shot right after steaming, and Coffeeness ran into it too. Step-by-step fixes are in [Casabrews CM5418 not pumping water](/guides/casabrews-cm5418-not-pumping-water/).

### No water, or every light flashing

The manual's troubleshooting table says a machine that can't pump water should be primed with the hot-water function. If all buttons flash at once, it says the flowmeter or temperature sensor (NTC) has failed and you should contact support. The guide above covers both.

### Wobbly baskets

TechRadar says the baskets lock in with a notch rather than a spring clip, so they wobble and clack when you move the portafilter. Coffeeness calls the portafilter a little flimsy and says the baskets kept popping out into its knock box. TechGearLab says the basket's nub doesn't sit quite right, so it can fall out when you knock out the puck.

### A gauge with no numbers

Tom's Guide points out that the pressure gauge has no numbers, so you can't read actual bar. It calls the gauge purely ornamental or inaccurate. Coffeeness is kinder: the dial has a marked "Espresso Zone" it found useful for spotting grind and tamping problems.

### Durability: anecdotes only

Homes & Gardens says low-star Amazon reviews mention loud noise, jolting vibration, faulty steamers, gauges that stop working, and one machine dead after 2 months. CNET warns that a machine this inexpensive may not last more than a few years of consistent use, and lists long-term durability as an open question for a newer brand. These are anecdotes. No source has failure-rate data, so treat any lifespan claim, including these, as thin evidence.

## Casabrews CM5418 cleaning and descaling schedule

The manual's descale warning comes after 500 brew cycles, when all indicator lights flash 5 times, and it repeats at every start-up until you cancel it. Mix 4 parts water to 1 part descaler, or 100 parts water to 3 parts citric acid. Then clear the warning by holding 1-cup, 2-cup and Steam for 3 seconds.

The manual also says no part goes in the dishwasher. TechGearLab scored cleaning 6.0 of 10. Tom's Guide says the updated units add a flush program but ship with no cleaning products, so you buy your own tablets. For the general method, see [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## Is the Casabrews CM5418 worth it?

Yes, if you want lattes and cappuccinos on a budget and don't own a good grinder. TechGearLab measured it inside the extraction range it calls standard, and the pressurized baskets forgive pre-ground coffee. Skip it if you drink straight espresso or make several milk drinks back to back.

It was $139.99 at Casabrews on Oct 3, 2026. TechRadar says colors other than silver cost more. The live price is in the price box on this page.

Two reviewers push back on the value case. Tom's Guide says the cheaper Casabrews 3700 Essential performs almost the same for less money. CNET's reviewer compared it side by side with the pricier Breville Bambino and found the Bambino more proficient, though the Casabrews impressed for its price.

**Buy it if:**
- You mostly drink milk drinks: TechRadar rates the wand's microfoam highly.
- You're a beginner using pre-ground coffee: TechRadar, Coffeeness and Casabrews all pitch it that way.
- Counter space is tight: 5.47 in wide, per the manufacturer spec.

**Skip it if:**
- You drink espresso straight: Tom's Guide found the stock baskets bitter and flat.
- You make drinks for several people: TechGearLab says it isn't built for back-to-back milk drinks, and Homes & Gardens agrees.

**Budget for extras:** a grinder if you buy beans whole, a milk jug (Casabrews' own was $15.00 at Casabrews on Oct 3, 2026), descaler ($12.99 at Casabrews on Oct 3, 2026), and optionally the bottomless portafilter Tom's Guide used ($45.00 at Casabrews on Oct 3, 2026).
