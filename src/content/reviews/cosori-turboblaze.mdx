The Cosori TurboBlaze (CAF-DC601) is a good 6-quart air fryer for two to four people, and the evidence for that is mostly one strong lab result. TechGearLab measured it averaging 395.7°F at a 400°F setting and ranked it second of ten models. The two weak points are a crisper plate with a big center hole and a TechRadar review that found results inconsistent. It was $89.99 at COSORI.com on Sep 27, 2026.

How this page was put together is on [how we research](/how-we-research/): every number below is credited to whoever measured or published it.

The two sources disagree on the low end of the temperature range: COSORI and its launch release say 90°F, which matters for proofing and dehydrating, while TechGearLab's spec box starts at 300°F. That may just be TechGearLab listing the air-fry range; I'd go with the manufacturer here, since the Proof and Dehydrate presets only make sense below 300°F.

COSORI's launch release claims it cooks 1 lb of fresh fries up to 46% faster than its own older CP168 model ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cosori-unveils-the-next-generation-6-quart-turboblaze-air-fryer-301961219.html)). That's the maker comparing against itself, so I don't lean on it.

## What independent tests found

TechGearLab ran a 20-minute temperature test at a 400°F setting and recorded an average of 395.7°F, 4.3°F under the set point ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze)). Its cooking results were good across the board: fries with a crunchy outside and fluffy inside, salmon with a juicy center and crisp skin, and wings with good browning, though they came out only slightly crispy. It scored cooking quality 8.5 and speed 8.4 out of 10, with cleaning (7.0) and temperature accuracy (7.0) the lowest sub-scores.

On browning, TechGearLab's wording is mixed: browning on fries wasn't perfectly even piece to piece, but it called the batch remarkably uniform overall. TechRadar's headline goes further and calls the TurboBlaze's results inconsistent ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/home/small-appliances/cosori-turboblaze-6l-air-fryer-review-a-convenient-multifunctional-air-fryer-with-inconsistent-results)); the full review text did not load, so I can't tell you which foods it meant. Home Appliance Owl quotes an evaluation calling it consistently inconsistent on fries and vegetables ([Home Appliance Owl](https://www.homeapplianceowl.com/air-fryers/cosori-turboblaze-review/)). So the one lab with published numbers liked it, and at least one other reviewer did not. That's a real disagreement, and it's the main reason the verdict is hedged.

Real Homes' reviewer called it one of the best air fryers they'd tried, with the needed preheat and the lack of a shake reminder as the only complaints; it publishes no measurements ([Real Homes](https://www.realhomes.com/reviews/cosori-turboblaze-air-fryer-review)).

## Noise

The DC fan is the TurboBlaze's selling point after capacity. TechGearLab calls its quiet operation a bonus that sets it apart, but publishes no decibel figure. COSORI's product page just calls it quiet, and neither the page, its launch release nor the US user manual gives a noise number. I found no decibel reading with a stated method, so I can't tell you how much quieter it is. For how it lines up against the Ninja, see [TurboBlaze vs Ninja AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/).

## Known problems and owner reports

- **The crisper plate hole.** TechGearLab calls the large hole in the center of the crisping tray the one notable design flaw, because small foods fall through ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze)). Home Appliance Owl flags the same thing ([Home Appliance Owl](https://www.homeapplianceowl.com/air-fryers/cosori-turboblaze-review/)). If you cook a lot of chopped vegetables or small frozen items, this is the flaw you'll notice most.
- **Drawer fit.** Home Appliance Owl quotes one verified Amazon buyer who found the drawer flimsy and hard to slide fully into place. That's a single report, not a pattern I can confirm.
- **Wall clearance.** The rear cable management means it can't sit flush against a wall; Home Appliance Owl says to allow about 5 in.
- **Hot exterior.** TechGearLab measured 140°F on the outside and notes that just meets the burn threshold. Keep it away from the counter edge if you have kids.
- **Error codes.** Owners mention E1, E2 and E3 codes, but I couldn't open the TurboBlaze manual to confirm what each means, so I'm not guessing here. If you see one, COSORI support is the right call.

## Recalls

The TurboBlaze is not part of COSORI's February 23, 2023 recall of about 2 million air fryers, which covered a wire connection that could overheat. The CPSC notice lists 21 model numbers, all in the CP158, CP137, CS158, CO137, CO158, CP258 and CAF-P581 families; the CAF-DC601 isn't among them, and it launched later, in October 2023 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Two-Million-COSORI-Air-Fryers-Recalled-by-Atekcity-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards)). I found no recall naming the TurboBlaze as of Sep 27, 2026.

## Cleaning

COSORI says the basket and crisper plate are PFAS-free ceramic nonstick and dishwasher safe. TechGearLab scored cleaning 7.0 of 10, its second-lowest sub-score, without singling out a cause.

## Price

$89.99 at COSORI.com on Sep 27, 2026, marked down 25% from $119.99, which is also the launch MSRP in COSORI's release. COSORI lists a two-year limited warranty, longer than the one year Ninja gives the AF141.

## Who should buy it, who should skip it

**Buy it** if you cook for two to four, want a 6-quart square basket, and care about heat accuracy and a quieter fan. At around $90 it undercuts the 5-quart Ninja AF141's $129.99 list price while offering more room and a 450°F top setting.

**Skip it** if most of what you cook is small, like diced vegetables or popcorn chicken bits, because of the crisper-plate hole; if you need it pushed tight against a backsplash; or if the mixed reports on evenness worry you more than one good lab result reassures you.
