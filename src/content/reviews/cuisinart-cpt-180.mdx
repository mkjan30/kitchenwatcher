The Cuisinart CPT-180 is a 4-slice toaster with two independently controlled sides, and the four outlets that tested it disagree on whether it toasts evenly. Popular Science names it Best 4-slice in its guide updated March 2024, while TechGearLab scored it 58 of 100, 15th of 16, mainly for uneven toast. Reviewed (2023) found one or two slots much darker than the rest and levers that needed several presses. It suits households that want two independent sides more than perfectly even toast.

I did not test this toaster. This Cuisinart CPT-180 review brings together the independent tests, Cuisinart's own manual and attributed owner reports, each linked or named below. For how it ranks against the other models we cover, see [the best toasters](/best/toasters/).

## What the CPT-180 is: specs from Cuisinart's manual

Everything in this section comes from Cuisinart's [US CPT-180 Series instruction booklet](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/65/6531aaae-4ca9-4ef2-8b24-b362a7e0ffe1.pdf) (2010, hosted by Home Depot) and the [CPT-180C booklet](https://cuisinart.ca/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-master-ca/en_CA/v1728637408324/Instruction%20Booklet/CPT-180C_en.pdf), the Canadian edition from 2013. The two agree on every spec below except the 120 V figure, which only the Canadian edition prints.

- **Dual control panels** (manufacturer spec): the lever and controls on the left run slots 1 and 2; the right ones run slots 3 and 4.
- **Slots** (manufacturer spec): 1½ inches (3.75 cm) wide, with an extra-lift lever for small items.
- **Browning dial** (manufacturer spec): six levels, where 1-2 is light, 3-4 medium and 5-6 dark.
- **Buttons** (manufacturer spec): Bagel, Defrost, Reheat and Cancel, each with an LED except Cancel.
- **Other** (manufacturer spec): slide-out crumb tray, cord storage, BPA-free food-contact parts, 120 V, limited three-year warranty.

The manual does not state a wattage, and I have not found one in a Cuisinart document. Other figures differ by source. TechGearLab lists 10.4 x 11 x 7 inches and 5.1 lb, while Popular Science lists 10.6 x 11.15 x 7.5 inches and 6.06 lb. TopTenReviews gives the slots as 1.4 inches, against 1.5 inches in the manual and in TechGearLab's specs.

## Does the CPT-180 toast evenly? Where the testers disagree

**TechGearLab (Lesley Robinson, February 2024)** gave bread 5.0 of 10. It found results inconsistent across the four slices, which it says tasted fairly raw in the center ([TechGearLab review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/toaster/cuisinart-4-slice-classic-cpt-180)). Bagels scored 6.0: one round came out darker on the bottom half, and in another the top inch did not toast well. Frozen waffles scored 6.0, browner toward the center than the edges.

**Reviewed (updated September 2023)** tested the CPT-180P1 variant and listed it under other 4-slice toasters, with no award. It says one or two slots often turned out slices much darker than the rest. It also says this toaster gave the fastest results of the group, although the same page calls the Cuisinart CPT-540 the quickest toaster in the group, so its speed ranking is unclear.

**Popular Science (updated March 2024)** named it Best 4-slice. Its tester made four slices of toast and two split English muffins and reported them well toasted edge to edge ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/reviews/best-toaster/)). It also reported no loss in performance with the two sides set differently: toast in the left slots came out as well done as with every slot on maximum, even with the right side set lower. Its only complaint was the lack of feedback.

**TopTenReviews (Aaron McCarter, May 2020)** called the toasting quick and even despite a few design drawbacks, and timed bread on a medium setting at 1 minute 27 seconds ([TopTenReviews](https://toptenreviews.com/toasters-cuisinart-toaster-review)).

The evidence conflicts. Two outlets reported uneven toasting and two did not. TechGearLab's is the only one of the four with published scores from a 16-model test, so I treat uneven toasting as a real, documented risk rather than a fluke.

## Why one side or slot comes out darker

The first cause is the design. Each side is its own toaster with its own dial, so two different settings give two different shades. Cuisinart's manual adds four points:

1. **Two slices:** use one set of slots only, 1 and 2 or 3 and 4 (Canadian edition only; the 2010 US booklet leaves this out).
2. **One slice:** set the dial lighter than usual (the US booklet adds: use the slot marked with the single-slice icon), because the toaster is designed to heat the whole chamber for two slices.
3. **Different breads:** moisture differences between breads change toasting time. The manual says to use a lower setting for slightly dry bread and a higher one for very fresh or whole wheat bread; thick slices take longer, and very thick pieces may need two cycles.
4. **Uneven slices:** the manual's tips say uneven toasting is usually due to bread slices of uneven thickness.

None of that explains slot-to-slot differences with evenly cut bread, which is what TechGearLab and Reviewed reported. For brand-neutral fixes, see [why a toaster toasts unevenly](/guides/why-is-my-toaster-toasting-unevenly/).

## Levers, controls and build quality

Reviewed found the levers on its CPT-180P1 often needed several presses before they stayed down and started toasting. It called the control panel flimsy and its first impression was of cheap build quality. TechGearLab disagreed on usability, calling the CPT-180 one of the easier-to-use models it tried, with an ease-of-use score of 7.4. Popular Science noted that its temperature knobs offer very little resistance, though it found the right setting easy to dial in after about a minute.

Cuisinart's manual says to press the carriage lever until it locks into the down position. If your lever pops straight back up, see [why a toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).

## Bagels, frozen foods and the two-sided controls

Per the manual, Bagel adds time to the cycle for thicker breads, and Reheat warms bread without browning it. Defrost first defrosts, then toasts. The manual gives dial ranges for it: 1-2 for refrigerated bread and thinner frozen items such as pancakes, 3-4 for toaster pastries, frozen waffles, thin French toast and refrigerated bagels, 5-6 for thicker frozen items such as bagels, hand-cut bread and thick French toast.

TechGearLab's bagel and waffle scores (both 6.0) show its uneven results carried over to bagels and frozen waffles. The independent sides are the main reason to buy it, and Popular Science reported no loss in performance with the two sides on different settings. More on the buttons in [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/) and [what the bagel setting does](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Cleaning and safety, per Cuisinart's manual

Unplug the toaster and let it cool completely before cleaning, and never put any part of it in water. Slide out the crumb tray, empty it, wipe it and put it back: the manual says never to run the toaster without it. TechGearLab and TopTenReviews both note the trays come out at the back, which is less convenient.

To remove stuck bread, the Cuisinart manual says to turn the toaster upside down and gently shake, and never to insert hard or sharp instruments. GE's manual for its own 2- and 4-slice toasters ([GE 49-9000001](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf)) says the opposite: do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it to remove crumbs or bread. Follow your own model's manual; for the CPT-180, that is Cuisinart's. More in [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/) and [how to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/).

The manual's other safeguards: unplug when not in use, never leave it unattended during use, never try to dislodge food while it is plugged in, and keep oversize foods, metal foil packages and utensils out. Do not put in buttered breads or pastries with fillings or frostings, which it calls a fire hazard. Do not run it under wall cabinets. The Canadian edition also rules out an appliance garage, warning that contact with the wall or door could turn it on. See [whether to leave a toaster plugged in](/guides/is-it-safe-to-leave-a-toaster-plugged-in/).

CPSC's [recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=toaster), searched for toasters on November 6, 2026, returned 24 records and none mention Cuisinart or Conair. That means no recall on record as of that date, not proof of a clean history. To check any model, see [the toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

## Who should buy the CPT-180, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you want two independent 2-slot sides, for example toast on one side and bagels on the other, plus a 3-year warranty. Popular Science's pick rests on exactly that.

**Skip it** if you need even results across all four slots every time. TechGearLab does not recommend it and points to the Mueller UltraToast 4-slice instead, which it says costs around half as much with comparable performance. If two slots are enough, the [Breville BTA720XL](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/) scored higher with TechGearLab (69 vs 58); see where both land in [our toaster ranking](/best/toasters/).
