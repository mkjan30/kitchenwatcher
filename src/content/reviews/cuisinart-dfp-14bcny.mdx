The Cuisinart Custom 14 is the food processor to buy if you cook in big batches and can leave it on the counter. This Cuisinart Custom 14 review draws on published tests, and they mostly agree: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) named it its winner on Aug 4, 2026, and [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor) called it a great mid-range model with 66 of 100. It purées well and runs quietly.

The trade-offs are weight, non-adjustable discs, and no separate dough attachment, which [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-food-processor/) flags for bakers. Skip it if you mostly chop small amounts or struggle to lift a heavy base. This page summarizes published tests; nothing here was tested hands-on ([how we research](/how-we-research/)). For the full ranking, see our [best food processors](/best/food-processors/).

## Is the Cuisinart 14 cup worth it? Who it suits

It's worth it for these kitchens:

- **Large-batch cooks.** ATK says a double batch of mayonnaise worked well and that it easily processed a double batch of pizza dough.
- **Purée makers.** TechGearLab scored its puréeing 8 of 10, its best sub-score. Nut butter is disputed: CNET calls it ideal for nut butter and long runs, but TechGearLab timed nut butter at 19 minutes and called the result inferior.
- **Counters with room to spare.** Homes & Gardens says it "may be a bit too bulky for some kitchens," so it suits a kitchen where it can stay out.

Skip it if:

- **You lift with difficulty.** CNET lists it at 18 lb and says it isn't suitable for someone who can't lift heavy objects.
- **You want adjustable slicing.** TechGearLab says neither disc adjusts; other sizes are sold separately.
- **You mostly chop small amounts.** ATK says it lacks a mini bowl for very small jobs.
- **You bake often and want a dedicated dough attachment.** CNET says it doesn't come with a dough hook, "making it not the best pick for bakers."

For a lighter, cheaper machine from our hub, look at the [Ninja BN601](/reviews/ninja-bn601/).

The two noise readings use different methods and distances, so don't compare them directly. Both still put it at the quiet end: Tom's Guide's second-quietest model, a Magimix, read 79.2 dBA, and in TechGearLab's test only the KitchenAid 5-cup mini chopper (57 dB) read lower.

**The weight confusion.** ATK and TechGearLab put the base at about 12.4 lb. Reviewers list 17 lb ([Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-food-processors)), 17.3 lb ([Homes & Gardens](https://www.homesandgardens.com/reviews/cuisinart-custom-14-cup-food-processor-review)), 18 lb (CNET) and 20 lb ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/cuisinart-14-cup-custom-food-processor)). Either way, the part you lift every time is the 12-plus-pound base.

## What the Custom 14 does well

### Purées

TechGearLab's purée score of 8 is its highest mark for this model, and it says the Custom was the least messy at tomato sauce. Tom's Guide found its hummus needed only occasional scraping, with no buildup under the blade. ATK says it was one of just a few models to turn yogurt perfectly green in its dye test. On nut butter the sources split: CNET calls it ideal, while TechGearLab says it took 19 minutes and the result was "definitively inferior."

### Large batches and dough

ATK says it chopped mirepoix uniformly, and that a double batch of mayonnaise worked well. ATK also says the 14-cup model easily processed a double batch of pizza dough. Reviewed named it Best for Pizza Dough: it kneaded dough into a smooth, elastic ball without stalling or moving around the countertop. TechGearLab singles it out in its pizza crust test even without a dough blade.

### Quiet running

TechGearLab measured 61.5 dB at 3 feet. Tom's Guide measured 65.2 dBA, the quietest of the models it compared. Reviewed says it is so quiet it won't shut down conversation. Only [Top Ten Reviews](https://www.toptenreviews.com/food-processors-cuisinart-review) calls it rather noisy, with no reading, and that page also says its team hadn't tested the units in person.

### Simple controls and a big feed tube

Reviewed describes two large levers: one runs it continuously and shuts it off, the other pulses. CNET sums them up as basically on/purée and off/pulse. Reviewed says the feed tube is large enough that a block of mozzarella or a beefsteak tomato needn't be cut in half first. Tom's Guide reports it sliced a whole russet potato in two seconds and shredded a ⅓-pound block of cheddar in about 10 seconds.

## Cuisinart 14 cup food processor problems

### The redesigned chopping blade

ATK says Cuisinart redesigned the chopping blade since its last round, and the new one leaves slightly bigger gaps at the bowl's sides and bottom. As a result, it couldn't effectively incorporate egg yolks into a single batch of mayonnaise. For context, ATK's best models had gaps as small as 2.9 mm at the side and 2.2 mm at the bottom, and its worst as much as 6.1 mm and 5.4 mm. ATK gives no figure for the Custom 14, only that its gaps are still narrower than those of lower-ranked models.

### Fixed slicing and shredding discs

TechGearLab says the slicing and shredding discs don't adjust, and other sizes mean buying more discs. Homes & Gardens notes the extra discs are sold separately. Tom's Guide says it would benefit from an adjustable slicing blade or several slicing blades.

### Small jobs in a big bowl

ATK says it lacks a mini bowl, and Cuisinart's own lists agree: the [US booklet](https://images.salsify.com/images/8a372f5b0abf171bc3cdf1a0710abc8afe2b7eb2/DFP-14BCNY.pdf) names the standard parts as the metal chopping blade, slicing disc, shredding disc, disc stem and spatula, and the [product page](https://www.cuisinart.com/custom-14-cup-food-processor/DFP-14BCNY.html) lists no mini bowl either. TechGearLab's spec table marks "Mini Bowl Blade: Yes," which conflicts with both. Homes & Gardens says its pesto, made with 2 cups of packed basil, still looked measly in the 14-cup bowl, though the blade chopped it into uniform pieces. Tom's Guide says onion halves bounced around the large bowl, though the pieces came out even.

### Fiddly assembly

CNET found the bowl and lid a bit fussy to align. Reviewed found the rear chute lock slightly inconvenient and the discs a little tricky to click into place, though Tom's Guide says the discs drop onto the stem easily. Reviewed also says it didn't shred mozzarella evenly, the only task where it faltered.

### Failures owners report

Systematic owner data is thin. I didn't read Reddit, Amazon or Best Buy reviews for this page, so there is no failure rate here. The best list available is [iFixit's student-contributed troubleshooting wiki](https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/Cuisinart_DFP-14BCN_Troubleshooting), written for the DFP-14BCN, a sibling model. It lists these faults:

- **Broken cover tabs** from pulling the cover too hard; the tabs engage the safety mechanism, so the machine won't run. See [why a food processor won't run with the lid on](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
- **A faulty safety switch**, which can stop the blades from spinning or let them run when parts aren't aligned.
- **A failed capacitor**: the motor hums but won't turn, while the shaft turns freely by hand.
- **Overheating**: the wiki says to wait 5 to 10 minutes if it stops mid-use and run smaller batches, and that it won't turn on for 5 minutes to 1 hour after overheating. Cuisinart's US booklet matches: the motor usually cools within 10 minutes, and in extreme cases it could take an hour. See [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).
- **Leaks** past the liquid limit, covered in [food processor leaking](/guides/food-processor-leaking/).

Cuisinart's [US booklet](https://images.salsify.com/images/8a372f5b0abf171bc3cdf1a0710abc8afe2b7eb2/DFP-14BCNY.pdf) is firm on one point: "Never try to override or tamper with cover interlock mechanism." If the machine doesn't stop within 4 seconds of turning the cover, it says to call 800-726-0190 and not use the machine.

## Dough: what's in the box

No source lists a separate dough blade. CNET says it doesn't come with a dough hook, TechGearLab says it doesn't have a dough blade, and Tom's Guide says it would benefit from one. ATK and Homes & Gardens list only the chopping blade, discs, stem and spatula. Reviewed describes the standard blade as a "chopping, mixing and dough blade," and Reviewed and Tom's Guide both report good dough results with it: pizza dough for Reviewed, cookie dough for Tom's Guide.

Cuisinart's [US booklet](https://images.salsify.com/images/8a372f5b0abf171bc3cdf1a0710abc8afe2b7eb2/DFP-14BCNY.pdf) (Custom 14, DFP-14N Series) also calls the metal blade a "chopping/mixing/dough blade." It lists the box contents as that blade, a slicing disc, a shredding disc, a detachable disc stem and a spatula. Its recommended maximum is 5 cups of all-purpose or 3 cups of whole-grain flour, the same as the [Canadian booklet](https://cuisinart.ca/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-master-ca/en_CA/v1731233013357//Instruction%20Booklet/DFP-14BCNYC_en.pdf).

## Recall check for older and used DFP-14 units

[CPSC recall 17-050](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) (Dec 13, 2016) covers about 8 million US units, including models beginning DFP-14, sold from July 1996 through December 2015. Only blades with four rivets are included: silver stainless steel with a beige plastic center hub. The CPSC says the riveted blade can crack over time and small metal pieces can break off into food. Conair received 69 reports of blade pieces in food, including 30 reports of mouth lacerations or tooth injuries.

The remedy is to stop using the riveted blade and contact Cuisinart for a free replacement at 877-339-2534. Units sold after December 2015 fall outside the recall dates, and we found no later CPSC recall for this model. Step-by-step check: [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Cleaning: is the Custom 14 dishwasher safe?

The sources disagree on the dishwasher. ATK's chart and Tom's Guide say the parts are dishwasher-safe. Cuisinart's [US booklet](https://images.salsify.com/images/8a372f5b0abf171bc3cdf1a0710abc8afe2b7eb2/DFP-14BCNY.pdf) goes further: all parts except the motor base are dishwasher safe, it recommends the dishwasher, and it says to insert the work bowl upside down (the Canadian booklet says the same). But ATK's FAQ advises against putting any food processor blade through the dishwasher because it can dull.

The stainless base shows marks: Homes & Gardens lists smudging as a con, and Tom's Guide says it needed buffing to avoid streaks. See [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/) and [are food processor parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## Price, warranty and the alternatives

Prices move. On Oct 18, 2026, [Cuisinart's own store](https://www.cuisinart.com/custom-14-cup-food-processor/DFP-14BCNY.html) and [Abt](https://www.abt.com/Cuisinart-Custom-14-14-Cup-Brushed-Metal-Food-Processor-DFP14BCNY/p/97939.html) both listed it at $299.95. The price box on this page tracks the current figure.

The warranty depends on which Cuisinart document you read. The current [US booklet](https://images.salsify.com/images/8a372f5b0abf171bc3cdf1a0710abc8afe2b7eb2/DFP-14BCNY.pdf) heads its warranty page "Full Five-Year Motor Warranty" and "Limited Three-Year Warranty," though its text spells out only the 3-year terms. Cuisinart's [product page](https://www.cuisinart.com/custom-14-cup-food-processor/DFP-14BCNY.html) lists both in its features, but its FAQ says only "a limited 3-year warranty on the entire unit." An [older US printing](https://web.archive.org/web/20200617024808/https://www.cuisinart.com/share/pdf/manuals/dfp-14bcny.pdf) (2016) gives only the 3-year limited warranty. Tom's Guide lists 3 years; Top Ten Reviews and the Canadian booklet say 5 years on the motor and 3 on the rest. Keep your receipt and ask Cuisinart (800-726-0190) which terms cover your unit.

- **Against the Ninja BN601:** cheaper and lighter, but louder. TechGearLab, which grouped the BN600 and BN601, measured the base at 4.5 lb and its noise at 80 dB. See [Ninja vs Cuisinart](/vs/ninja-bn601-vs-cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/).
