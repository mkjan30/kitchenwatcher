This Cuisinart SM-50 review comes down to one trade-off: a 500 W, 12-speed, 5.5-quart tilt-head mixer with a three-year warranty, for far less than a KitchenAid Artisan. But [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2593-the-best-stand-mixers) rated it Recommended with Reservations and measured only 3.6 usable quarts. TechGearLab scored it 56 of 100. It suits cakes, cookies and occasional bread, not bread-first bakers.

This page is built from published third-party measurements and Cuisinart's manual, not hands-on use; see [how we research](/how-we-research/). For ranked alternatives, see our [best stand mixers](/best/stand-mixers/).

## Why the 5.5-quart bowl acts like 3.6 quarts

America's Test Kitchen says it measured every bowl, and it puts the SM-50's actual capacity at 3.6 quarts against the 5.5-quart label. It also found big gaps between the attachments and the bowl. Two of the whisk's wires sit fairly close to the bowl, but the rest are shorter and farther away, so ingredients stayed just out of reach. Its testers scraped the bowl often and tried higher speeds, with mixed success.

ATK's chart marks the SM-50's adjustable tool clearance "No," and Cuisinart's manual describes no beater-height adjustment. The Artisan's chart entry says its tool height is adjustable.

Cuisinart's own limits are modest too. The [SM-50 manual](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/Cuisinart-Precision-Master-55-Quart-Stand-Mixer-sm-50-UserManual.pdf) caps yeast dough at 6 cups of white flour. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/best-stand-mixers) reports the manufacturer recommends kneading only enough for two loaves. If those limits sound tight for your baking, see [what size stand mixer you need](/guides/what-size-stand-mixer-do-i-need/).

## How well does the SM-50 whip cream and egg whites?

The evidence on whipping is split. America's Test Kitchen says the SM-50 struggled to whip egg whites. Reviewed says it excelled at whipping egg whites and mixing cake mix.

TechGearLab sits in between. It scored whipping 6.0 of 10 and timed 2.67 minutes for 1 cup of cream and 2.75 minutes for half a cup. The Artisan took 1.83 and 1.75 minutes, so the SM-50 was about 50 seconds slower on the full cup.

If you buy one, follow the manual's method. Use room-temperature whites in a spotlessly clean bowl with a clean, dry whisk. Start on Speed 1, increase gradually to Speed 6 until foamy, then gradually to Speed 12. The manual warns that whites beaten too fast at the start lose structure.

## Can the Cuisinart SM-50 knead bread dough?

Heavy dough is the SM-50's weakest area. TechGearLab scored kneading 4.0 of 10, and its stiff dough took 13 minutes, the slowest of the 10 mixers it compared. The Artisan took 7. America's Test Kitchen says the SM-50 struggled to incorporate ingredients for cookie and ciabatta dough.

Reviewed found it slowed as soon as flour went into creamed eggs and sugar for cookie dough. Within the two-loaf limit, it produced a smooth, satiny ball of bread dough. Its conclusion: best for cake bakers.

When the load gets too heavy, the manual says the mixer "may have overheated" and an overload protection device shuts it down. The fix, per the manual: turn it off and unplug it, remove some ingredients and let it stand a few minutes. Then plug it in and reset the speed. More on this in [stand mixer overheating](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/), and on choosing the right tool in [dough hook vs paddle](/guides/dough-hook-vs-paddle/).

## Is the SM-50 quiet, easy to use and easy to clean?

Reviewed rates the SM-50 easier to use than the KitchenAid, with a simple head-tilt switch and a smooth dial with large numbers. It tied with the Smeg as the quietest it tested. America's Test Kitchen also calls it easy to operate, steady and quiet, and liked the light that illuminates the bowl. TechGearLab disagrees on noise: its cons list for the SM-50 includes a loud motor.

TechGearLab also disagrees on the dial: it found the dial needs more force to turn. It also says the bowl lock occasionally needs an extra wiggle to seat. It praised the splash guard's wide feed chute.

Cleanup rules, per the manual: wipe the base with a damp cloth and never immerse it. The bowl can go in the dishwasher, but never use a wire brush, steel wool or bleach. The paddle, dough hook and splash guard are top-rack dishwasher safe, and the chef's whisk is hand-wash only. ATK's chart lists the splash guard as hand-wash only; the manual says top rack is fine. See [are stand mixer parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-stand-mixer-parts-dishwasher-safe/) and [how to clean a stand mixer](/guides/how-to-clean-a-stand-mixer/).

## Attachments and the three-year warranty

The manual describes a front attachment port for separately sold attachments, including a meat grinder, a pasta maker and a frozen dessert maker. It says to use only one accessory or attachment at a time. America's Test Kitchen groups Cuisinart with brands that make proprietary attachments, as opposed to the few with a universal hub that takes KitchenAid accessories. I found no source confirming that KitchenAid attachments fit the SM-50, so don't count on it; KitchenAid-side compatibility is covered in [KitchenAid attachments compatibility](/guides/kitchenaid-attachments-compatibility/).

The warranty is a limited three years from the original purchase date, for defects in materials and workmanship under normal home use. It applies in the U.S. and Canada only, to consumers buying for personal, family or household use. Keep your proof of purchase.

## Cuisinart SM-50 recall status and safety rules

I found no Cuisinart stand-mixer recall in the [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=mixer) as of Oct 5, 2026. Its mixer search returned four recalls, none from Cuisinart. A [search for Cuisinart in recall titles](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Cuisinart) returned six: grill brushes, a gas grill, a blender and juicer combo and food processors, no mixer. A [search for Conair](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Conair), the company named in Cuisinart's recall notices, returned 11 recalls and no mixer. See the [stand mixer recall check](/guides/stand-mixer-recall-check/) to search for yourself.

The manual's safeguards include:

- Turn the dial to OFF and unplug before fitting or removing accessories or attachments, after use and before cleaning.
- Keep hands, hair, clothing, spatulas and other utensils away from the beaters during operation.
- Do not operate it in an appliance garage or under a wall cabinet.
- Do not exceed the maximum capacities in the instruction book.

## Cuisinart stand mixer vs KitchenAid, in short

On paper the SM-50 wins: 500 W and a 5.5-quart label against the Artisan's 325 W (per Reviewed) and 5 quarts. The speed tests favor the Artisan: TechGearLab timed it faster at whipping and kneading. On capacity the SM-50 keeps a small lead, 3.6 usable quarts to the Artisan's 3 in ATK's measurements. Reviewed (updated 2024) puts the SM-50 at about half the Artisan's price.

Wattage clearly doesn't predict speed here, and Reviewed says the same of mixers generally: neither wattage nor bowl size is a good indicator of performance; see [stand mixer wattage explained](/guides/stand-mixer-wattage-explained/). The full comparison is [Cuisinart SM-50 vs KitchenAid Artisan](/vs/cuisinart-sm-50-vs-kitchenaid-ksm150ps/).

## Who should buy the SM-50 and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you mostly bake cakes and cookies in small batches, want a mixer two of three testers called quiet, and want three years of warranty cover. Reviewed's and ATK's ease-of-use notes back that up.

**Skip it** if you bake bread or stiff dough often, or hate stopping to scrape the bowl. TechGearLab's 13-minute knead and ATK's tool-gap finding both point the same way. The [KitchenAid Artisan KSM150PS](/reviews/kitchenaid-ksm150ps/) kneaded the same stiff dough in 7 minutes in TechGearLab's test. Tilt-head or bowl-lift is its own question; see [tilt-head vs bowl-lift mixers](/guides/tilt-head-vs-bowl-lift-mixer/). The full ranking is in [best stand mixers](/best/stand-mixers/).
