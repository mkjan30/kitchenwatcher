The De'Longhi Stilosa review verdict depends on who you ask. Tom's Guide calls it its favorite budget espresso machine. TechGearLab ranked it 10th of 11 at 44 of 100 and measured an 11.8% extraction yield. My read of the evidence: at about $100 it is worth it only if you add a decent grinder and accept that the US EC260BK ships with a weak tamper, no milk jug and, per Coffee Chronicler, a pressurized basket.

It sits in our [best espresso machines](/best/espresso-machines/) hub as a budget portafilter pick, and this page is about the US model only.

## Why reviewers disagree about the Stilosa

Tom's Guide's reviewer, a former barista, found the Stilosa could pull "consistently delicious shots" with experimentation, and it stresses pairing it with a quality grinder ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/best-picks/best-espresso-machines)). It suggests a coffee scale because the machine has no single or double shot buttons.

TechGearLab ran a standardized recipe: 15 g of coffee in, 27 g out in 15 seconds, for an extraction yield of 11.8% ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/espresso-machine/delonghi-stilosa)). It cites 18% to 22% as the industry standard. It blames "smaller baskets that simply can't hold very much coffee," and notes plastic aeration inserts in the portafilter that leave crema thin and quick to fade. Its verdict: an inexpensive option that "gets the job done, but doesn't do it exceptionally well."

My inference, which neither source states: grinder, dose and basket explain much of the gap. A skilled user with a good grinder gets more from it than a fixed lab recipe does.

Expert Reviews' positive review is a different machine. It covers the UK EC230, which comes with unpressurised single and double baskets ([Expert Reviews](https://www.expertreviews.co.uk/home-garden/small-appliances/coffee-machines/delonghi-stilosa-coffee-machine-review)). Its numbers don't transfer to the US EC260BK.

## Stilosa espresso machine problems reviewers report

### Thin, bitter shots and the US basket

The 11.8% yield is the headline problem. TechGearLab ties it to the small baskets and the aeration inserts, and describes the shots as watery and bitter. Expert Reviews says the EC260 keeps the pressurised baskets of the EC235, which work with coarser pre-ground coffee but "won't eke out the best flavour from good coffee." Coffee Chronicler says the non-pressurized basket is standard only on European and Asian versions, so US buyers get the crema-enhancing kind ([Coffee Chronicler](https://coffeechronicler.com/gear/espresso-machines/)).

Tom's Guide lists "single-walled portafilters" as a pro, which contradicts the other three. Our [pressurized vs non-pressurized basket guide](/guides/pressurized-vs-non-pressurized-basket/) explains why it matters.

### Portafilter sneeze: wait before you unlock

The Stilosa has no solenoid valve, so pressure stays in the group after the shot. Coffee Chronicler says to wait before removing the portafilter or risk a "portafilter sneeze" of wet grounds. Expert Reviews flags the same risk on the UK EC230, plus dripping after brewing.

### You stop the shot yourself

There is no single or double volumetric dosing. Tom's Guide says you have to watch the shot and switch it off yourself, and suggests a coffee scale to keep ratios consistent.

### No brewing and steaming back to back

TechGearLab says you pull all the espresso first and then steam all the milk. After steaming, the machine needs time to cool or it will burn the next coffee.

### Slow flow, limescale and descaling

None of the reviews I read report leaks on the EC260BK. De'Longhi's [EC230/235/260 instructions](https://dls.delonghigroup.com/files/docs/5/A1-5713245423.pdf) say to descale every 200 coffees with De'Longhi descaler only. They warn that other descalers may cause faults the guarantee doesn't cover, and TechGearLab reports the same 200-shot interval.

A troubleshooting guide from [CoffeeFixHub](https://coffeefixhub.com/guides/delonghi-stilosa-not-working-fix), which says it follows that shared manual, lists limescale, over-packed or over-tamped grounds and a badly seated tank as causes of slow or no flow. For the steps, see [how to descale an espresso machine](/guides/how-to-descale-an-espresso-machine/).

## How good is the Stilosa's steam wand?

Tom's Guide found the wand "a touch overzealous," needing full concentration to avoid over-textured milk. It still made fine microfoam for lattes and flat whites, and Tom's Guide rates it among the top wands it has seen at the price.

TechGearLab scored milk steaming 4.0 of 10. It got milk "with big bubbles that quickly faded away," and its roundup says the Stilosa "struggles to steam milk" because higher steam pressure heats milk too fast to control ([TechGearLab best espresso machines](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-espresso-machine)). Coffee Chronicler adds that latte art is tricky with the US panarello wand, though it is removable. Expert Reviews found the EC260's panarello froth "bubbly and light," well short of the UK EC230's traditional wand.

The disagreement is real and I can't settle it. My guess, which no source states, is that skill matters more here than on an automatic wand.

## What to add to make the Stilosa worth it

It was $99.95 (list $149.95) on De'Longhi's US store on Oct 3, 2026, and Tom's Guide's deal widget showed the same that day. The add-ons decide whether that is a bargain:

- **A grinder.** Tom's Guide calls a quality grinder non-negotiable and suggests a hand grinder such as the sub-$100 1Zpresso Q Air. That can nearly double the outlay.
- **A better 51 mm tamper.** Tom's Guide calls the included plastic one "just awful."
- **A milk jug.** None comes in the box, per Tom's Guide and De'Longhi's own box list.
- **A coffee scale,** because you stop the shot by hand.
- **Possibly a 51 mm non-pressurized basket or portafilter.** Coffee Chronicler suggests a premium basket such as an IMS.

I found no sourced prices for the tamper, jug, scale or basket, so I haven't totaled them.

## Who the Stilosa suits, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you want a real portafilter machine for about $100, already own or will buy a decent grinder, and enjoy tinkering. Tom's Guide's results show what it can do in practiced hands.

**Skip it** if you want consistent shots straight out of the box, or milk drinks without fuss. TechGearLab's numbers are what an unmodified Stilosa gives on a standard recipe. Tom's Guide credits the [Breville Bambino Plus](/reviews/breville-bambino-plus/) with automated steaming, at a much higher price. The [Casabrews CM5418](/reviews/casabrews-cm5418/) is the other budget option in our hub; the [Casabrews CM5418 vs De'Longhi Stilosa](/vs/casabrews-cm5418-vs-delonghi-stilosa/) page covers that head-to-head.

## Stilosa warranty and what's in the box

De'Longhi's US store lists the machine, portafilter, tamper, single and double filters, a measuring spoon, and the instruction manual with a quick start guide. There is no milk jug.

On Oct 3, 2026 the product page showed a "2-Year included warranty" badge and "1 Year Warranty + 1 year with product registration" in the box list. Together they suggest the second year depends on registering the machine. Register it, and confirm the term with De'Longhi.
