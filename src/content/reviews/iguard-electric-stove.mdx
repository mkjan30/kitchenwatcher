iGuard for Electric Stoves is a plug-in box that sits between an electric range and its outlet, senses by radar when nobody is in the kitchen, warns, then cuts power to the burners. It's aimed at forgotten cooking, the leading factor in home cooking fires. It has award attention but no independent measurements or owner track record yet, and sources disagree on its price and ship date.

It's one of three devices in our [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/), and the only one designed to act before a fire starts rather than after. This page compiles what iGuard, the press and fire-safety bodies have published; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Why a stove shut-off at all

NFPA found unattended cooking was the leading factor in U.S. home cooking fires and casualties in 2017 to 2021, that ranges or cooktops were involved in 53 percent of reported home cooking fires and 88 percent of cooking-fire deaths, and that households with electric ranges had a higher risk of cooking fires than those with gas ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). The U.S. Fire Administration's rule is simple: if you leave the kitchen, turn the burner off ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). A shut-off is built for the times that rule gets forgotten. It can't do anything about a fire that starts while you're standing at the stove.

## What iGuard says it does

Per iGuard's product and FAQ pages (manufacturer claims):

- Detects when you leave the kitchen, alerts before shut-off, and turns the stove off if cooking is unattended.
- Forgotten-cooking protection works without Wi-Fi; the app adds monitoring, remote shut-off where supported, and lock and schedule controls.
- An extended cooking timer for long simmers, after which it returns to automatic protection.
- If it has shut the stove off while you were out, it turns the stove back on when it sees you've returned.
- Plug-in, no electrician required. iGuard's general FAQ says the electric model takes a handyman about 15 minutes. {/* VERIFY: 15-minute install claim is from iGuard's general FAQ and may describe older iGuardStove models */}

[Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/gear/home-of-the-future-awards-2026/) named it the best stove safety device in its 2026 Home of the Future Awards (July 2026), writing that its radar is precise enough to tell a person sitting still from an empty room. That's a description of the design, not a published measurement.

Three things stand out. First, price: iGuard's page shows $549 on its buy buttons but also a "$699 total, Shipping Extra" line, and [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/seven-fascinating-inventions-unveiled-at-the-2026-consumer-electronics-show-180987980/) reported $399 at CES in January. [OddityMall](https://odditymall.com/radar-automatic-stove-shutoff/) describes the $549 as a $199 deposit plus $350 at shipment. I'd confirm the checkout total, including shipping, before you commit. {/* VERIFY: $549 vs "$699 total" on iGuard's product page, Sep 27, 2026 */}

Second, timing. Smithsonian said it would ship later in 2026; OddityMall said new orders were shipping in August; iGuard's product page now says new inventory arrives monthly, while its homepage still offers a notify-me sign-up for 2026 pre-sales. The 3-prong version for older outlets is a priority list only.

Third, the shut-off delay, the number that matters most. Smithsonian reported five minutes without movement; [Graying with Grace](https://www.grayingwithgrace.com/stove-safety-devices-aging-in-place/) says an adjustable timer, typically 5 to 45 minutes; iGuard's own pages don't give a figure. {/* VERIFY: default shut-off delay — five minutes (Smithsonian) vs 5-45 minutes adjustable (Graying with Grace); iGuard gives none */}

## Certification

A device that sits in series with a 240-volt range circuit should carry a safety listing. iGuard's FAQ says "iGuardStoves" are certified to CSA C22.2 No. 205 in Canada and UL 2017 in the U.S., but it doesn't say whether that covers the new radar plug-in model, and the product page shows no listing mark. I couldn't find a certification record for this model. {/* VERIFY: whether CSA C22.2 No. 205 / UL 2017 certification applies to the new radar plug-in model */}

## What owners report

This model went on sale in 2026 and I found no owner reviews of it. Best Buy's reviews are for the older IGSE-4, a different product, and an Amazon listing couldn't be confirmed as this model.

## iGuard vs FireAvert

FireAvert is the obvious alternative, and the difference is when each acts. Per Graying with Grace, FireAvert's electric models listen for your smoke alarm and cut power to the stove after it sounds, for about $249.99. iGuard acts on an empty kitchen, before there's smoke. Neither has published independent measurements that I could find.

## Who should buy it, who should skip it

It's worth a look if you have an electric range with a 4-prong outlet and someone in the home, often an older relative, who forgets the stove is on, and you're comfortable being an early buyer of a new product. Ask iGuard for the checkout total and the certification that applies to this model first.

Skip it for a gas stove (this model is electric only), for an older 3-prong outlet until that version ships, or if the risk you're worried about is a fire while someone is cooking. For that, keep an extinguisher by the exit, such as the [Kidde FA110G](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/), and see the [StoveTop FireStop review](/reviews/stovetop-firestop-rangehood/) for a device that reacts to flame instead of absence.
