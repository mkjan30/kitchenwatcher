iGuard for Electric Stoves is a $549 box that plugs in between an electric range and its wall outlet, uses radar to notice when nobody is in the kitchen, warns you, then cuts power to the burners. It's aimed at forgotten cooking, a risk Popular Science notes rises with age. {/* MEASURE: verdict — one sentence, based on our shut-off delay and false shut-off counts */}

It's one of three devices in our [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/), and the only one that tries to stop a fire from starting instead of fighting one. I'm testing it the way [our testing page](/how-we-research/) describes.

## Why a stove shut-off at all

NFPA found unattended cooking was the leading factor in U.S. home cooking fires and their casualties, and that ranges or cooktops were involved in 53 percent of reported home cooking fires in 2017 to 2021. The same report found households with electric ranges had a higher risk of cooking fires than those with gas ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). The U.S. Fire Administration's rule is simple: if you leave the kitchen, turn the burner off ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). iGuard is built for the times that rule gets forgotten.

## Specs and price

From iGuard's product page (manufacturer claims, not my testing):

- Price: $549 for the plug-in electric model on September 26, 2026, with shipping extra. {/* MEASURE: price paid including shipping and tax, dollars */}
- Plugs: a 4-prong version (NEMA 14-50) available now, and a 3-prong version (NEMA 10-50) for older stoves on pre-order.
- Install: plug-in, no electrician required, per iGuard.
- Detects when you leave the kitchen, alerts before shut-off, and turns the stove off if cooking is unattended.
- An extended cooking timer for long simmers.
- App with alerts (first kitchen activity of the day, no activity by a set morning time) and remote shut-off where supported. iGuard says the fire protection itself works without Wi-Fi.
- No UL, ETL or CSA listing is shown on the product page. I'm checking.

Popular Science named it the best stove safety device in its 2026 Home of the Future Awards and wrote that the radar can tell a person sitting still from an empty room ([Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/gear/home-of-the-future-awards-2026/)). Coverage from CES disagrees on details: Smithsonian reported a five-minute no-movement shut-off, a $399 price and gas support ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/seven-fascinating-inventions-unveiled-at-the-2026-consumer-electronics-show-180987980/)), while the product page lists $549 for this electric model. I'm going with the product page and measuring the timing myself.

## iGuard vs FireAvert

FireAvert is the obvious alternative. The difference is when each one acts. Per Graying with Grace, standard FireAvert models listen for your smoke alarm and cut power after it sounds, and cost about $249.99 for electric ([Graying with Grace](https://www.grayingwithgrace.com/stove-safety-devices-aging-in-place/)). iGuard acts on an empty kitchen, before there's smoke. We haven't tested FireAvert, so that's a description, not a verdict.
