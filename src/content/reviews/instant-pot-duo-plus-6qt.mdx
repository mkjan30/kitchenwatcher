The Instant Pot Duo Plus V4 is a well-rated 6-quart pressure cooker whose main advantage, per independent reviews, is a quieter steam release than the cheaper Rio. Its weak spots: it was the slowest of the Instant Pots Wirecutter timed to pressure, and Wirecutter recommends it over the Rio only on sale. This Instant Pot Duo Plus review is a research summary of the sources listed, not our own use; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

For how it ranks against the other models, see [best electric pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/).

## Which Duo Plus is this? V3 vs V4 Whisper-Quiet

Reviews of the "Duo Plus" cover different versions, so check which one a review tested. This page covers the V4 "Whisper-Quiet" 6-quart, model DPPC604: 1000 W and 120 V, per the specifications table in the [Duo Plus V4 manual](https://p.widencdn.net/irxr19/112-0169-01_113-0058-01_IB_DuoPlusV4_US_Manual_R24-FINAL-06-30-2025_LoRes.pdf). The 8-quart V4 is DPPC804, 1200 W.

## What do reviewers like about the Duo Plus?

The steam release is the point every reviewer makes. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker) named the Duo Plus V4 an Editors' Choice and its Best Overall Pressure Cooker with an overall score of 86, the highest of the five cookers it lists. It called the Duo Plus V4 the most user-friendly of the group, listed a "quieter, safer steam valve" as a pro, and noted the lever sits on the other side of the lid from where the steam comes out.

Tom's Guide gave the Whisper-Quiet model its Recommended badge and lists quiet steam release and easy dishwasher cleanup among its reasons to buy. TechRadar, reviewing the earlier version, also liked that the switch keeps fingers away from the steam release valve.

## Where the Duo Plus evidence disagrees

**Time to pressure.** Wirecutter timed 16 min 8 s with a pot of beans, against 14 min 24 s to 14 min 48 s for the Pro, Rio and Duo, and said it "didn't find a significant difference" among those four. TechRadar recorded preheats of about 6 to 10½ minutes for different foods on the earlier version, and the V4 manual says it "can take 10-15 minutes" to reach pressure. Different foods, versions and methods, so I have not averaged them.

**Sous vide temperature.** Tom's Guide lists "no temperature adjustment for sous vide" as a con. The V4 manual's sous vide steps say the default temperature flashes and can be changed by turning the dial. The two sources conflict; the manual is the more specific of the two.

**Maximum pressure.** Tom's Guide's spec table lists 15 PSI. Wirecutter says the Instant Pot Max "can reach a higher pressure than other Instant Pot models (up to 15 psi)," and the V4 manual gives no psi figure. Treat the 15 PSI number as unconfirmed.

**Value.** TechGearLab ranks it first overall. Tom's Guide recommends it but suggests beginners stick with the cheaper Rio. Wirecutter recommends it only if it is on sale near the Rio's price.

## Instant Pot Duo Plus problems reviewers and the manual document

Reviewer-observed problems, each from one source:

- **Finicky sauté.** TechGearLab got its best results on low rather than high, and says the steel pot can be hard to scrub after sautéing.
- **Rice program overcooked.** Tom's Guide got mushy rice at the 12-minute default and still called it overcooked after cutting the water.
- **Sous vide crème brûlée still runny** after 90 minutes and a night in the fridge, per Tom's Guide.
- **Uneven low-pressure broccoli** on the earlier version, per TechRadar.
- **Hot lid top**, 185 °F on the metal part, per TechRadar.

The V4 manual's error table explains the messages owners search for. C7 or NoPr means the element failed, there is too little liquid, or the switch was left on Vent. Food burn means the cooker detected high temperature at the bottom of the inner pot and lowered the heat; the manual says starch deposits may be blocking heat. Lid means the lid is in the wrong position, PrSE means pressure built in a non-pressure program, and C5 means no pot or no water. The fixes are in our [Instant Pot error codes guide](/guides/instant-pot-error-codes/) and the [burn message guide](/guides/instant-pot-burn-message/).

Owner-report evidence for this page is thin: no owner-complaint source could be read in full, so none is quoted here.

## Safety rules in the Duo Plus V4 manual

The V4 manual's safeguards are specific. Do not fill past the PC MAX line, and do not use the cooker for deep frying or pressure frying with oil. Turn it off if steam escapes the steam release valve or float valve "in a steady stream for longer than 3 minutes," or from the sides of the lid. Never force the lid open, and replace the sealing ring every 12 to 18 months with authorized Instant Pot rings only.

The [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=Duo), checked November 13, 2026, shows no recall for the Instant Pot Duo line. The Instant-brand recalls on record are the Gem 65 (18-110) and the Smart Bluetooth model (15-741), both different products. To check any model yourself, see the [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/), and for the wider picture, [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

The V4 manual has no canning program. Whether a multicooker is safe for canning is covered in [can you can in an Instant Pot](/guides/can-you-can-in-an-instant-pot/).

## Duo Plus test run and sealing-ring care, per the manual

The V4 manual's test run is 3 cups of water, Pressure Cook for 5 minutes with Keep Warm off, then move the switch to Vent. Instant's Rio Wide manual says "3 quarts water" instead, and the [water test guide](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/) covers that conflict.

For sealing-ring odor, the manual suggests 1 cup of water plus 1 cup of white vinegar, Pressure Cook 5 to 10 minutes, then quick release. More in [Instant Pot sealing ring smell](/guides/instant-pot-sealing-ring-smell/) and [how to clean an Instant Pot](/guides/how-to-clean-an-instant-pot/). The manual recommends quick release for foods that tend to overcook, like vegetables and seafood; the trade-offs are in [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

**You want the cheapest Instant Pot with the basics.** Wirecutter says the Duo Plus adds only sous vide, cake and sterilize, plus low pressure, for about $30 more than the Rio at its time of publication.
