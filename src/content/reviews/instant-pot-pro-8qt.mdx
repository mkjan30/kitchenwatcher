Our Instant Pot Pro review in short: a strong pot for searing, with weak slow-cook and bake settings. America's Test Kitchen named the Pro 8-quart its winning multicooker in May 2025, citing a flat-bottomed pot that allows for even searing, stay-cool handles and a pressure-release switch that keeps your hand away from the steam. The trade-off: ATK found it weak at slow cooking, and its Bake setting failed Wirecutter's cake test.

We didn't test it ourselves; this page pulls together independent testers and the US manual ([how we research](/how-we-research/)). It ranks second on our [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) list, behind the Duo Plus.

## What the Instant Pot Pro 10-in-1 functions actually do

Instant sells the Pro as a 10-in-1, and TechRadar counts ten cooking methods. The manual's control panel lists eight Smart Programs: Pressure Cook, Rice/Grain, Steam, Sauté, Slow Cook, Sous Vide, Yogurt and Bake. Independent results split them into three groups.

- **Strong.** ATK called the pressure-cooking, rice, sautéing, yogurt and steaming functions "all excellent." Tom's Guide reported a tender beef stew from Pressure Cook.
- **Weak: slow cooking.** ATK says the Pro 8 couldn't slow-cook large cuts of meat well. TechRadar's 2021 review, whose listed dimensions match the 6-quart, recorded a slow-cooked chili at 176 F after six hours on high and was happy with the result ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/reviews/instant-pot-pro)). The sources disagree; ATK's 2025 comparison is the newer one.
- **Disputed: sous vide.** The manual includes a sous vide cooking chart, but ATK says the Pro has no fan to circulate the water and isn't as accurate as a good sous vide machine, so it isn't true sous vide.
- **Weak: bake.** The manual sets Bake to 350 F for 30 minutes by default. Wirecutter's small cake rounds came out as "uncooked goo" even after an hour. ATK says the baking function uses steam, so it suits cheesecake but not much else.

## Pot, lid and steam release: the Pro's real upgrades

The pot is the reason to pay more. It has handles (ATK calls them stay-cool, Tom's Guide silicone grips), and the manual rates it stovetop-friendly and oven-safe to 450 F. Wirecutter says it works on the stove, even induction, so you can brown on a burner or finish a dish in the oven in the same pot. That applies to the inner pot only: the manual says never to put the cooker base on a stovetop.

The lid seals itself, per the manual and Wirecutter. A switch on the lid releases steam, and ATK says it keeps your hand away from the hot steam. Wirecutter says the diffuser cap makes release gentler and noticeably quieter than the Rio's; ATK says slightly more gentle. The Pro also works with Instant's QuickCool tray, sold separately; Wirecutter says it cut up to 15 minutes off natural release.

Two practical warnings come from the manual. The metal portion of the lid gets extremely hot, so don't touch it during cooking; TechRadar measured the lid at 140 F (60 C), the only hot area on the outside after its curry test. And the manual's C8 code means an incorrect inner pot: use only a stainless Pro Series pot with handles, so buy replacements to match.

## Instant Pot Pro problems testers and owners report

- **Slow cooking.** Weak for large cuts, per ATK.
- **Bake.** Failed Wirecutter's cake test on the Pro 6.
- **No preheat advantage.** Instant claims 20% faster preheating. Wirecutter timed the Pro 6 at 14 min 24 s against 14 min 48 s for the Duo and 16 min 8 s for the Duo Plus, calling it no significant difference.
- **Sticking.** The pot isn't nonstick; Tom's Guide's white rice stuck to the bottom.
- **Steam rack.** Some of TechRadar's broccoli fell through it, and Tom's Guide says it isn't as sturdy as a basket.
- **Thin manual.** TechRadar says the manual is brief on timings and rice-to-water ratios, and Wirecutter says the printed manual is less detailed than the online PDF.

## Safety rules from the Instant Pot Pro manual

The US manual is specific. Use only the Pro lid with the Pro base. Don't fill past the Max PC Fill line, or past the Half Fill line for foods that expand such as rice, beans and pasta, and use at least 2 cups of liquid in the 8-quart. Never force the lid open; open it only after the float valve has dropped.

Replace the sealing ring every 12 to 18 months, using only authorized rings. Don't deep fry or pressure fry with oil. Use natural release for high-starch foods (soups, stews, chilis, pasta, oatmeal, congee) and foods that expand (beans, grains); the manual warns natural release can take up to 40 minutes with some dishes unless you use QuickCool. See [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

The CPSC recall database showed no recall for the Instant Pot Pro when searched on November 13, 2026. The Instant Pot-branded recalls it does list (18-110, Gem 65 multicooker, fire hazard; 15-741, Smart pressure cooker, shock hazard) covered other models; see the [pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/) and [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

The Pro has no canning Smart Program, but its manual's pressure table says Low pressure is "also for canning of high-acid foods." The [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/publications/nchfp/factsheets/electric_cookers.html) disagrees: it says its USDA canning processes are not recommended in electric multicookers, even when the maker's directions include canning, because they were developed for stovetop pressure canners holding four or more quart jars. Follow the NCHFP and use a stovetop canner. More in [can you can in an Instant Pot](/guides/can-you-can-in-an-instant-pot/).

## The water test on the Pro: optional, and measured differently

The Pro manual calls the first water test optional. It says to fill the inner pot "to the first line (1)" with water and run Pressure Cook on High for 5 minutes with the vent reminder and Keep Warm off. Instant's Rio manuals measure it differently and disagree with each other: the Rio Wide Plus manual says 3 cups of water, the Rio Wide manual 3 quarts; the [Instant Pot water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/) guide compares them.

## Is 8 quarts the right size? ATK and Wirecutter disagree

ATK says 8 quarts is the best size for most home cooks and made the Pro 8 its Winner; it lists the Pro 6 as having the same features in a smaller package. Wirecutter picks the Pro 6 and calls the 8-quart good for large groups or stock. Wirecutter also says most people would be better served by the 8-quart Pro than by the similarly priced Rio Wide Plus. See [what size Instant Pot](/guides/what-size-instant-pot/) to choose.

## Pro, Pro Plus or the UK Pro: which one is this?

This page covers the US Instant Pot Pro: the Pro 60 and Pro 80, sold as a 10-in-1, with no smart features listed by Tom's Guide. The Pro Plus is Instant's app-connected model, per [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-instant-pot/), and has Wi-Fi, per Tom's Guide.

There is also a 2025 UK-only "Instant Pot Pro" with Wi-Fi, which [Tom's Guide reviewed](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/home-appliances/instant-pot-pro-2025-review) at £249 and says will not be released in the US. Its timings don't apply to the US model. To check yours, the manual says the model name is on the silver rating label on the back.

## Who should buy the Instant Pot Pro and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you sear and sauté a lot, want a pot with handles that can go on a burner or in the oven, and cook for large groups or make stock often, the use Wirecutter suggests for the 8-quart.

**Skip it** if slow cooking is your main use: ATK found the Pro weak there, and the Cosori excelled at it in ATK's tests. Skip it if you hoped to bake, since Wirecutter's cake failed. Skip the 8-quart in a small kitchen at 15.83 lb (manufacturer spec), and skip the Pro if you want air frying, which Tom's Guide lists among its downsides. For plain pressure cooking, see the [Duo Plus review](/reviews/instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/).
