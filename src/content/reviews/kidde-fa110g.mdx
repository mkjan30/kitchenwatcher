The Kidde FA110G (sold as the Basic 110) is a 2.5 lb disposable dry-chemical extinguisher rated 1-A:10-B:C, and it is one of the cheapest ways to put a UL-rated extinguisher by a kitchen door. {/* MEASURE: verdict — one sentence, who should buy it, based on our discharge tests */} Before you trust an older one, check the handle: plastic-handle FA110G units made from 2012 to August 2017 are on a [CPSC recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/Kidde-Recalls-Fire-Extinguishers-with-Plastic-Handles-Due-to-Failure-to-Discharge-and-Nozzle-Detachment-One-Death-Reported).

This page sits in our [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/). I'm testing it the way [our testing page](/how-we-research/) describes: bought at retail, measured, then emptied on purpose.

## Specs and price

Every number here is from Kidde's own data sheet for part 466142MTL (the current FA110G build), not from my testing:

- Rating: UL 1-A:10-B:C. Kidde lists it for Class A (trash, wood, paper), Class B (liquids and gases) and Class C (energized electrical equipment) fires.
- Agent: monoammonium phosphate dry chemical, 2.5 lb average.
- Unit weight 3.9 lb, 14.2 in tall, 3.25 in diameter.
- Discharge time 8 to 12 seconds; discharge range 6 to 8 feet; operating pressure 100 psi.
- Seamless aluminum cylinder; valve, handle and lever listed as aluminum and steel.
- Pressure gauge, plastic strap bracket, USCG and DOT approved.
- Disposable, 6-year limited warranty. Kidde's product page calls it a 12-year service-free design and says NFPA 10 still calls for annual external checks.

What I can say from primary sources: the U.S. Fire Administration's first advice for a pan fire is to cover it with a lid or baking sheet and turn off the burner, and never use water on grease ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA found that more than half of non-fatal cooking-fire injuries happened when people tried to fight the fire themselves ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). If a fire is spreading, get out and call 911. An extinguisher is for a small fire with a clear way out behind you.

## The 2017 recall: is your Kidde FA110G affected?

On November 2, 2017, Kidde and the CPSC recalled about 37.8 million plastic-handle and push-button extinguishers in the U.S. (recall 18-022). Per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2018/Kidde-Recalls-Fire-Extinguishers-with-Plastic-Handles-Due-to-Failure-to-Discharge-and-Nozzle-Detachment-One-Death-Reported), the units could clog or need excessive force to discharge, and the nozzle could detach with enough force to be an impact hazard. Kidde knew of about 391 reports of failed or limited activation or nozzle detachment, about 16 injuries, and a 2014 death after a car crash fire.

The FA110G is on the CPSC's plastic-handle list for date codes from January 2, 2012 through August 15, 2017. Here is how I'd check yours:

1. **Look at the handle.** The recall covers plastic-handle and push-button units. Kidde's current data sheet for part 466142MTL lists the valve, handle and lever as aluminum and steel. A metal handle points to the current build.
2. **If the handle is plastic, find the date code.** The CPSC says units from 2007 on carry a 10-digit date code printed on the side of the cylinder, near the bottom.
3. **Read digits five through nine.** They are the day of the year and the year, in DDDYY form. So 15216 would mean the 152nd day of 2016, which falls inside the recall window.
4. **If it's in range, stop relying on it.** The CPSC lists the remedy as replacement. Contact Kidde at 800-880-6788 (the number in the CPSC notice). Kidde still lists the recall on its [product alerts page](https://www.kidde.com/home-safety/en/us/support/product-alerts/).
