The Moccamaster KBGV Select suits people who brew a full or half pot at once and want a long warranty. Tom's Guide and TechRadar praise its speed, roughly six to six and a half minutes a pot, and its 5-year warranty. Skip it if you want delay-brew, a thermal carafe or dishwasher-safe parts; TechGearLab also found uneven bed saturation and a flimsy carafe handle. It sits in our [best coffee makers](/best/coffee-makers/) hub.

My verdict, from the sources below: a simple brewer with a strong warranty and readily available spare parts. The independent evidence on cup quality is split, and the SCA claim does not check out on the SCA's own list.

## Brew temperature and speed: measured vs claimed

The brew temperature you see quoted everywhere is a claim, not a measurement. Technivorm's product page says its brewers hit the 196-205°F Golden Cup range every cycle, and [Coffeeness](https://www.coffeeness.de/en/moccamaster-review/) repeats that range.

Tom's Guide says the copper element heats water to 196-208°F, without saying it measured that. NBC Select gives 196-205°F and attributes the 205°F figure to Technivorm.

TechGearLab measured consistency rather than absolute temperature. It brewed five back-to-back pots and found an average fluctuation of 2.5 degrees between brews; the most stable models it compared varied 0.47 degrees. TechGearLab does not state the unit.

It called the temperature nearly industry-standard but did not print a number. None of the sources I read published its own measured brew-water temperature.

Speed is the one point every source agrees on. A full carafe takes about 6 minutes per NBC Select, just under 6 per TechRadar, and about 6.5 per Tom's Guide. TechGearLab measured only a 2-second variation in brew time from one pot to the next, and found very similar pH between fresh coffee and coffee 2 hours after brewing.

Cup quality is where reviewers split. Tom's Guide and TechRadar praise the coffee. TechGearLab scored brew quality 5.6 of 10 and the machine 48 of 100 overall, and said other machines cost less and make better coffee.

## Moccamaster KBGV hot plate temperature and the 100-minute auto-off

NBC Select reports the hot plate holds 175 to 185°F, depending on how much you brewed. Coffeeness gives the same range. Neither says it measured this, so treat it as an unverified figure. Technivorm's product page says only that the plate has an independent heating element, and gives no temperature.

The US KBGV turns the hot plate off after 100 minutes, per Technivorm's product page, NBC Select and TechRadar. UK and Australian versions shut off at 40 minutes, per TechRadar. TechRadar notes you can flick the power switch back on to keep it warm longer, though it also says coffee left on a hot plate for hours turns bitter.

The only measured carafe temperatures come from TechRadar's UK model. The coffee was 181°F right after pouring and 171°F at 30 minutes. At one hour, 20 minutes after that model's plate had switched off, it was down to 136°F. A US machine's plate would still have been on at that point, so don't read 136°F as the US one-hour figure.

Sources describe the bottom switch differently. NBC Select calls it a hot plate control: low for 2 to 6 cups, high for 8 to 10. Technivorm's current page calls it a full or half carafe selector that adjusts the brewing process, and Tom's Guide, TechRadar and TechGearLab describe it the same way. Moccamaster USA's own [entry in the SCA Best New Product Awards](https://new.sca.coffee/entries/kbgv-select-moccamaster-usa) says it does both: the switch adjusts saturation time and hot plate temperature.

## Moccamaster KBGV Select problems reviewers report

These are the problems I found, each with its source. No source gives a failure rate, and I did not find an owner-review breakdown I could open, so the evidence on how often each one happens is thin.

- **Uneven water distribution.** TechGearLab says the spray head left a distinct rectangular divot in the brew bed, which suggests unbalanced extraction. Coffeeness describes the spray arm as raining down evenly, so the two disagree.
- **Flimsy carafe.** TechGearLab says the thin plastic handle feels cheap, the carafe has no measurement marks, and the pour is sloppy at a moderate rate. Coffeeness also calls the handle a little flimsy. TechRadar adds that the carafe lid tends to flip open when you pour the last of the coffee.
- **No programming.** TechGearLab lists it as not programmable, and Tom's Guide's only con is no delayed brew.
- **Hand-wash only.** TechGearLab and TechRadar say no parts are dishwasher-safe. TechGearLab adds that the manual's cleaning instructions are unclear and you remove three parts to reach the filter basket.
- **Fixed reservoir.** NBC Select, Tom's Guide and Coffeeness say the water tank does not come off, which makes filling and cleaning harder.
- **Hot surfaces.** NBC Select warns that the carafe, metal outlet arm, brew basket and hot plate all get hot enough to burn.
- **Flimsy basket.** Coffeeness calls the plastic filter basket flimsy.

## Is the KBGV Select actually SCA certified?

It is not on the SCA's current list. Technivorm's product page calls the KBGV SCA certified and SCA approved. Tom's Guide calls it SCA-approved, TechRadar says it meets the SCA "Gold Cup" standard, and NBC Select says all Moccamaster brewers are SCA certified.

The [SCA's Certified Home Equipment list](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer), fetched October 3, 2026, names brewers from OXO, Fellow, Breville, Bonavita, GE and others. No Technivorm or Moccamaster model appears. The SCA's October 10, 2023 news post announcing the KBGV Select's certification returned a 404 error on the same date.

The SCA page says certification carries a three-year licensing period, so a 2023 certification may have lapsed, or the list may be incomplete. I can't confirm either, so I don't call it certified. For what the badge means, see [what an SCA-certified coffee maker is](/guides/what-is-sca-certified-coffee-maker/).

## Warranty, repairs and descaling

Technivorm gives a 5-year warranty and says its technicians will repair any Moccamaster for the life of the brewer. Tom's Guide notes those lifetime repairs come with a small fee. Coffeeness says the warranty covers manufacturing defects but excludes the carafe and removable parts. TechRadar says spare parts are readily available.

Technivorm's product page says to descale every 100 cycles, or at least every three months; TechRadar and Coffeeness give the same interval. Tom's Guide says Technivorm recommends every 3 months or so in hard-water areas. TechGearLab timed a descaling cycle at about 25 minutes. For the steps, see [how to descale a coffee maker](/guides/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/); for what to use, see [vinegar vs descaler](/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/).

## What "Moccamaster 53941" means

53941 is the SKU for the Polished Silver KBGV Select, per the variant data on Technivorm's US store. Each finish has its own number: Matte Black is 53948, Juniper 53923, Ocean 53632. It is the same machine in a different color. On Oct 3, 2026, most finishes listed at $369 on Technivorm's US store; Brushed Gold and Candy Apple Red listed at $389.

## Who should skip the KBGV Select

If you drink coffee slowly over a morning, a glass carafe on a hot plate is the wrong design. See [Moccamaster KBT vs KBGV Select](/vs/moccamaster-kbt-vs-moccamaster-kbgv-select/) for the thermal version, and [thermal vs glass carafe](/guides/thermal-vs-glass-carafe/) for the general trade-off.

If the price is too much, the [OXO Brew 8-Cup](/reviews/oxo-brew-8-cup/) is on the SCA's current certified list. If you want a timer to brew before you wake up, look at the programmable [Ninja CE251](/reviews/ninja-ce251/). Both are ranked in the [best coffee makers](/best/coffee-makers/) hub.
