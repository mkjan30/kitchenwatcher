The Ninja AF141 (Air Fryer Pro 4-in-1) is a sensible pick if you cook for one to three people and want a simple, well-reviewed basket air fryer. Owners rate it very highly: 4.9 of 5 across 520 Best Buy reviews. The catch is size. Consumer Reports measured 3.8 usable quarts against the 5-quart label. It lists at $129.99 but was out of stock at SharkNinja on Sep 27, 2026.

## What independent tests found

Consumer Reports is the only independent lab I could find with a published AF141 number outside a paywall, and it's the one that matters most for this model: 5 quarts claimed, 3.8 quarts measured ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/air-fryers/ninja-pro-af141/m413991/)). That's about three-quarters of the label. CR's scores for controls, convenience, cleaning and noise are behind its membership wall, and RTINGS' review is paywalled too, so I can't tell you how it rated on those.

Ninja's "4 lbs of fries" is a fill-weight claim for the drawer, not what fits in one even layer. Air fryers brown best in a single layer, so plan on the measured 3.8 quarts, not the 5 on the box, when you decide whether it's big enough.

What nobody with a readable page has published for the AF141: a temperature-accuracy run, preheat time, a decibel reading, or exterior surface temperature. The COSORI TurboBlaze has those from TechGearLab; the AF141 doesn't, so any comparison on accuracy is one-sided. It's covered as far as the evidence goes in [TurboBlaze vs Ninja AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/).

## What owners report

The Best Buy review pool is large and unusually one-sided: 466 of 520 reviews are five stars, only five are one or two stars, and 98% say they'd recommend it, per Best Buy's review page. Ease of use comes up in 195 reviews, overall performance in 160.

- **Noise.** The most common complaint, raised in 13 Best Buy reviews. Kitchenwink also lists it as a drawback, along with a learning curve on cook times, but gives no numbers ([Kitchenwink](https://kitchenwink.com/ninja-air-fryer-pro-af141-review/)).
- **Capacity for families.** Kitchenwink notes some buyers find it too small for a larger household, which fits CR's 3.8-quart measurement.
- **Design trade-offs.** Preheat Club, which says openly that it hasn't lab-tested the models it covers, lists no window, a single basket and a PTFE nonstick basket as the compromises ([Preheat Club](https://preheatclub.com/reviews/ninja-af141)). It also works out running cost at roughly 10 cents per 20-minute cook at a $0.17/kWh electricity rate.

I didn't find a readable source for two complaints sometimes attributed to this model, heating staying on with the basket out and a misbehaving timer, so they're not listed as problems here.

## Cleaning: the dishwasher question

The listings disagree. SharkNinja's own page says the nonstick basket and crisper plate are dishwasher safe, and Consumer Reports' spec list agrees. Abt's listing calls the coating ceramic and says the parts are hand-wash only. I'd go with the manufacturer, but check the care section of the manual that comes in the box before you load the dishwasher, since coated baskets last longer washed by hand either way.

## Recalls

I found no CPSC recall naming the AF141 as of Sep 27, 2026. SharkNinja's most recent large recall, announced May 1, 2025, covers about 1.85 million Foodi OP300-series pressure cookers whose lid could be opened during pressure cooking; the listed models are all OP-series, not AF-series air fryers ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/SharkNinja-Recalls-1-8-Million-Foodi-Multi-Function-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard-Serious-Burn-Injuries-Reported)).

## Price and availability

On Sep 27, 2026, SharkNinja listed the AF141 at $129.99 but showed it out of stock with a back-in-stock sign-up, the same as the day before. Abt listed it at $129.99 the same day. Consumer Reports' page shows a starting price of $119.00 across retailers, undated. At $129.99 it costs about $40 more than the 6-quart COSORI TurboBlaze was selling for on the same date, which is hard to justify for a smaller basket unless you specifically want a Ninja.

## Who should buy it, who should skip it

**Buy it** if you cook for one to three, want four functions and nothing more, and value a big, consistent pool of happy owners over lab data. Its footprint also suits smaller counters.

**Skip it** if you regularly cook for four or more (3.8 usable quarts is tight), if you want a 450°F setting for wings, or if fan noise bothers you. And skip paying list price: when it's out at SharkNinja, check stock and price elsewhere first.
