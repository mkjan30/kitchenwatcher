The Ninja Professional Plus (BN601) is a 9-cup food processor that independent reviewers rate well for slicing and shredding. TechGearLab scored it 64 of 100, tied with the Hamilton Beach 10-Cup and listed 6th of 8. It called the Ninja "A Compact Bargain Option." CNET picked it as best for features, and Food Network named it Best Value. Its weak points are fine purées, noise, a pulse that is slow to stop and a stacked blade that is awkward to wash by hand.

This Ninja Professional Plus food processor review is research-based. Nobody here has used the machine; every number below is credited to the reviewer or manual it came from, and [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method. For the other five models we cover, see the [best food processors](/best/food-processors/).

## What independent reviewers found in chopping, slicing, purée and dough

### Chopping and the Chop program

CNET got pico de gallo in "scarcely two hits" of the pulse button. It also says the BN601 is the only model in its test with a separate Chop button, which pulses three times. TechGearLab scored chopping 6 overall: onion and carrot 7 each, which it called impressive, and nuts 5. It docked the pulse for being slow to spin down. Ninja's BN600C guide says to run Chop once for a large chop, twice for small and three times to mince.

### Slicing and shredding

TechGearLab scored both slicing and shredding 7, with a potato slice of 9, zucchini 8 and a carrot shred of 8. Tomato (5) and cheese (6) were weaker. The disc is reversible, slicer on one side and shredder on the other, per Ninja's BN600C guide (CNET lists it as a double-sided disc). TechGearLab notes the slicing thickness is fixed and there is one medium shred size.

### Purée: the reviewers disagree

TechGearLab scored purée 7 and called it excellent. Food Network says it "doesn't puree as finely" as its other picks, and its pesto came out coarser. [BestReviews](https://bestreviews.com/articles/kitchen/food-processors/kitchen-ninja-professional-plus-kitchen-system-with-autoiq-review) (2022) called its pesto nicely puréed but said it was not as dependable as a blender for finely puréed foods. The evidence is split, so don't buy it expecting silky results.

### Dough

TechGearLab scored pizza dough 6 and pie crust 8, and said the motor sounded like it was working pretty hard on the pizza dough. BestReviews said the motor seemed to be working especially hard on its dough setting. Ninja's manual says never to run HIGH with the dough blade. More on what a processor can handle is in [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/).

## Ninja food processor problems owners and reviewers report

These are listed in order of how much evidence backs them. No owner-review dataset was opened for this page, so how often owners hit each one is not stated.

1. **Noise.** CNET called it "perhaps the loudest" model in its test, and TechGearLab measured 80 dB at 3 ft. BestReviews called it quieter than some, so the sources do not fully agree.
2. **Pulse lag.** TechGearLab says it is slow to spin down after you let go of the pulse. BestReviews says the pulse felt unresponsive and over-processed food.
3. **Hand-washing the blade.** TechGearLab scored cleaning 5 and found the dual-blade assembly hard to clean by hand. Food Network says it is easy to clean by hand, so the reviewers disagree here too.
4. **Flashing lights, won't start.** Per the BN600C guide's troubleshooting table, flashing control-panel lights mean the bowl and lid are not properly installed. See [Ninja food processor blinking lights](/guides/food-processor-wont-turn-on/) and [how the safety interlock works](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/).
5. **Motor cuts out.** The guide describes a thermal switch: unplug, let it cool about 15 minutes, then clear any jam. It names exceeding the maximum capacity as "the most typical cause" of overload. The [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/) guide covers this across brands.
6. **Base slides.** The guide says the suction feet stick only to smooth surfaces and will not stick to some surfaces such as wood, tile and non-polished finishes.

## Is the Ninja Professional Plus 850 or 1,000 watts?

It is rated 850 watts. SharkNinja's own parts store lists the BN601 motor base (part XMBBN601) as an "850-watt motor base," and Ninja's BN600C owner's guide lists "Power: 850 Watts." TechGearLab also lists 850 W.

CNET and Food Network print 1,000 W, and BestReviews calls it a "1000-peak-watt" motor. SharkNinja uses the same "1000-peak-watt" wording on its BZ601 page. None of these sources says how the peak figure is measured; the BN600C guide and the parts store both give 850 W.

Wattage is a rough guide at best. In TechGearLab's test, the 450 W Hamilton Beach made nut butter in 10 minutes and the Ninja did it in 7, so nearly double the rated power bought about three minutes. Head-to-head details are in [Hamilton Beach 70730 vs Ninja BN601](/vs/hamilton-beach-70730-vs-ninja-bn601/).

## Ninja BN601 vs BN600: shared parts, and where the BZ601 fits

The BN600 and BN601 share parts. SharkNinja's parts store lists its "BN600 Motor Base" (XMBBN600) as compatible with the BN601, BN601NP and BN601WBKT. Its BN601 motor base is listed for the BN601, BN602, FP601A and FP601CO. TechGearLab reviews them as one model, "BN600/BN601."

The BN600C guide marks its Puree program "(Not available on all models)," so the documented difference may be the set of presets and the included accessories. No Ninja page we could read spells the difference out.

## What Ninja's manual says before you use it

Quoted from the [Ninja BN600C owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf), the Canadian sibling; the US BN601 guide could not be read.

- The blade assembly is "NOT locked in place." Grasp it only by the top of the shaft, and remove it before emptying the bowl.
- Do not fill past the 9-cup line.
- Do not blend hot liquids: pressure can build and steam can burn.
- Do not load ice cubes for crushing.
- Do not process dry ingredients or grind with the chopping blade.
- Use only DISC or LOW with the slicing and shredding disc.
- Never try to defeat the interlock.
- Parts go on the top rack of the dishwasher only. Never submerge or spray the base.

For hot soup and ice, see [food processor vs blender](/guides/food-processor-vs-blender/). ## Has the Ninja food processor been recalled?

Not as of Oct 9, 2026. We searched the CPSC recall database ([saferproducts.gov API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=SharkNinja)) for recall titles containing "SharkNinja" and "Ninja" and found no food processor recall. The two SharkNinja recalls it returns are 25-247 (Foodi pressure cookers, 2025-05-01) and 16-034 (BL660 blenders, 2015-11-12); neither is a food processor. To check any model, see the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Who the BN601 suits, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you mostly slice and shred and want a 9-cup machine. CNET says it suits people who can't justify a larger model but need more than the micro models offer.

**Skip it** if you need more than 9 cups, silky purées or a quiet machine.
