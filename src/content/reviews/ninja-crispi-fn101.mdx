## The glass: how hot it gets

TechGearLab measured a 280°F maximum on the Crispi's glass exterior and called it an unnecessary burn hazard for a countertop appliance ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-crispi)). For comparison, the same site measured 130°F on the COSORI TurboBlaze's exterior ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze)).

Ninja's own owner's guide agrees on the risk. It says the PowerPod, containers and crisper plates become extremely hot during use and tells you not to touch the glass, only the handles, with oven mitts.

## The glass: thermal shock and cracking

Nonstick coating wears out slowly. Glass tends to fail in one go. Ninja's FN100 Series owner's guide says sudden temperature changes can break the containers, and lists what not to do:

- Don't add cold food or liquid to hot glass, or hot food to cold glass.
- Don't set hot glass on a cool or wet surface; let it cool before it goes in the sink, fridge or freezer.
- Don't use the containers in an oven, broiler or on a stovetop.
- Stop using any container that has been dropped, chipped, cracked or deeply scratched.

In July 2025 a TikTok user reported her Crispi's glass shattering across the counter on its second use, saying she had followed the manual ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/ninja-crispi-explodes/)). That is one report, not a pattern, and I found no CPSC recall for the Crispi as of Sep 26, 2026.

The practical cost of those rules is time. You can't rinse the container right after dinner or move leftovers straight to the fridge in it. {/* MEASURE: glass condition log — every hot-glass handoff done per the manual, cool time before sink or fridge, and any chip, scratch or crack found under raking light */} {/* MEASURE: photo — glass inspected under raking light after testing */}
