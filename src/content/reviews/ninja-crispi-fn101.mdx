The Ninja Crispi (FN101) is a 1,500 W air fryer that cooks in glass, and it suits people who want to cook, store and reheat in the same container. The deciding evidence is the glass itself: TechGearLab measured the outside at 280°F, and Ninja's own guide warns sudden temperature changes can break it. It was $179.99 on Sep 27, 2026, in stock at Target but not at SharkNinja.

This page compiles lab results, Ninja's owner's guide and owner reports; the method is on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## The glass: how hot it gets

TechGearLab measured a 280°F maximum on the Crispi's glass exterior and flags it as a safety concern for households with children ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-crispi)). For scale, the same site recorded 140°F on the outside of the COSORI TurboBlaze and noted even that just meets the burn threshold ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze)). A metal basket fryer hides its hot parts inside a plastic shell; the Crispi's container is the outside wall.

Ninja's FN100 Series owner's guide agrees on the risk. It says the PowerPod, the glass containers and the crisper plates become extremely hot during use, and tells you to lift the containers only by their handles with oven mitts, never by the glass. It also says children should not use the appliance and need close supervision near it.

Nobody I could find has published how long the glass takes to cool to a safe handling temperature, or how hot the handles get. That's the missing number on this product.

## The glass: thermal shock and cracking

Coated baskets wear out slowly. Glass tends to fail in one go. The owner's guide says sudden temperature changes can break glassware, and sets these rules:

- Don't add cool or cold food or liquid to hot glass, or hot food or liquid to cold glass.
- Don't let hot glass touch cool or wet surfaces; let it cool before it goes in the sink, the dishwasher, the fridge or the freezer.
- Don't use the containers in an oven, under a broiler or on a stovetop, or with any heat source except the Crispi.
- Stop using a container that has been dropped, chipped, cracked or deeply scratched, because it could break or shatter.

There's a tension here worth knowing about. SharkNinja's product page lists the system as freezer-to-air fryer safe, and the guide says the containers are freezer safe. But the guide doesn't say either way whether you can put a container straight from the freezer under the PowerPod, only that you shouldn't add cold food to hot glass. Commenters on one widely shared report said Ninja markets the Crispi as going from fridge to air fryer in one bowl ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/ninja-crispi-explodes/)). If you use it that way, you're relying on the marketing, not the manual.

That report: in July 2025, a TikTok user said her Crispi's glass container shattered on its second use and that she had followed the manual; The Mary Sue contacted SharkNinja and published no reply ([The Mary Sue](https://www.themarysue.com/ninja-crispi-explodes/)). A law firm's page separately describes a December 2025 SaferProducts.gov report of an FN101 overheating and tripping a GFCI outlet and the home's main breaker ([Farzan Law](https://farzanlaw.com/ninja-fn101gnbl-crispi-air-fryer-overheating-tripping-breakers-and-posing-fire-risk/)). {/* VERIFY: SaferProducts.gov report 20251218-F42EC-2147325429 — seen only via the law firm page, not on SaferProducts.gov */} These are individual reports, not an established failure rate. I found no CPSC recall for the Crispi as of Sep 27, 2026.

The practical cost of the rules is time. You can't rinse the container right after dinner or put leftovers straight into the fridge in it; the guide says to let the glass cool gradually to room temperature first.

## What independent tests found on cooking

The two reviews with the most detail disagree. Food Network's reviewer liked it: chicken breast done in 16 minutes against the guide's 20–25, diced zucchini in 7, muffins in 10, and better frozen fries once switched to Max Crisp ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/ninja-crispi-glass-air-fryer-review)). Its complaints were uneven Recrisp results, which can overcook vegetables, and limited room for baking.

TechGearLab rated it 66 of 100, eighth of ten models. It found the air fry mode averaged about 18°F below its set point, crisping on fries and wings inconsistent, and salmon excellent at 16 minutes ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-crispi)). Its lowest scores were ease of use (4.2 of 10) and temperature accuracy (3.0); its highest were cleaning (9.0) and speed (8.9). There's no temperature dial at all: you pick a mode and a time.

## What owners report

On Target, 1,706 reviews average 4.49 of 5, and 77% would recommend it, per Target's product page. That's solid, though value (3.9 of 5) and quality (4.1) are its weakest category ratings there. Target's review summary lists burning or uneven cooking, the lack of temperature control, limited crisping compared with basket fryers, and durability concerns after one to two years as the recurring complaints. {/* VERIFY: Target complaint themes incl. 1–2 year durability — from Target's review summary, individual reviews not read */} Owners who like it most often mention the glass itself and easy cleanup.

## Cleaning

The glass containers, crisper plates and lids go in the dishwasher; the PowerPod never goes in water or the dishwasher, per the guide. That's why TechGearLab gave it its best sub-score for cleaning. The one catch is the cool-down wait before the glass can go in the sink.

## Price and availability

$179.99 at both SharkNinja and Target on Sep 27, 2026; SharkNinja showed it out of stock with a notify-me form, Target showed it in stock. That's about double the COSORI TurboBlaze's price on the same day.

## Who should buy it, who should skip it

**Buy it** if you want to avoid coated baskets, like cooking and storing in the same container, and mostly cook salmon, chicken or reheats where exact temperature matters less.

**Skip it** if you have small children around the counter, since the glass itself reaches 280°F; if you want to set a temperature; if you tend to rinse cookware straight off the heat; or if you want the crispest fries and wings for the money.
