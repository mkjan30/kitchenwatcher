The Ninja DZ201 is worth it if you want to cook a main and a side at once and can find it well below the newer DZ901's $249.99. Its two 4-quart baskets are popular with owners, who rate it 4.8 of 5 at Best Buy. But TechGearLab measured it running cool, the baskets don't always cook alike, and Ninja's site now redirects the DZ201 to the DZ901.

## Is the DZ201 being discontinued?

It looks that way. As of Sep 27, 2026, both Ninja's old DZ201 product link and its current SharkNinja product link redirect to the DZ901 page. The DZ901 is a 10-quart model with two 5-quart baskets, listed at $249.99, and its page doesn't mention the DZ201. I found no official discontinuation notice.

Retailers still sell it. Target listed the DZ201 in stock at $199.99 on Sep 27, 2026, and Consumer Reports showed it from $187.99 across 11 retailers the same day ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/air-fryers/ninja-foodi-2-basket-dz201/m401887/)). Food Network's list, updated Aug 14, 2026, showed $160 against a regular $200 ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-air-fryer)). My read: Ninja has stopped selling it direct, and retail stock will thin out.

## What independent tests found

**Heat runs cool.** TechGearLab measured an average of 386.4°F at a 400°F setting ([TechGearLab best air fryers](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer)) and scored it 6.7 of 10 for temperature accuracy ([TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-foodi-8-quart-2-basket)). It says the baskets stayed within a few degrees of each other even when set to different temperatures.

**The two baskets don't always cook alike.** TechGearLab found the left basket cooked some foods faster than the right. That matches the most common complaint in Target's owner reviews, where the right basket cooks less consistently on matching settings. Nobody has published a figure for the gap, so I can't tell you how many minutes or degrees it is.

**Speed and results.** TechGearLab measured a 3-minute preheat and scored cooking speed 8.6 of 10 and cooking quality 7.7. It ranked the DZ201 fifth of ten with a score of 73, and named it a Top Pick for meal prepping. Food Network named it Best Dual-Basket and credits Smart Finish with getting both foods done together.

**Usability gaps.** TechGearLab lists no auto-pause when a basket is pulled, no preheat alert, and large temperature steps that make recipes hard to follow exactly.

**Capacity is smaller than the label.** Consumer Reports measured 6 quarts of usable capacity against the 8 quarts claimed. Food Network's main con is that each basket is only 4 quarts. TechGearLab measured 98.4 square inches of cooking area across both baskets, against 87.8 in the single-basket Instant Vortex Plus 6 quart.

## What owners report

- **Mostly happy.** Best Buy owners give it 4.8 of 5 across 4,364 reviews, and 97% would recommend it.
- **Smell, noise and price** are Best Buy's top complaints, with 23, 20 and 14 mentions.
- **Speed: owners disagree.** Best Buy's review summary says owners praise fast cooking times. Kitchen Deets calls slowness the biggest owner complaint, even with preheating ([Kitchen Deets](https://kitchendeets.com/ninja-dz201-review/)).
- **Durability.** Some Target reviewers raise concerns after extended use, without a consistent failure pattern.

## DZ201 or DZ901?

Going by Ninja's DZ901 page, the DZ901 has two 5-quart baskets instead of two 4-quart ones and swaps air broil for Max Crisp. It's 16.93 by 14.57 by 12.6 inches and weighs 19.4 lb, at $249.99 on Sep 27, 2026. I found no independent measurements of the DZ901 yet, so the only proven advantage is basket size. The DZ201 makes sense while it sells for about $50 or more below the DZ901.

## Who should buy it, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you regularly cook two foods at different settings for three or four people and find it near $160 to $190.

**Skip it** if one basket is enough. The [Instant Vortex Plus 6 quart](/reviews/instant-vortex-plus-6qt/) measured much closer to its set temperature, and the [Ninja AF101](/reviews/ninja-af101/) is smaller for one or two people. Skip it too if precise temperatures matter to your recipes, given the 386.4°F reading and coarse steps.
