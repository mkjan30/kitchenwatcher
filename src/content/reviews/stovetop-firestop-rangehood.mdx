The StoveTop FireStop Rangehood is a pair of powder canisters that hang by magnet under a range hood and, per the maker, burst open when flames from a stovetop fire touch a fuse underneath. It's a cheap extra layer for an electric stove, but it isn't UL-listed, its own manual says it may not suppress all stovetop fires, and a 2016 batch was [recalled by the CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires).

It's one of three devices in our [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/), and mounting is covered step by step in [how to install StoveTop FireStop](/guides/stovetop-firestop-installation/). This page draws on the maker's documents, the CPSC, a published review and local news; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## What the maker's own manual warns

The Rangehood user manual (675-3-117 Rev B, dated February 2017) is more candid than the product page. It says the product:

- may not suppress all stovetop fires, and reignition is possible;
- is not recommended for gas stoves, because the powder may put out the burner flame;
- is designed only for indoor residential stovetop grease fires using no more than 3/8 inch of oil before food is added;
- may briefly flare or splash grease when the powder hits the fire, which the manual calls normal;
- makes a loud pop when it activates, and should be discarded after activation or if dropped or damaged.

The manual also tells you to turn the burner off right after activation, if it's safe to do so.

## Not UL-listed

StoveTop FireStop's FAQ says it does not have UL certification yet. The company says no single UL standard covers a product like this and that it conforms to elements of UL 300A, 199, 711 and 1254. That is the maker's description of its own product, not a listing by UL, and I couldn't find any independent certification. {/* VERIFY: UL status per maker FAQ, read Sep 27, 2026 */}

## What independent sources found

No lab I could find has published measured activation times, powder coverage or knock-down results. What exists:

- **Reviewed.com** tried one in its parking lot under the eye of a former fire chief, without setting a real fire. It noted the pop is slightly louder than a firecracker and each pod holds a surprising amount of powder, and listed single use and the chance of putting out a gas burner flame as cons ([Reviewed, updated May 2022](https://www.reviewed.com/home-outdoors/best-right-now/the-best-fire-extinguishers-for-homeowners)).
- **Maple Grove Fire Department (Minnesota)** ran an on-camera demonstration reported by local community station [CCX Media on April 15, 2024](https://ccxmedia.org/cities/maple-grove-fire-department-demonstrates-stovetop-firestops-that-could-save-lives-and-property-damage/). An investigator heated a small pot of oil on a propane burner until it ignited; when the flames reached the canister mounted above, it released its powder, and the report says the fire went out. It was a single demonstration set up to show the product, not a measured or repeated test.
- **Oshkosh Fire Department (Wisconsin)** gave canisters free to food-pantry visitors aged 60 and older, [WTAQ reported on April 2, 2024](https://wtaq.com/2024/04/02/stovetop-firestops-distributed-in-oshkosh-to-help-reduce-cooking-fires/). The department's spokesman also told residents to keep working smoke alarms and to call the fire department after any activation.

Those are real signs of confidence from fire services, but they aren't the same as a certification or a published test.

## The 2019 recall

On December 6, 2019, WilliamsRDM recalled about 9,300 pairs of StoveTop FireStop Rangehood (675-3D) and Microhood (677-2) suppressors, CPSC recall 20-037. Per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires), the suppressors can fail to activate. The company knew of two that failed during product demonstrations; no injuries were reported.

- **Affected:** units made November 7 to 13, 2016, sold in November 2016 through HD Supply, Interline, Louisville Fire and Safety, Central Wholesalers and HomeDepot.com for about $56 (Rangehood).
- **How to check a Rangehood:** silver metal with a red label and date code **4516** stamped under the magnet assembly, per the CPSC.
- **Remedy:** free replacement; the CPSC lists the company at 888-616-7976.

Those units are also well past the six-year life the maker gives, so they should be replaced regardless.

## Magnets vs stainless hoods

The canisters hold on with magnets, and many stainless range hoods aren't magnetic. The maker's FAQ says that if the magnets won't stick but your hood is 27 to 35 inches above the stove, order its alternative mounting kit; the kit page lists it at $2.75 (Sep 27, 2026) and says it fits the Rangehood and Plus models. A fridge magnet tells you which case you're in. For microwaves or cabinets 15 to 27 inches above the cooktop, the maker's 2017 FAQ points to a different model, the Microhood. The [installation guide](/guides/stovetop-firestop-installation/) walks through both mounting methods.

## What owners report

I couldn't load Amazon's or Home Depot's review pages, and found no forum threads, so I can't describe a pattern of owner complaints. The known issues are the recall and the stainless-hood magnet problem, both documented above by the CPSC and the maker.

## It doesn't replace the basics

NFPA found unattended cooking was the leading factor in U.S. home cooking fires and casualties from 2017 to 2021 ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). The U.S. Fire Administration's advice is to stand by your pan and turn the burner off if you leave the kitchen ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA's [tip sheet](https://sanfordfl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/CookingSafety.pdf) says to smother a small pan fire with a lid and turn off the burner, and if you have any doubt, get out and call 9-1-1 from outside. A canister is an extra layer on top of that, not a substitute, and the maker says it supplements a handheld extinguisher. The [Kidde FA110G](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/) is our pick for that.

## Who should buy it, who should skip it

Consider it if you have an electric stove under a range hood 27 to 35 inches up, and you want something that can react to a pan fire when nobody is standing there, accepting that it isn't UL-listed and that the maker itself doesn't promise it will put out every fire.

Skip it for a gas stove (the maker doesn't recommend it), for deep frying beyond 3/8 inch of oil, or if you want a certified product. If the bigger risk in your home is forgetting the stove is on, the [iGuard stove shut-off](/reviews/iguard-electric-stove/) is designed to cut power before a fire starts.
