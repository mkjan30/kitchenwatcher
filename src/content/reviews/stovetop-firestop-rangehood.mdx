The StoveTop FireStop Rangehood is a pair of powder-filled canisters that hang by magnets under a range hood and are designed to burst open when flames from a stovetop fire touch a fuse on the bottom. {/* MEASURE: verdict — one sentence, whether it activated and what it did to the fire in our outdoor tests */} It is not UL-listed, and a batch made in November 2016 was [recalled by the CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires).

It's one of three devices in our [kitchen safety devices guide](/best/kitchen-safety-devices/). I cover mounting step by step in [how to install StoveTop FireStop](/guides/stovetop-firestop-installation/).

## Specs and price

All of this is manufacturer spec, from StoveTop FireStop's product page and FAQ:

- Model 675-3D, sold as a pair. The maker says one pair protects a typical four-burner stove.
- Each canister is 3.44 x 3.44 x 3 in; the listing gives 2 lb shipping weight.
- Activates only on direct, sustained flame contact with the fuse, per the FAQ, not from normal cooking heat, steam or smoke.
- Mounts by magnet to the underside of a hood 27 to 35 in above the cooking surface, centered between the front and back burners on each side.
- Six-year effective life from manufacture, with a replace-by date on the label.
- The powder agent is not named on the product page.

The maker listed it at $56.95 per pair on September 26, 2026. {/* MEASURE: price — about $X at [retailer] on publish date */}

## Mounting height and hood type

The Rangehood is designed for hoods 27 to 35 in above the cooking surface, per the maker's FAQ. For a microwave or cabinet 15 to 27 in above the cooktop, the maker points you to a different model, the Microhood. {/* MEASURE: mounting height on our test kitchens' hoods, inches */}

## Magnets vs stainless hoods

The canisters hold on with magnets. The maker's FAQ says if the magnets won't stick but your hood is in the 27 to 35 in range, order its alternative mounting kit: adhesive discs and metal washers that give the magnet something to grab. The maker says it built the kit because many hoods are stainless steel and won't hold a magnet, so test yours with a fridge magnet before you buy. {/* MEASURE: pull-off force on painted steel vs stainless vs stainless with adhesive washer, lb */} {/* MEASURE: photo — hero: pair mounted under a range hood, burners visible */}

## Not UL-listed

StoveTop FireStop's own FAQ says it does not have UL certification yet. The company says the product doesn't fit an existing UL standard and that it conforms to elements of UL 300A, 199, 711 and 1254. That is the maker's claim, not a listing. I'm reporting it so you can weigh it; our tests won't substitute for a certification.

## The 2019 recall

On December 6, 2019, WilliamsRDM recalled about 9,300 pairs of StoveTop FireStop Rangehood (675-3D) and Microhood (677-2) suppressors (CPSC recall 20-037). Per the [CPSC notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2020/WilliamsRDM-Recalls-Cooktop-Fire-Suppressors-Due-to-Risk-of-Failure-to-Activate-and-Suppress-Fires), the sealed openings that let the powder out could fail to open. The company knew of two units that failed to operate during a product demonstration; no injuries were reported.

- **Affected:** units made November 7 to 13, 2016, sold in November 2016 for about $56 (Rangehood).
- **How to check a Rangehood:** the CPSC says recalled units have date code **4516** stamped into the top of the can, under the magnet assembly.
- **Remedy:** replacement, per the CPSC.

Those units are also past the six-year life the maker gives, so they should be replaced either way. {/* MEASURE: photo — date code under the magnet assembly and the replace-by label */}

## It doesn't replace the basics

The U.S. Fire Administration's advice is to stay with your pan, and if it catches fire, cover it with a lid and turn off the burner ([USFA](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/cooking/)). NFPA found unattended cooking was the leading factor in home cooking fires and deaths ([NFPA, Home Cooking Fires, 2023](https://content.nfpa.org/-/media/Project/Storefront/Catalog/Files/Research/NFPA-Research/US-Fire-Problem/Fire-causes/oscooking.pdf)). A canister over the stove is an extra layer on top of that. Keep a working extinguisher by the exit too; I'm testing the [Kidde FA110G](/reviews/kidde-fa110g/).
