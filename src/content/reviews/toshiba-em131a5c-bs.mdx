This Toshiba EM131A5C-BS review comes down to one line: it is a quiet, well-rated budget countertop microwave, not a powerhouse. Tom's Guide calls it "Best overall" and RTINGS calls it the best cheap countertop model. It is rated 1,100 W output, but RTINGS measured a 737 W heating rate.

RTINGS measured 45.8 dBA at full power, and it has a steam sensor. The weak point is a membrane panel that needs seven presses for 2:30 at 50% power. The CPSC recall database lists no recall for it.

Nobody at Kitchen Watcher used this microwave; everything here comes from the manual and independent testers, as explained in [how we research](/how-we-research/). It is one of six models ranked in our [best microwaves](/best/microwaves/) guide.

## Toshiba EM131A5C wattage: 1,550, 1,100, 1,000 or 737?

Four wattage figures circulate for this model, and they measure different things:

- **1,550 W** is the rated input, the power it draws from the wall, per the manual.
- **1,100 W** is the rated output, the cooking power Toshiba claims, per the manual. RTINGS says the appliance label also lists 1,100 W.
- **1,000 W** appears in Tom's Guide's text ("With 1000 watts"), while its spec box prints the 1,550 W input as "Power". Neither matches the manual's output rating.
- **737 W** is RTINGS's tested heating rate at 100% power. The RTINGS pages I read don't explain the gap between it and the 1,100 W rating for this model, and I won't guess.

A fifth figure exists too: RTINGS notes the manufacturer's website lists it as a 900-watt or 100-watt microwave, which RTINGS says "appears to be a mistake".

RTINGS still gave it a Power score of 9.4, and [its best-countertop list](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/countertop) says it "cooks food very fast". For what input, output and measured watts mean for any microwave, see [what wattage microwave do I need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Does this Toshiba microwave have a sensor? What the sensor menu covers

Per the manual, the Sensor Menu has six programs: Sensor Reheat, Frozen Pizza, Frozen Entree, Potato, Rice and Veggie. RTINGS describes the sensor as one that "detects steam". Tom's Guide contradicts itself, saying "no sensors" in one place and praising "its humidity sensor" in another. The manual settles it: there is a sensor.

The manual's sensor rules:

- Don't open the door before the sensor-detected time appears on the display.
- Covers need a vent hole so steam can escape.
- Avoid bread and dry foods such as biscuits or cake on Sensor Reheat.
- For best results, food must weigh more than 4 oz, the room must be no warmer than 95 °F, and the cavity, glass tray and utensils must be dry.

Other functions listed in the manual:

- Soften/Melt, Custom Cook and Popcorn programs
- 1 lb Defrost, plus Custom Defrost by weight from 0.1 to 6.0 lb, which beeps halfway so you can turn the food
- One Touch Start pads 1 to 6, each running 1 to 6 minutes at 100%
- START/ADD 30 SEC
- A Favorite button that stores up to three programs of up to two cooking stages each
- Kitchen timer and a lock (hold the pad for 3 seconds; a long beep confirms)

RTINGS also lists add-time and a lock, and notes the Favorite button is programmable.

## How evenly does it heat, and how loud is it?

RTINGS's marshmallow test found 0% underheated area both on a dinner plate and across the turntable. In its mashed-potato test at 100% power, the coldest spot read 68 °F and the spread between coldest and hottest was 132 °F. At 50% the minimum was again 68 °F with a 106 °F spread.

RTINGS's own position, on its comparison page, is that "you won't notice a difference in performance between microwaves when it comes to heating evenness". It says evenness depends on the food, the amount and where the dish sits.

Tom's Guide lists "Very even cooking performance" as a pro. But the guide's author says they "prioritized trusted brands with years of user testimonial", so that claim may not come from a test of this unit. Evidence on evenness is thin, and the two sources approach it differently.

Noise is better documented: RTINGS measured 45.8 dBA at full power and calls it "a very quiet microwave". Both RTINGS and Tom's Guide say it has a dedicated mute button, though the text of Toshiba's manual doesn't name a mute or sound-off function.

## Complaints: the control panel, capacity and missing modes

1. **The control panel.** RTINGS says the membrane touch panel is "less responsive than a glass touch panel, physical buttons, or dials". Setting 3:00 at full power takes one action, but 2:30 at 50% takes seven.
2. **Less room than the box says.** RTINGS measured 1.06 cu ft inside, not 1.2 cu ft. RTINGS still says it fits most dinner plates, and the 12.4-inch turntable matches the manual.
3. **No extra cooking modes.** Tom's Guide's only listed con is that it "Lacks the versatility of a combi microwave". RTINGS lists no convection and no inverter.
4. **Owner reviews.** I didn't open any retailer's review pages for this model, so I'm not quoting star ratings or complaint counts.

I found no independent long-term durability data for this model.

## Is the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS safe? The self-start reports and recalls

[Consumer Reports' safety alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) by Althea Chang-Cook, published June 25, 2026, counts 28 incidents reported on SaferProducts.gov between January 2023 and April 2026 of Toshiba-branded microwaves auto-starting or adding cooking time on their own. All but four involved the ML2-EC10SA air fryer-microwave combo. CR writes that models EM131A5C and EM925A5A-SS "were also each cited in one incident". CR says it "has not tested these models and has not experienced a similar problem with the Toshiba microwaves we have tested".

CR says Toshiba-brand microwaves are made by Midea. Midea's statement to CR named only the ML2-EC10SA: it said a sticking power button can trigger the 30-second add-time function and that it "found no evidence that this issue poses a safety risk". It told owners of that model to call 866-646-4332 for evaluation and free service. CR has asked the CPSC to investigate.

This is not a recall. A [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Toshiba) search for Toshiba returned no microwave recall on Nov 27, 2026, only laptops, laptop batteries and adapters, DVD-player adapters, a DVD home theater system and a TV. CR advises monitoring the microwave while it runs, not storing items inside and unplugging it after use; report problems through SaferProducts.gov.

If materials inside ignite, the manual says to keep the door closed, turn the oven off and unplug it, and shut off power at the fuse or circuit breaker panel. The manual says it should be serviced only by qualified service personnel; Midea customer assistance is 800-842-1289, per the manual.
