The Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy NS-ZCC10 is a top pick at three independent testers: America's Test Kitchen's Winner, Reviewed's Best Overall and Engadget's splurge pick. You pay for that in time and money. Zojirushi's manual lists 48 to 55 minutes for white rice, and ATK's page listed it at $239 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026.

This Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy review covers what those testers found, what goes wrong with the NS-ZCC10, and whether it is worth the price for you. For how it ranks against five other models, see [our rice cooker rankings](/best/rice-cookers/).

## Who the NS-ZCC10 suits, and who should skip it

**Buy it** if you eat rice several times a week, can start it 50 minutes before dinner (or use the timer), and want consistent results across rice types. Reviewed says the fuzzy logic adjusts for a mismeasured water-to-rice ratio, but none of the tests I read checked that directly.

**Skip it** if you need rice in under 30 minutes, since even Quick takes 36 to 42 minutes per the manual. Skip it if your budget is closer to $140: ATK's page listed the [Cuckoo CR-0655F](/reviews/cuckoo-cr-0655f/) at $140 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026. ATK got great long-grain white rice from it, though its short-grain white and brown rice were a little wet at Cuckoo's recommended ratios. Skip the 5.5-cup size if you cook more than 5.5 cups of raw rice at once; ATK says the 10-cup version (the NS-ZCC18 in Zojirushi's manual) performed as well as its winner.

## What ATK, Reviewed and Engadget found

### America's Test Kitchen: Winner, Highly Recommended

[ATK's October 2025 review](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers) found it "consistently produced excellent rice in both small and large batches." In its 12-hour keep-warm test, the NS-ZCC10 held rice at a food-safe temperature. ATK ran that test with medium-grain brown rice, while Zojirushi's manual says to keep only white rice warm.

ATK liked the white water lines and the plastic handles on both sides of the pan. Its one con: long-grain white rice cooks best on Mixed, not White, which ATK says wasn't obvious until it checked the manual.

### Reviewed: Best Overall, with a control-panel complaint

[Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-rice-cookers) named it Best Overall for consistent, flavorful batches of brown, short-grain white and jasmine rice. It was the only cooker in that test with side handles on the inner pan. The con: the "control panel isn't intuitive," and testers had to hold the cooker still with one hand and press hard to get a response.

### Engadget: the splurge pick for patient cooks

[Engadget's roundup](https://www.engadget.com/home/kitchen-tech/best-rice-cooker-120015478.html) timed white rice at 48 minutes and brown at just under two hours. Its tester said Quick tasted almost identical to the regular batch but cut only about ten minutes. Its listed cons are long cook times and price.

**Where they agree:** rice quality. **Where they disagree:** Engadget lists "easy to use and clean" as a pro, while Reviewed calls the control panel unintuitive. **Where evidence is thin:** none of these sources has long-term durability or failure-rate data.

## How long the Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy takes, and why

White rice takes 48 to 55 minutes on Regular and 36 to 42 on Quick, per Zojirushi's manual; brown takes 84 to 107. Softer runs 52 to 56 minutes, Harder 37 to 44, and Porridge 56 to 64. Zojirushi's rice cooker FAQ explains the wait: rice should soak about 30 minutes and steam about 15, and its micom cookers do both automatically.

The one measured white-rice time lines up with the manual: Engadget timed 48 minutes. The sources disagree on what Quick saves. Engadget saw about 10 minutes, the manual's ranges imply 12 to 13, and Zojirushi's FAQ says Quick on advanced models saves "up to 20 minutes." An Engadget editor's 2022 piece on a Neuro Fuzzy she bought (it doesn't give the model size) put white rice at "around 40 or so minutes," shorter than the 48 minutes in Engadget's roundup. For brown rice timing across cookers, see [brown rice in a rice cooker](/guides/brown-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/).

## What the Neuro Fuzzy logic does, and what it doesn't

Zojirushi's product page says the fuzzy-logic chip makes "fine adjustments to temperature and heating time." None of the tests I read measured that directly. ATK adds a broader pattern: not every fuzzy-logic cooker it tested made excellent rice, but every cooker that did had fuzzy logic.

It is not an induction cooker. The manual lists the rice cooking system as direct heating. If you're weighing a pricier induction model, read [induction vs micom rice cookers](/guides/induction-vs-micom-rice-cooker/); ATK found the induction cookers it tested didn't consistently perform better or faster.

## The Mixed setting, the timer and the cup

Per the manual and product page, the menus are White (Regular/Sushi, Softer, Harder), Mixed, Porridge, Sweet, Semi-brown, Brown, Rinse-free and Quick, with two timer presets. The timer can't be used with Quick, Mixed or Sweet.

That matters for jasmine and basmati. ATK and Zojirushi's FAQ both point long-grain white to Mixed, with 1¼ cups of water per cup of rice measured in the included cup, and Mixed tops out at 4 cups. Capacity is counted in Zojirushi's 180 mL cup, so read [rice cooker cup size](/guides/rice-cooker-cup-size/) and the [rice cooker water ratio guide](/guides/rice-cooker-water-ratio/) before measuring with a US cup.

## Keep Warm limits: what the manual allows

Zojirushi's manual is direct: "Do not keep rice warm for more than 12 hours." It also says not to keep any food but white rice warm.

Extended Keep Warm holds about 140°F and returns to regular Keep Warm after 8 hours. It can't start once regular Keep Warm has run 12 hours, and it doesn't work for Mixed, Sweet, Porridge, Semi-brown or Brown. The Reheat function warms rice that is already on Keep Warm.

CNN Underscored describes Extended Keep Warm as running 8 more hours after the 12-hour regular period. That conflicts with the manual's 12-hour line, and I follow the manual.

FoodSafety.gov says to keep cooked food hot, at 140°F or above. FDA's Bad Bug Book says vomiting-type Bacillus cereus outbreaks have generally been linked to rice products. It adds that the toxin behind them is heat-resistant, stable even after 30 minutes at 121°C. Neither gives a safe holding time, so I don't either; see [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Long-term costs: the pan, the battery and the warranty

None of the sources I read has independent long-term durability data. Owner longevity stories show up in search results, but I haven't opened those threads, so they aren't cited here.

What Zojirushi documents: the inner pan is excluded from the 1-year warranty, and the replacement B201 pan was $69.85 at Zojirushi's store on Nov 18, 2026. The manual says the clock's lithium battery lasts about 4 to 5 years and that owners shouldn't replace it themselves. It also says coating discoloration after long use doesn't affect nonstick quality or sanitary properties; see [inner pot coating peeling](/guides/rice-cooker-inner-pot-coating-peeling/). Error codes H01, H02, H04, E01 and E02 are explained in [Zojirushi rice cooker error codes](/guides/zojirushi-rice-cooker-error-codes/).

## Cleaning the NS-ZCC10 without damaging it

Per the manual and FAQ: no dishwasher, no vinegar in the pan, no brushes or abrasive cleansers, and don't soak the pan for long because its top edge can rust. Wet #320 sandpaper is only for the heating plate and center sensor. The pan and inner lid come out for washing; the inner lid gasket doesn't. Full steps are in [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).
