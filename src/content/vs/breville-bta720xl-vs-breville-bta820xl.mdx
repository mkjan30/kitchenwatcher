For most buyers weighing the Breville Bit More vs Die Cast, the Bit More (BTA720XL) is the better pick. It scored 69 to the Die-Cast's 66 at [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) and holds its Editors' Choice, and America's Test Kitchen recommends both. The Die-Cast Smart (BTA820XL) cost $75 to $90 more at Amazon on Nov 6, 2026, per ATK's listed prices, for motorized lowering and a die-cast body, not better toast scores. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) disagrees: it calls the Die-Cast "the best toaster we've tested."

## Toast results: three labs, three different verdicts

The three labs don't agree, and they tested at different times with different methods. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) (Apr 15, 2026) toasted white sandwich bread on light, medium and dark, and averaged medium over five runs. It timed the Bit More at 2 min 32 s and the Die-Cast at 2 min 11 s.

ATK's toast-quality notes favor the Bit More. It said the Bit More "produced nice medium and dark brown toast" but "hardly toasted on its lowest setting." The Die-Cast "consistently made toast with pale spots," ATK reported. Both still made its Recommended list.

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) (Aug 14, 2025) scored both 6.0 for bread. The Bit More pulled ahead on bagels, 7.0 to 6.3, and both scored 8.0 on frozen waffles. It also noted that on the Bit More "one corner of each slice remained a bit lighter than the rest," and listed "burns bread on highest settings" as a Die-Cast con.

[Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) (Oct 22, 2025) takes the opposite view of the Die-Cast. It named it Best Overall for its "top-notch toasting of bread and thick (sliced) bagels," and its only listed con was the price. Reviewed didn't cover the Bit More, so it offers no head-to-head. Two of three labs lean toward the Bit More, but the margin is small and the sources genuinely conflict.

## What the extra $75 to $90 buys: motor, Lift & Look and a die-cast body

The price gap pays for how the bread moves and what the body is made of. Breville's [BTA820XL instruction book](https://assets.ajmadison.com/ajmadison/itemdocs/BTA820-840XL-Instructions.pdf) (US/Canada, Issue A11, which also covers the BTA830XL and BTA840XL) describes a TOAST|CANCEL button "with motorized raising and lowering," a motorized Lift & Look button that raises and lowers the carriage without interrupting the cycle, and a diecast body. Reviewed describes the bread lowering and rising "with a theatrically slow speed."

On the Bit More, Lift & Look is a manual lever that doubles as a high lift for small items, and the body is full stainless steel, per Breville's [US instruction book for the BTA720 and BTA730](https://assets.breville.com/BTA720/BTA720_BTA730_USCM_IB_F26_LR.pdf), which also lists the Bagel button that ATK and TechGearLab report. The [Australian and New Zealand edition](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) calls that button Crumpet.

Both have A Bit More and Frozen buttons and 12 browning settings on a slide marked 1 to 5, per the two books. BestReviews' Breville roundup (updated September 2024) describes the Die-Cast as having a "five-setting browning control," which conflicts with Breville's manual. More on how the numbers work is in [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/).

## How reliable are they? Owner reports and warranty

Owner evidence on the Die-Cast's durability is thin and mixed. Zappos showed 170 reviews for the BTA820XL on Nov 6, 2026, with 58% at five stars and 27% at one or two stars. The visible reviews date from 2009 to 2024, and some are syndicated from Breville's own site. They include one owner whose unit was "already shot" after a little over a year, one who returned an unevenly toasting first unit and was happy with the second, and one reporting at least four years of use.

For the Bit More, the reported flaws come from the labs: TechGearLab's lighter corner and ATK's weak lowest setting. Both carry a 1-year warranty, per TechGearLab. A motor is one more part that can fail, but that's reasoning, not data: no source compares failure rates. The full list of Bit More problems is in the [Breville Bit More review](/reviews/breville-bta720xl/).

## What both Breville manuals say about jams, crumbs and unplugging

Both instruction books carry the same warnings. Metal utensils "such as a knife, should not be inserted into the toaster to remove jammed toast." Don't use the toaster without the crumb tray in place, because failing to clean it regularly "may result in a fire hazard."

To turn the toaster off, remove the plug, and unplug it whenever it isn't in use. For jams, see [how to get toast out of a toaster](/guides/how-to-get-toast-out-of-toaster/).

Both Breville books also tell you to turn the toaster upside down over a trash bin and shake out crumbs. GE's G9TMA2SSPSS manual says the opposite: "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it." Follow your own model's book; the [toaster cleaning guide](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) covers the conflict.

A CPSC recall search for Breville on Nov 6, 2026 returned only a 2015 pressure cooker recall, no toasters. You can check any model with the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

## Bit More or Die-Cast: which one to buy

- **Buy the Bit More (BTA720XL)** if you want the cheaper, better-scoring toaster that both ATK and TechGearLab endorse. Accept a slower cycle (2 min 32 s to medium, per ATK) and a lighter corner, per TechGearLab.
- **Buy the Die-Cast Smart (BTA820XL)** if hands-free motorized lowering or the die-cast look is worth $75 to $90 to you, and you accept ATK's pale-spot finding. Reviewed's Best Overall pick backs this choice.
- **Skip both** if fast, perfectly even toast is the priority; neither scored above 6.0 for bread at TechGearLab. See the [best toasters ranking](/best/toasters/) for other options.
