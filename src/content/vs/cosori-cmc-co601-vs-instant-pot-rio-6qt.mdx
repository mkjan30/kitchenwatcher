In the Cosori pressure cooker vs Instant Pot choice at the budget end, the evidence favors the Cosori CMC-CO601-SUS if you want slow cooking and low pressure for less money: [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers) made it a Best Buy and rated it Highly Recommended. Pick the Instant Pot Rio 6-quart if you want a stainless steel pot instead of nonstick. ATK put the Rio in Recommended with Reservations. Among the testers I read, only ATK has published results on the Cosori, so its evidence is thin.

ATK's review, published May 14, 2025, is the only independent test I found that covers both cookers. Its testers made beef stew and baked beans on pressure and slow settings, cooked white rice, tracked water temperatures, and rated browning and sautéing.

## What America's Test Kitchen found on each cooker

### Cosori 6-quart pressure cooker review: ATK's Best Buy

ATK says the Cosori "excelled in both pressure and slow cooking tests" and was easy to use with simple preset buttons. Its sauté worked well, and ATK found its manual steam release "safe and effective." ATK calls slow cooking the biggest disappointment across the multicookers it tested, and it points anyone who wants to slow-cook to the Cosori, since its overall winner, the Instant Pot Pro 8-quart, can't slow-cook well.

The downsides ATK lists are practical ones. The dark pot made it "hard to monitor browning and simmering," the display could be a touch larger, and there's no automatic quick-release button. ATK measured a 7 in cooking surface and calls it "relatively narrow"; elsewhere in the review it says broader surfaces gave its testers more room to sauté or spread food in a single layer. It also warns that the nonstick coating "won't last forever."

### Instant Pot Rio: Recommended with Reservations at ATK

ATK says the Rio "made decent rice" and made good beans and beef stew that took a few extra minutes to fully tenderize. Its problems were the functions around pressure cooking: it "didn't slow-cook well," and browning was hard because the sauté setting "seemed underpowered."

ATK also found the pot has no handles, which makes it harder to lift out when hot, and that the interface is "crowded with unnecessary buttons." [Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-electric-pressure-cooker/), by contrast, names the Rio its top pick, calling it typically the most affordable Instant Pot with all the settings most home cooks need. So the two testers disagree on the Rio, and only ATK covers the Cosori.

## Nonstick ceramic or stainless steel: which pot is easier to clean?

This is the biggest physical difference. Cosori's [owner's manual](https://cosori.com/cdn/shop/files/KAAPPCCSNUS0005Y_CMC-CO601-SUS_6.0-Quart_Pressure_Cooker_manual_US_A3-230423.pdf) says the inner pot "is made of aluminum metal with nonstick ceramic coating." Instant's product page describes the Rio's pot as 18/8 stainless steel with a tri-ply bottom.

Cleaning advice follows from that. Cosori recommends hand washing the pot, lid and rack, and says they can go on the dishwasher's top rack "but may wear more quickly over time." Instant's page calls the Rio's lid and inner pot dishwasher safe. ATK's two cautions on the Cosori, that the dark surface hides browning and the coating won't last forever, are the only sourced durability points. I found no source that measured how long either pot lasts.

## Which has more programs, and does low pressure matter?

Cosori's function charts list eight pressure programs: Pressure Cook, Rice, Steam, Sterilize, Oatmeal/Porridge, Stew/Broth, Bean/Grain and Meat/Poultry. Each can run on Low or High pressure. The non-pressure side adds Slow Cook (6 h default, up to 24 h), Sauté (Low 284 °F, High 356 °F, or custom), Ferment, Sous Vide (77 to 194 °F) and Keep Warm. The manual's own intro calls it "9-in-1," so counts vary depending on how you tally them.

Instant lists 7 cooking functions for the Rio (pressure cook, slow cook, sauté, steam, rice, yogurt, keep warm) and "13 one-touch options." Its page doesn't state pressure levels.

[Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-electric-pressure-cooker/) reports the Rio offers only high pressure, unlike the Duo and Pro. It adds that most pressure cooker recipes are designed for high pressure and that eggs, seafood and vegetables can all be cooked on high. So the Cosori's low setting matters only if your recipes call for it.

How you release pressure after cooking matters as much as the program; see [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

## Releasing pressure and opening the lid

The Cosori uses a steam release switch with Seal and Vent positions and a float valve; per the manual, the lid unlocks once the float valve has dropped. Its manual notes that "natural release can take up to 40 minutes depending on the dish." ATK wished for an automatic quick-release button, but called the manual release safe.

Instant's page calls the Rio's release "Easy-Release." Wirecutter describes an auto-sealing lid with a steam-release switch, and says the lid weighs nearly 2.5 lb and its metal top heats up during cooking. If either lid leaks steam, see [Instant Pot not sealing](/guides/instant-pot-not-sealing/); if it won't open, see [the float valve guide](/guides/instant-pot-float-valve/).

## Fill lines, warranty and recall checks for both cookers

Neither cooker shows up in the CPSC's recall database. A query of the CPSC's [recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Cosori) on Nov 13, 2026 returned one Cosori recall, 23-130 from February 23, 2023, covering about 2 million Cosori air fryers for a fire and burn hazard. It doesn't involve this pressure cooker.

A [query for Instant](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Instant) returned one pressure cooker recall, 15-741 from July 2015, covering about 1,000 Instant Pot "Smart" pressure cookers for a shock hazard; it isn't the Rio, and a separate query found no recall for the Rio. How to run this check yourself is in [the pressure cooker recall check](/guides/pressure-cooker-recall-check/).

Cosori's manual says not to fill above the "PC MAX-2/3" line, and not past ½ for foods that expand, such as rice. It also says not to use the cooker for deep frying or pressure frying, and to avoid recipes with more than ¼ cup / 60 mL of oil. Instant claims the Rio has "over 10 safety mechanisms" (manufacturer claim). For how these safeguards work, see [are pressure cookers safe](/guides/are-pressure-cookers-safe/).

Warranty: Arovast Corporation, which issues Cosori's warranty, covers the cooker for 2 years from the original purchase date, per the manual. Instant's page lists a One (1) Year Limited Warranty for the Rio.

## First run: the water test on each cooker

Cosori's manual asks for a test run before first use: 3 cups / 750 mL of water, Pressure Cook for 5 minutes on HIGH, with Keep Warm turned off. It notes a plastic smell at first is normal and to contact support if it doesn't fade after the test run. I didn't open the 6-quart Rio's own manual, so I can't give its water volume. Two other Rio-family manuals, the Rio Wide and the Rio Plus Wide, disagree with each other (3 quarts vs 3 cups); the conflict is laid out in [the Instant Pot water test guide](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/).

## Which budget pressure cooker should you buy?

Neither is the outright best budget pressure cooker for everyone. It depends on the pot and the programs you'll use.

**Choose the Cosori CMC-CO601-SUS if you want:**
- Slow cooking that ATK rates well, up to 24 hours per the manual
- Low pressure and sous vide (77 to 194 °F)
- A 2-year warranty instead of 1 year
- The lower price ($90 vs $156 on ATK's listings, Nov 13, 2026)

**Choose the Instant Pot Rio 6-quart if you want:**
- No nonstick coating: an 18/8 stainless pot with a tri-ply bottom
- A lid and pot Instant calls dishwasher safe
- Wirecutter's top pick rather than ATK's ranking

If you're leaning Instant, Wirecutter says the Duo Plus adds a few settings (sous vide, cake, sterilize) and a low-pressure option for about $30 more at the time it published; see [Instant Pot Rio vs Duo Plus](/vs/instant-pot-rio-6qt-vs-instant-pot-duo-plus-6qt/).
