In the Cosori TurboBlaze vs Instant Vortex Plus matchup, the TurboBlaze is the better pick for most kitchens: crispier fries and tots, a 450°F ceiling and a 2-year warranty. The Vortex Plus wins if thermostat accuracy matters most. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer) ranked the TurboBlaze #2 (79 points) and the Vortex Plus #3 (78) of 10, one point apart.

Nobody at Kitchen Watcher cooked with either unit; this comparison is built from lab data, manuals and maker specs, as explained in [how we research](/how-we-research/).

- **Pick the TurboBlaze** for crispy snacks (TechGearLab cooking score 8.5 vs 7.0), high-heat cooking to 450°F (COSORI manual) or a longer warranty (2 years vs 1, per both manuals).
- **Pick the Vortex Plus** for recipe-exact temperatures (401.5°F at a 400°F setting, per TechGearLab), dehydrating up to 72 hours (Instant manual) or an easy-to-use time and temperature dial (Food Network).

## Which cooks better food? The sources disagree

[TechGearLab's TurboBlaze review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze) found it produced exceptional fries and tater tots, juicy salmon with crisp skin, and juicy wings, and scored its cooking quality 8.5. Its [Vortex Plus review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/instant-pot-vortex-plus) scored 7.0, citing lackluster wing crispness and unpleasantly dry salmon.

[Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-air-fryer), updated Aug 14, 2026, named the Vortex Plus its Best Overall air fryer. It never tested the TurboBlaze, only the COSORI Pro. So one lab rates the Vortex Plus below the TurboBlaze on food, and another rates it above everything it tried. The only head-to-head data favors the TurboBlaze.

## Which holds temperature more accurately?

The Vortex Plus is the more accurate air fryer. TechGearLab measured it at 401.5°F at a 400°F setting, within 1.5°F, for a 10/10 accuracy score, using two calibrated oven thermometers. The TurboBlaze scored 7/10.

TechGearLab's two TurboBlaze numbers don't match: 395.7°F in the full review and 391.6°F in the roundup. Either way it ran about 4 to 8°F cool. A gap that size matters most for recipes with exact temperatures and least for frozen food you check by eye.

## Which gets hotter, and which needs a preheat?

The TurboBlaze runs hotter and has more programs. Per COSORI's manual, its Air Fry program covers 300–450°F with a 1–60 minute timer, and Broil runs 425–450°F. Dehydrate goes to 24 hours, and Proof and Keep Warm are among its nine functions. Instant's manual caps Vortex Plus Air Fry at 180–400°F for up to 1 hour, fixes Broil at 400°F, and lets Dehydrate run up to 72 hours across six programs.

Both manuals build in a preheat, though the makers' marketing plays it down. COSORI's manual recommends preheating: 4 minutes for 345–450°F, 3 minutes for 240–340°F and 2 minutes for 170–235°F. Its product page says preheating is not always required. Instant's manual says Reheat and Dehydrate do not preheat, implying the other four programs do. Yet its product page says "no preheating" is required.

TechGearLab's TurboBlaze review cites a 4-minute preheat. Its Vortex Plus review gives 3 minutes and calls the preheat longer than competitors', while the roundup lists a "5-min preheat" as a con. More in [do you need to preheat an air fryer](/guides/do-you-need-to-preheat-an-air-fryer/).

## Which takes up less counter space?

Cooking room is almost identical: 85.6 sq in for the TurboBlaze and 87.8 sq in for the Vortex Plus, per TechGearLab. The Vortex Plus is slightly bigger outside, 12.2 x 12.1 x 14.9 in against 11.7 x 11.5 x 14.2 in. Both manuals ask for 5 in of clearance. COSORI lists 13.2 lb; Instant's page gives no weight. For sizing help, see [what size air fryer do I need](/guides/what-size-air-fryer-do-i-need/).

## Which is quieter and easier to clean?

Food Network puts the Vortex Plus fan noise at up to 66 dB. No decibel figure has been published for the TurboBlaze; TechGearLab lists "quiet" as a pro, which isn't a number. TechGearLab also reported a distinct plastic flavor in Vortex Plus fries and tots despite repeated cleaning, and says the TurboBlaze noticeably lacks it. COSORI's manual calls a new-unit plastic smell normal and prescribes a test run ([new air fryer plastic smell](/guides/new-air-fryer-plastic-smell/)).

Both scored 7.0 for cleaning at TechGearLab. COSORI says the TurboBlaze basket and crisper plate are dishwasher safe. Instant's product page says the basket is top-rack dishwasher safe, but its manual lists only the tray and says to clean the basket with a damp cloth and mild soap. TechGearLab is split too: its Vortex review text calls the parts top-rack dishwasher-safe, while its spec table says "No."

The coating claims conflict as well. COSORI calls its coating PFAS-free ceramic, but TechGearLab's table marks both baskets as PTFE-coated. TechGearLab's roundup also says COSORI told it the basket coating includes PFAS. If a coating starts flaking, see [air fryer basket coating peeling](/guides/air-fryer-basket-coating-peeling/).

## Known flaws, error codes and recalls: has either been recalled?

- **TurboBlaze:** a large center hole in the crisper plate lets small food fall through, and the touchscreen shows fingerprints (TechGearLab).
- **Vortex Plus:** the tray slides out when food is tipped out, the exterior hit 146°F, and the warranty is 1 year (TechGearLab, Instant manual).
- **Error codes:** COSORI's manual lists E01/E21 and E02/E22 as open and short circuits in the temperature monitor, and E04 as the unit being plugged into a 220–240V outlet. Instant's manual lists E1 as an open circuit and E2 as a temperature-sensor short circuit. Fixes are in the [TurboBlaze error codes](/guides/cosori-turboblaze-error-codes/) and [Vortex Plus error codes](/guides/instant-vortex-plus-error-codes/) guides.

The [CPSC recall](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Two-Million-COSORI-Air-Fryers-Recalled-by-Atekcity-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards) of Feb 23, 2023 covered about 2 million COSORI air fryers over a wire connection that could overheat, with 205 reported incidents. The CAF-DC601 is not among its 21 model numbers, so the TurboBlaze was not recalled. A search of cpsc.gov on Sep 27, 2026 turned up no recall of the Vortex Plus 6-qt. Background: [are air fryers safe](/guides/are-air-fryers-safe/).

- **Crispy snacks and frozen food:** TurboBlaze. Cooking quality (8.5 vs 7.0) is the widest instant vs cosori air fryer gap in TechGearLab's scores.

- **High heat or long dehydrating:** split. The TurboBlaze reaches 450°F; the Vortex Plus dehydrates up to 72 hours.
