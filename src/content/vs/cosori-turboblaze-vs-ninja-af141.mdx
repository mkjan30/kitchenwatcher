For most people the COSORI TurboBlaze is the better buy: on Sep 27, 2026 it cost $40 less, it has the bigger basket, twice the warranty, and it's the only one of the two with independent lab data (TechGearLab's Top Pick for families). Pick the Ninja AF141 if you want a lighter, simpler 5-quart machine and don't need heat above 400°F, knowing no lab has published measurements for it yet.

## What independent tests found

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer) made the TurboBlaze a Top Pick for families. It measured an average of 391.6°F at a 400°F setting, so it ran slightly cool, and a 130°F hottest exterior point, the coolest of the air fryers in its roundup. It described the preheat as optional and quick, at 4 minutes. Its separate [TurboBlaze review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/cosori-turboblaze) gives slightly different figures, 395.7°F and 140°F; the roundup numbers are used here because they come from the same comparison as the rest of the table.

Nobody I could read has tested the AF141. TechGearLab mentions it only as the newer version of the AF101 it tested, and its "Ninja Pro XL" entry is a 6.5-quart model, not this one. RTINGS has a head-to-head of these two, but its data loads behind JavaScript and a paywall, so I haven't used it. If you want to know how the Ninja line performs in a lab, the closest proxy is the AF101 in [Ninja AF101 vs AF141](/vs/ninja-af101-vs-ninja-af141/).

## Speed claims

COSORI says the TurboBlaze cooks up to 46% faster than its older CP168 model on a pound of fresh fries, per its October 2023 [launch press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cosori-unveils-the-next-generation-6-quart-turboblaze-air-fryer-301961219.html). That's a claim against COSORI's own older model, not against Ninja, and no lab has checked it. SharkNinja doesn't publish a speed figure for the AF141.

## Coating: the sources disagree

COSORI's product page describes a PFAS-free ceramic-coated basket. TechGearLab's spec table says the TurboBlaze's cooking surface contains PTFE. Both can't be right for the same unit; a newer production run may differ from the one TechGearLab tested. If avoiding PTFE matters to you, ask COSORI which coating your unit has before buying. SharkNinja calls the AF141's basket "nonstick" without saying what the coating is.

## Recalls and safety

The TurboBlaze is not on the February 2023 CPSC recall of about 2 million COSORI air fryers. That recall covered 21 older model numbers sold from June 2018 through December 2022, after 205 reports of fires and overheating, and the TurboBlaze launched in October 2023 ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Two-Million-COSORI-Air-Fryers-Recalled-by-Atekcity-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards)). COSORI says the TurboBlaze shuts off automatically when the basket is removed or if it overheats.

No Ninja air fryer is on SharkNinja's most recent CPSC recall, which in May 2025 covered about 1.85 million Foodi OP300-series pressure cookers ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2025/SharkNinja-Recalls-1-8-Million-Foodi-Multi-Function-Pressure-Cookers-Due-to-Burn-Hazard-Serious-Burn-Injuries-Reported)).

## What owners report

Owner evidence is uneven. For the AF141, Target's review feed held 537 written reviews on Sep 27, 2026 (740 ratings, 4.58 average), though its product page showed no rating summary that day. Of the 39 one- and two-star reviews, nine complain about loud running, rattling or grinding, and 11 describe a unit that was dead on arrival or quit within a year, several at three to four months. That's a small share of a mostly five-star pile, but it's the pattern among the complaints. For the TurboBlaze, COSORI's own site shows a 4.7-star average, but a brand's own store is not an independent source. Target doesn't carry it, and Best Buy's listing for the smart version had three reviews, all five stars and all written for promotions. I couldn't find a consistent owner complaint pattern for the TurboBlaze in readable sources.

## Who should buy which

- **Buy the TurboBlaze** if you want the most capacity per dollar, heat up to 450°F, adjustable fan speed, and a 2-year warranty, and you're comfortable with the coating question above.
- **Buy the AF141** if counter weight matters (it's about 2.5 lb lighter per the makers' specs), you only need the four basic functions, or you'd rather stay with Ninja's parts and service network.
- **Cooking for one or two?** Neither; look at the 4-quart Ninja AF101 in the [best air fryers ranking](/best/air-fryers/).
