Cuisinart CPT-122 vs GE toaster, in short: buy the CPT-122 if you mostly toast sandwich bread and want the slightly smaller toaster with a 3-year warranty. Buy the GE G9TMA2SSPSS if bagels and frozen bread matter more. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) scored both 62/100, America's Test Kitchen named the GE its Best Conventional Toaster, and Reviewed named the CPT-122 Best Value.

"GE toaster" here means GE's 2-slice G9TMA2SSPSS; its manual also covers the 4-slice G9TMA4SSPSS. Neither toaster wins outright. Nobody here tested either one: this page lines up three independent test sources with GE's owner's manual and Cuisinart's instruction booklets, as explained in [how we research](/how-we-research/).

**Pick the CPT-122 if** plain bread is most of what you toast and counter space is tight. The trade-off is weak bagels (TechGearLab bagel score 4.5).

**Pick the GE if** you toast bagels or frozen bread often and want a stainless body. The trade-offs are a 1-year warranty, a hot exterior, and sources that disagree on how evenly it toasts bread.

## Toasting bread: the CPT-122 has the stronger bread score

The Cuisinart compact toaster is the better bet for plain bread. In [TechGearLab's CPT-122 review](https://techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/toaster/cuisinart-cpt-122) it scored 8.0 for bread and took about two minutes to bring two slices to medium. TechGearLab also saw one corner brown slightly more than the rest. Reviewed ran five back-to-back 2-slice batches on medium and got 10 identical slices in about six minutes, plus dark toast in under three minutes.

The GE's bread results depend on the source. [TechGearLab's GE review](https://techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/toaster/ge-2-slice-g9tma2sspss) scored it 6.5, found medium mostly even, and found the highest settings very dark. Its roundup lists "uneven toasting coverage" as a con. ATK, by contrast, found relatively evenly toasted white bread at a 1 min 39 s average, and Reviewed called it even and consistent, with three distinct shades.

That's a real disagreement, and I can't settle it. TechGearLab's GE review still calls the CPT-122 a superior choice overall.

## Bagels and English muffins: the GE has the edge

TechGearLab scored the GE 6.5 for bagels against the CPT-122's 4.5. On the Cuisinart, the cut side browned but didn't crisp, and edges stayed lighter than the center. ATK found the GE's bagels relatively even. The GE isn't flawless here either: TechGearLab's first GE bagel trial was good, but a repeat came out overdone.

Size matters too. Reviewed found a fat New York-style bagel a tight fit in the CPT-122. Neither bagels nor English muffins popped up high enough to grab without raising its lever for extra lift. It says the same lift-lever step is needed for English muffins in the GE. TechGearLab credits the CPT-122's high-lift lever for lifting English muffins.

GE's manual says to put bagels in with the cut side toward the inside of the toaster. More on that in [the toaster bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/).

## Frozen bread and waffles: a split result

TechGearLab found the GE excelled at frozen bread, which it browned to a golden brown. Its frozen waffles came out pale and undercooked, though, and strudels stayed underdone, for a 4.5. GE's manual says to set the dial, lower the lever, then press Frozen.

On its frozen setting, the CPT-122 took about two and a half minutes on frozen waffles, per TechGearLab. They came out crisp outside with one section darker, for a 5.0. So the Cuisinart leads narrowly on waffles. TechGearLab's CPT-122 review doesn't report a frozen-bread result, so there's no head-to-head on frozen bread.

## Size, build and cleaning: the CPT-122 is easier to live with

As a budget 2 slice toaster, the CPT-122 is the easier one to live with. TechGearLab scored its ease of use 8.6 against 7.2 and liked its front slide-out crumb tray. TechGearLab found the GE's rear crumb tray, with its tiny handle, less convenient to remove and clean. It also says the stainless body shows fingerprints.

Weight is a source conflict. TechGearLab lists 2.6 lb for the CPT-122 and 2.9 lb for the GE. Reviewed lists 3.7 lb and 5.66 lb (its GE entry, "GE 2-Slice Toaster", gives no model number). Reviewed's figures make the GE the noticeably heavier toaster; TechGearLab's make them nearly equal.

ATK found that the GE's sides and top get hot during use, so nothing should touch it. GE's manual adds the back and the crevices around the opening as potentially hot surfaces.

## Safety and care: what the GE and Cuisinart manuals say

[GE's owner's manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) (49-9000001) gives these rules:

- "Recommended 4 inch clearance on all sides."
- Before first use, run it empty "in a well ventilated room to cure the new elements"; a slight odor is expected.
- The lever "won't lock down unless the toaster is connected to the electricity supply." See [toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).
- Operate with the crumb tray in place, and "Unplug from outlet when not in use and before cleaning."
- "Do not hold the appliance upside down and do not shake it" to remove crumbs.
- For stuck bread, unplug and let it cool. "Never use a metal object for this purpose."
- If smoke comes out, push Cancel and empty the crumb tray if it's full.

Cuisinart's [CPT-122 instruction booklet](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/6e/6e5ae4b6-2eaf-4d50-82b7-d521f5345e66.pdf) says to unplug the toaster and let it cool completely before cleaning, and never to put any part of it in water. Wipe the outside with a clean damp cloth, with no abrasive cleansers. Slide out the crumb tray, discard the crumbs, wipe it and put it back, and never run the toaster without it. For bread left inside, turn the toaster upside down and gently shake, and never insert hard or sharp instruments into the slot.

Manuals disagree on shaking toasters upside down: Cuisinart's booklet and [Breville's BTA720/BTA730 booklet](https://assets.breville.com/Instruction-Booklets/ANZ/BTA730_IB_A13_WEB.pdf) say to turn the toaster upside down and shake, while GE's says not to. [How to clean a toaster](/guides/how-to-clean-a-toaster/) covers that. If one ever flames, see [toaster fire: what to do](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/) and [how to prevent kitchen fires](/guides/how-to-prevent-kitchen-fires/).

## Recall status: neither model is covered

The CPSC record shows no recall for any Cuisinart toaster or for the G9TMA2SSPSS. The only GE toaster recall in the CPSC database is [08-413](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2008/wal-mart-recalls-general-electric-toasters-due-to-fire-and-shock-hazards) from September 30, 2008. It covered about 210,000 GE toasters sold at Wal-Mart, models 169115 and 169116, because a short between the heating element and the bread cage could cause fire and shock. Wal-Mart had received 140 reports of fires, sparks or tripped breakers, and no injuries.

Check any toaster with the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

## Which should you buy?

- **Mostly sandwich bread or a small kitchen:** the CPT-122. It has the higher bread score, a slightly smaller body and a 3-year warranty (per TechGearLab).
- **Bagels and frozen bread, stainless look:** the GE G9TMA2SSPSS. Accept a 1-year warranty, a hot exterior and mixed reports on evenness.
