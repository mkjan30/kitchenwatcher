The Cuisinart vs KitchenAid food processor question has no single answer, because the big reviewers split. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) (Aug 2026) names the Cuisinart Custom 14 its winner and rates the KitchenAid 13-Cup "Recommended with Reservations"; CNET and Reviewed name the KitchenAid best overall. Pick the Cuisinart for power, purée and a base that stays put. Pick the KitchenAid for a lighter machine, a 9-cup liquid max, adjustable slicing and a dough blade.

## Which food processor is better, KitchenAid or Cuisinart? The quick verdict

**Pick the Cuisinart Custom 14 if:**

- You knead pizza dough often. Reviewed found it kneaded to a smooth, elastic ball without manual help, stalling or moving.
- You want a base that stays put. ATK lists a 12.45 lb base.
- You purée a lot. TechGearLab scored its purée 8 of 10 and measured it at 61.5 dB at 3 ft.

**Pick the KitchenAid 13-Cup if:**

- You process soups or sauces. ATK lists a 9-cup liquid max against the Cuisinart's 3 cups.
- You lift it in and out of a cabinet. ATK lists a 5.5 lb base.
- You want adjustable slicing and a dough blade in the box (ATK, CNET).

TechGearLab didn't review the KFP1318, so its numbers cover only the Cuisinart.

## Why ATK, CNET and Reviewed disagree on these two

The verdicts split because the tests differed. ATK ran 13 tests, including a yogurt dye test, mayonnaise and a double batch of heavy, sticky dough, and rated performance, ease of use and cleanup. CNET's guide was one tester making pico de gallo and hummus and shredding and slicing carrots across nine models. Reviewed chopped onions, minced parsley, ground almonds, sliced potatoes, tomatoes and pepperoni, shredded mozzarella and kneaded pizza dough.

CNET's KitchenAid win rests on hummus speed, low noise and easy storage. ATK's reservations rest on things CNET didn't report on: pulse overrun, the light base moving while kneading, and cleanup. Evidence is thin in places: CNET is one person, and the only measured noise number is TechGearLab's, for the Cuisinart alone.

## Chopping and purée: Custom 14 vs 13-Cup

ATK found the Cuisinart chopped mirepoix uniformly and passed its yogurt dye test, but its redesigned blade leaves slightly bigger gaps and couldn't emulsify single-batch mayonnaise (a double batch worked). TechGearLab scored it 8 for purée and 6 for chopping; nut butter took 19 minutes.

ATK reports the KitchenAid's pulse ran more than three seconds after release and over-chopped, though its drizzle hole made fluffy mayonnaise. CNET found it made hummus quickly and with less noise than any other model it tried; Reviewed calls it the best chopper it has tried, chopping onions and herbs quickly and evenly. On chopping, the evidence is split. Reviewed leans KitchenAid, but ATK's pulse complaint counts against it, and Reviewed also says the Cuisinart chops and slices as well as a sharp knife.

## Dough: KFP1318 dough blade vs DFP-14BCNY S-blade

The sources conflict on dough. Reviewed found the Cuisinart kneaded pizza dough to a smooth, elastic ball without manual adjustment, stalling or moving, while the KitchenAid needed manual adjustment and can move around the counter. ATK found the KitchenAid made a smooth, elastic double batch, but its light base jumped around and dough oozed into its multi-part blade and glued it in place.

CNET lists "does not come with a dough hook" as a Cuisinart con. That's a missing tool, not a missing ability: TechGearLab notes the Cuisinart has no dough blade yet ranks it among the standouts on its pizza crust test, and ATK says the 14-cup easily processed a double batch of pizza dough with 4 cups of flour. TechGearLab also says the 720 W motor shows its limits on denser doughs, so the evidence isn't one-sided. For flour limits and method, see [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/).

## Slicing and shredding: fixed discs vs adjustable slicer

ATK found the KitchenAid sliced potatoes and tomatoes well with easy thickness adjustment, but potatoes needed trimming for the feed tube, food got trapped while shredding and carrot juice sprayed. TechGearLab notes the Cuisinart can't adjust thickness without extra discs, and scored it 7 for slicing and 6 for shredding.

Reviewed found the Cuisinart shredded mozzarella unevenly and the KitchenAid shredded cheese evenly. CNET's tester wrote that she'd probably pass on all of the processors she tried in favor of a mandoline for slicing.

## Liquid max, size and weight

The liquid maximum is the biggest sourced spec gap: ATK lists 3 cups for the Cuisinart and 9 cups for the KitchenAid. TechGearLab's numbers line up: about 3¼ cups of water reached the Cuisinart's max fill line, and it didn't leak at that level. More on fill lines is in [food processor leaking](/guides/food-processor-leaking/).

The Cuisinart is heavy and squat, the KitchenAid light and tall (18 lb and 15 in high vs 9.7 lb and 17.43 in, as listed by CNET). Reviewed says the Cuisinart fits under a cabinet; CNET warns its heft may discourage pulling it out. For sizing by household, see [what size food processor do I need](/guides/what-size-food-processor-do-i-need/).

## Cleaning, storage and controls

[KitchenAid's cleaning page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Cleaning_and_Care/How_to_Clean_the_Base_and_Attachments_-_Food_Processor) says all 13-cup parts are top-rack dishwasher safe on gentle cycles such as normal, not high-temperature ones. Don't lay the BPA-free bowl on its side or immerse the base. ATK found the ridged rim traps food and the caddy hard to repack; CNET counts the caddy and cord wrap as pluses.

ATK calls the Cuisinart's simple bowl and blade easy to clean; CNET found its bowl and lid fussy to align. Reviewed says Cuisinart encourages washing the parts in the dishwasher; the Cuisinart booklet itself couldn't be read, so rack guidance is unconfirmed. See [are food processor parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## KitchenAid lights and lockouts: when the 13-cup won't start

Per [KitchenAid's 13-cup support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Food_Processors_and_Choppers/Food_Processor/13_cup/Operation/Not_Turning_on%2F%2FMalfunction%2F%2FFail_to_Operate_-_Food_Processor), "the unit will not start if food is loaded in the large Feed Tube above the max fill line." The bowl and lid must be aligned and locked, and the feed tube clicks when seated.

If the speed light turns off and Off/Pulse flashes, it has overheated and may take 5-15 minutes to cool. If the speed light stays on while Off/Pulse flashes, press Off/Pulse to reset the motor.

The Cuisinart booklet couldn't be read, so for that model see the [Cuisinart Custom 14 review](/reviews/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny/) and [food processor won't turn on](/guides/food-processor-wont-turn-on/).

## Cuisinart riveted-blade recall and the KitchenAid record

[CPSC recall 17-050](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2017/Cuisinart-Food-Processors-Recalled-by-Conair) (Dec 13, 2016) covers riveted blades in Cuisinart processors with model numbers beginning DFP-14, sold July 1996 through December 2015. The recalled blades are silver stainless steel with four rivets and a beige plastic center hub; only four-rivet blades are included. Conair received 69 reports of broken blade pieces in food, including 30 reports of mouth lacerations or tooth injuries.

CPSC says to stop using the blade and contact Cuisinart (877-339-2534) for a free replacement. Check the rivets on a used or older unit.

For KitchenAid, [CPSC recall 01-004](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2001/CPSC-KitchenAid-Announce-Recall-to-Repair-Food-Processors) (Oct 5, 2000) lists only 5- and 7-cup models (KFP300, KFP350, KFP450 families) sold April 1997 through August 2000. No CPSC recall record covers the KFP1318 (CPSC recall database search, Oct 9, 2026). Details are in the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Who should buy the Cuisinart and who should buy the KitchenAid

Buy the Cuisinart Custom 14 if you knead dough, purée often and can leave a 12-plus-pound base on the counter. Buy the KitchenAid 13-Cup if you lift it from a cabinet, purée soups by the batch, or want adjustable slicing.
