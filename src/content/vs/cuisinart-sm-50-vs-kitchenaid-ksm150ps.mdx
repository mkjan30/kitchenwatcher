In the Cuisinart Precision Master vs KitchenAid Artisan matchup, the Artisan is the stronger mixer on independent measurements. TechGearLab scored it 92 to the SM-50's 56, and America's Test Kitchen says it completed all 12 extra batches of its abuse test. Buy the Cuisinart if price and a three-year warranty matter more and you mostly make cake batter (Reviewed calls it best for cake bakers). Expect to scrape: three outlets report a gap between its tools and its bowl. I explain how I build pages like this on [how we research](/how-we-research/).

**Pick the KitchenAid Artisan KSM150PS** if you bake bread or whip small batches: TechGearLab measured it kneading whole-wheat sandwich bread in 7 minutes and whipping 1 cup of cream to stiff peaks in 1 min 50 s. It also has more add-ons (over 14 vs 5, per Homes & Gardens).

**Pick the Cuisinart SM-50** if you want the lower price ($217 at Amazon on Oct 5, 2026, in America's Test Kitchen's listing, vs $380 at Amazon for the Artisan in Food Network's) and a three-year warranty (per Cuisinart's manual), and you don't mind stopping to scrape the bowl.

## Which mixes better? Whipping, bread and cookie dough results

### Whipping cream and egg whites

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) scored whipping 10.0 for the Artisan and 6.0 for the SM-50, and timed 1 cup of cream to stiff peaks at 1 min 50 s against 2 min 40 s. On egg whites the sources conflict. America's Test Kitchen says the SM-50 struggled, noting only two whisk wires sit fairly close to the bowl. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/best-stand-mixers) (updated June 27, 2024) says it excelled at whipping egg whites.

### Bread dough: the outlets disagree

TechGearLab measured whole-wheat sandwich bread at 7 minutes on the Artisan and 13 on the SM-50, with kneading scores of 10.0 and 4.0. [Homes & Gardens](https://www.homesandgardens.com/advice/kitchenaid-vs-cuisinart-stand-mixers) reached the opposite verdict: the Cuisinart worked its bread dough for 10 minutes on a high speed without supervision, while the KitchenAid moved around the counter. [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-stand-mixer) also says the Artisan jumped on the countertop with heavier breads as it increased the speed. America's Test Kitchen says the Artisan rocked and got very warm but completed all 12 extra batches of its abuse test.

KitchenAid's manual says to mix or knead yeast dough only at Speed 2. Homes & Gardens says it kept the KitchenAid on lower speeds and it still moved; Food Network doesn't say which speeds it reached. ATK notes Speed 2 got good results but took significantly longer. So the measured clock favors the Artisan, and two outlets report it walking on heavy dough. If you bake dense loaves often, read [dough hook vs paddle](/guides/dough-hook-vs-paddle/) before choosing.

### Cookies and cake batter

America's Test Kitchen and Homes & Gardens both report frequent scraping with the SM-50; Homes & Gardens says the wider bowl means the paddle doesn't touch every side. Reviewed says the SM-50 slowed when flour went into cookie dough. Homes & Gardens gave creaming to the KitchenAid and called whipped cream a tie.

## Why the Cuisinart's tools don't reach the bowl

The SM-50's most-reported weakness is the gap between its tools and its bowl. America's Test Kitchen says ingredients stayed just out of reach, TechGearLab lists a large bowl/attachment gap as a con, and Homes & Gardens says the flat beater struggled to reach the bottom on small batches.

The two manuals differ here. [KitchenAid's manual](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/KitchenAid-Artisan-Series-5Qt-Tilt-Head-KSM150PS-Use-Care.pdf) describes a screw that raises the beater (counterclockwise) or lowers it (clockwise) until it just clears the bowl. The [KitchenAid dime test](/guides/kitchenaid-dime-test/) covers how. The [SM-50 instruction booklet](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/Cuisinart-Precision-Master-55-Quart-Stand-Mixer-sm-50-UserManual.pdf) describes no height adjustment, and America's Test Kitchen lists its tool clearance as not adjustable.

## How much dough each mixer handles, per the manuals

Cuisinart's manual lists maximums of 6 cups white flour for yeast dough, 12 large egg whites and 6 cups of cream, and suggests speeds 3 to 4 for kneading bread. KitchenAid's manual covers several tilt-head models and sets limits by bowl size: no more than 8 cups all-purpose or 6 cups whole-wheat flour in a 4.8 L bowl, the size that matches the Artisan's 5 qt (the manual doesn't list bowls by model). It says to knead yeast dough only at Speed 2, warning that other speeds create high potential for unit failure.

So the smaller KitchenAid bowl is rated for more flour on paper (8 cups all-purpose vs 6 cups white). ATK's measured working capacities are lower than both labels. For batch sizing, see [what size stand mixer do I need](/guides/what-size-stand-mixer-do-i-need/).

## Does 500 W beat 325 W? What the watt rating means

Not in the measured results. KitchenAid's manual says its maximum rating is based on the attachment that draws the greatest load, and other attachments may draw significantly less. TechGearLab's numbers show the 325 W Artisan out-kneading and out-whipping the 500 W SM-50, and TechGearLab writes that some of its top performers did better with 325 watts than others did with 500. The [stand mixer wattage guide](/guides/stand-mixer-wattage-explained/) explains why the label misleads.

## Warranty, overheating and recall status

Cuisinart gives a limited three-year warranty in the US and Canada. Its overload protection shuts the motor off; the manual says to turn it off and unplug it, remove some ingredients and let it stand a few minutes. KitchenAid gives a one-year full warranty: your choice of replacement or covered parts and labor. Its manual says the top may get hot under heavy loads with extended mixing, which is normal, and to let it cool 30 minutes if it won't restart. [KitchenAid's support article](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Bowl_Lift_Mixer/Operation/Mixer_Shuts_Off_-_Stand_Mixer) says mixers with thermal overload protection need about 30 minutes to cool completely.

Owner and tester reports: Homes & Gardens says some customer reviews report Cuisinart motor issues over time. America's Test Kitchen says the Artisan's bowl sometimes got stuck on its base after hard work. More on heat cut-outs is in [stand mixer overheating](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/).

CPSC recall-title searches for KitchenAid, Cuisinart and "mixer" show no stand-mixer recall for either brand. [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/) rates False a January 2023 rumor that KitchenAid recalled mixer attachments over lead. The [stand mixer recall check](/guides/stand-mixer-recall-check/) shows how to search a model yourself.

## Attachments, dishwasher-safe parts and add-ons

The SM-50's front port takes Cuisinart's pasta maker, meat grinder and ice cream attachments, per its manual. Homes & Gardens counts 5 Cuisinart add-ons against over 14 for KitchenAid. Don't plan on swapping tools between brands; Homes & Gardens warns it could damage the machine. KitchenAid model fit is covered in [KitchenAid attachments compatibility](/guides/kitchenaid-attachments-compatibility/).

Per Cuisinart's manual, the paddle, dough hook and splash guard are top-rack dishwasher safe and the whisk is hand-wash only. Per KitchenAid's manual, the bowl, pouring shield, flat beater and white dough hook can go in the dishwasher and the wire whip can't. America's Test Kitchen lists both splash guards as hand-wash only; the manuals allow the dishwasher. Details: [are stand mixer parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-stand-mixer-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## Is the Cuisinart a cheaper alternative to the KitchenAid Artisan?

On price, yes. On Oct 5, 2026, America's Test Kitchen's listing showed the SM-50 at $217 at Amazon (on sale from $320), and Food Network's showed the Artisan at $380 at Amazon (regularly $500). TechGearLab gives list prices of $320 and $450, and Top Ten Reviews' price widget showed a "KitchenAid Artisan" at $299.99 at Amazon the same day (variant not stated). Homes & Gardens and Reviewed both call the Cuisinart about half the Artisan's price. Prices shown by review sites vary from day to day, so check the live price.

## Who should buy the Cuisinart SM-50 and who should buy the Artisan

- **Buy the KitchenAid Artisan** if you bake bread, whip small amounts, or want the larger add-on range. It beats the SM-50 on TechGearLab's whipping, kneading and beating scores, and its beater height is adjustable.
- **Buy the Cuisinart SM-50** if budget and a three-year warranty come first and you mostly make cake batter. Plan to scrape the bowl, and note Homes & Gardens found the KitchenAid creamed butter and sugar better.
