For the GE toaster vs Black and Decker toaster question, the three labs that tested both disagree. Pick the GE G9TMA2SSPSS if you toast bagels and want speed: America's Test Kitchen named it Best Conventional Toaster. Pick the BLACK+DECKER T2569B if price matters most: TechGearLab named it Best Toaster on a Budget and Reviewed found it even. Nobody at Kitchen Watcher tested either toaster.

[America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1949-long-slot-toasters) rated the Black+Decker "Recommended with Reservations". [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) ranked it above the GE, 68 to 62. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-toasters) found both toasted evenly and listed both under other toasters it tested, with no award.

## GE or Black+Decker by use case

- **Bagels:** the GE. ATK found it toasted bagels relatively evenly, and TechGearLab scored GE bagels 6.5 against 5.3. Reviewed is the outlier: it says the Black+Decker's bagel setting toasts both sides.
- **Frozen waffles:** the Black+Decker. TechGearLab scored it 7.5 against the GE's 4.5, and its GE review describes "pale and undercooked" waffles.
- **Plain sandwich bread:** close. TechGearLab scored Black+Decker bread 8.0 and GE 6.5; ATK found the opposite.
- **Lowest price:** the Black+Decker, $30 at Amazon as listed on ATK's page and $29.99 at Amazon as listed on Reviewed's page, both on Nov 6, 2026. On those listings it costs $19 to $29 less than the GE, so it is the pick if you want the cheaper toaster.
- **Warranty:** the Black+Decker, 2 years per Black+Decker's product page against 1 year for the GE per its owner's manual.
- **Exterior heat:** the Black+Decker. ATK says the GE's sides and top get hot. Reviewed says the Black+Decker stayed coolest to the touch of the models it tested, apart from the stainless steel on top of the slots.

## Why the testers disagree on the Black+Decker T2569B

The Black+Decker is where the labs split hardest. ATK wrote that it "just didn't toast evenly", and that bagels "had entire sections that appeared completely untoasted". Reviewed says it "really shines at toasting evenly" and showed minimal variation across five batches in a row. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-toaster) leans toward Reviewed: it praises batch-to-batch uniformity and scores bread 8.0. It also notes slightly uneven browning, with centers "more golden than the edges", and a shade dial running "hotter than labeled".

Method explains part of it. ATK toasted single slices of white sandwich bread on light, medium and dark, timed medium over five runs, then tried artisan bread and large sesame bagels. TechGearLab made more than 400 pieces of toast, almost 250 bagels and over 150 frozen waffles across 16 toasters. Reviewed says it looks for a range of shades, even toasting on all sides and consistency across repeated use.

A dial that runs hot may also mean the same number gives different toast from one test to the next. The three write-ups don't give me enough to settle it, so treat the T2569B's evenness as disputed, not proven either way.

## GE 2 slice toaster: what testers found

ATK found the GE produced "relatively evenly toasted slices of white bread and bagels" and it was the faster of the pair at 1 min 39 s. Its cons: the sides and top get hot, and as a conventional toaster it handles a more limited selection of breads than ATK's long-slot picks.

[TechGearLab's GE review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/toaster/ge-2-slice-g9tma2sspss) is cooler on it. It observed "some unevenness", said the highest settings gave "darker, almost burnt toast", and found the waffles "pale and undercooked". Frozen bread was its strength. It also says the stainless steel shows fingerprints and the rear crumb tray's "tiny handle" makes it less convenient to remove and clean.

Reviewed says the GE "toasts evenly and produces three distinct shades of doneness". Its one con: English muffins need the lift lever to rise high enough to grab.

## Buttons, crumb trays and daily use

Both have Bagel, Frozen and Cancel buttons and a shade dial, per the GE owner's manual and Black+Decker's product page. The [GE owner's manual](https://pdf.lowes.com/productdocuments/e17a288a-af8b-4410-ade3-d6dfb6cb7764/65455388.pdf) says the lever "won't lock down unless the toaster is connected to the electricity supply". It also says to operate the toaster empty in a well-ventilated room before first use to cure the new elements, and recommends 4 inch clearance on all sides. If your lever pops straight back up, see [toaster won't stay down](/guides/toaster-wont-stay-down/).

TechGearLab calls the Black+Decker's cord "short, inflexible, and positioned at the front". Its crumb tray is "a bit of a pain to remove" and "requires two hands to put back in". For bagel orientation and what the numbers on the dial mean, see the [toaster bagel setting guide](/guides/toaster-bagel-setting/) and [toaster settings explained](/guides/toaster-settings-explained/).

## Recall status and GE manual safety rules

Neither model appears in the CPSC recall database as of Nov 6, 2026. The 2008 GE recall ([CPSC 08-413](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2008/Wal-Mart-Recalls-General-Electric-Toasters-Due-to-Fire-and-Shock-Hazards)) covered GE models 169115 and 169116 sold by Wal-Mart, about 210,000 units. CPSC says "an electrical short circuit can occur between the heating element and the bread cage", with 140 reports of fires, sparks or tripped breakers and no injuries.

The 2007 Black & Decker recall ([CPSC 08-111](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2008/Black--Decker-Brand-Toasters-Recalled-By-Applica-Consumer-Products-Inc-Due-to-Fire-Hazard)) covered the Infrawave ST2000, about 70,000 units, where an electrical component could overheat and ignite the circuit board. An older 2002 recall ([CPSC 02-099](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2002/CPSC-Applica-Consumer-Products-Inc-Announce-Recall-of-Black--Decker-Brand-VersaToast-Toasters)) covered VersaToast models T1200, T1250, T1400 and T1450, about 2.1 million units. Neither covers the T2569B. To check your own model, use the [toaster recall check](/guides/toaster-recall-check/).

The GE manual says to "operate with crumb tray in place" and to unplug the toaster from the outlet when not in use. For stuck bread it says to unplug and let it cool, and "never use a metal object". It also says not to hold the toaster upside down or shake it to remove crumbs. I haven't read a Black+Decker T2569B manual, so I won't paraphrase its rules. If a toaster does catch fire, see [toaster fire: what to do](/guides/toaster-fire-what-to-do/).

## Drawbacks the testers reported for each toaster

TechGearLab's cons for the GE are fingerprints and uneven toasting coverage; for the Black+Decker, the inconvenient cord and the crumb tray on the back. Reviewed says English muffins don't reach the top of the Black+Decker's slots even with its high-lift lever. On the GE, they need the lift lever to rise high enough to grab. Owner reports from retailer reviews aren't summarized here yet because we haven't read them in full.

## Which should you buy: GE or Black+Decker?

Each toaster holds one lab award, and Reviewed found both toasted evenly. The Black+Decker also carries the strongest negative: ATK's verdict that it toasts unevenly.

- **Buy the GE** if you toast bagels often or want the faster toaster, and you can keep its hot sides and top clear of anything touching it.
- **Buy the Black+Decker** if you want the lowest price and longer warranty, or you toast frozen waffles.
