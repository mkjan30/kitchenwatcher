In the Hamilton Beach vs Ninja food processor choice, buy the Hamilton Beach 70730 if you chop, purée and shred but never make dough. Hamilton Beach's own guide says not to knead dough in it. Buy the Ninja BN601 for dough, better slicing and less noise: TechGearLab measured 80 vs 96 dB at 3 ft. You give up 1 cup of capacity and get a dual blade TechGearLab found harder to hand-clean.

The Hamilton Beach lists at $70 against $130 for the Ninja (TechGearLab's list prices, updated June 11, 2025) and scored higher for purée in TechGearLab's ratings (9 vs 7). Everything here comes from TechGearLab and CNET measurements, Food Network's notes, both manufacturers' manuals and the CPSC recall database. I didn't handle either machine; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Chopping and purée: Hamilton Beach finer, Ninja faster

TechGearLab scored the Hamilton Beach higher for chopping (7 vs 6) and purée (9 vs 7). Food Network found the Ninja didn't purée pesto as finely as other models it tried. CNET's pico de gallo test points the other way on speed: about two pulses on the Ninja, about seven on the Hamilton Beach, which CNET said diced evenly.

The [Hamilton Beach guide](https://useandcares.hamiltonbeach.com/files/840213703.pdf) says to cut food into 1-inch pieces, chop at most 2 to 3 cups at a time, and not to purée potatoes, which turn gummy.

## Dough decides it: the 70730 isn't meant to knead

Hamilton Beach's guide says not to use the 70730 for kneading dough, grinding grain, coffee beans or spices, slicing frozen meat or crushing ice. CNET's spec box lists a "dough hook" for it, which contradicts the manual; follow the manual. TechGearLab still made pizza dough in it (score 6) and said the unit shook "somewhat violently".

The Ninja has a DOUGH program and a dough blade in the [BN600C Series owner's guide](https://media-www.canadiantire.ca/manual/product/0430733/0430733p-en-fr-b2f015db-d198-4da5-8f5b-cef22e38a97c.pdf). The guide notes that included accessories vary by model, and CNET lists a dough hook for the BN601. The guide says "DO NOT run the unit on HIGH if using the dough blade", and rules out crushing ice and processing dry ingredients or grinding with the chopping blade. TechGearLab said its motor was "working pretty hard" on pizza dough, but it scored pie crust 8. More in [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/).

## Does either one leak? Hamilton Beach did in TechGearLab's test

In TechGearLab's water test the Hamilton Beach reached its max fill line at about 2.5 cups and leaked once the motor started. TechGearLab doesn't give a leak result for the Ninja, so there is no head-to-head evidence on the Ninja's seal. Hamilton Beach's guide warns that hot liquid can be ejected by sudden steaming and says not to fill above the max line. Ninja's guide says not to exceed the 9-cup (2.1 L) line and "DO NOT blend hot liquids", citing pressure buildup and steam burns. See [food processor leaking](/guides/food-processor-leaking/).

## Slicing and shredding: the Ninja scores higher

TechGearLab scored the Ninja 7 for both shredding and slicing, against the Hamilton Beach's 6 and 5; the Hamilton Beach partly crushed tomatoes. Hamilton Beach says to chill firm cheese at least 30 minutes and freeze soft mozzarella 30 minutes first, and its bowl scraper can't be used with the disc. Tips in [how to shred cheese](/guides/how-to-shred-cheese-in-a-food-processor/).

## Which is quieter, and which overheats? Noise, nut butter and motor rest

TechGearLab measured 96 dB for the Hamilton Beach and 80 dB for the Ninja at 3 feet, but its roundup doesn't describe the method. CNET, which tested both machines among nine, called the Ninja "perhaps the loudest" and gave no decibel figure, so the two sources conflict. Nut butter took 7 minutes on the Ninja and 10 on the Hamilton Beach, whose top wobbled, per TechGearLab.

Hamilton Beach says to rest the motor about 4 minutes after long continuous use, and after an overload shutoff to unplug and cool it 15 minutes. Ninja's thermal switch needs about 15 minutes unplugged. See [food processor overheating](/guides/food-processor-overheating/).

## Cleaning: both scored 5, but sources split on the Ninja

Both scored 5 for cleaning at TechGearLab, which said the Ninja's dual-blade design is harder to clean by hand. Food Network called the Ninja easy to hand-clean, so the evidence conflicts. Hamilton Beach allows the top rack but not the SANI cycle; Ninja says top rack only, with the blade removed from the bowl first. Details in [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/).

## Safety locks, suction feet and parts storage compared

Neither runs until the bowl and lid are locked. On the Ninja, flashing panel lights mean the bowl or lid isn't seated, and its suction feet won't grip wood, tile or unpolished surfaces, per its guide. The Hamilton Beach stores its parts inside the bowl. If either won't start, see [food processor won't turn on](/guides/food-processor-wont-turn-on/).

## Owner ratings, durability and recall status

CNET reported (April 2025) 4.6 stars from more than 35,000 Amazon ratings for the Hamilton Beach and 4.8 from more than 5,000 for the Ninja. TechGearLab's cons: the Hamilton Beach is loud, leaks and mixes poorly; the Ninja is hard to clean by hand. These are TechGearLab's findings, not owner reports. No source measured lifespan, so long-term durability evidence is thin.

The CPSC database shows no food processor recall for Ninja or SharkNinja. Its only Hamilton Beach food processor entry, 14-193 (May 2014), covers bébé baby food makers, not the 70730. See the [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/).

## Which to buy: Hamilton Beach or Ninja

- **Dough or pie crust:** Ninja. Hamilton Beach says not to knead in the 70730.
- **Hummus, purées, salsa:** Hamilton Beach (purée 9 vs 7, TechGearLab).
- **Soups and liquids:** neither is a clear pick. The Hamilton Beach leaked at its max line in TechGearLab's test, and Ninja's guide says not to blend hot liquids.
- **Noise-sensitive homes:** Ninja (80 vs 96 dB, TechGearLab).
- **Tightest budget:** Hamilton Beach ($70 vs $130 list, TechGearLab).
