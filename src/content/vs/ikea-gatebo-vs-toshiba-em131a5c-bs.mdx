For plain microwaving, the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS is the stronger machine. In the IKEA GÅTEBO vs Toshiba microwave matchup, [RTINGS measured](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/tools/compare/ikea-gatebo-vs-toshiba-em131a5c-bs/127814/43507) a 737 W heating rate against 571 W, a 1.06 vs 0.74 cu ft chamber and 45.8 vs 49.2 dBA. Choose the IKEA GÅTEBO only if you will air fry, grill or bake in it, or want its 5-year guarantee.

RTINGS' own verdict is that the Toshiba is better for most people: more powerful, quieter, roomier and with a cooking sensor. The GÅTEBO's case is what the Toshiba can't do at all, plus four more years of warranty. Nobody at Kitchen Watcher has used either oven; everything below comes from the sources linked on this page.

## Choose the Toshiba if you mostly reheat; choose the GÅTEBO if it replaces an oven

**Choose the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS if:**

- You mostly reheat, defrost and cook frozen food. Its sensor menu covers Sensor Reheat, Frozen Pizza, Frozen Entrée, Potato, Rice and Veggie, per [Toshiba's manual](https://images.homedepot-static.com/catalog/pdfImages/fb/fb6df202-147c-4227-8107-017db04f8ed2.pdf).
- You use bigger dishes: its turntable is 12.4 in against the GÅTEBO's 11.3 in (both manuals).
- You want the cheaper of the two. On Nov 27, 2026 (Black Friday week), Tom's Guide's price widget showed the Toshiba at $133.97 at Amazon, a sale price marked down from $148.99; IKEA's own page listed the GÅTEBO at $199.00 with no markdown.

**Choose the IKEA GÅTEBO if:**

- You have no oven or air fryer and little counter space. As an IKEA microwave air fryer, it adds convection at 100–425°F, a grill and two-stage cooking, per IKEA's US manual.
- A 5-year limited guarantee matters more to you than speed. Toshiba's warranty is one year.

## Power: what 900 W vs 1,100 W means in heating speed

RTINGS' heating-rate test puts the GÅTEBO at 571 W and the Toshiba at 737 W, with power scores of 6.8 and 9.4. [RTINGS' GÅTEBO review](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/ikea/gatebo) adds that many 900 W models heat faster than the GÅTEBO, though "it's not a major difference." In this combi microwave vs regular microwave pairing, the combi gives up plain heating speed for extra modes.

In [Tom's Guide's first impressions](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/kitchen-dining/ikea-gatebo-3-in-1-microwave-first-impressions), Millie Fender reported tomato soup hot in 3 min 30 s with one stir, and pasta in 4 minutes. Tom's gives no matching Toshiba time, so no source has timed both on the same food.

The wattage figures themselves are muddled. IKEA's US manual lists 900 W output in its spec table but elsewhere says the oven is rated 1,000 W by the IEC test. For the Toshiba, [Tom's Guide's roundup](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/best-microwaves,review-6316.html) lists 1,550 W in its spec box (the manual's input rating) and 1,000 W in its text, while the manual says 1,100 W output. More on reading these labels in [what wattage microwave do I need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Space: both ovens hold less than advertised

The GÅTEBO's manual says 0.94 cu ft and notes that actual food capacity is less; RTINGS measured 0.74 cu ft. The Toshiba is advertised at 1.2 cu ft and RTINGS measured 1.06. RTINGS measured chamber heights of 7.8 vs 9.3 in and depths of 12.4 vs 15.2 in.

RTINGS says the GÅTEBO fits most smaller dinner plates but not a large casserole dish. Outside, the two take similar counter space: RTINGS measured both at 17.0 in deep with the handle. Sources disagree on the GÅTEBO's turntable, 11.3 in in the manual and RTINGS against 11.42 in on IKEA's page and Tom's. See [what size microwave do I need](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/).

## What the GÅTEBO adds: air fry, convection, grill and two-stage cooking

IKEA's US manual rates the grill at 1,000 W and convection at 1,350 W. Convection and air fry run at ten settings from 100°F to 425°F, and there are microwave-plus-convection and microwave-plus-grill combinations and two-stage cooking. The air fry basket and grill tray sit on the glass tray and must not be used in microwave-only mode. The manual's frozen-fries guide is 300 g for 25:00 at 425°F.

Tom's Guide quotes different numbers: up to 240°C/460°F, and a 21-minute fries time it attributes to the manual. The [Gadgeteer](https://the-gadgeteer.com/2026/01/16/ikea-gatebo-the-199-microwave-that-can-replace-an-oven-for-small-kitchens/) lists 150–240°C and notes that UK and European models are rated 2,100 W at 230–240 V and that specs differ by region. Neither matches the US manual, so US buyers should follow it.

In [Tom's Guide's air-fryer face-off](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/air-fryers/can-ikeas-new-3-in-1-microwave-really-replace-your-air-fryer-i-compared-them-to-find-out), fries needed the full 21 minutes and were far less crisp and browned than those from Fender's Dreo ChefMaker air fryer. A chicken skewer took about twice as long. She concluded she would not replace her air fryer with it.

In her first-impressions piece, a grilled cheese on the grill setting took 6 minutes with a flip, and she found the bread quite dry. If air frying is the main goal, see the [best air fryers](/best/air-fryers/) and [whether a microwave air fryer combo is worth it](/guides/microwave-air-fryer-combo-worth-it/).

## What the Toshiba adds: a sensor menu and a deeper chamber

Toshiba's manual lists six sensor programs, a Favorite memory pad and a child lock held for 3 seconds. RTINGS confirms the steam sensor and a mute button. Tom's Guide contradicts itself, saying the Toshiba has no sensors and then praising its humidity sensor. The full picture is in the [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS review](/reviews/toshiba-em131a5c-bs/).

## IKEA GÅTEBO review: what RTINGS, Tom's Guide and owners found

RTINGS (review published 2026-03-03) scores the GÅTEBO 6.7 for everyday use. Its pros are fairly quick heating, easy simple tasks and quiet running. Its cons are slower heating than many 900 W models, small capacity, awkward complex tasks and a membrane panel. Tom's Guide calls it its best combi microwave, but lists sensor cooking as a pro, which RTINGS and the US manual contradict.

Owner evidence is thin. IKEA's US page showed 4.8 of 5 from only 4 reviews on 2026-11-27, and [House Digest](https://www.housedigest.com/2081494/ikea-microwave-compare-competititon) notes how few verified reviews exist. House Digest says it hit shelves in December 2025, so long-term durability is unknown.

## Controls and noise: the Toshiba runs quieter

RTINGS rates both 7.0 for ease of use. Both have membrane touch panels, mute and lock buttons, and need 7 presses for 2:30 at 50%. The GÅTEBO also has a door lock, on by default, that locks the door during heating and for 30 minutes after, though it can be unlocked at any time, per its manual. The Toshiba is quieter, 45.8 vs 49.2 dBA (RTINGS).

IKEA's manual says not to use abrasives or metal scrapers on the door glass and to keep the waveguide cover clean. More in [how to clean a microwave](/guides/how-to-clean-a-microwave/).

## Warranty, the CR safety alert and recall status

IKEA's limited guarantee runs 5 years from purchase with the receipt; Toshiba's warranty is one year (both manuals). As of 2026-11-27, the CPSC lists no recall for either model.

[Consumer Reports' June 25, 2026 safety alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/), which is not a recall, counts 28 SaferProducts.gov reports of Toshiba-brand microwaves starting or adding time on their own. One cites model EM131A5C (CR gives no color suffix); 24 involve the ML2-EC10SA combo. CR says it has not tested these models and has not experienced a similar problem with the Toshibas it has tested. Details: [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/) and the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## If neither fits: a stronger combo or a cheaper plain model

For a combo with more power, [RTINGS says](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/tools/compare/ikea-gatebo-vs-toshiba-ml-ec42p-ss/127814/43528) the Toshiba ML-EC42P(SS) is a better choice than the GÅTEBO for most people (power score 8.5 vs 6.8). For a plain budget microwave, see [Toshiba EM131A5C-BS vs Black+Decker EM925AB9](/vs/toshiba-em131a5c-bs-vs-black-decker-em925ab9/).

**Bottom line:** buy the Toshiba if you mostly reheat and want speed, room and a sensor. Buy the GÅTEBO if it has to stand in for an oven or air fryer and the 5-year guarantee matters to you.
