Instant Pot Pro vs Breville Fast Slow Pro: for most buyers, the evidence favors the Instant Pot Pro 8-qt. America's Test Kitchen named it its top multicooker. On Nov 13, 2026, ATK's page listed it at $150 at Amazon against $330 for the Breville. Pick the Breville if slow cooking and automatic steam release matter more than price: Consumer Reports calls it "masterful at slow cooking".

- **Choose the Instant Pot Pro** if you want a stainless pot with stay-cool handles that "allows for even searing", per [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2242-multicookers), more capacity (7.6 L vs 6 L, manufacturer spec) or the lower price.
- **Choose the Breville Fast Slow Pro** if you slow-cook often (beef stew tender in 5 hours, per [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/multi-cookers/best-multi-cookers-of-the-year-a7136971834)) or want the cooker to vent itself (praised by [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-pressure-cooker)).

## Searing and sauté: stainless pot vs ceramic nonstick

The Instant Pot Pro's flat-bottomed stainless pot "allows for even searing", per America's Test Kitchen, which lists an 8.25-inch cooking surface and praised the stay-cool handles. Instant's manual calls the pot stovetop-friendly and oven-safe to 450 °F, and lists five sauté levels.

The Breville's 6-quart bowl has a ceramic coating with three heat settings for sauté and reduce, per its booklet. ATK found it "took longer to heat up, but it seared beef well". Consumer Reports saw no scratches on the coating after its tests. [Wirecutter](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/reviews/best-electric-pressure-cooker/) notes the Breville's nonstick pot has no handles, while the Pro's stainless pot does.

## Slow cooking: the Breville's clearest advantage

This is where the evidence favors the Breville. Consumer Reports found its beef stew tender in 5 hours and its chili tender in 7 hours on slow cook. ATK also says it slow-cooked beef stew.

The Instant Pot Pro is weaker here: ATK says it "couldn't slow-cook large cuts of meat well". Instant's own manual says to set slow cook for 4 hours or more and warns, "Cooking less than 3 hours will result in uncooked food."

## Pressure levels on paper: the two Breville manuals point different ways

Instant's Pro manual lists Low at 5.8–7.2 psi (30–50 kPa) and High at 10.2–11.6 psi (70–90 kPa). Breville's [US instruction book](https://assets.breville.com/BPR700/BPR700_USC_IB_G17.pdf) lists eight pressure levels in psi: presets from 3.0 psi (Dessert) and 6.0 psi (Risotto) up to 12.0 psi for Stock, Beans, Meat and Bone-in Meat, and an adjustable range of 1.5 to 12.0 psi. Breville's [Australian booklet](https://s.productreview.com.au/products/manuals/316271_56564e59ba9b1.pdf) gives the same machine in kPa: presets from 40 kPa for risotto to 80 kPa for stock, legumes and casseroles, adjustable from 10 to 80 kPa.

Set beside Instant's figures, the US booklet's 12.0 psi top setting sits above the Pro's 11.6 psi High ceiling, while the Australian booklet's 80 kPa top sits below the Pro's 90 kPa ceiling. We can't say which reflects the machine better, and we haven't converted one manual's units into the other's. These are printed specs, not measurements.

No source we read timed both machines to pressure. Wirecutter timed the Pro 6-qt at 14 minutes 24 seconds with a pot of beans and found no significant difference from other Instant Pots. ATK only says the Breville took longer than other models to reach its maximum pressure-cooking temperature.

## Steam release and the attached lid: convenience vs cleaning

The Breville can vent itself at the end of cooking (Auto Quick or Auto Pulse), per its US booklet, and TechGearLab liked that the valve opens automatically or at the push of a button. Consumer Reports also notes its lid doesn't lock during steaming. The trade-off is the lid. ATK says it is attached and stands upright when open, and its testers' hands bumped it while sautéing, which triggered a safety shutoff. Removing it for cleaning means unscrewing the sealing nut and pulling the lid off its arm, per the booklet, and ATK and TechGearLab both flag this as a chore.

The Instant Pot Pro's lid lifts off the base, per its manual, and ATK liked its pressure-release switch and the diffuser on the vent. Both manuals forbid forcing the lid. Instant's says "NEVER attempt to force the lid open", and Breville's says "do not force lid open" if it won't turn to unlock.

## Breville Fast Slow Pro review evidence: praise, reservations, owner reports

Recommenders split on the Breville. TechGearLab made it its Runner Up at 84/100, and Consumer Reports calls it "one of our top-rated multi-cookers". ATK gave it only Recommended with Reservations.

Owner data is thin and mostly Australian. On Nov 13, 2026, [ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/breville-fast-slow-pro-bpr700bss) showed a 3.9 average from 259 reviews, with Build Quality at 3.3, Value for Money at 3.1, Pressure Cooking at 3.6 and Slow Cooking highest at 4.8. Owners there report lids that won't seal, units that take very long or fail to reach pressure, units that stopped working around the two-year mark, and warranty disputes. We found few US owner reviews to compare.

The [CPSC recall record](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Breville) shows one Breville pressure-cooker recall: 16-066 in December 2015, for about 35,600 units of the older BPR600XL, whose gasket could be inserted upside down. It does not cover the BPR700.

## Instant Pot Pro 8-qt evidence in brief

ATK named the Pro 8-qt its winner, and Instant's manual lists the Pro 80 at 15.83 lb (manufacturer spec).

Beyond slow cooking, Wirecutter, which tested the Pro 6-qt, says its bake setting left "uncooked goo" in small cake rounds even after an hour. The [saferproducts.gov records](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=Instant%20Pot) show no recall for the Pro line; the only Instant Pot entry is a 2015 recall alert for Instant Pot "Smart" cookers. The full picture is in our [Instant Pot Pro review](/reviews/instant-pot-pro-8qt/).

## Capacity and fill rules: 7.6 L vs 6 L

The Pro 80 holds 8 qt (7.6 L) against the Breville's 6 qt (6 L) (both manufacturer specs). The Pro manual asks for at least 2 cups of liquid in the 8-qt (1½ cups in the 6-qt), with a Max PC Fill line and a Half Fill line for foods that expand.

Breville's US booklet marks a MAX line at 4.5 quarts, a ½ line at 3 quarts for foods that expand, and a MIN line at 1 quart of food and liquid combined for pressure cooking, slow cooking or steaming. (The Australian booklet words its minimum differently: the level 2 mark or one-third full, and 4 cups / 1 litre of liquid when steaming.)

The US booklet also says apple sauce, cranberries, pearl barley, oatmeal or other cereals, split peas, noodles, macaroni, rhubarb and spaghetti can foam and clog the release valve and "should not be cooked in a pressure cooker." See [what size Instant Pot you need](/guides/what-size-instant-pot/) and [how much water goes in a pressure cooker](/guides/how-much-water-in-pressure-cooker/).

## Safety rules both manuals share, and the canning conflict

Both forbid pressure frying with oil: Breville's US booklet says "Do not use this appliance for pressure frying with oil", and Instant's Pro manual rules out deep frying or pressure frying with oil. Breville's Australian booklet goes further, also ruling out deep frying and adding "Do not sauté or shallow fry under pressure". Release methods are covered in [natural release vs quick release](/guides/natural-release-vs-quick-release/).

The Pro manual says Low pressure is "Also for canning of high-acid foods", but the [National Center for Home Food Preservation](https://nchfp.uga.edu/publications/nchfp/factsheets/electric_cookers.html) says it does not support USDA canning processes in electric multi-cookers. We don't recommend it; see [can you can in an Instant Pot](/guides/can-you-can-in-an-instant-pot/). The Pro manual's optional water test is to fill the pot to line 1 with water and run High pressure for 5 minutes. Other models are covered in the [Instant Pot water test](/guides/instant-pot-water-test/) guide.

## Instant Pot Pro vs Breville Fast Slow Pro: which should you buy?

If you are choosing between these two premium pressure cookers, buy the Instant Pot Pro 8-qt if you mainly pressure-cook and sear: it is ATK's top pick, at $150 at Amazon on Nov 13, 2026. Buy the Breville Fast Slow Pro only if slow cooking and automatic venting justify $330 at Amazon on the same date, and you accept the attached lid and mixed owner reliability reports. It ranks last of six on our [best pressure cookers](/best/pressure-cookers/) list for that reason.
