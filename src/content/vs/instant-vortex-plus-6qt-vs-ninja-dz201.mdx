Ninja DZ201 vs Instant Vortex Plus 6 qt comes down to baskets versus accuracy. Pick the DZ201 if you routinely cook a main and a side at once or feed four or more. Pick the Vortex Plus if you cook for three to four (TechGearLab's sizing) and want accurate heat in one basket for less money. At a 400°F setting, [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer) measured the Vortex Plus at 401.5°F and the DZ201 at 386.4°F.

Kitchen Watcher compiles independent lab data and manufacturer manuals rather than testing hands-on; see [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## 2 basket vs single basket air fryer: what the DZ201 buys you

Two baskets let you cook two foods at different settings and have them finish together. Per Ninja's [DZ201 owner's guide](https://www.teklib.com/library/ninja-foodi-6in1-8qt-2basket-air-fryer-dz201-manual/), Smart Finish times both zones to end at once, and Match Cook copies Zone 1's settings to Zone 2. Each zone has its own heating element and fan. Both are manufacturer features.

The trade-off is basket size. Food Network lists the small 4-quart size of each basket as the DZ201's main con, so a single large item or a big batch of wings fits better in the Vortex's one 6-quart basket. Total floor space still favors the Ninja, 98.4 versus 87.8 square inches per TechGearLab.

The two DZ201 zones are not identical in practice. [TechGearLab's DZ201 review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/air-fryer/ninja-foodi-8-quart-2-basket) found the left basket cooked faster than the right. For sizing by household beyond these two models, see [what size air fryer you need](/guides/what-size-air-fryer-do-i-need/).

## Which one holds temperature better, and which cooks better?

The Vortex Plus holds its set temperature better in TechGearLab's measurements: within 1.5°F of target, while the DZ201 averaged about 14°F cool at a 400°F setting. TechGearLab's own DZ201 review text is kinder, calling its temperature accuracy stellar and within a few degrees of the set point, so its written verdict and its measured table disagree.

Yet TechGearLab scored the Ninja higher on cooking quality (7.7 vs 7.0) and cooking speed (8.6 vs 8.0). These are separate metrics, so the sources don't settle which one "cooks better". Instant's manual adds a Turn Food prompt partway through air frying, and cooking resumes after 10 seconds if you ignore it. TechGearLab also notes the DZ201 has no auto-pause when you pull a basket, so pause it first; see the [DZ201 review](/reviews/ninja-dz201/).

## Which takes less counter space and runs quieter?

The Vortex Plus has the smaller footprint, 12.2 x 12.1 in against the DZ201's 15.3 x 12 in per TechGearLab, but it stands taller at 14.9 in. Instant's manual asks for at least 5 inches of space around the unit and says not to fill the basket more than 3/4 full. Food Network found the Vortex fan loud, up to 66 dB. None of the sources we read published a dB figure for the DZ201.

## Ease of use and cleaning

TechGearLab rated the Vortex easier to use (8.0 vs 6.4) and the Ninja easier to clean (8.0 vs 7.0). Consumer Reports lists dishwasher-safe parts for the DZ201; Instant's manual says the Vortex cooking tray is dishwasher safe and the basket should be wiped with a damp cloth and mild soap.

Each has a known complaint. TechGearLab still tasted plastic in fries and tots from the Vortex after repeated cleaning and preheating; see [new air fryer plastic smell](/guides/new-air-fryer-plastic-smell/). Consumer Reports lists nonstick baskets on the DZ201; what to do if a nonstick coating starts peeling is in [air fryer basket coating peeling](/guides/air-fryer-basket-coating-peeling/). For routine care, see [how to clean an air fryer](/guides/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer/).

## Running cost: 1690 W vs 1700 W

Rated power is effectively the same, 1690 W for the DZ201 per its owner's guide and 1700 W for the Vortex per Instant's manual. Neither source gives a per-zone figure, so cook time is what drives the difference. The math is in [air fryer electricity cost](/guides/air-fryer-electricity-cost/).

## Error codes and recall status

Instant's manual lists E1 (open circuit) and E2 (temperature sensor short circuit), both with the instruction to contact Consumer Care; see [Instant Vortex Plus error codes](/guides/instant-vortex-plus-error-codes/). The DZ201 owner's guide shows a generic "E" message meaning the unit isn't working properly and to contact customer service. Before buying either model, search both model numbers in the [CPSC recall database](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls).
