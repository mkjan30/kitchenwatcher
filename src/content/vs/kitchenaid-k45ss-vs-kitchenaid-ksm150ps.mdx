In the KitchenAid Classic vs Artisan choice, buy the **Artisan KSM150PS** if you bake bread often, want a bowl handle or want a color beyond white, black or silver. Buy the **Classic K45SS** if you bake now and then and want to save money. Per KitchenAid's manual, the Artisan's bowl takes 8 cups of all-purpose flour against 7, and it draws 325 W against 275 W max. The measured gap is small: TechGearLab whipped 1 cup of cream in 1 min 50 s on the Artisan and 2.08 minutes (about 2 min 5 s) on the Classic.

The clearest measured difference is durability. America's Test Kitchen (ATK) ran both through dough abuse testing, and only the Classic's tilt head broke. Everything here comes from the manual and published lab results; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

## Which to buy: Artisan for bread and a handle, Classic for value

- **Artisan if you knead bread weekly.** [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-stand-mixer) scored its kneading 10 against 7 for the Classic, and it finished all 12 extra batches in ATK's abuse test.
- **Artisan if you want a bowl handle.** Only the Artisan has one, per TechGearLab and ATK.
- **Artisan if color matters.** TechGearLab counts 29 Artisan colors against 2 for the Classic; ATK says "upward of 20", and [Prudent Reviews](https://prudentreviews.com/kitchenaid-classic-vs-artisan/) says over 20 for the Artisan and black, white or silver for the Classic, adding that colors change each year.
- **Classic if you bake occasionally.** ATK measured slightly more usable bowl volume in the Classic and prices the Artisan about $150 higher "for roughly equivalent size and performance".

## 4.5 vs 5 quarts: what each bowl actually holds

The extra half quart is worth one cup of all-purpose flour. [KitchenAid's manual](https://www.williams-sonoma.com/netstorage/pdf/KitchenAid-Artisan-Series-5Qt-Tilt-Head-KSM150PS-Use-Care.pdf) says not to use recipes with more than 7 cups in the 4.28-L bowl or 8 cups in the 4.8-L bowl. Both stop at 6 cups of whole-wheat flour.

ATK measured usable capacity and got the opposite order: 3.25 qt for the Classic and 3 qt for the Artisan, both well under the label. ATK doesn't explain why, so treat the manual's flour limits as the practical guide. For general sizing, see [what size stand mixer do I need](/guides/what-size-stand-mixer-do-i-need/).

## 275 W vs 325 W: the motor gap in measured numbers

The manual lists both ratings as "Watts MAX" and says the maximum rating "is based on the attachment that draws the greatest load". Prudent Reviews adds that wattage measures how much electricity a motor can draw, not how much force reaches the dough. [Stand mixer wattage explained](/guides/stand-mixer-wattage-explained/) covers why that number predicts little on its own.

The measured whipping gap is about 15 seconds. TechGearLab timed 1 cup of cream at 1.83 minutes (1 min 50 s) on the Artisan and 2.08 minutes (about 2 min 5 s) on the Classic, and half a cup at 1.75 and 2.00 minutes. Its [Classic review](https://www.techgearlab.com/reviews/kitchen/stand-mixer/kitchenaid-classic-series) also found vanilla and sugar left at the bottom of the bowl after the half-cup whip.

Prudent Reviews measured power draw and said the rating gap "barely showed up" in real mixing. Both mixers drew roughly 90 to 110 W while kneading pizza dough.

## Bread dough is where the Artisan pulls ahead

TechGearLab saw its clearest gap on kneading: 10 for the Artisan, 7 for the Classic. The Artisan kneaded its whole-wheat sandwich dough in 7 minutes; the Classic's passed the windowpane test at nine. The Classic's focaccia rose about 1.5 inches against 1.75 to 2 inches for most others, and TechGearLab's Classic reviewer allowed that temperature and humidity could have played a part. Prudent Reviews disagrees with the gap, saying both handled pizza dough well.

The manual sets the same rule for both: don't exceed Speed 2 with yeast dough, because "Use of any other speed creates high potential for unit failure". [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-stand-mixer) reported the Artisan "jumped on the countertop" with heavier breads as the speed rose. For tool and speed choices, see [dough hook vs paddle](/guides/dough-hook-vs-paddle/) and the [KitchenAid mixer speed guide](/guides/kitchenaid-mixer-speed-guide/).

## Durability: ATK's abuse test, heat and recall status

ATK says that during abuse testing the Artisan "rocked and got very warm, but completed all 12 extra batches in a row of bread and pizza dough". The Classic's "tilt head broke on an extreme abuse test". ATK also notes the Artisan's bowl sometimes sticks on its base after hard work. TechGearLab's Classic review says its motor "heats up during extended use and struggles slightly with dense mixtures", and that the motor sound changed noticeably at the highest speed.

Some heat is normal. The manual says under heavy loads with extended mixing "the top of the unit may become hot. This is normal." If the mixer fails to operate, the manual says to turn it off for 10 to 15 seconds; if it still won't start, let it cool for 30 minutes. More in [stand mixer overheating](/guides/stand-mixer-overheating/) and [KitchenAid mixer won't turn on](/guides/kitchenaid-mixer-wont-turn-on/).

A search of the CPSC recall database for "mixer" returns four recalls (MEDport, Rival, Hamilton Beach and Sunbeam), none from KitchenAid. [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/) rated the viral KitchenAid "lead recall" rumor False in January 2023 and found no evidence of any KitchenAid mixer recall. See the [stand mixer recall check](/guides/stand-mixer-recall-check/).

## Bowl handle, pouring shield and speed lever

The Artisan's bowl has a handle and the Classic's doesn't, per TechGearLab and ATK; TechGearLab's Classic reviewer missed it when moving heavy mixtures. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/best-stand-mixers) says the Artisan's speed lever has sharp edges and, because it offers some resistance, it's easy to push too hard and start the mixer faster than intended.

The pouring shield is a source conflict. TechGearLab, ATK, Food Network and Reviewed list one with the Artisan, and TechGearLab and ATK say the Classic has none. Prudent Reviews says neither includes one, and the manual marks it "Accessory sold separately". Check the box contents on the listing before you buy if you want one.

## Same attachments, same cleaning rules

TechGearLab lists an attachment hub on both. KitchenAid's [Beater Care support page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Stand_Mixers/Tilt_Head_Mixer/Beater_Concerns/Beater_Care) says its stainless beaters fit all tilt-head models except the Artisan Mini and the KN15E1X. ATK says KitchenAid's coated aluminum parts "become chipped after frequent use", and TechGearLab says the Classic's coating can chip with heavy use. Details in [KitchenAid attachments compatibility](/guides/kitchenaid-attachments-compatibility/).

Per the manual, the bowl, flat beater and white dough hook may go in the dishwasher, and "the wire whip is not dishwasher-safe". TechGearLab's Classic review agrees the whisk needs hand washing, though ATK's spec list calls the Classic's whisk dishwasher-safe; follow the manual. See [are stand mixer parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-stand-mixer-parts-dishwasher-safe/) and [how to clean a stand mixer](/guides/how-to-clean-a-stand-mixer/).

## Is the Artisan worth about $150 more?

Both carry KitchenAid's One Year Full Warranty, with a choice of hassle-free replacement or service at an authorized center, per the manual. ATK puts the Artisan about $150 above the Classic, and Prudent Reviews says about 25% more; prices vary by color and sale.
