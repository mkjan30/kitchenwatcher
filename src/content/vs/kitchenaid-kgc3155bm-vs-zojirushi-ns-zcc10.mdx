It is ATK's Winner, Reviewed's Best Overall and Serious Eats' test-kitchen cooker. It was $242 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026, per Serious Eats' listing ($239 in ATK's). Pick the KitchenAid KGC3155BM if you cook beans, oats or grains several times a week and want a scale and water tank to do the measuring.

ATK rates the KitchenAid Highly Recommended and Reviewed calls it its Best Upgrade. Serious Eats' listing showed it at $260 at Amazon on Nov 18, 2026, marked down from $400.

## Which makes better rice? What ATK, Reviewed, Serious Eats and CHOICE found

### Zojirushi NS-ZCC10: what the reviews found

[ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment-reviews/2328-rice-cookers), which says it cooked 50 batches of rice, found it "consistently produced excellent rice in both small and large batches." Its catch: long-grain white cooks best on the Mixed setting, not White. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-rice-cookers) tested brown, short-grain white and jasmine rice in it and says it consistently made the best-quality rice of any cooker Reviewed tried.

[Serious Eats](https://www.seriouseats.com/the-best-rice-cookers) calls it "fuzzy logic done well" but notes brown rice took a long time. More detail is in the [Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 review](/reviews/zojirushi-ns-zcc10/).

### KitchenAid Grain and Rice Cooker: what the reviews found

ATK says it "turned out perfectly cooked brown and white rice every time," though without fuzzy logic. Reviewed reports the rice was fluffy and tender. Serious Eats named it the most foolproof cooker and points to its "toothsome Firm setting," but says it has fewer rice-specific features than the Zojirushi or Cuckoo. CHOICE, which reviewed the Australian-market "Rice and Grain" without giving a model number, gave perfect scores to its first runs, then saw inconsistent cook times on later runs that improved after a settings refresh.

ATK, Reviewed and Serious Eats each tested both cookers in the same roundup, but none published a head-to-head score for just these two. The fair reading is that both make very good rice, and the Zojirushi has more top-slot wins.

## Beans, oats and grains: where the KitchenAid grain and rice cooker earns its price

KitchenAid's [cook-time and capacity table](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Cooking_Tips/Cooking_Times_and_Capacity_-_Grain_and_Rice_Cooker) lists approximate times (manufacturer spec): white rice 35 min, brown 70, quinoa 25, steel-cut oats 60, lentils 35, and beans 90 soaked or 150 dry. Barley and farro run 55 min at up to 1.6 C dry.

CHOICE lists these cooking times for its initial runs: jasmine 35 min, short-grain brown 70, steel-cut oats 50, quinoa 24, chickpeas 120 and beans 65. Reviewed reports quinoa was perfectly cooked, black beans came out "toothsome and flavorful" and steamed dumplings soft.

The Zojirushi's manual lists rice and porridge menus only. Zojirushi's [rice-cooker FAQ](https://www.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers) says its cookers "are specifically designed for rice," and that beans need different times and soaking. For quinoa in a standard cooker, see [can you cook quinoa in a rice cooker](/guides/can-you-cook-quinoa-in-a-rice-cooker/).

## How a rice cooker with a scale works, and where it fails

A rice cooker with a scale replaces the measuring cup. Per KitchenAid's [preset rice cycle page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Cycles_and_Functions/Using_the_Preset_Rice_Cycle_-_Grain_and_Rice_Cooker), you fill the tank to MAX, pick the rice type and pour in the dry rice. The scale reads it in cups, ounces or grams, and the cooker then calculates and dispenses the water. Liquid shouldn't be added while it weighs.

KitchenAid's [Not Weighing Ingredients](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Operation/Not_Weighing_Ingredients) page says the cooker must sit level with all four feet down and nothing leaning on it. If food went in too early, lift the pot out for 3 seconds while the scale recalibrates; too little or too much food flashes a red message. The [Water Fill Message](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Error_Codes/Water_Fill_Message) page says that if the tank isn't seated correctly, water can't be detected, and calls for a DESCALE cleaning every 2–4 months.

CHOICE found the scale accurate but says it sometimes needed taring, and it flags food that isn't leveled. CHOICE's advice: keep the cooker level, the tank water at room temperature, and the bowl base and cook plate free of residue and moisture, and let it cool at least 30 minutes between cycles. The touch controls occasionally ignored taps when residue got on the screen. Serious Eats notes a KitchenAid Compact model has a scale but no water reservoir.

## Official cook times: KitchenAid 35 minutes, Zojirushi 48 to 55

These are manufacturer figures, not measurements. The NS-ZCC manual gives, for the 1.0 L (5.5-cup) size at 120 V with 64.4°F water in a 68°F room: Regular white 48–55 min, Quick 36–42, Mixed 57–61 and Brown 84–107. KitchenAid's support table gives white 35 min and brown 70.

Faster isn't automatically better. ATK found that cookers with lower temperatures at the start and longer cooking periods made the best rice. Zojirushi's FAQ says its micom cookers soak the rice about 30 minutes and steam it about 15, which is why they can take longer than the stovetop.

## Counter size, keep-warm limits and cleanup compared

### Counter size

ATK says the KitchenAid is "about the size of a mini toaster oven," and Serious Eats calls it the biggest cooker it tested, largely because of the tank (elsewhere on the same page it says only an Instant model was bigger). CHOICE disagrees, calling the footprint "relatively small" and about the space of a typical rice cooker or small air fryer. Measure your counter against the larger figures in the table.

### Keep-warm limits

KitchenAid's [Keep Warm page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Cycles_and_Functions/Using_the_Keep_Warm_Function_-_Grain_and_Rice_Cooker) says it "defaults to 2 hours or can be manually set for up to 6 hours." KitchenAid's rice-cycle page says it switches to Keep Warm "for 6 hours," and CHOICE reports "up to two hours."

The Zojirushi manual says: "Do not keep rice warm for more than 12 hours." Its Extended Keep Warm holds "approx 140° F," returns to regular keep warm after 8 hours, and can't be used after 12 hours or more of regular keep warm. ATK reports the Zojirushi kept rice at a food-safe temperature for 12 hours in its test; food-safety detail is in [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

### Cleanup

KitchenAid's [cleaning page](https://producthelp.kitchenaid.com/Countertop_Appliances/Countertop_Cooking/Rice_and_Grain_Cooker/Grain_and_Rice_Cooker/Cleaning_and_Maintenance/Cleaning_the_Grain_and_Rice_Cooker) says the ceramic pot is dishwasher-safe but hand washing is recommended to maximize the life of the non-stick coating. KitchenAid says to clean the pot, inner lid and steam collector after every cycle, run DESCALE (water and vinegar) every 2–4 months, use PRE-WASH POT for stuck residue, and avoid abrasive cleaners. ATK adds that the reservoir is one more part to clean.

The Zojirushi manual rules out vinegar in the pan and abrasive cleansers, and its FAQ says parts are not dishwasher-safe. See [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).

## Drawbacks the testers found, recalls and thin long-term evidence

Zojirushi: Reviewed says the control panel isn't intuitive and needed a firm press, and ATK flags the Mixed-setting quirk for long-grain white. KitchenAid: price and size (Reviewed, ATK), scale sensitivity and missed touch-screen taps (CHOICE), and no rice paddle in the box (Serious Eats). CHOICE borrowed its unit from KitchenAid for a limited time and couldn't judge long-term consistency, so treat reliability evidence as thin.

Neither model appears in the CPSC Recall API when searched by product name ("cooker") and by brand on Nov 18, 2026. The KitchenAid recalls it lists are electric kettles (2018), coffeemakers and toasters (2005) and food processors (2000), and it lists no Zojirushi recall. A 2023 social-media claim that KitchenAid recalled stand-mixer attachments over lead is rated False by [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kitchenaid-recall-lead/). See the [rice cooker recall check](/guides/rice-cooker-recall-check/).

## Which should you buy: Zojirushi for rice, KitchenAid for grains?

Buy the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 if rice is the point. It was $239 to $242 against $260 to $300 for the KitchenAid in the Nov 18, 2026 listings above, holds 2–11 cups cooked against 2–8 (ATK), and keeps rice warm up to 12 hours against 6.

Buy the KitchenAid KGC3155BM if you'll use its 21 presets for beans, lentils and oats every week and want the scale and tank to replace measuring.
