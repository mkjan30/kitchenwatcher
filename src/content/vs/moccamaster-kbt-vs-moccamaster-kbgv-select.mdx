In the Moccamaster KBT vs KBGV Select choice, the brewing side is the same on paper: same copper element and 196-205°F brew spec (manufacturer spec). [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2461-best-coffee-makers-automatic-drip) measured both at 6 minutes 36 seconds. The difference is the carafe and drip-stop.

Pick the KBT if the pot sits after brewing: America's Test Kitchen found its carafe kept coffee hot for 2 hours. The KBGV Select's hotplate shuts off at 100 minutes (manufacturer spec). Pick the KBGV Select for half pots, an automatic drip-stop or more colors.

## Quick verdict: KBT for slow drinkers, KBGV Select for half pots

- **KBT:** America's Test Kitchen's winner. It measured freshly brewed coffee at 186°F against the KBGV Select's 180°F, and the thermal carafe kept coffee hot for 2 hours.
- **KBGV Select:** a half/full selector (more in our [KBGV Select review](/reviews/moccamaster-kbgv-select/)), an automatic drip-stop, 24 colors and a hotplate that switches off at 100 minutes (manufacturer spec).
- **Neither** is programmable. CNET lists "not programmable" as a KBT con, and [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/technivorm-moccamaster-kbgv-select) says the KBGV Select has no delayed brew.

## What is identical inside both brewers

Moccamaster's product pages and instruction manual give both models a 1.25 L, 40 oz, 10-cup capacity and a full carafe in 4 to 6 minutes at 196-205°F. Both carry a 5-year warranty with repairs available for the life of the brewer. The [Moccamaster manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0528/3545/1043/files/v23.0_ENG_MM_IM_web.pdf?v=1621537159) excludes the carafe and parts from that warranty, and recommends brewing no less than 4 cups at a time.

The independent data only partly backs up the shared internals. America's Test Kitchen measured the same 6:36 average brew time for both, with water in the ideal temperature range for 91% of the cycle on each. But it also observed coffee flowing through the KBGV Select's basket a bit more quickly, and it measured lower strength and extraction from the KBGV (see the table).

## Heat: how long coffee stays hot in each carafe

The KBT keeps coffee hot longer, but how much longer depends on who you ask. America's Test Kitchen reported the thermal carafe kept coffee piping hot for 2 hours. The KBGV Select's glass carafe lost heat much faster over the same 2 hours. Moccamaster's own KBT page is more modest: the carafe maintains coffee quality for up to an hour.

[CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-coffee-maker/) goes furthest, saying the KBT 741's carafe keeps coffee hot for 6 hours. CNET's page shows no temperature measurement behind that figure, so I'd treat it as the outlier. Of the sources that state a basis, the range is one to two hours.

The KBGV Select uses a hotplate instead. Moccamaster's manual lists a holding temperature of 175-185°F, and the US model switches off at 100 minutes; Tom's Guide notes the UK model stops after 40. A hotplate keeps coffee warm by keeping it on heat, which is the trade-off covered in [thermal vs glass carafe](/guides/thermal-vs-glass-carafe/). I found no measurement of how KBGV coffee changes over those 100 minutes.

## Does KBT coffee taste different from KBGV Select coffee?

Evidence here is thin. America's Test Kitchen's 21-person tasting panel described KBT coffee as very strong but smooth, and found the KBGV Select's a bit thin in body and slightly weak. That's one panel, at one time. Tom's Guide's Erin Bashford, by contrast, gave the KBGV Select an Editor's Choice in April 2026.

Moccamaster claims its brewers hit the 18-22% "Golden Cup" extraction range every cycle. America's Test Kitchen's lab numbers only partly agree. It measured 18.9% extraction and 1.22% TDS for the KBT, but 17.6% extraction and 1.14% TDS for the KBGV Select. Those KBGV figures sit just under both the 18% mark and the SCA's 1.15% TDS floor that ATK cites. That lines up with its tasters calling the KBGV a bit weak.

It is one lab run per model, and I found no second independent measurement.

## Drip-stop and half batches: the practical difference

The KBT's drip-stop is a manual switch on the brew basket that lets you slow or stop the flow. Leave it closed by mistake and the basket overflows, per [Black Ink Coffee's troubleshooting guide](https://blackinkcoffee.com/pages/technivorm-moccamaster-review). That makes the KBT more hands-on.

The KBGV Select's drip-stop is automatic: lift the carafe and the flow stops. Its selector adjusts brewing for a half or full carafe, and Moccamaster says the KBGV is certified for both. Tom's Guide reports about 4 minutes for a half batch against about 6.5 minutes for a full one.

Want a thermal carafe with an automatic drip-stop? That's the KBGT. [Crazy Coffee Crave's model-letter guide](https://crazycoffeecrave.com/which-technivorm-moccamaster-should-you-buy/) explains the naming: G is the automatic drip-stop, V the volume selector and T the thermal carafe with no hotplate.

## Size, colors and the $20 price gap

Moccamaster lists the KBT at 15.25 in tall and the KBGV Select at 14 in, so the KBT needs 1.25 in more under a cabinet. The KBGV is 1.25 in wider (12.75 vs 11.5 in). Both weigh 6 lb.

On October 3, 2026, Moccamaster's US store listed the KBT at $349 in 4 colors and the KBGV Select at $369 in 24 colors. Candy Apple Red and Brushed Gold cost $389. The $20 buys the auto drip-stop, half/full selector, hotplate and color choice.

## SCA certification: what is actually confirmed

Moccamaster's KBT page calls it "ECBC certified and SCA approved," and its KBGV page says the KBGV is certified by the SCA for both full and half carafes. Tom's Guide repeats the SCA-approved claim, and America's Test Kitchen's spec lists mark both "SCA Certified: Yes."

The SCA's own [Certified Home Equipment page](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer), as fetched October 3, 2026, lists brewers from OXO, Breville, Fellow, Bonavita and others, but no Technivorm or Moccamaster. The sources conflict, so I can't confirm current certification. Background is in [what SCA certified means](/guides/what-is-sca-certified-coffee-maker/).

## Complaints from reviewers and owners

- **No programming:** neither has a timer or delay brew (CNET, Tom's Guide).
- **Size and price:** CNET lists the KBT as pricier than other options and says it takes up a lot of space.
- **Plastic parts and assembly:** Tom's Guide says the parts are mostly plastic and must go on in a set order.
- **Carafe care:** neither carafe is dishwasher safe, per Moccamaster's manual.
- **Carafe changes:** America's Test Kitchen notes the KBT's thermal carafe was redesigned in 2025 with an improved pour spout, so check which version a listing ships with.

## Which Moccamaster should you buy?

- **Buy the KBGV Select** if you finish the pot before the hotplate's 100-minute shut-off, brew half pots often, want the automatic drip-stop or want a specific color.
- **Buy the KBT** if coffee sits for up to two hours, several people pour at different times, or you don't want a hotplate. It's also $20 cheaper.
- **Look at the KBGT** if you want a thermal carafe and an automatic drip-stop.
- **Spending less?** The [OXO Brew 8-Cup](/reviews/oxo-brew-8-cup/) is on the SCA's own list.

## Maintenance is the same for both

Moccamaster says to descale every 100 cycles or at least every three months, and not to use distilled or reverse-osmosis water because it can damage the copper element. The steps are in [how to descale a coffee maker](/guides/how-to-descale-a-coffee-maker/), and [vinegar vs descaler](/guides/vinegar-vs-descaler-coffee-maker/) covers what to use.
