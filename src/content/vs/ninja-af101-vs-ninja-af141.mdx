For one or two people, the Ninja AF101 is the safer buy because it's the one with independent evidence: TechGearLab named it Editors' Choice and measured 396.8°F at a 400°F setting. The AF141 adds a quart and 200 watts on paper (5 qt, 1750 W vs 4 qt, 1550 W), but I found no lab that has published measurements for it, so choosing it means buying on specs.

## What independent tests found

[TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-air-fryer) gave the AF101 its Editors' Choice, measured an average of 396.8°F at a 400°F setting and a 160°F hottest exterior point, and noted it needed only a 3-minute preheat. [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-air-fryer) named the AF101 its best mid-size air fryer at $120.

Neither publication has tested the AF141. TechGearLab only notes that the AF141 is a newer version with much in common with the AF101. Its table also includes a "Ninja Pro XL", but that's a 6.5-quart model, not the AF141, so its numbers don't apply here. Some comparison sites say the AF141 crisps frozen food about 25% faster; I couldn't find that claim on a Ninja page or in any lab data, so I'd ignore it.

## What owners report

The AF101 has a long track record: 4.56 stars across 1,314 ratings on Target's listing on Sep 27, 2026. Complaints there include fan noise and one owner saying the fan slowed after about 50 uses. One practical problem comes up often enough to plan for, the crisper plate sticking in the basket: a Best Buy Q&A thread on the AF101 had 26 owner answers, mostly advising a firm straight pull on the center handle or warming the silicone bumpers with hot water.

The AF141 is too new at these retailers to have a record. Target's listing had no ratings on Sep 27, 2026, and I didn't find a readable owner-review source with a pattern of complaints. That lack of history is the main risk in choosing it.

## Care and parts

Ninja's AF100-series owner's guide says the basket and crisper plate can go in the dishwasher, stuck-on food should be soaked in warm soapy water, and abrasive cleaners, steel wool and scouring pads are out. The warranty doesn't cover the basket or crisper plate, which Ninja treats as wear parts. On Sep 27, 2026, SharkNinja sold a replacement AF101 basket for $33.99 and a crisper plate for $18.99. The full routine is in [how to clean the Ninja AF101](/guides/how-to-clean-ninja-af101/).

## Availability

The AF101 was $119.99 and in stock at Target on Sep 27, 2026. SharkNinja's store still sells its parts, but in earlier research the fryer itself wasn't listed there. {/* VERIFY: AF101 not listed on sharkninja.com — earlier research Sep 26, not re-checked */} If it disappears, parts availability matters more for how long you'll keep it running. The AF141 was $129.99 at both SharkNinja and Target the same day.

## Who should buy which

- **Buy the AF101** if you cook for one or two and want the model with lab data behind it. It's also $10 cheaper.
- **Buy the AF141** if you regularly cook for three or four and need the extra quart. Nothing I found suggests it's worse; there's just no independent evidence yet.
- **Want 6 quarts with lab data?** The COSORI TurboBlaze and Instant Vortex Plus both have TechGearLab measurements. See [COSORI TurboBlaze vs Ninja AF141](/vs/cosori-turboblaze-vs-ninja-af141/) and the [best air fryers ranking](/best/air-fryers/).
