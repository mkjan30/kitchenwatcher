The Ninja vs Cuisinart food processor choice comes down to price against capacity and quiet.

The Ninja Professional Plus BN601 (9 cups) is the pick for presets, a dough blade and a light base. Food Network's page listed it at $110 at Amazon on Oct 9, 2026. The Cuisinart Custom 14 DFP-14BCNY (14 cups) is the pick for big batches, quieter running and finer purées. ATK's page listed it at $182 at Amazon the same day.

On those Oct 9 listings the Cuisinart cost roughly $70 to $90 more. For that you get 5 more cups, a motor TechGearLab measured 18.5 dB quieter, and a slightly better purée score. You give up the Ninja's programs and its 4.5 lb base.

## Ninja BN601 vs Cuisinart 14 cup: the quick verdict

**Choose the Ninja BN601 if:**
- You want the cheaper machine: $110 at Amazon on Oct 9, 2026, as listed by [Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/how-to/packages/shopping/product-reviews/best-food-processors).
- You like one-touch programs: [CNET](https://www.cnet.com/home/kitchen-and-household/best-food-processor/) notes it was the only model it tried with a separate Chop button, which pulses the blades three times.
- You move it in and out of a cupboard. [TechGearLab](https://www.techgearlab.com/topics/kitchen/best-food-processor) measured its base at 4.5 lb, and in TechGearLab's test it made nut butter in 7 minutes.

**Choose the Cuisinart Custom 14 if:**
- You cook in big batches and want 14 cups (manufacturer spec).
- Noise matters: TechGearLab measured 61.5 dB against the Ninja's 80 dB, and scored its purée 8 against 7.
- You want the long-standing favorite. [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/guides/how-to-use-a-food-processor/the-best-food-processors/) named it the winner in August 2026, and [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-food-processors) found it kneaded pizza dough without stalling or moving on the counter.

## Is the Ninja a cheaper alternative to the Cuisinart food processor?

Yes, if 9 cups is enough. On Oct 9, 2026, Food Network's page listed the Ninja at $110 at Amazon, while ATK's and Reviewed's pages listed the Cuisinart at $182 and $199.99 at Amazon. That puts the Cuisinart about $70 to $90 higher. CNET's page showed the Cuisinart at $239 at Amazon the same day, so the outlets' price widgets do not agree.

The extra money buys 5 more cups, a motor TechGearLab measured 18.5 dB quieter, and one more point for purée. What you give up is the Ninja's preset programs, its dedicated dough blade and a base about 8 lb lighter. TechGearLab's list prices of $130 and $320 are well above those listings, so check the price tracker on this page before you buy rather than trusting any single figure.

## Chopping and pureeing: where the Cuisinart pulls ahead

The Ninja chops fast. CNET made pico de gallo in scarcely two hits of the pulse button, and its separate Chop button pulses three times for you. Food Network praised its double blades for larger quantities, but found it did not purée pesto as finely as the other machines it tried. TechGearLab says it takes a little longer to spin down after you release Pulse, which makes precise control harder.

The Cuisinart's chopping record is mixed. ATK got uniform mirepoix from it, yet says the redesigned chopping blade "leaves slightly bigger gaps". In ATK's test it could not incorporate egg yolks into a single batch of mayonnaise; a double batch worked. TechGearLab scored its onion chop 5 and carrot chop 3 out of 10, against the Ninja's 7 and 7.

For purée, TechGearLab's scores (8 against 7) and Food Network's pesto note point the same way. For chopping the evidence is thin: CNET ran both machines but did not score them, and TechGearLab's overall chop scores tie at 6.

## Dough, slicing and shredding: the sources split

Dough is the most contested point. The Ninja has a dough blade and a Dough program; its BN600C guide says not to run HIGH with the dough blade fitted. TechGearLab still scored the Cuisinart higher on pizza dough (8 against 6) and noted the Ninja's motor sounded like it was working hard.

CNET says the Cuisinart has no dough hook and calls it not the best pick for bakers. Reviewed found the opposite in practice: the Cuisinart's standard blade kneaded pizza dough into a smooth, elastic ball without stalling or moving around the counter.

For slicing and shredding, TechGearLab scored them 7 to 7 on slicing and 7 to 6 on shredding in the Ninja's favor. Reviewed found the Cuisinart shredded mozzarella unevenly, and TechGearLab notes it has no slicing or shredding adjustment unless you buy extra discs. For technique, see [can you knead dough in a food processor](/guides/can-you-knead-dough-in-a-food-processor/), [how to shred cheese](/guides/how-to-shred-cheese-in-a-food-processor/) and [how to slice vegetables](/guides/how-to-slice-vegetables-in-a-food-processor/).

## Noise, size and weight on the counter

TechGearLab measured the Ninja at 80 dB and the Cuisinart at 61.5 dB from 3 feet. CNET calls the Ninja perhaps the loudest model it tried, and Reviewed says the Cuisinart is quiet enough not to shut down conversation, so the sources point the same way.

The Cuisinart is wider but shorter: 9.38 x 12.5 x 15 inches against the Ninja's 7.76 x 9.88 x 16.02, per CNET. Reviewed says the Cuisinart fits under a cabinet.

Weight is where the figures conflict. TechGearLab and ATK measured the Cuisinart base at 12.4 and 12.45 lb, while CNET lists 18 lb and Reviewed 17 lb. Neither says whether its figure includes the bowl and parts, and CNET warns that the heft discourages pulling it out of the cabinet. Either way the Ninja's 4.5 lb measured base is far easier to lift. For bowl sizing, see [what size food processor do I need](/guides/what-size-food-processor-do-i-need/).

## Cleaning both machines: the reviews disagree on the Ninja

Ninja's BN600C guide says all attachments are dishwasher safe and recommends the top rack only, with the blade assembly removed from the bowl first. The sources disagree on hand-washing. TechGearLab lists "difficult to clean by hand" as a con and scored the Ninja's cleaning 5 against the Cuisinart's 6. Food Network says it is easy to clean by hand.

ATK says the Cuisinart's bowl and blade are easy to handle and clean, and all three blades store inside the bowl. Reviewed calls it easy to clean and says Cuisinart encourages washing the parts in the dishwasher. CNET found its bowl and lid a bit fussy to align, and Reviewed found the chute lock slightly inconvenient. See [how to clean a food processor](/guides/how-to-clean-a-food-processor/) and [are food processor parts dishwasher safe](/guides/are-food-processor-parts-dishwasher-safe/).

## Cuisinart riveted-blade recall and Ninja manual limits

CPSC recall 17-050, announced December 13, 2016, covers the riveted blades in about 8 million Cuisinart processors sold in the US (about 300,000 more in Canada), including model numbers beginning DFP-14. The recalled blades have four rivets, are silver stainless steel and have a beige plastic center hub; they were sold July 1996 through December 2015. The CPSC lists 69 reports of broken blade pieces in food, including 30 reports of mouth lacerations or tooth injuries. It says to stop using the riveted blade and contact Cuisinart (877-339-2534) for a free replacement.

This matters for a used or older Custom 14, not one sold after 2015. When we checked on October 9, 2026, the saferproducts.gov recall database listed no later recall covering DFP-14 models. It also listed no Ninja food processor recall; its SharkNinja entries are Foodi pressure cookers and BL660 blenders. Our [food processor recall check](/guides/food-processor-recall-check/) covers that check for every brand.

Ninja's guide for the BN600C (the Canadian sibling) says not to fill past the 9-cup line, not to blend hot liquids, and not to load the bowl with ice cubes for crushing. If the thermal switch trips, unplug the unit and let it cool for approximately 15 minutes. The control panel lights flash until the bowl and feed chute lid are both locked in place.

The [safety interlock guide](/guides/food-processor-safety-interlock/) and [food processor overheating guide](/guides/food-processor-overheating/) go further. We could not read Cuisinart's own booklet, so ATK's 3-cup liquid maximum is the only Cuisinart limit cited here.

## Ratings and the complaints reviewers repeat

CNET's April 2025 update relays an Amazon rating of 4.8 out of 5 from more than 5,000 ratings for the Ninja; we found no current, sourced count for either model. The complaints that repeat across reviewers are noise and hand-cleaning for the Ninja (CNET, TechGearLab) and purée fineness (Food Network). For the Cuisinart they are the redesigned blade and no mini bowl (ATK), the slow 19-minute nut butter (TechGearLab), and its weight and fussy lid alignment (CNET). We did not find a reliable failure rate for either model.

## Which should you buy: Ninja BN601 or Cuisinart Custom 14?

- **Buy the Ninja BN601 if** you want to spend about $110 (Amazon, Oct 9, 2026, per Food Network's listing), like preset programs (CNET), and want a 4.5 lb base that's easy to store (TechGearLab).
- **Buy the Cuisinart Custom 14 if** you need 14 cups, want the quieter motor (61.5 dB, TechGearLab) and finer purées, and prefer ATK's current winner.
- **Consider neither if** your budget is under $100: see [Hamilton Beach vs Ninja](/vs/hamilton-beach-70730-vs-ninja-bn601/). For a premium alternative at the Cuisinart's price, see [Cuisinart vs KitchenAid](/vs/cuisinart-dfp-14bcny-vs-kitchenaid-kfp1318/).
