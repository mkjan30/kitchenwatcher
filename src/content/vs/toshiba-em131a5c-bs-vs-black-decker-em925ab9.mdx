For most people, the Toshiba EM131A5C-BS is the better-documented buy: 1.2 cu ft, 1,100 W output (manufacturer spec), a sensor menu and a 12.4-inch turntable, and an independent tester has published measurements for it. The Black+Decker EM925AB9 (0.9 cu ft, 900 W per Tom's Guide and Reviewed) cost $49 less on Nov 27, 2026, but I found no manual for it, and Reviewed says owner reviews raise durability doubts.

Kitchen Watcher did not test either model. This Toshiba vs Black and Decker microwave comparison is built from the Toshiba manual, two buying guides, a test lab's review, a second lab's public picks and a safety alert, as described in [how we research](/how-we-research/).

## Quick verdict: which one should you buy?

For the Toshiba EM131A5C vs EM925AB9 choice, turntable size, counter width and price decide most cases:

- **Pick the Toshiba if** you heat dinner plates or casserole dishes (12.4-inch turntable, manufacturer spec), want sensor reheat, or want the model with independent measurements behind it. It was $133.97 at Amazon on Nov 27, 2026, a Black Friday sale price marked down from $148.99, per Tom's Guide's price widget.
- **Pick the Black+Decker if** your counter or budget is tight. It was $84.97 at Amazon in the same widget on the same day, a sale price marked down from $106.99, and its published width is roughly 1.4 to 1.9 inches less than the Toshiba's, depending on which figures you use. Check that your plates fit a 10.6-inch turntable first (per Tom's Guide).

## Size: 1.2 vs 0.9 cu ft, and will your plate fit?

The 1.2 vs 0.9 cu ft difference matters most at the turntable. The Toshiba's is 12.4 inches (manufacturer spec); the Black+Decker's is 10.6 inches, per Tom's Guide. Tom's Guide says the cubic-foot figure "doesn't give you a realistic estimate of usable space" and advises measuring the turntable and checking that your dish can rotate on it.

TechGearLab says the Toshiba's 12.4-inch turntable fits large casserole dishes and dinner plates. No source I read published a usable-space figure for the Black+Decker.

The trade-off is counter space. The Black+Decker is 18.7 inches wide per Reviewed; the middle figure in Tom's Guide's listing is 19.1 inches. The Toshiba is 20.5 inches per its manual (20.6 inches wide per Tom's Guide). [TechGearLab](https://djd1xqjx2kdnv.cloudfront.net/reviews/kitchen/microwave/toshiba-em131a5c) says the Toshiba's big turntable comes at the cost of significant counter space. For a general walk-through of sizes, see [what size microwave do I need](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Power: 1,100 W vs 900 W, and why you'll see 1,550 W

Microwaves have two wattage ratings. Input is what the oven draws from the outlet; output is the cooking power. Toshiba's manual lists 1,550 W rated input and 1,100 W rated output. Tom's Guide's spec box prints "Power: 1550 watts" while its text says 1,000 W, so the same page states the wattage two different ways. Output, 1,100 W, is the figure to compare, and RTINGS also describes it as a 1,100-watt microwave.

The Black+Decker's 900 W comes from Tom's Guide and Reviewed, and neither says whether it's input or output.

The only published measurements I could read are for the Toshiba. TechGearLab took refrigerated water from 43°F to 122°F in 45 seconds, a 79°F rise. Reviewed lists "not very quick at heating" as a Black+Decker con but published no timings. More on what those ratings mean in [what wattage microwave do I need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Heating evenness: the sources disagree on the Toshiba

Tom's Guide credits the Toshiba with "very even cooking performance." TechGearLab found the opposite. In its heat map test, hot spots concentrated in the center of the turntable while the outer edges stayed cooler; it describes uneven heating patterns in its verdict and scored heating 5.3 out of 10. Pizza it reheated read mostly between 100°F and 180°F on its infrared thermometer.

The two can't both be the full picture, and I'd weight the one with a heat map.

For the Black+Decker, the only evidence is Reviewed's line that it "performed well in most tests," with no per-model results. Neither model is described as an inverter oven: the Toshiba manual describes no inverter, and neither Tom's Guide nor Reviewed mentions one on the Black+Decker. Why that matters, and why some labs think it matters less than marketing says, is in [is an inverter microwave worth it](/guides/is-an-inverter-microwave-worth-it/).

## Controls, presets and the Toshiba's sensor menu

The Toshiba manual lists a Sensor Menu (Sensor Reheat, Frozen Pizza, Frozen Entree, Potato, Rice, Veggie), an Auto Menu with Custom Cook and Soften/Melt, a Popcorn pad, 1 lb Defrost and weight-based Custom Defrost, and a Favorite button that stores up to three cooking procedures. It also has Start/Add 30 Sec, one-touch start pads for 1 to 6 minutes, and a child lock (hold for 3 seconds). Tom's Guide mentions a mute button. Tom's also says the oven has "no sensors" and then praises "its humidity sensor"; the manual's Sensor Menu settles that it has one.

TechGearLab scored the Toshiba's defrost 9.5 out of 10, thawing a meat block in under 10 minutes without cooking it, but called sensor reheat "disappointing." [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/best/countertop) says the membrane touch panel doesn't give as much feedback as physical controls, and that adjusting the power level takes more steps.

The Black+Decker has six one-touch presets: popcorn, potato, pizza, frozen vegetable, beverage and dinner plate, per Tom's Guide. Reviewed lists 10 power levels, a +30 Seconds button, a child lock and a push-button door; Tom's also mentions the child lock and push-button door. Tom's only listed con is a quiet alarm. Neither guide describes a sensor.

The gap to name: none of these sources has run these two models against each other.

## Durability, the Toshiba self-start alert and recalls

Reviewed says of the Black+Decker that "consumer reviews raise questions about its long-term durability." None of the sources I read publishes failure data for either model.

For the Toshiba, [Consumer Reports' safety alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) of June 25, 2026, on Toshiba microwaves starting on their own counts 28 incidents reported on SaferProducts.gov. Model EM131A5C is cited in one; all but four involve the ML2-EC10SA air fryer-microwave, and Midea's statement addresses only that model. CR says it "has not experienced a similar problem with the Toshiba microwaves we have tested."

It is a safety alert, not a recall. The full account is in [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/).

Neither model appears in the [CPSC recall database](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&ProductName=microwave), checked Nov 27, 2026. Consumer Reports says Toshiba-brand microwaves are made by Midea. I couldn't confirm who makes the Black+Decker. To check any model yourself, see [how to check for a microwave recall](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).
