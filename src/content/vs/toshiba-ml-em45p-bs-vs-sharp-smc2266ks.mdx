In the Toshiba vs Sharp microwave choice, buy the Sharp SMC2266KS if you cook big dishes and have 24.4 inches of counter width. Sharp rates it 2.2 cu ft, and RTINGS named it its Best Upper Mid-Range countertop pick. Buy the Toshiba ML-EM45P if you want to spend less or have a tighter counter. Reviewed named it Best Microwave For Most People. No outlet has tested both.

Kitchen Watcher doesn't test products. This page compares published specs and other people's findings; [how we research](/how-we-research/) explains the method.

**Choose the Sharp SMC2266KS if:**

- You need room: Sharp rates it 2.2 cu ft with a 16.5-in turntable, and [RTINGS](https://www.rtings.com/microwave/reviews/sharp/smc2266ks) says it fits "a full-size casserole dish or large mixing bowl".
- You want it quiet: RTINGS calls it "exceptionally quiet" and scores its In-Use Noise 9.0 out of 10.
- You want an inverter: Sharp's spec sheet lists inverter technology; the ML-EM45P's page lists none.

**Choose the Toshiba ML-EM45P if:**

- Price matters: $154.58 at Amazon per Reviewed's price listing, against a $239.99 Black November sale price ($269.99 MSRP) on Sharp's store, both on Nov 27, 2026.
- Your counter is tight: Toshiba lists it at 21.8 in wide against Sharp's 24.4 in.
- You reheat and make popcorn a lot: [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/small-appliances/best-right-now/best-countertop-microwaves) says it "excelled in popcorn and reheating tests".

## Size and footprint: 1.6 vs 2.2 cu ft microwave

The Sharp is 2.6 inches wider than the Toshiba. Toshiba lists the ML-EM45P at 21.8 in wide, 19.2 in deep and 12.9 in tall, with a 13.6-in turntable. Toshiba says it fits a 13-inch pizza. Sharp's spec sheet lists the SMC2266KS at 24.4 in wide, 19.6 in deep and 13.6 in tall, with a 16.5-in tray and a 17.8 x 18.9 x 11.2 in cavity.

Advertised capacity overstates usable room. Sharp's own spec sheet says its capacity is calculated from maximum width, depth and height. It adds that "actual capacity for holding food is less." RTINGS still scores the Sharp's internal capacity 9.7 out of 10 and says it rivals many over-the-range models.

The Toshiba's depth is in dispute. Toshiba says 19.2 in; Reviewed's spec list says 17.91 in. Plan around the larger figure and check the owner's manual for clearance before you buy; we haven't read either model's manual.

General sizing advice is in [what size microwave do I need](/guides/what-size-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Power and speed: same rating, one scored

Both are rated 1,200 W, per Toshiba's page and Sharp's spec sheet, which calls it output power. Toshiba's page also lists 1.2 kW as "power consumption", so it doesn't say plainly whether 1,200 W is input or output.

RTINGS scores the Sharp's Power 9.9 out of 10 and says it "cooks very quickly at maximum power". Nobody has published a speed measurement for the ML-EM45P. Reviewed says it excelled in its popcorn and reheating tests but publishes no times.

For what the wattage figures mean, see [what wattage microwave do I need](/guides/what-wattage-microwave-do-i-need/).

## Inverter (Sharp) vs non-inverter (Toshiba ML-EM45P)

Sharp's spec sheet says its inverter technology "provides even distribution of cooking power". That is a manufacturer claim. RTINGS says on its countertop list that it has "found no real difference in how evenly microwaves heat your food". Reviewed lists "not the most even heating" as a Toshiba con.

The sources disagree about how much evenness differs between models at all. The wider debate is in [is an inverter microwave worth it](/guides/is-an-inverter-microwave-worth-it/).

## Sensor cooking, presets and controls

Toshiba lists a Smart Humidity Sensor, 10 programs and 10 power levels in its spec table, though a feature bullet on the same page says "5 Power Levels". It also lists a memory function to preset your daily menu, a position-memory turntable, a mute function you press for 3 seconds, and standby power cut by 50%. Reviewed calls the controls straightforward.

Sharp's spec sheet lists a Sensor Cook menu, a +30 key, Express Cook for one-touch cooking of 1 to 6 minutes, Melt/Soften settings, auto defrost, two-stage cooking, a control lock and 11 power levels.

RTINGS says a simple task like 3 minutes at 100% takes one button. Reduced power or a set cook time takes multiple presses. There's no dedicated mute button; you hold 8 for three seconds, per RTINGS.

## Noise, display and everyday annoyances

RTINGS calls the Sharp "exceptionally quiet" and scores its In-Use Noise 9.0 out of 10. It also says its membrane touch panel "is less responsive than physical buttons or dials".

The Toshiba's weak points come from Reviewed. The control panel isn't always easy to see in the dark, the look is "functional rather than beautiful", and it's too large for small kitchens. Reviewed's own product page lists "attractive design" as a pro, so even that source is split on looks.

## Toshiba and Sharp prices, warranty and recall check

On Nov 27, 2026, Reviewed's price listing showed the Toshiba at $154.58 at Amazon, and Sharp's store had the SMC2266KS at a $239.99 Black November sale price against a $269.99 MSRP. Sharp's spec sheet lists a 1-year limited warranty on parts and labor, plus 4 more years on the magnetron tube (part only). Toshiba's product page doesn't state a warranty term.

[CPSC Recall API](https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json&RecallTitle=Toshiba) searches for "Toshiba" and "Sharp" on Nov 27, 2026 returned no microwave recall. [Consumer Reports' June 25, 2026 alert](https://www.consumerreports.org/product-safety/some-toshiba-microwaves-are-starting-on-their-own-a4659854545/) about Toshiba microwaves starting on their own is not a recall. It names the ML2-EC10SA, ML2-EC09SA, EM131A5C and EM925A5A-SS, not the ML-EM45P. Details are in [Toshiba microwave turning on by itself](/guides/toshiba-microwave-turning-on-by-itself/) and the [microwave recall check](/guides/microwave-recall-check/).

## Which large countertop microwave should you buy?

- **Casseroles and big platters:** the Sharp, with its 16.5-in tray and 2.2 cu ft rating. RTINGS says it fits a full-size casserole dish.
- **A tight counter or a budget under $175:** the Toshiba ML-EM45P, 21.8 in wide and $154.58 at Amazon per Reviewed's listing on Nov 27, 2026.
- **Torn between them:** look at the ML-EM45PIT, the Toshiba RTINGS actually tested. It was RTINGS's Best Mid-Range pick and heats "about as fast as the Sharp SMC2266KS", though RTINGS says its chamber is smaller than the Sharp's.
