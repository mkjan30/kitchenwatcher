In the Zojirushi vs Cuckoo rice cooker choice, buy the Zojirushi NS-ZCC10 for the most consistent rice with the least fuss. Buy the Cuckoo CR-0655F to pay about $100 less (Amazon prices on ATK's page, Nov 18, 2026) and get faster white rice. America's Test Kitchen named the Zojirushi its winner, and CNN Underscored called it its best rice cooker. The Cuckoo is CNN's budget pick and Engadget's best for most people, but ATK put it in Recommended with Reservations.

## Which makes better rice, the Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy or the Cuckoo?

ATK and CNN Underscored tested both models and found the Zojirushi Neuro Fuzzy more consistent than the Cuckoo. Engadget, which also tested both, picked the Cuckoo as best for most people. ATK says the NS-ZCC10 "consistently produced excellent rice in both small and large batches." CNN Underscored found it still made edible rice when its testers added a cup too much or too little water. [Reviewed](https://www.reviewed.com/cooking/best-right-now/the-best-rice-cookers) named it Best Overall; Reviewed tested the Cuckoo CR-0631F, not the CR-0655F, so it adds nothing on the Cuckoo here.

The Cuckoo's results are good but less even. ATK got great long-grain white rice from it, but short-grain white and brown rice came out a little wet at Cuckoo's recommended water ratios. CNN found its rice "a bit stickier and gummier" than the Zojirushi's, though still fluffy. Engadget wanted a touch longer cook on brown rice. Evidence on its brown rice is thin: two short comments, no measurements.

## Which cooks faster, the Cuckoo or the Zojirushi?

The Cuckoo is faster on white rice, though no single tester timed both on the same rice. CNN Underscored timed it at 34 minutes, and 32 on Quick Cook. Engadget timed Zojirushi white rice at 48 minutes and brown rice at "just shy of two hours"; Quick mode saved about ten minutes. CNN's Zojirushi sushi rice took 42 minutes, or 31 on Quick Cook.

The manuals agree. Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual lists 48 to 55 minutes for regular white rice, 36 to 42 on Quick and 84 to 107 for brown. Cuckoo's manual lists 35 to 53 minutes for white ("Glutinous") and 32 to 44 on Turbo in its cook-time table, though its Turbo note says about 31 to 36 minutes. Zojirushi's [rice cooker FAQ](https://www.zojirushi.com/pages/faq-rice-cookers) says its cookers soak rice for about 30 minutes and steam it for about 15, which is why they're slow.

## Which is easier to use day to day: water lines, buttons and lid

ATK found the Zojirushi's water lines "clearly labeled in white writing." But long-grain white cooks best on Mixed, not White, which ATK says isn't obvious. Reviewed found the control panel "less than intuitive" and had to hold the cooker still and press hard.

The Cuckoo's lines are imprinted into the metal, which ATK found hard to read, and Engadget says the recessed markings need a closer eye when cleaning so starchy film doesn't get trapped in them. ATK also found the buttons hard to decipher and noted five loud beeps. Engadget says the lid springs open hard enough to rock the unit; CNN notes it has no carrying handle.

Cuckoo's manual calls white rice "Glutinous," which is easy to misread as sticky rice. Its My Mode sets SOAK and HEAT in 4 steps each: longer soaking gives softer, stickier rice, and the manual warns a longer HEAT can scorch.

## How long can each keep rice warm?

Zojirushi's NS-ZCC manual says "Do not keep rice warm for more than 12 hours." Only white rice should be kept warm; Extended Keep Warm holds about 140°F. CNN Underscored says Extended Keep Warm runs an extra 8 hours after the regular 12. The sources conflict, and the manual governs.

ATK found the NS-ZCC10 kept rice at a food-safe temperature for 12 hours. Cuckoo's manual recommends keeping rice warm for less than 12 hours. The display blinks once rice has been warm for 24 hours, and the manual says to avoid keeping mixed or brown rice warm.

The FDA's [Bad Bug Book](https://www.fda.gov/media/83271/download) says the vomiting type of Bacillus cereus illness most often affects rice and other starchy foods. More on safe holding is in [how long rice can stay in a rice cooker](/guides/how-long-can-rice-stay-in-rice-cooker/).

## Which is easier to clean: Cuckoo's Auto Clean vs Zojirushi hand care

The Cuckoo has an Auto Clean steam cycle: its manual says to fill water to level 2, and CNN timed the cycle at 25 minutes. Its manual rules out tough or metal scrubbers and says the coating can peel if the pot is misused.

Zojirushi's FAQ says its rice-cooker parts are not dishwasher safe. The NS-ZCC manual bans vinegar in the pan and limits sandpaper to the heating plate and center sensor. See [how to clean a rice cooker](/guides/how-to-clean-a-rice-cooker/).

## Japanese vs Korean rice cooker: does it matter for these two?

Japanese vs Korean rice cooker pages often assume the Korean one uses pressure. Not here: Zojirushi's manual lists "Direct Heating," CNN calls the CR-0655F the Cuckoo 6-Cup Micom, and ATK lists fuzzy logic for both. Neither is sold as a pressure or induction model (ATK tested Cuckoo's pressure model, the CRP-P0609S, separately); [induction vs micom rice cookers](/guides/induction-vs-micom-rice-cooker/) explains the difference.

Reviewed says the NS-ZCC10 is assembled in Japan. None of the sources here says where the CR-0655F is made.

## Cuckoo or Zojirushi on price and warranty

On Nov 18, 2026, ATK's page listed the Zojirushi at $239 and the Cuckoo at $140 at Amazon; CNN Underscored listed them at $242 and $140 at Amazon. CNN's text elsewhere calls the Cuckoo "around $80," which none of its own listings show, so treat that figure with caution.

Engadget lists a 1-year warranty for both. Cuckoo's manual confirms 1 year on parts and labor, and Zojirushi's own limited warranty for electrical products covers manufacturing defects for one year from the original retail purchase. A CPSC Recall API search on Nov 18, 2026 for "Zojirushi" and "Cuckoo" in recall titles returned no records, and a search of product names containing "rice" listed neither brand. See the [rice cooker recall check](/guides/rice-cooker-recall-check/).
